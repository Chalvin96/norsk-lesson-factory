## Where is everyone going? {#where-is-everyone-going role=orient objectives=obj-basic-prepositions-place-time-01}

Maja and Jonas are arranging to meet after work. Their friend Nora may join them later.

::: {.reading translation="Hi, Jonas. Where are you?" dialogue_id=anchor-meeting speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Hei, Jonas. Hvor er du?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’m at work. I’m leaving in ten minutes." dialogue_id=anchor-meeting speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg er på jobb. Jeg drar om ti minutter.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Can you meet me at the café by the station?" dialogue_id=anchor-meeting speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Kan du møte meg på kafeen ved stasjonen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I’m walking to the café." dialogue_id=anchor-meeting speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, jeg går til kafeen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Nora is at the doctor’s now. She is coming in an hour." dialogue_id=anchor-meeting speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Nora er hos legen nå. Hun kommer om en time.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Wasn’t she at the doctor’s two days ago?" dialogue_id=anchor-meeting speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Var hun ikke hos legen for to dager siden?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, she was. She was ill for three days, so she has another appointment." dialogue_id=anchor-meeting speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jo. Hun var syk i tre dager, så hun har en ny time.
:::

::: {.reading translation="All right. I worked at the office for nine hours today. I’ll stay at the café for an hour." dialogue_id=anchor-meeting speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Greit. Jeg jobbet på kontoret i ni timer i dag. Jeg blir på kafeen i en time.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That works. See you soon." dialogue_id=anchor-meeting speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Det passer. Vi sees snart.
:::

The prepositions **på**, **om**, **til**, **hos**, **for**, and **i** tell us how places and times relate to an action. The fixed frame **for … siden** uses **for** with an amount of time and **siden** to locate an event before now. Choosing the right preposition or time frame lets you distinguish being somewhere from going somewhere, future time from past time, and duration from other time relationships.
## Being in a place: i and på {#being-in-a-place role=model objectives=obj-basic-prepositions-place-time-01}

Norwegian commonly uses both **i** and **på** for location. English often translates both as *in* or *at*, so direct translation is not enough.

Use **i** with many enclosed places and with towns, cities, and countries:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er i butikken.
- en: I am in the shop.
- no: Hun bor i Oslo.
- en: She lives in Oslo.
- no: De er i Norge.
- en: They are in Norway.
- no: Barna leker i parken.
- en: The children are playing in the park.
:::

Use **på** in many familiar expressions connected with workplaces, schools, offices, cafés, and activities:

::: examples
- no: Jonas er på jobb.
- en: Jonas is at work.
- no: Maja er på kafeen.
- en: Maja is at the café.
- no: Læreren er på skolen.
- en: The teacher is at the school.
- no: Vi er på kontoret.
- en: We are at the office.
:::

These are useful phrase patterns, not a perfect geometric system. A person can physically be inside both a shop and a café, but ordinary Norwegian commonly says **i butikken** and **på kafeen**. Learn the preposition together with the place expression.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Hun jobber i Oslo.
- en: She works in Oslo.
- no: Hun jobber på kontoret.
- en: She works at the office.
- no: Vi møtes i parken.
- en: We are meeting in the park.
- no: Vi møtes på kafeen.
- en: We are meeting at the café.
:::

A common mistake is to assume that every building takes **i** because someone is physically inside it. Norwegian often chooses the preposition according to the conventional expression and the activity associated with the place.

## Location or destination? {#location-or-destination role=contrast objectives=obj-basic-prepositions-place-time-01}

The next decision is whether someone is already in a place or moving toward it.

For a simple destination, **til** often means *to*:

::: examples
- no: Jeg går til kafeen.
- en: I am walking to the café.
- no: Hun reiser til Oslo.
- en: She is travelling to Oslo.
- no: Vi kjører til butikken.
- en: We are driving to the shop.
:::

Compare the destination with the resulting location:

::: examples
- no: Jonas går til kafeen.
- en: Jonas is going to the café.
- no: Jonas er på kafeen.
- en: Jonas is at the café.
- no: Maja reiser til Oslo.
- en: Maja is travelling to Oslo.
- no: Maja er i Oslo.
- en: Maja is in Oslo.
:::

The compact rule is:

- Use **i** or **på** to say where someone is.
- Use **til** for a simple destination: where someone is going.

Do not replace every location preposition with **til** merely because the verb describes an action. What matters is whether the place is the destination.

::: examples
- no: Hun jobber i Bergen.
- en: She works in Bergen.
:::

Here, Bergen is her work location, not a destination.

::: examples
- no: Hun reiser til Bergen.
- en: She travels to Bergen.
:::

Here, Bergen is the destination.

Some fixed expressions with activities can also use **på** after a movement verb, particularly **dra på jobb**, “go to work.” The basic **til** contrast remains most useful with clear destinations such as a named city, a shop, a café, or another person.

## Visiting people and professionals: hos and til {#visiting-people-and-professionals role=contrast objectives=obj-basic-prepositions-place-time-01}

Use **hos** when someone is at another person’s home, workplace, or professional service.

::: examples
- no: Jeg er hos Maja.
- en: I am at Maja’s place.
- no: Nora er hos legen.
- en: Nora is at the doctor’s.
- no: Barna er hos bestemor.
- en: The children are at their grandmother’s.
- no: Han er hos frisøren.
- en: He is at the hairdresser’s.
:::

For movement toward that person or professional, use **til**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg drar til Maja.
- en: I am going to Maja’s place.
- no: Nora skal til legen.
- en: Nora is going to the doctor.
- no: Barna går til bestemor.
- en: The children are going to their grandmother’s.
:::

Compare the two questions:

::: examples
- no: Hvor er Nora? Hun er hos legen.
- en: Where is Nora? She is at the doctor’s.
- no: Hvor skal Nora? Hun skal til legen.
- en: Where is Nora going? She is going to the doctor.
:::

Do not use **hos** as a general translation of English *at*. It is associated with a person or professional, not an ordinary city or object.

::: examples
- no: Jeg er i Oslo.
- en: I am in Oslo.
:::

Not a **hos** phrase: Oslo is a city, not someone Nora is visiting.
## Looking forward from now: om {#looking-forward-from-now role=model objectives=obj-basic-prepositions-place-time-01}

Use **om** plus an amount of time to say how long it is from now until a future event.

::: examples
- no: Jeg drar om ti minutter.
- en: I am leaving in ten minutes.
- no: Nora kommer om en time.
- en: Nora is coming in an hour.
- no: Vi møtes om to dager.
- en: We are meeting in two days.
- no: Toget går om en halvtime.
- en: The train leaves in half an hour.
:::

English uses *in* here, but Norwegian does not normally use **i** for this future meaning. The question behind the expression is: “How long from now?”

::: examples
- no: Kafeen stenger om en time.
- en: The café closes in an hour.
:::

This does not say that closing lasts one hour. It says that one hour will pass before the café closes.
## Looking back from now: for … siden {#looking-back-from-now role=contrast objectives=obj-basic-prepositions-place-time-01}

Use **for … siden** to locate an event a stated amount of time before now. The amount goes between **for** and **siden**.

::: examples
- no: Nora var hos legen for to dager siden.
- en: Nora was at the doctor’s two days ago.
- no: Bussen kom for ti minutter siden.
- en: The bus arrived ten minutes ago.
- no: Vi møttes for en uke siden.
- en: We met a week ago.
- no: Han flyttet til Oslo for tre år siden.
- en: He moved to Oslo three years ago.
:::

The two time patterns point in opposite directions:

::: examples
- no: Vi møtes om to dager.
- en: We are meeting in two days.
- no: Vi møttes for to dager siden.
- en: We met two days ago.
:::

A useful mental timeline is:

::: examples
- no: for to dager siden — nå — om to dager
- en: two days ago — now — in two days
:::

Do not use **om** simply because a time quantity follows it. First decide whether the event lies in the future or the past.
## Saying how long something lasts: i {#saying-how-long-something-lasts role=contrast objectives=obj-basic-prepositions-place-time-01}

Use **i** with an amount of time when the expression tells how long a state or activity lasts.

::: examples
- no: Hun var syk i tre dager.
- en: She was ill for three days.
- no: Jeg jobbet i ni timer.
- en: I worked for nine hours.
- no: Vi skal være på kafeen i en time.
- en: We are going to be at the café for an hour.
- no: De bodde i Norge i to år.
- en: They lived in Norway for two years.
:::

Here, **i en time** describes the length of the activity or situation. It does not tell us when the activity begins.

Compare future time and duration:

::: examples
- no: Vi drar om en time.
- en: We are leaving in an hour.
:::

One hour passes before the departure.

::: examples
- no: Vi blir der i en time.
- en: We are staying there for an hour.
:::

The stay lasts one hour.

This distinction explains two lines from the opening scene:

::: examples
- no: Nora kommer om en time.
- en: Nora is coming in an hour.
- no: Jonas blir på kafeen i en time.
- en: Jonas is staying at the café for an hour.
:::
## More practice with duration: i {#more-practice-with-duration role=model objectives=obj-basic-prepositions-place-time-01}

Use **i** before an amount of time to measure how long an activity lasts.

::: examples
- no: Jeg leste boka i to timer.
- en: I read the book for two hours.
- no: Hun skrev i tjue minutter.
- en: She wrote for twenty minutes.
- no: Vi gikk i en time.
- en: We walked for an hour.
:::

In each sentence, the time expression gives the duration of the activity.
## Back at the café {#back-at-the-cafe role=model objectives=obj-basic-prepositions-place-time-01}

Jonas has now arrived. Maja sends Nora a message.

::: {.reading translation="Jonas is at the café now. Where are you?" dialogue_id=cafe-follow-up speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jonas er på kafeen nå. Hvor er du?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I am still at the doctor’s, but I’m leaving in five minutes." dialogue_id=cafe-follow-up speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Jeg er fortsatt hos legen, men jeg drar om fem minutter.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. It doesn’t take long to walk to the café." dialogue_id=cafe-follow-up speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Fint. Det tar ikke lang tid å gå til kafeen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, that’s fine. I’m walking to the café." dialogue_id=cafe-follow-up speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Nei, det går fint. Jeg går til kafeen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. We are staying here for an hour." dialogue_id=cafe-follow-up speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Bra. Vi blir her i en time.
:::

The same forms now work together:

- **på kafeen** gives Jonas’s location.
- **hos legen** describes Nora’s visit location.
- **om fem minutter** looks forward to her departure.
- **til kafeen** gives Nora’s destination.
- **i en time** gives the planned duration of the café visit.

## Common mistakes to avoid {#common-mistakes-to-avoid role=contrast objectives=obj-basic-prepositions-place-time-01}

Do not choose a Norwegian preposition solely by matching one English word.

::: examples
- no: Jeg kommer om ti minutter.
- en: I am coming in ten minutes.
:::

Here, English *in* means future distance, so Norwegian uses **om**.

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobber i ti minutter.
- en: I work for ten minutes.
:::

Here, the time expression is duration, so Norwegian uses **i**.

Keep location and destination separate:

::: examples
- no: Hun er hos tannlegen.
- en: She is at the dentist’s.
- no: Hun skal til tannlegen.
- en: She is going to the dentist.
:::

Finally, learn common place expressions as complete units:

::: examples
- no: i Oslo
- en: in Oslo
- no: i butikken
- en: in the shop
- no: på jobb
- en: at work
- no: på kafeen
- en: at the café
- no: hos legen
- en: at the doctor’s
:::
