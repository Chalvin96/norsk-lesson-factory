## An evening plan {#an-evening-plan role=orient}

Maja is preparing dinner for some friends. Henrik is helping her work out how everyone will get home.

::: {.reading translation='Do you know if Nora is coming tonight?' dialogue_id='evening-plan' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Vet du om Nora kommer i kveld?
:::

::: {.reading translation='No, but I can send her a message.' dialogue_id='evening-plan' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Nei, men jeg kan sende henne en melding.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Ask if she needs a ride.' dialogue_id='evening-plan' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Spør om hun trenger skyss.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes. If she needs a ride, I can pick her up.' dialogue_id='evening-plan' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Ja. Hvis hun trenger skyss, kan jeg hente henne.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Good. I don’t know if the bus runs after eleven.' dialogue_id='evening-plan' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Fint. Jeg vet ikke om bussen går etter elleve.
:::

::: {.reading translation='If the bus doesn’t run, I’ll drive her home.' dialogue_id='evening-plan' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Hvis bussen ikke går, kjører jeg henne hjem.
:::

The two speakers use two different Norwegian words where English uses **if**. Maja uses **om** when she is asking indirectly about something she does not know. Henrik uses **hvis** when he describes a condition and its possible result.

The distinction matters because Norwegian does not normally use one word for both meanings.

## The central choice {#the-central-choice role=model objectives=obj-hvis-vs-om-01}

Use **hvis** when the clause gives a **condition**:

::: examples
- no: Hvis Nora trenger skyss, kan Henrik hente henne.
- en: If Nora needs a ride, Henrik can pick her up.
:::

Nora’s need for a ride is the condition. Henrik can pick her up if that condition is met.

Use **om** when the clause is an **embedded yes/no question**: a yes/no question placed inside a larger sentence.

::: examples
- no: Maja vet ikke om Nora trenger skyss.
- en: Maja doesn’t know if Nora needs a ride.
:::

The underlying question is:

::: examples
- no: Trenger Nora skyss?
- en: Does Nora need a ride?
:::

The expected answer could be yes or no. When that question becomes part of Maja vet ikke …, Norwegian introduces it with **om**.

A compact rule is:

- **hvis** introduces a condition.
- **om** introduces an embedded yes/no question.

**Practice request: anchor-meaning-check**

**Purpose:** Notice that the choice depends on meaning, not on the English word alone.
**Context:** Maja and Henrik’s conversation about Nora and the bus.
**Learner action:** For each clause introduced by hvis or om in the dialogue, identify whether it gives a condition or reports an unanswered yes/no question.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner can distinguish the two communicative functions before producing the forms independently.

{{exercise: anchor_meaning_check}}

## Conditions with hvis {#conditions-with-hvis role=model objectives=obj-hvis-vs-om-01}

A condition tells us what must happen, be true, or remain true for another situation to follow.

::: examples
- no: Hvis Nora kommer, lager Maja mer mat.
- en: If Nora comes, Maja will make more food.
- no: Hvis det blir sent, tar Henrik en taxi.
- en: If it gets late, Henrik will take a taxi.
- no: Vi spiser ute hvis været er fint.
- en: We’ll eat outside if the weather is nice.
:::

In each example, one situation depends on another:

- Nora comes → Maja makes more food.
- It gets late → Henrik takes a taxi.
- The weather is nice → they eat outside.

You may encounter a condition with the hvis-clause first or later in the sentence:

::: examples
- no: Hvis det regner, spiser vi inne.
- en: If it rains, we’ll eat inside.
- no: Vi spiser inne hvis det regner.
- en: We’ll eat inside if it rains.
:::

For this lesson, simply recognize that both sentences use **hvis** because rain is the condition affecting where they eat. Clause placement is not assessed here.

A condition does not have to be likely or certain:

::: examples
- no: Hvis toget er forsinket, ringer jeg deg.
- en: If the train is delayed, I’ll call you.
:::

The speaker may have no idea whether the train will be delayed. It is still a condition because the call depends on the delay.

**Practice request: condition-recognition**

**Purpose:** Build a clear meaning-based association between conditions and hvis.
**Context:** Everyday plans involving transport, weather, meals, and meeting times.
**Learner action:** Complete mixed messages with **hvis** or **om**, deciding from meaning whether each clause states a condition or an unresolved yes/no question.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner chooses hvis because one event or decision depends on another.

{{exercise: condition_recognition}}

**Practice request: conditional-sentence-building**

**Purpose:** Retrieve hvis while expressing complete condition–result relationships.
**Context:** Changes to Maja and Henrik’s evening plan, such as late buses, bad weather, or an extra guest.
**Learner action:** Combine each written condition and result into one natural Norwegian sentence with **hvis**, using the supplied clause order.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner can select and use **hvis** productively in a complete condition–result sentence; clause placement is not scored.

{{exercise: conditional_sentence_building}}

## Embedded yes/no questions with om {#embedded-yes-no-questions-with-om role=model objectives=obj-hvis-vs-om-01}

Sometimes a speaker does not ask a yes/no question directly. Instead, the question is placed inside another sentence.

Direct question:

::: examples
- no: Kommer Nora i kveld?
- en: Is Nora coming tonight?
:::

Embedded question:

::: examples
- no: Vet du om Nora kommer i kveld?
- en: Do you know if Nora is coming tonight?
:::

The larger sentence may show uncertainty, knowledge, checking, asking, or finding out:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vet ikke om butikken er åpen.
- en: I don’t know if the shop is open.
- no: Kan du sjekke om døra er låst?
- en: Can you check if the door is locked?
- no: Hun spør om vi har tid.
- en: She asks if we have time.
- no: Vi må finne ut om toget går på søndag.
- en: We need to find out if the train runs on Sunday.
:::

Each om-clause contains a question that could be answered yes or no:

::: examples
- no: Er butikken åpen?
- en: Is the shop open?
- no: Er døra låst?
- en: Is the door locked?
- no: Har vi tid?
- en: Do we have time?
- no: Går toget på søndag?
- en: Does the train run on Sunday?
:::

A useful English test is to replace **if** with **whether**. If the meaning remains essentially the same, the Norwegian word is usually **om**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vet ikke om butikken er åpen.
- en: I don’t know whether the shop is open.
- no: Kan du sjekke om døra er låst?
- en: Can you check whether the door is locked?
:::

This test works because **whether** points clearly to an unresolved yes/no question. It does not normally introduce an ordinary condition.

**Practice request: question-embedding**

**Purpose:** Connect direct yes/no questions with om-clauses.
**Context:** Maja needs information before dinner begins.
**Learner action:** Turn written direct questions about guests, food, transport, and timing into embedded questions after phrases such as Vet du …, Jeg vet ikke …, and Kan du sjekke ….
**Success demonstrates:** The learner selects om when a yes/no question becomes part of a larger sentence.

{{exercise: question_embedding}}

**Practice request: whether-test**

**Purpose:** Give the learner a practical strategy for ambiguous English sentences.
**Context:** Short bilingual messages containing English if.
**Learner action:** Choose a pair that reports an unresolved question and then states a consequence under the same possible fact. Infer both connectors from meaning.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner can use the whether test to identify om-contexts without relying on memorized verbs alone.

{{exercise: whether_test}}

## Compare the meanings directly {#compare-the-meanings-directly role=contrast objectives=obj-hvis-vs-om-01}

The clearest way to understand the distinction is to compare sentences about the same situation.

::: examples
- no: Hvis Lea er hjemme, ringer jeg henne.
- en: If Lea is at home, I’ll call her.
- no: Jeg vet ikke om Lea er hjemme.
- en: I don’t know if Lea is at home.
:::

The first sentence describes the condition for calling. The second contains the unanswered question Er Lea hjemme?

Another pair:

::: examples
- no: Hvis møtet slutter tidlig, tar vi toget.
- en: If the meeting ends early, we’ll take the train.
- no: Vi må finne ut om møtet slutter tidlig.
- en: We need to find out if the meeting ends early.
:::

In the first sentence, taking the train depends on the meeting ending early. In the second, the speakers want an answer to the question Slutter møtet tidlig?

One more contrast:

::: examples
- no: Spør Jonas om han kommer.
- en: Ask Jonas if he is coming.
- no: Hvis du spør Jonas, svarer han sikkert.
- en: If you ask Jonas, he’ll probably answer.
:::

In Spør Jonas om han kommer, the om-clause expresses what you should ask Jonas: Kommer du?

In Hvis du spør Jonas, svarer han sikkert, asking Jonas is the condition for getting an answer.

The verb **spørre** does not automatically require **om**, and uncertainty does not automatically require **om**. Always examine the role of the whole clause.

**Practice request: paired-meaning-contrast**

**Purpose:** Discriminate between hvis and om when vocabulary remains nearly identical.
**Context:** Paired sentences about people being home, buses running, shops being open, and meetings ending.
**Learner action:** Complete each sentence with the word that matches its intended relationship: a condition or an embedded yes/no question.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner chooses between hvis and om from sentence meaning rather than from familiar nouns or verbs.

{{exercise: paired_meaning_contrast}}

## A common mistake: translating if mechanically {#common-mistake-translating-if-mechanically role=contrast objectives=obj-hvis-vs-om-01}

English allows the same word in both of these sentences:

::: examples
- no: Hvis bussen er sen, ringer jeg.
- en: If the bus is late, I’ll call.
- no: Jeg vet ikke om bussen er sen.
- en: I don’t know if the bus is late.
:::

For the ordinary meanings practiced here, use **hvis** as the clear default for conditions and **om** for embedded yes/no questions:

::: examples
- no: Hvis bussen er forsinket, ringer jeg.
- en: If the bus is late, I’ll call.
- no: Jeg vet ikke om bussen er forsinket.
- en: I don’t know if the bus is late.
:::

A mechanical translation can produce errors such as:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vet ikke hvis bussen er forsinket.
- en: I don’t know if the bus is late.
:::

Here, the speaker is not presenting the delay as a condition. The speaker is reporting an unanswered question, so the sentence needs **om**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vet ikke om bussen er forsinket.
- en: I don’t know if the bus is late.
:::

Bokmål also accepts **om** in this conditional sentence:

::: examples
- no: Om bussen er forsinket, ringer jeg.
- en: If the bus is late, I’ll call.
:::

For ordinary conditions in this lesson, however, use the clearer default **hvis**:

::: examples
- no: Hvis bussen er forsinket, ringer jeg.
- en: If the bus is late, I’ll call.
:::

Do not choose according to how uncertain the speaker feels. Both conditions and embedded questions can involve uncertainty. Ask what the clause is doing:

- Does it state the circumstance for a result? Use **hvis**.
- Does it contain an unanswered yes/no question? Use **om**.

**Practice request: translation-error-repair**

**Purpose:** Correct direct transfer from English if.
**Context:** Short Norwegian messages in which hvis and om have been confused.
**Learner action:** Replace only the incorrect connector and briefly identify whether the corrected clause is a condition or an embedded yes/no question.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner can diagnose and repair the central hvis–om error from meaning.

{{exercise: translation_error_repair}}

## Back to Maja, Henrik, and Nora {#back-to-maja-henrik-and-nora role=model objectives=obj-hvis-vs-om-01}

Henrik sends Nora a message. A few minutes later, she replies.

::: {.reading translation='I don’t know if I’ll catch the six o’clock bus.' dialogue_id='back-to-maja-henrik-nora' speaker_id='nora' speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Jeg vet ikke om jeg rekker bussen klokka seks.
:::

::: {.reading translation='If you don’t catch it, I can pick you up at the station.' dialogue_id='back-to-maja-henrik-nora' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Hvis du ikke rekker den, kan jeg hente deg på stasjonen.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Thanks! Can you also ask Maja if I should bring anything?' dialogue_id='back-to-maja-henrik-nora' speaker_id='nora' speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Takk! Kan du også spørre Maja om jeg skal ta med noe?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes. If she needs anything, I’ll send you a message.' dialogue_id='back-to-maja-henrik-nora' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Ja. Hvis hun trenger noe, sender jeg deg en melding.
:::

Nora’s first sentence contains uncertainty about a yes/no question: Rekker jeg bussen? She therefore uses **om**.

Henrik then describes what he can do under a particular condition. He uses **hvis**.

Nora asks Henrik to pass on another yes/no question: Skal jeg ta med noe? That embedded question uses **om**.

Finally, Henrik gives another condition: he will send a message if Maja needs something. That condition uses **hvis**.

**Practice request: dialogue-continuation**

**Purpose:** Apply both forms while maintaining a coherent everyday exchange.
**Context:** Henrik contacts Maja after receiving Nora’s message.
**Learner action:** Complete a short written continuation in which Henrik asks for information with om and explains what he will do under a condition with hvis.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner selects each form appropriately across connected turns rather than in isolated sentences.

{{exercise: dialogue_continuation}}

## Transfer to a new situation {#transfer-to-a-new-situation role=contrast objectives=obj-hvis-vs-om-01}

Imagine that Emil and Sara are planning a Sunday walk. They need to check whether a café is open, and they must decide what to do if it rains.

Possible meanings include:

::: examples
- no: Sara undersøker om kafeen er åpen på søndag.
- en: Sara is checking if the café is open on Sunday.
- no: Hvis kafeen er åpen, spiser de lunsj der.
- en: If the café is open, they’ll have lunch there.
- no: Emil vet ikke om det skal regne.
- en: Emil doesn’t know if it is going to rain.
- no: Hvis det regner, tar de bussen i stedet.
- en: If it rains, they’ll take the bus instead.
:::

The same fact can appear first as a question and then as a condition. Sara may need to find out **whether** the café is open. Once she considers the consequences, she can say what they will do **if** it is open.

**Practice request: new-plan-transfer**

**Purpose:** Transfer the hvis–om distinction from the dinner scene to a different everyday plan.
**Context:** Emil and Sara arranging a Sunday walk, checking opening hours, and responding to possible rain.
**Learner action:** Write a short connected plan from supplied facts, using om for information they need to discover and hvis for conditions affecting their decisions.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner independently chooses hvis for conditions and om for embedded yes/no questions in a new context.

{{exercise: new_plan_transfer}}

## Retrieval and review {#retrieval-and-review role=recap}

**Practice request: retrieval-and-review**

Without looking back, complete a short written review containing one unknown yes/no issue and one condition based on that issue. Use **om** to report what someone needs to find out, then use **hvis** to state what will happen under one possible condition.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner can retrieve the distinction from meaning: **om** for an embedded yes/no question and **hvis** for a condition.

{{exercise: retrieval_and_review}}
