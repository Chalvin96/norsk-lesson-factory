## Lunch plans {#lunch-plans role=orient objectives=obj-negation-ikke-basic-01}

Maja and Elias work in the same office. They are deciding when to go for lunch.

::: {.reading translation="Aren’t you going to eat with us?" dialogue_id="lunch-plans" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Skal du ikke spise med oss?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but I don’t have a packed lunch today." dialogue_id="lunch-plans" speaker_id="elias" speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Jo, men jeg har ikke matpakke i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Aren’t you buying anything in the cafeteria?" dialogue_id="lunch-plans" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Kjøper du ikke noe i kantina?
:::

::: {.reading translation="The cafeteria isn’t open yet." dialogue_id="lunch-plans" speaker_id="elias" speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Kantina er ikke åpen ennå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Isn’t it open?" dialogue_id="lunch-plans" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Er den ikke åpen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, it doesn’t open until eleven." dialogue_id="lunch-plans" speaker_id="elias" speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Nei, den åpner ikke før elleve.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then we aren’t going yet. We’ll wait until eleven." dialogue_id="lunch-plans" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Da går vi ikke ennå. Vi venter til elleve.
:::

The practical problem is simple: Elias wants lunch, but he has no packed lunch and the cafeteria is still closed. Maja and Elias use **ikke** several times to say that something is not true or to ask about a negative situation.

Useful expressions from the scene include:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har ikke matpakke.
- en: I don’t have a packed lunch.
- no: Kantina er ikke åpen ennå.
- en: The cafeteria isn’t open yet.
- no: Er den ikke åpen?
- en: Isn’t it open?
- no: Vi går ikke ennå.
- en: We aren’t going yet.
:::

The meaning of **ikke** is usually English **not**. The important difference is its position.

## Statements: put ikke after the finite verb and subject {#statements-finite-verb-subject role=model objectives=obj-negation-ikke-basic-01}

A simple positive statement gives information:

::: examples
- no: Elias har matpakke.
- en: Elias has a packed lunch.
:::

The corresponding negative statement uses **ikke**:

::: examples
- no: Elias har ikke matpakke.
- en: Elias doesn’t have a packed lunch.
:::

The verb that carries the tense is called the **finite verb (finitte verb)**. In these examples, **har** is the finite verb. In a basic statement beginning with the subject, the order is:

**subject + finite verb + ikke + rest**

Compare the following pairs:

::: examples
- no: Kantina er åpen.
- en: The cafeteria is open.
- no: Kantina er ikke åpen.
- en: The cafeteria isn’t open.
- no: Vi går nå.
- en: We are going now.
- no: Vi går ikke nå.
- en: We aren’t going now.
- no: Maja spiser i kantina.
- en: Maja eats in the cafeteria.
- no: Maja spiser ikke i kantina.
- en: Maja doesn’t eat in the cafeteria.
- no: Jeg drikker kaffe.
- en: I drink coffee.
- no: Jeg drikker ikke kaffe.
- en: I don’t drink coffee.
:::

Norwegian does not normally add an equivalent of English **do** or **does** before **ikke**. English says **I do not drink**, but Norwegian simply places **ikke** after the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: Jeg drikker ikke kaffe.
- en: I don’t drink coffee.
:::

A common mistake is to copy English word order too closely:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg ikke drikker kaffe.
- en: Intended meaning: I don’t drink coffee.
:::

In a basic Norwegian statement, the finite verb comes before **ikke**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg drikker ikke kaffe.
- en: I don’t drink coffee.
:::

## When another element comes first {#initial-element-statements role=model objectives=obj-negation-ikke-basic-01}

A statement does not always begin with the subject. A time expression such as **i dag** or **nå** can come first. The finite verb still takes the second position. The placement of **ikke** then depends on the subject: with a full noun subject, **ikke** usually comes before the subject; with an unstressed pronoun subject, the pronoun comes before **ikke**.

**first element + finite verb + ikke + full noun subject + rest**

**first element + finite verb + unstressed pronoun + ikke + rest**

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Elias har ikke matpakke i dag.
- en: Elias doesn’t have a packed lunch today.
- no: I dag har Elias ikke matpakke.
- en: Today Elias doesn’t have a packed lunch.
:::

The meaning is almost the same. The second sentence gives **today** special prominence, but the position of **ikke** is still connected to the finite verb and subject.

More examples:

::: examples
- no: Vi går ikke til kantina nå.
- en: We aren’t going to the cafeteria now.
- no: Nå går vi ikke til kantina.
- en: Now we aren’t going to the cafeteria.
- no: Maja jobber ikke hjemme på mandager.
- en: Maja doesn’t work from home on Mondays.
- no: På mandager jobber Maja ikke hjemme.
- en: On Mondays Maja doesn’t work from home.
:::

Do not put **ikke** immediately after the opening time expression:

::: examples
- no: ✗ I dag ikke har Elias matpakke.
- en: Intended meaning: Today Elias doesn’t have a packed lunch.
:::

The finite verb comes before the subject in both patterns. With a full noun subject, **ikke** comes before it:

::: examples
- no: I dag har Elias ikke matpakke.
- en: Today Elias doesn’t have a packed lunch.
:::

## Statements with two verbs {#statements-two-verbs role=model objectives=obj-negation-ikke-basic-01}

Sometimes a sentence has a finite verb followed by another verb. **Ikke** still follows the finite verb, so it comes before the second verb:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan ikke spise nå.
- en: I can’t eat now.
- no: Vi skal ikke gå ennå.
- en: We aren’t going to leave yet.
- no: Elias vil ikke kjøpe kaffe.
- en: Elias doesn’t want to buy coffee.
:::

Compare the positive and negative forms:

::: examples
- no: Maja kan vente.
- en: Maja can wait.
- no: Maja kan ikke vente.
- en: Maja can’t wait.
:::

Here **kan** is the finite verb. **Vente** is the second verb, so **ikke** comes between them.

::: examples
- no: Vi skal spise klokka elleve.
- en: We are going to eat at eleven.
- no: Vi skal ikke spise før elleve.
- en: We aren’t going to eat before eleven.
:::

Here **skal** is the finite verb. The position of **ikke** does not change merely because another verb follows.

## Yes–no questions with a pronoun subject {#negative-pronoun-questions role=model objectives=obj-negation-ikke-basic-01}

A yes–no question has no question word. It begins with the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: Kommer du nå?
- en: Are you coming now?
:::

To make this a negative question, put **ikke** after the pronoun subject:

**finite verb + pronoun subject + ikke + rest**

::: examples
- no: Kommer du ikke nå?
- en: Aren’t you coming now?
:::

This is the pattern heard in the dialogue:

::: examples
- no: Er den ikke åpen?
- en: Isn’t it open?
:::

The finite verb **er** comes first, the pronoun **den** comes next, and **ikke** follows the pronoun.

More examples:

::: examples
- no: Spiser du ikke med oss?
- en: Aren’t you eating with us?
- no: Har han ikke matpakke?
- en: Doesn’t he have a packed lunch?
- no: Skal dere ikke gå snart?
- en: Aren’t you going to leave soon?
- no: Kan hun ikke vente?
- en: Can’t she wait?
:::

With two verbs, **ikke** still follows the finite verb and pronoun subject:

::: examples
- no: Skal du ikke spise?
- en: Aren’t you going to eat?
:::

The order is **skal + du + ikke + spise**.

A frequent mistake is to use statement order in a question:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Du spiser ikke med oss?
- en: Intended basic question: Aren’t you eating with us?
:::

That order can occur with special conversational intonation, but it is not the basic question pattern taught here. For a standard yes–no question, begin with the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: Spiser du ikke med oss?
- en: Aren’t you eating with us?
:::

## Questions with a named person or other full noun {#negative-full-noun-questions role=contrast objectives=obj-negation-ikke-basic-01}

In a neutral negative yes–no question with a full noun as the subject, **ikke** normally comes before that subject:

**finite verb + ikke + full noun subject + rest**

Compare the two subject types:

::: examples
- no: Kommer hun ikke i dag?
- en: Isn’t she coming today?
- no: Kommer ikke Maja i dag?
- en: Isn’t Maja coming today?
:::

In the first question, **hun** is a pronoun, so it comes before **ikke**. In the second, **Maja** is a full noun subject, so **ikke** comes before it.

More aligned contrasts:

::: examples
- no: Har han ikke matpakke?
- en: Doesn’t he have a packed lunch?
- no: Har ikke Elias matpakke?
- en: Doesn’t Elias have a packed lunch?
- no: Er den ikke åpen?
- en: Isn’t it open?
- no: Er ikke kantina åpen?
- en: Isn’t the cafeteria open?
- no: Skal de ikke spise nå?
- en: Aren’t they going to eat now?
- no: Skal ikke Maja og Elias spise nå?
- en: Aren’t Maja and Elias going to eat now?
:::

This contrast is about subject form, not about the meaning of **ikke**. Both question patterns ask whether a negative situation is true.

## Questions beginning with a question word {#negative-question-word-questions role=contrast objectives=obj-negation-ikke-basic-01}

A negative question can also begin with a question word such as **hvorfor**. The finite verb follows the question word. After that, the same subject contrast remains useful:

::: examples
- no: Hvorfor kommer du ikke?
- en: Why aren’t you coming?
- no: Hvorfor kommer ikke Elias?
- en: Why isn’t Elias coming?
:::

With a pronoun subject:

**question word + finite verb + pronoun + ikke**

::: examples
- no: Hvorfor spiser dere ikke nå?
- en: Why aren’t you eating now?
:::

With a full noun subject:

**question word + finite verb + ikke + full noun subject**

::: examples
- no: Hvorfor spiser ikke Maja nå?
- en: Why isn’t Maja eating now?
:::

Further examples:

::: examples
- no: Hvorfor er den ikke åpen?
- en: Why isn’t it open?
- no: Hvorfor er ikke kantina åpen?
- en: Why isn’t the cafeteria open?
- no: Hvorfor kan du ikke vente?
- en: Why can’t you wait?
- no: Hvorfor kan ikke Elias vente?
- en: Why can’t Elias wait?
:::

The compact question rule is therefore:

- With a pronoun subject, place **ikke** after the subject.
- With a name or full noun subject, place **ikke** before the subject.

## Back at the cafeteria {#back-at-cafeteria role=model objectives=obj-negation-ikke-basic-01}

::: {.reading translation="Now the cafeteria is open. Aren’t you coming?" dialogue_id="cafeteria-return" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Nå er kantina åpen. Kommer du ikke?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I’m coming. But I’m not buying hot food." dialogue_id="cafeteria-return" speaker_id="elias" speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Jo, jeg kommer. Men jeg kjøper ikke varm mat.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Don’t you eat hot food for lunch?" dialogue_id="cafeteria-return" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Spiser du ikke varm mat til lunsj?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, I’m not doing that today." dialogue_id="cafeteria-return" speaker_id="elias" speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Nei, jeg gjør ikke det i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Why don’t you buy a bread roll?" dialogue_id="cafeteria-return" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Hvorfor kjøper du ikke et rundstykke?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good idea. Then I don’t need to wait any longer." dialogue_id="cafeteria-return" speaker_id="elias" speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
God idé. Da trenger jeg ikke å vente mer.
:::

The situation has changed, but the placement remains consistent:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kjøper ikke varm mat.
- en: I’m not buying hot food.
:::

This is a statement, so **ikke** follows the finite verb and subject.

::: examples
- no: Spiser du ikke varm mat?
- en: Don’t you eat hot food?
:::

This is a question with a pronoun subject, so **ikke** follows **du**.

::: examples
- no: Hvorfor kjøper du ikke et rundstykke?
- en: Why don’t you buy a bread roll?
:::

This question begins with **hvorfor**, but **ikke** still follows the pronoun subject.

## Common placement mistakes {#common-placement-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-negation-ikke-basic-01}

Keep these contrasts in mind.

**In a statement**

::: examples
- no: Jeg spiser ikke nå.
- en: I’m not eating now.
- no: ✗ Jeg ikke spiser nå.
- en: Intended meaning: I’m not eating now.
:::

**In a statement beginning with a time expression**

::: examples
- no: I dag jobber Maja ikke hjemme.
- en: Today Maja isn’t working from home.
- no: ✗ I dag ikke jobber Maja hjemme.
- en: Intended meaning: Today Maja isn’t working from home.
:::

**In a question with a pronoun**

::: examples
- no: Kommer du ikke?
- en: Aren’t you coming?
- no: ✗ Kommer ikke du?
- en: Intended neutral meaning: Aren’t you coming?
:::

The second order can emphasize **du**, but the basic neutral pattern taught here is **du ikke**.

**In a question with a full noun subject**

::: examples
- no: Kommer ikke Elias?
- en: Isn’t Elias coming?
:::

The full noun subject normally follows **ikke** in a neutral negative question.

**With two verbs**

::: examples
- no: Vi kan ikke vente.
- en: We can’t wait.
- no: ✗ Vi kan vente ikke.
- en: Intended meaning: We can’t wait.
:::
