## A Saturday morning at home {#saturday-morning-home role=orient objectives=obj-present-tense-01}

Mina and Jonas share a flat in Bergen. On most weekdays they leave home early, but this Saturday their routines are different.

::: {.reading translation="I’m making coffee now." dialogue_id="saturday-morning" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg lager kaffe nå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. I’m working from home today." dialogue_id="saturday-morning" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Fint. Jeg jobber hjemme i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="You usually work at the office." dialogue_id="saturday-morning" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Du jobber vanligvis på kontoret.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but I have an online meeting now." dialogue_id="saturday-morning" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja, men jeg har et møte på nettet nå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I clean the kitchen every Saturday." dialogue_id="saturday-morning" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg gjør rent på kjøkkenet hver lørdag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="It’s quiet here now, so I’m working well." dialogue_id="saturday-morning" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Det er stille her nå, så jeg arbeider godt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And I play music every morning." dialogue_id="saturday-morning" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Og jeg spiller musikk hver morgen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Not right now!" dialogue_id="saturday-morning" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ikke akkurat nå!
:::

The scene contains two kinds of meaning. Some actions are happening now or during the current day: Jonas is making coffee, and Mina is working at home. Other actions are habits: Mina usually works at the office, and Jonas cleans every Saturday.

Norwegian uses the same verb form for both meanings.

## One present tense, two common meanings {#present-tense-two-common-meanings role=model objectives=obj-present-tense-01}

The form used in the dialogue is the **present tense (presens)**. At this level, use it for:

- something happening now or around now;
- something that happens regularly or is generally true.

Compare these pairs:

::: examples
- no: Jeg lager kaffe nå.
- en: I’m making coffee now.
- no: Jeg lager kaffe hver morgen.
- en: I make coffee every morning.
:::

The Norwegian verb is **lager** in both sentences. The time expression tells us whether this is a current action or a habit.

::: examples
- no: Mina jobber hjemme i dag.
- en: Mina is working from home today.
- no: Mina jobber hjemme hver fredag.
- en: Mina works from home every Friday.
:::

Again, **jobber** does not change. English often distinguishes I am working from I work, but basic Bokmål normally uses one present-tense form for both.

Useful time expressions make the intended meaning clear:

::: examples
- no: nå
- en: now
- no: i dag
- en: today
- no: vanligvis
- en: usually
- no: hver morgen
- en: every morning
- no: hver lørdag
- en: every Saturday
:::

## Forming the present tense of regular verbs {#forming-present-tense-regular-verbs role=model objectives=obj-present-tense-01}

Norwegian verbs are often introduced with **å**, equivalent to English to:

::: examples
- no: å jobbe
- en: to work
- no: å lage
- en: to make
- no: å spille
- en: to play
- no: å arbeide
- en: to work
:::

For the regular verbs in this lesson, remove **å** and add **-r** to the infinitive. When the infinitive already ends in **-e**, this produces the familiar ending **-er**.

Infinitive — Present tense — English

::: examples
- no: å jobbe — jobber
- en: to work — work, am/is/are working
- no: å lage — lager
- en: to make — make, am/is/are making
- no: å spille — spiller
- en: to play — play, am/is/are playing
- no: å arbeide — arbeider
- en: to work — work, am/is/are working
- no: å vaske — vasker
- en: to wash — wash, am/is/are washing
- no: å bo — bor
- en: to live — live, am/is/are living
:::

See the forms in complete statements:

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobber på kontoret.
- en: I work at the office.
- no: Jonas lager kaffe.
- en: Jonas is making coffee.
- no: Vi spiller musikk hver kveld.
- en: We play music every evening.
- no: De vasker kjøkkenet nå.
- en: They are washing the kitchen now.
- no: Mina bor i Bergen.
- en: Mina lives in Bergen.
:::

A compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use the infinitive without å, then add -r.
:::

Do not leave a regular verb in its infinitive form after the subject.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg jobbe hjemme i dag.
- en: I’m working from home today.
:::

Intended meaning: I’m working from home today.

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobber hjemme i dag.
- en: I’m working from home today.
:::

## The verb does not change with the subject {#verb-does-not-change-with-subject role=contrast objectives=obj-present-tense-01}

English has a special **-s** form in statements such as she works. Norwegian does not make that distinction. The same present-tense form follows every subject.

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobber hjemme.
- en: I work at home.
- no: Du jobber hjemme.
- en: You work at home.
- no: Mina jobber hjemme.
- en: Mina works at home.
- no: Vi jobber hjemme.
- en: We work at home.
- no: De jobber hjemme.
- en: They work at home.
:::

The same is true of other regular verbs:

::: examples
- no: Jeg lager middag.
- en: I make dinner.
- no: Jonas lager middag.
- en: Jonas makes dinner.
- no: Vi lager middag.
- en: We make dinner.
:::

A common mistake is to search for a special form corresponding to English works or makes. Bokmål keeps **jobber** and **lager** unchanged.

## Three essential present-tense forms {#three-essential-present-tense-forms role=model objectives=obj-present-tense-01}

Three extremely common verbs in the dialogue do not follow the simple regular pattern transparently. Learn their present forms as complete items.

Infinitive — Present tense — Core meaning

::: examples
- no: å være — er
- en: be
- no: å ha — har
- en: have
- no: å gjøre — gjør
- en: do, make
:::

**Er: am, is, are**

Norwegian uses **er** with every subject.

::: examples
- no: Jeg er hjemme.
- en: I am at home.
- no: Jonas er på kjøkkenet.
- en: Jonas is in the kitchen.
- no: Vi er på kontoret.
- en: We are at the office.
- no: Det er stille her nå.
- en: It is quiet here now.
:::

English changes among am, is, and are. Norwegian uses **er** in all these statements.

**Har: have, has**

Norwegian similarly uses **har** with every subject.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har et møte nå.
- en: I have a meeting now.
- no: Mina har kaffe.
- en: Mina has coffee.
- no: Vi har mye arbeid i dag.
- en: We have a lot of work today.
:::

Do not create a separate Norwegian form for English has. Both have and has correspond to **har** here.

**Gjør: do, does**

Use **gjør** for do or does.

::: examples
- no: Jeg gjør rent hver lørdag.
- en: I clean every Saturday.
- no: Jonas gjør mye hjemme.
- en: Jonas does a lot at home.
- no: Vi gjør arbeidet nå.
- en: We are doing the work now.
:::

The expression **gjør rent** literally uses does clean and means cleans or does the cleaning.

## Norwegian present tense and English -ing {#norwegian-present-tense-and-english-ing role=contrast objectives=obj-present-tense-01}

Return to Mina’s first statement:

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobber hjemme i dag.
- en: I’m working from home today.
:::

English uses am working because the activity is current. Norwegian does not normally copy this structure. **Jobber** already expresses the present action.

A common English-based mistake is:

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg er jobber hjemme.
- en: I’m working at home.
:::

Intended meaning: I’m working at home.

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobber hjemme.
- en: I’m working at home.
:::

Use **er** when it means am, is, or are by itself:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er hjemme.
- en: I am at home.
:::

Use the regular present-tense verb when stating the action:

::: examples
- no: Jeg arbeider hjemme.
- en: I am working at home.
:::

Compare the meanings carefully:

::: examples
- no: Mina er på kontoret.
- en: Mina is at the office.
- no: Mina jobber på kontoret.
- en: Mina works at the office.
:::

The first statement identifies Mina’s location. The second states her activity or routine. Do not add **er** merely because the English translation contains is or am before an **-ing** verb.

## Time expressions clarify the situation {#time-expressions-clarify-the-situation role=contrast objectives=obj-present-tense-01}

The verb form alone does not distinguish a habit from an action happening now. Context does that work.

::: examples
- no: Jonas vasker kjøkkenet nå.
- en: Jonas is washing the kitchen now.
- no: Jonas vasker kjøkkenet hver lørdag.
- en: Jonas washes the kitchen every Saturday.
- no: Mina arbeider hjemme i dag.
- en: Mina is working from home today.
- no: Mina arbeider vanligvis på kontoret.
- en: Mina usually works at the office.
- no: Vi lager middag nå.
- en: We are making dinner now.
- no: Vi lager middag hver kveld.
- en: We make dinner every evening.
:::

Notice how the same target forms recur: **vasker**, **arbeider**, and **lager**. Only the surrounding time information changes the interpretation.

## Back in the flat {#back-in-the-flat role=model objectives=obj-present-tense-01}

Mina finishes her online meeting. Jonas is still in the kitchen.

::: {.reading translation="Now I have time." dialogue_id="back-in-the-flat" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Nå har jeg tid.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. I’m washing the cups, and you’re making food." dialogue_id="back-in-the-flat" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Bra. Jeg vasker koppene, og du lager mat.
:::

::: {.reading translation="All right. I usually make food on Saturdays." dialogue_id="back-in-the-flat" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Greit. Jeg lager vanligvis mat på lørdager.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And I’m cleaning." dialogue_id="back-in-the-flat" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Og jeg gjør rent.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That is a good plan." dialogue_id="back-in-the-flat" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Det er en god plan.
:::

The forms remain simple even though some statements describe the present moment and another describes Mina’s Saturday habit:

- **har** states what Mina has now;
- **vasker**, **lager**, and **gjør** describe activities;
- **er** identifies the plan as good;
- **vanligvis** signals a routine.
