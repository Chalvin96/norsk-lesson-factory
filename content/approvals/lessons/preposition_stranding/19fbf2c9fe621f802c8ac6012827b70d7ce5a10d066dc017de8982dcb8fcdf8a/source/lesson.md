## Planning a party {#planning-a-party role=orient objectives=obj-preposition-stranding-01}

Maja and Jonas are preparing a small party in their shared flat. They need music, but they do not own the right equipment.

::: {.reading translation="Have you seen the blue speaker that we listened to in the shop?" dialogue_id="planning-party" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Har du sett den blå høyttaleren som vi hørte på i butikken?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but it was expensive. Do you know who we can borrow a speaker from?" dialogue_id="planning-party" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, men den var dyr. Vet du hvem vi kan låne en høyttaler av?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Maybe Sindre. He is the neighbour whom I borrowed the projector from last year." dialogue_id="planning-party" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Kanskje Sindre. Han er naboen som jeg lånte projektoren av i fjor.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good idea. What are we going to connect the speaker to?" dialogue_id="planning-party" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
God idé. Hva skal vi koble høyttaleren til?
:::

::: {.reading translation="The small laptop on the kitchen table." dialogue_id="planning-party" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Den lille laptopen på kjøkkenbordet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And who are we going to make the playlist with?" dialogue_id="planning-party" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Og hvem skal vi lage spillelista sammen med?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Lea. She knows lots of songs that people like dancing to." dialogue_id="planning-party" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Lea. Hun kjenner mange sanger som folk liker å danse til.
:::

Several expressions in this conversation contain a preposition:

::: examples
- no: høre på noe
- en: listen to something
- no: låne noe av noen
- en: borrow something from someone
- no: koble noe til noe
- en: connect something to something
- no: gjøre noe sammen med noen
- en: do something together with someone
:::

When Maja and Jonas ask about the missing person or thing, that information moves to the beginning of the question. The preposition normally remains in its natural place later in the sentence.

## The central pattern {#central-pattern role=model objectives=obj-preposition-stranding-01}

A word such as av, med, om, på or til is a preposition (preposisjon). In ordinary statements, it usually comes before the person or thing connected to it.

::: examples
- no: Vi kan låne høyttaleren av Sindre.
- en: We can borrow the speaker from Sindre.
- no: Vi skal koble høyttaleren til laptopen.
- en: We are going to connect the speaker to the laptop.
:::

When the person or thing becomes the focus of a question, the question word moves to the beginning. The preposition stays behind. This pattern is called preposition stranding.

::: examples
- no: Hvem kan vi låne høyttaleren av?
- en: Who can we borrow the speaker from?
- no: Hva skal vi koble høyttaleren til?
- en: What are we going to connect the speaker to?
:::

The same thing happens when the person or thing is described by a relative clause.

::: examples
- no: Sindre er naboen som vi kan låne høyttaleren av.
- en: Sindre is the neighbour whom we can borrow the speaker from.
- no: Dette er laptopen som vi skal koble høyttaleren til.
- en: This is the laptop that we are going to connect the speaker to.
:::

The compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Put the question word at the beginning of a question, or the described person or thing before the relative clause. Leave the associated preposition in its normal position later in the clause.
:::

## Asking natural questions {#asking-natural-questions role=model objectives=obj-preposition-stranding-01}

Begin with an ordinary statement and notice which noun phrase follows the preposition.

::: examples
- no: Du snakker med Nora.
- en: You are talking with Nora.
:::

If Nora is the unknown person, replace her with hvem and move hvem to the beginning. Keep med behind.

::: examples
- no: Hvem snakker du med?
- en: Who are you talking with?
:::

The ordinary question word order still applies after the question word.

::: examples
- no: Dere snakker om festen.
- en: You are talking about the party.
- no: Hva snakker dere om?
- en: What are you talking about?
- no: Hun venter på en kollega.
- en: She is waiting for a colleague.
- no: Hvem venter hun på?
- en: Who is she waiting for?
- no: De bor i det gule huset.
- en: They live in the yellow house.
- no: Hvilket hus bor de i?
- en: Which house do they live in?
:::

The question word can be followed by a noun, as in hvilket hus. The principle remains the same: huset is connected to i, so i stays later in the question.

## A useful subject contrast {#subject-contrast role=contrast objectives=obj-preposition-stranding-01}

Compare these two questions:

::: examples
- no: Hvem snakker med Maja?
- en: Who is talking with Maja?
- no: Hvem snakker Maja med?
- en: Who is Maja talking with?
:::

In the first question, hvem is the person doing the talking. The phrase med Maja remains complete.

In the second, Maja is doing the talking. Hvem represents the person connected to med, so med is stranded later in the question.

## Formal and everyday placement {#formal-everyday-placement role=contrast objectives=obj-preposition-stranding-01}

It is possible to place some prepositions before the question word:

::: examples
- no: Med hvem snakket du?
- en: With whom did you speak?
:::

This sounds formal or marked in many everyday situations. Natural conversational Bokmål normally prefers:

::: examples
- no: Hvem snakket du med?
- en: Who did you speak with?
:::

Use the stranded pattern as the ordinary spoken and neutral everyday model.

## Describing people and things with relative clauses {#relative-clauses role=model objectives=obj-preposition-stranding-01}

A relative clause (relativsetning) identifies or describes a person or thing.

Start with two statements:

::: examples
- no: Jeg snakket med naboen. Naboen har en høyttaler.
- en: I spoke with the neighbour. The neighbour has a speaker.
:::

The repeated noun can become the person described by a relative clause:

::: examples
- no: Naboen som jeg snakket med, har en høyttaler.
- en: The neighbour whom I spoke with has a speaker.
:::

Naboen appears before the relative clause. Inside the clause, med remains where the complete phrase med naboen would normally occur.

The same pattern works with things and places:

::: examples
- no: Vi hørte på høyttaleren.
- en: We listened to the speaker.
- no: Dette er høyttaleren som vi hørte på.
- en: This is the speaker that we listened to.
- no: Gjestene skal være i dette rommet.
- en: The guests are going to be in this room.
- no: Dette er rommet som gjestene skal være i.
- en: This is the room that the guests are going to be in.
- no: Folk liker å danse til denne sangen.
- en: People like dancing to this song.
- no: Dette er sangen som folk liker å danse til.
- en: This is the song that people like dancing to.
:::

Not every relative clause contains a stranded preposition:

::: examples
- no: Lea kjenner en musiker som bor i Bergen.
- en: Lea knows a musician who lives in Bergen.
:::

Here som represents the person who lives in Bergen. It is not connected to a preposition.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Lea kjenner musikeren som Jonas snakket om.
- en: Lea knows the musician whom Jonas talked about.
:::

Here musikeren is connected to om in the underlying expression snakke om noen. Om therefore remains later in the relative clause.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-preposition-stranding-01}

Do not remove the preposition

The verb or expression still requires its preposition.

::: examples
- no: Hva snakker dere om?
- en: What are you talking about?
- no: ✗ Hva snakker dere?
- en: ✗ What are you talking?
:::

Without om, snakker normally lacks the intended meaning of talking about a topic.

Similarly:

::: examples
- no: Dette er sangen som alle danser til.
- en: This is the song that everyone dances to.
- no: ✗ Dette er sangen som alle danser.
- en: ✗ This is the song that everyone dances.
:::

Do not repeat the person or thing

Once hvem or the described noun supplies the missing information, do not add an extra pronoun after the preposition.

::: examples
- no: Hvem snakket du med?
- en: Who did you speak with?
- no: ✗ Hvem snakket du med ham?
- en: ✗ Who did you speak with him?
:::

Likewise:

::: examples
- no: Dette er naboen som jeg lånte verktøyet av.
- en: This is the neighbour whom I borrowed the tool from.
- no: ✗ Dette er naboen som jeg lånte verktøyet av ham.
- en: ✗ This is the neighbour whom I borrowed the tool from him.
:::

Do not move every preposition to the sentence end

Only the preposition connected to the questioned or described element is stranded.

::: examples
- no: Hvem var du på kino med i går?
- en: Who were you at the cinema with yesterday?
:::

Med is not literally the last word because i går gives extra time information. It is still stranded: the person who would normally follow med has moved to the beginning.

::: examples
- no: Dette er vennen som jeg var på kino med i går.
- en: This is the friend whom I was at the cinema with yesterday.
:::

Think of the preposition as staying in its natural grammatical position, not as being forced to become the final word.

## Back at the flat {#back-at-the-flat role=model objectives=obj-preposition-stranding-01}

Sindre arrives with a speaker and several cables.

::: {.reading translation="Is this the cable that you were looking for?" dialogue_id="flat-return" speaker_id="sindre" speaker_name="Sindre" voice_profile="masculine"}
Er dette kabelen som dere lette etter?

::: {.reading translation="Yes, that is the one! Here is the laptop that we are going to connect the speaker to." dialogue_id="flat-return" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Ja, det er den! Her er laptopen som vi skal koble høyttaleren til.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Who are you going to make the playlist with?" dialogue_id="flat-return" speaker_id="sindre" speaker_name="Sindre" voice_profile="masculine"}
Flott. Hvem skal dere lage spillelista sammen med?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Lea. But she sent three playlists, and I do not know which one we should listen to." dialogue_id="flat-return" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Lea. Men hun sendte tre spillelister, og jeg vet ikke hvilken vi skal høre på.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Choose the one that people can dance to." dialogue_id="flat-return" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Velg den som folk kan danse til.
:::

The continuation uses the same pattern for two purposes: asking for missing information and identifying a particular person or thing.
