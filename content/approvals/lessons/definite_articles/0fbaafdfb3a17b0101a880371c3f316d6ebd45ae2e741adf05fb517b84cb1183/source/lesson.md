## At the lost-property desk {#at-the-lost-property-desk role=orient objectives=obj-definite-articles-01}

After a community cooking class, Nora and Amir are collecting their things. Several bags, cups, and books are still on a table near the door.

::: {.reading translation="Is this your cup?" dialogue_id="lost-property-desk" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Er dette koppen din?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, my cup is blue." dialogue_id="lost-property-desk" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Nei, koppen min er blå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="What about the book?" dialogue_id="lost-property-desk" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Hva med boka?
:::

::: {.reading translation="The book is yours. You put it on the table." dialogue_id="lost-property-desk" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Boka er di. Du la den på bordet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, of course. But where is my bag?" dialogue_id="lost-property-desk" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, selvfølgelig. Men hvor er posen min?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Is the bag under the chairs?" dialogue_id="lost-property-desk" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Er posen under stolene?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No. Wait—there it is, between the cups!" dialogue_id="lost-property-desk" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Nei. Vent — der er den, mellom koppene!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. Now we have the book, the cup, and the bag." dialogue_id="lost-property-desk" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Bra. Nå har vi boka, koppen og posen.
:::

The first time an object is mentioned, Norwegian can introduce it with en, ei, or et:

::: examples
- no: en kopp
- en: a cup
- no: ei bok
- en: a book
- no: en pose
- en: a bag
:::

Once Nora and Amir know which object they mean, they use forms such as koppen, boka, and posen. Norwegian normally expresses simple English the by adding an ending to the noun.

## From “a cup” to “the cup” {#from-a-cup-to-the-cup role=model objectives=obj-definite-articles-01}

A noun used for an unspecified or newly introduced thing is in the indefinite form (ubestemt form). A noun used for a particular, identifiable thing is in the definite form (bestemt form).

Compare:

Indefinite / Definite

::: examples
- no: Her er en kopp.
- en: Here is a cup.
- no: Koppen er blå.
- en: The cup is blue.
- no: Jeg har ei bok.
- en: I have a book.
- no: Boka ligger på bordet.
- en: The book is on the table.
- no: Vi trenger et kart.
- en: We need a map.
- no: Kartet er i bilen.
- en: The map is in the car.
:::

English places the before a noun. In these simple Norwegian noun phrases, definiteness belongs at the end of the noun:

::: examples
- no: kopp + en → koppen
- en: the cup
- no: bok + a → boka
- en: the book
- no: kart + et → kartet
- en: the map
:::

These endings connect with noun gender, which you already know from en, ei, and et.

Masculine nouns

For a simple masculine singular noun, add -en:

::: examples
- no: en stol → stolen
- en: a chair → the chair
- no: en nøkkel → nøkkelen
- en: a key → the key
- no: en pose → posen
- en: a bag → the bag
- no: en bil → bilen
- en: a car → the car
:::

The ending is attached directly to the noun. There is no separate word corresponding to English the.

::: examples
- no: Jeg finner en nøkkel. Nøkkelen ligger ved døra.
- en: I find a key. The key is by the door.
:::

The first sentence introduces a key. The second sentence refers to that same key.

Feminine nouns

When a feminine noun is introduced with ei, its definite singular form commonly ends in -a:

::: examples
- no: ei bok → boka
- en: a book → the book
- no: ei dør → døra
- en: a door → the door
- no: ei jente → jenta
- en: a girl → the girl
- no: ei veske → veska
- en: a handbag → the handbag
:::

::: examples
- no: Det står ei jente utenfor. Jenta venter på bussen.
- en: There is a girl outside. The girl is waiting for the bus.
:::

Bokmål also permits masculine forms for many feminine nouns, such as en bok → boken. This chapter uses the clear feminine pattern ei bok → boka consistently. Both systems are Bokmål, but do not mix the introduction and ending accidentally while learning the pattern.

Neuter nouns

For a simple neuter singular noun, add -et:

::: examples
- no: et kart → kartet
- en: a map → the map
- no: et eple → eplet
- en: an apple → the apple
- no: et vindu → vinduet
- en: a window → the window
- no: et bord → bordet
- en: a table → the table
:::

Sometimes the spelling looks slightly different because the noun already ends in -e:

::: examples
- no: eple + et → eplet
- en: an apple → the apple
- no: vindu + et → vinduet
- en: a window → the window
:::

Learn the complete definite form as part of the noun pattern.

::: examples
- no: Hun åpner et vindu. Vinduet er stort.
- en: She opens a window. The window is large.
:::

## Building definite singular forms {#building-definite-singular-forms role=model objectives=obj-definite-articles-01}

Use the noun’s gender to select its basic definite singular ending:

Gender / Indefinite singular / Definite singular

::: examples
- no: en kopp
- en: a cup
- no: koppen
- en: the cup
- no: ei bok
- en: a book
- no: boka
- en: the book
- no: et kart
- en: a map
- no: kartet
- en: the map
:::

A compact rule:

::: {.rule}
For simple singular nouns, remove en, ei, or et from before the noun and attach the corresponding definite ending: usually -en, -a, or -et.
:::

This is a form rule, but context still determines whether the noun should be definite.

Compare these small exchanges:

::: examples
- no: Har du en penn?
- en: Do you have a pen?
- no: Ja, men pennen er tom.
- en: Yes, but the pen is empty.
- no: Det er et bakeri her.
- en: There is a bakery here.
- no: Bakeriet åpner klokka sju.
- en: The bakery opens at seven.
- no: Jeg ser ei veske.
- en: I see a handbag.
- no: Veska står ved døra.
- en: The handbag is by the door.
:::

The speaker first asks about any suitable pen. The reply then describes the particular pen that has been identified.

The first sentence introduces the bakery. The second treats it as known information.

Again, the movement is from a newly introduced object to a particular known object.

Common mistake: adding a separate “the”

Do not translate English structure word for word:

::: examples
- no: stolen
- en: the chair
:::

not a separate word plus stol

For a simple noun with no adjective, the ending itself carries the meaning of the.

Another common mistake is leaving en, ei, or et in front while also adding the definite ending. In this lesson’s simple noun phrases, use either the indefinite form or the definite form:

::: examples
- no: en stol
- en: a chair
- no: stolen
- en: the chair
:::

Do not combine both patterns.

## More than one: definite plural nouns {#more-than-one-definite-plural-nouns role=model objectives=obj-definite-articles-01}

Norwegian also adds an ending when a plural noun means the books, the chairs, or the apples.

Start by comparing indefinite and definite plural forms:

Indefinite plural / Definite plural

::: examples
- no: bøker
- en: books
- no: bøkene
- en: the books
- no: stoler
- en: chairs
- no: stolene
- en: the chairs
- no: jenter
- en: girls
- no: jentene
- en: the girls
- no: epler
- en: apples
- no: eplene
- en: the apples
- no: poser
- en: bags
- no: posene
- en: the bags
:::

For these common plural patterns, the definite plural ends in -ene. When the indefinite plural ends in -er, the change often looks like this:

::: examples
- no: stoler → stolene
- en: chairs → the chairs
- no: jenter → jentene
- en: girls → the girls
- no: poser → posene
- en: bags → the bags
:::

The final -r disappears before -ene.

Some nouns have a changed plural stem, but the definite ending still attaches to the plural form:

::: examples
- no: bok → bøker → bøkene
- en: book → books → the books
:::

The vowel change in bok → bøker belongs to the noun’s plural pattern. The definite form needed here is bøkene.

Neuter nouns ending in -e often follow the same visible pattern:

::: examples
- no: eple → epler → eplene
- en: apple → apples → the apples
:::

A few very common neuter plurals have no ending in the indefinite plural and use -a in the definite plural:

::: examples
- no: et barn → barn → barna
- en: a child → children → the children
:::

Treat barna as a common complete form. For the regular nouns used throughout most of this chapter, -ene is the central definite plural pattern.

## Known groups {#known-groups role=contrast objectives=obj-definite-articles-01}

The plural meaning follows the same basic logic as the singular meaning.

::: examples
- no: Det står stoler i rommet. Stolene er nye.
- en: There are chairs in the room. The chairs are new.
- no: Nora finner bøker på bordet. Bøkene tilhører læreren.
- en: Nora finds books on the table. The books belong to the teacher.
- no: Vi kjøper epler. Eplene er til kurset.
- en: We buy apples. The apples are for the class.
:::

In the original scene, Amir asks:

::: {.reading translation="Is the bag under the chairs?" dialogue_id="known-groups" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Er posen under stolene?
:::

He does not mean any chairs anywhere. He means the visible chairs near the lost-property table. Later, Nora sees her bag:

::: {.reading translation="There it is, between the cups!" dialogue_id="known-groups" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Der er den, mellom koppene!
:::

Again, koppene means a particular group of cups in their shared surroundings.

## Returning to Nora and Amir {#returning-to-nora-and-amir role=model objectives=obj-definite-articles-01}

A few minutes later, the class teacher comes back.

::: {.reading translation="Have you found your things?" dialogue_id="returning-to-nora-and-amir" speaker_id="teacher" speaker_name="Læreren"}
Har dere funnet tingene deres?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. The handbag and the book are mine." dialogue_id="returning-to-nora-and-amir" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja. Veska og boka er mine.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And the cup is mine." dialogue_id="returning-to-nora-and-amir" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Og koppen er min.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Can you put the chairs by the table?" dialogue_id="returning-to-nora-and-amir" speaker_id="teacher" speaker_name="Læreren"}
Flott. Kan dere sette stolene ved bordet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Should the cups be in the kitchen?" dialogue_id="returning-to-nora-and-amir" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja. Skal koppene stå på kjøkkenet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, and the bags can be by the door." dialogue_id="returning-to-nora-and-amir" speaker_id="teacher" speaker_name="Læreren"}
Ja, og posene kan stå ved døra.
:::

The scene now contains both definite singular and definite plural nouns:

::: examples
- no: veska
- en: the handbag
- no: boka
- en: the book
- no: koppen
- en: the cup
- no: bordet
- en: the table
- no: stolene
- en: the chairs
- no: koppene
- en: the cups
- no: posene
- en: the bags
:::

Each noun identifies one known object or a known group. Norwegian does this through endings rather than a separate simple equivalent of English the.
