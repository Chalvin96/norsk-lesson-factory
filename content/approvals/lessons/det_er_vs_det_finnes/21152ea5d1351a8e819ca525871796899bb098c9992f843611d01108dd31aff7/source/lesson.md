## A room for the weekend {#a-room-for-the-weekend role=orient objectives=obj-det-er-vs-det-finnes-01}

Sara and Jonas have arrived in a small town for a weekend trip. Their hotel room is not ready, so they ask the receptionist, Ingrid, about the hotel and the surrounding area.

::: {.reading translation="Welcome! I’m Ingrid." dialogue_id="hotel-arrival" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Velkommen! Det er jeg som heter Ingrid.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Hi. We have booked a double room." dialogue_id="hotel-arrival" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Hei. Vi har bestilt et dobbeltrom.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, it’s room 214. It’s on the second floor." dialogue_id="hotel-arrival" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Ja, det er rom 214. Det er i andre etasje.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Is the room ready now?" dialogue_id="hotel-arrival" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Er rommet klart nå?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, unfortunately. It isn’t ready yet, but there is a luggage room by reception." dialogue_id="hotel-arrival" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Nei, dessverre. Det er ikke klart ennå, men det finnes et bagasjerom ved resepsjonen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Is there a café at the hotel?" dialogue_id="hotel-arrival" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Fint. Finnes det en kafé på hotellet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, but there is a pleasant café in the town square. It’s the red building by the bank." dialogue_id="hotel-arrival" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Nei, men det finnes en hyggelig kafé på torget. Det er den røde bygningen ved banken.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And what about dinner tonight?" dialogue_id="hotel-arrival" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Og hva med middag i kveld?
:::

::: {.reading translation="There are several restaurants in the town centre. The Italian restaurant is popular, but it is often full there." dialogue_id="hotel-arrival" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Det finnes flere restauranter i sentrum. Den italienske restauranten er populær, men det er ofte fullt der.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thank you. Then we’ll leave our luggage here first." dialogue_id="hotel-arrival" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Takk. Da setter vi bagasjen her først.
:::

The two expressions do different jobs in this scene. Ingrid uses **det er** when she identifies or describes something:

::: examples
- no: Det er rom 214.
- en: It is room 214.
- no: Det er i andre etasje.
- en: It is on the second floor.
- no: Det er ikke klart ennå.
- en: It is not ready yet.
:::

She uses **det finnes** when she says that something exists or is available:

::: examples
- no: Det finnes et bagasjerom ved resepsjonen.
- en: There is a luggage room by reception.
- no: Det finnes flere restauranter i sentrum.
- en: There are several restaurants in the town centre.
:::

The central choice is therefore one of meaning:

- Use **det er** to identify something or describe it.
- Use **det finnes** to say that something exists or is available.

English often uses *there is* or *there are* for existence. Norwegian can express that meaning with **det finnes**. The form **finnes** is the present tense of **å finnes**, meaning *to exist* or *to be found*.

## Identifying and describing with det er {#identifying-and-describing-with-det-er role=model objectives=obj-det-er-vs-det-finnes-01}

Use **det er** when you tell someone what a person or thing is, or when you give information about it.

**Identifying something**

::: examples
- no: Det er hotellet vårt.
- en: That is our hotel.
- no: Det er resepsjonisten.
- en: That is the receptionist.
- no: Det er rom 214.
- en: It is room 214.
- no: Det er nøkkelen din.
- en: That is your key.
:::

In each example, the speaker points out or identifies a particular person or thing. The meaning is not simply that a hotel, receptionist, room, or key exists. The speaker tells us which person or thing we are looking at or talking about.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Det er hotellet vårt.
- en: That is our hotel.
- no: Det finnes et hotell ved stasjonen.
- en: There is a hotel by the station.
:::

The first sentence identifies a particular building as *our hotel*. The second tells us that a hotel exists in a particular place.

**Describing something**

Once a person, place, or thing is part of the conversation, **det er** can describe it.

::: examples
- no: Det er stort.
- en: It is large.
- no: Det er ganske dyrt.
- en: It is quite expensive.
- no: Det er åpent hele natten.
- en: It is open all night.
- no: Det er nær sentrum.
- en: It is near the town centre.
:::

The surrounding context tells us what **det** refers to. For example:

::: examples
- no: Hvordan er rommet? Det er lyst og stille.
- en: What is the room like? It is bright and quiet.
- no: Hvor er frokostsalen? Det er i første etasje.
- en: Where is the breakfast room? It is on the first floor.
:::

The thing is already known: *the room* or *the breakfast room*. The answer supplies a description or location.

## Existence and availability with det finnes {#existence-and-availability-with-det-finnes role=model objectives=obj-det-er-vs-det-finnes-01}

Use **det finnes** when the important information is that something exists, can be found, or is available.

::: examples
- no: Det finnes en kafé på torget.
- en: There is a café in the town square.
- no: Det finnes et kart i resepsjonen.
- en: There is a map at reception.
- no: Det finnes mange fine turstier i området.
- en: There are many pleasant hiking trails in the area.
- no: Det finnes gratis parkering bak hotellet.
- en: There is free parking behind the hotel.
:::

These sentences introduce useful information. The café, map, paths, and parking have not necessarily been mentioned before. The speaker announces their existence or availability.

The same form works with one thing and with several things:

::: examples
- no: Det finnes en restaurant i nærheten.
- en: There is a restaurant nearby.
- no: Det finnes tre restauranter i nærheten.
- en: There are three restaurants nearby.
:::

Norwegian does not change **finnes** according to whether the following noun is singular or plural. English changes between *there is* and *there are*, but Norwegian keeps **det finnes**.

Availability is a common reason to use the expression:

::: examples
- no: Det finnes ledige rom i helgen.
- en: There are rooms available this weekend.
- no: Det finnes kaffe i frokostsalen.
- en: There is coffee in the breakfast room.
- no: Det finnes ikke flere billetter.
- en: There are no more tickets available.
:::

The negative form **det finnes ikke** says that something does not exist or is not available.

::: examples
- no: Det finnes ikke en minibank på hotellet.
- en: There is no cash machine at the hotel.
- no: Det finnes ikke ledige bord akkurat nå.
- en: There are no tables available right now.
:::

## Questions about existence {#questions-about-existence role=model objectives=obj-det-er-vs-det-finnes-01}

To ask whether something exists or is available, place **finnes** before **det**:

::: examples
- no: Finnes det en kafé på hotellet?
- en: Is there a café at the hotel?
- no: Finnes det ledige rom i kveld?
- en: Are there rooms available tonight?
- no: Finnes det en buss til sentrum?
- en: Is there a bus to the town centre?
:::

The question asks about existence or availability. It does not ask for a description of a known café, room, or bus.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Finnes det en restaurant på hotellet?
- en: Is there a restaurant at the hotel?
- no: Er restauranten åpen?
- en: Is the restaurant open?
:::

The first question establishes whether a restaurant exists. The second asks for information about a restaurant that is already known.

## The same place, two different messages {#same-place-two-different-messages role=contrast objectives=obj-det-er-vs-det-finnes-01}

A location phrase does not decide the form by itself. Both expressions can occur with a location, but the speaker’s purpose differs.

::: examples
- no: Det finnes en kafé på torget.
- en: There is a café in the town square.
- no: Det er den røde bygningen på torget.
- en: It is the red building in the town square.
:::

The first sentence announces that a café exists. The second identifies which building the café is.

Another pair:

::: examples
- no: Det finnes et bagasjerom ved resepsjonen.
- en: There is a luggage room by reception.
- no: Det er ved resepsjonen.
- en: It is by reception.
:::

In the first sentence, the luggage room is new information. In the second, the listener already knows that there is a luggage room and asks where it is.

Consider a short exchange:

::: {.reading translation="Is there a pharmacy nearby?" dialogue_id="pharmacy-location" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Finnes det et apotek i nærheten?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, there is a pharmacy by the bus station." dialogue_id="pharmacy-location" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Ja, det finnes et apotek ved busstasjonen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Which building is it?" dialogue_id="pharmacy-location" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hvilken bygning er det?
:::

::: {.reading translation="It is the yellow building next to the bakery." dialogue_id="pharmacy-location" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Det er den gule bygningen ved siden av bakeriet.
:::

First Ingrid confirms existence. Then she identifies the building. The physical place has not changed, but the communicative job has.

**Compact rule**

Ask what the sentence contributes:

- If it tells the listener **what or which thing it is**, or **what that known thing is like**, use **det er**.
- If it tells the listener **that something exists or is available**, use **det finnes**.

## A likely misconception {#likely-misconception role=contrast objectives=obj-det-er-vs-det-finnes-01}

It is tempting to translate English word by word:

::: examples
- no: Det er en kafé på torget.
- en: There is a café in the town square.
:::

Sentences of this kind can be heard in everyday Norwegian. However, **det er** has several uses, and relying on it for every English *there is* can hide the distinction this lesson is teaching.

When your clear purpose is to say that something exists or is available, **det finnes** makes that meaning explicit:

::: examples
- no: Det finnes en kafé på torget.
- en: There is a café in the town square.
:::

When you identify or describe a known thing, use **det er**:

::: examples
- no: Det er kafeen vi leter etter.
- en: It is the café we are looking for.
- no: Det er veldig hyggelig der.
- en: It is very pleasant there.
:::

The useful learner strategy is not “translate *there* as **det**.” Instead, decide whether you are announcing existence or giving information about something already in the conversation.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-det-er-vs-det-finnes-01}

**Using det finnes to describe a known thing**

Avoid:

::: examples
- no: Rommet finnes stort og lyst.
- en: 'Not: The room is large and bright. (in this situation)'
:::

Here, the sentence describes the room. Use **det er**:

::: examples
- no: Rommet er stort og lyst. Det er også stille.
- en: The room is large and bright. It is also quiet.
:::

**Finnes** does not work as a substitute for every form of English *is* or *are*.

**Using det er when the task is specifically to express availability**

If a guest wants to know whether rooms are available, the intended meaning is existence or availability:

::: examples
- no: Finnes det ledige rom i helgen?
- en: Are there rooms available this weekend?
:::

A question such as **Er rommet ledig?** has a different context: it asks whether one particular, already identified room is available.

::: examples
- no: Finnes det ledige rom?
- en: Are there any rooms available?
- no: Er rommet ledig?
- en: Is the room available?
:::

The first asks whether any suitable rooms exist. The second asks about a specific room.

**Changing finnes for a plural noun**

Do not create a separate plural form after **det**. Keep **finnes**:

::: examples
- no: Det finnes ett ledig rom.
- en: There is one room available.
- no: Det finnes fem ledige rom.
- en: There are five rooms available.
:::

## Returning to Sara and Jonas {#returning-to-sara-and-jonas role=model objectives=obj-det-er-vs-det-finnes-01}

Sara and Jonas come back to reception later that afternoon.

::: {.reading translation="Now we’re back. Is the room ready?" dialogue_id="hotel-return" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Nå er vi tilbake. Er rommet klart?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, it is ready. Here is the key." dialogue_id="hotel-return" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Ja, det er klart. Her er nøkkelen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thank you. Are there towels in the room?" dialogue_id="hotel-return" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Takk. Finnes det håndklær på rommet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, there are towels in the bathroom." dialogue_id="hotel-return" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Ja, det finnes håndklær på badet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And internet?" dialogue_id="hotel-return" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Og internett?
:::

::: {.reading translation="There is free Wi-Fi throughout the hotel. The password is printed on the key card." dialogue_id="hotel-return" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Det finnes gratis trådløst internett på hele hotellet. Passordet står på nøkkelkortet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Is it this password?" dialogue_id="hotel-return" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Er det dette passordet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, it is correct." dialogue_id="hotel-return" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Ja, det er riktig.
:::

The dialogue now moves back and forth between the two meanings:

- **Det er klart** describes the known room.
- **Det finnes håndklær** states that towels are available.
- **Det finnes gratis trådløst internett** announces the availability of a service.
- **Det er riktig** describes the password Jonas has identified.

## From controlled choice to short connected use {#controlled-choice-to-connected-use role=model objectives=obj-det-er-vs-det-finnes-01}

Imagine that Jonas sends Sara a message while exploring the hotel. He tells her that a small gym is available downstairs. Then he identifies the door and describes the room.

His message needs to move through three stages:

1. Announce that the gym exists.
2. Identify which door leads to it.
3. Describe the gym.

The target forms make that progression clear:

::: examples
- no: Det finnes et lite treningsrom i kjelleren.
- en: There is a small gym in the basement.
- no: Det er døra ved siden av heisen.
- en: It is the door next to the lift.
- no: Det er lyst og ganske moderne.
- en: It is bright and quite modern.
:::

The first sentence gives Sara new information. The next two sentences identify and describe something now established in the conversation.

## Transfer to a new situation: a neighbourhood visit {#transfer-to-a-neighbourhood role=recap objectives=obj-det-er-vs-det-finnes-01}

The next day, Sara and Jonas visit Jonas’s friend Emil, who has recently moved to a new neighbourhood. The setting changes, but the same meaning choice remains useful.

Emil might introduce the area like this:

::: examples
- no: Det finnes en matbutikk rett rundt hjørnet.
- en: There is a grocery shop just around the corner.
- no: Det finnes også et bibliotek i nærheten.
- en: There is also a library nearby.
:::

When Sara sees a building and asks about it, Emil identifies it:

::: examples
- no: Det er biblioteket.
- en: That is the library.
:::

He can then describe it:

::: examples
- no: Det er nytt og veldig lyst.
- en: It is new and very bright.
:::

If Jonas asks about transport, he asks whether a service exists:

::: examples
- no: Finnes det en buss til sentrum?
- en: Is there a bus to the town centre?
:::

Emil can confirm its existence and then identify the stop:

::: examples
- no: Ja, det finnes en buss hvert tiende minutt.
- en: Yes, there is a bus every ten minutes.
- no: Det er holdeplassen på den andre siden av gata.
- en: It is the stop on the other side of the street.
:::
