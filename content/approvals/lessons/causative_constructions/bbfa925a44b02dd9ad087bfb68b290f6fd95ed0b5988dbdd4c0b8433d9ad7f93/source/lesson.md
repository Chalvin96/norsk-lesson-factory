## A busy afternoon at the community centre {#busy-afternoon-community-centre role=orient objectives=obj-causative-constructions-01}

Sara coordinates activities at a community centre. A photographer is coming to take pictures for the centre’s website, but the main room is still untidy. Sara asks her colleague Jonas for help.

::: {.reading translation="The photographer is coming in an hour, and the room still looks untidy." dialogue_id="busy-afternoon-community-centre" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Fotografen kommer om en time, og rommet ser fortsatt rotete ut.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I asked Emil to arrange the chairs, but he isn’t finished." dialogue_id="busy-afternoon-community-centre" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg ba Emil rydde stolene, men han er ikke ferdig.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Can you get him to hurry?" dialogue_id="busy-afternoon-community-centre" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Kan du få ham til å skynde seg?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Should I ask Nora to move the tables too?" dialogue_id="busy-afternoon-community-centre" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. Skal jeg be Nora flytte bordene også?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, let her finish the posters first." dialogue_id="busy-afternoon-community-centre" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Nei, la henne gjøre ferdig plakatene først.
:::

::: {.reading translation="All right. The photographer will probably want to move some things herself." dialogue_id="busy-afternoon-community-centre" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Greit. Fotografen vil sikkert flytte på noen ting selv.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s fine. Let her decide where the chairs should be." dialogue_id="busy-afternoon-community-centre" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Det går bra. La henne bestemme hvor stolene skal stå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="You seem stressed." dialogue_id="busy-afternoon-community-centre" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Du virker stresset.
:::

::: {.reading translation="A little. But it makes me calmer that you’re helping." dialogue_id="busy-afternoon-community-centre" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Litt. Men det gjør meg roligere at du hjelper til.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then I’ll get Emil to tidy up faster, and then I’ll make coffee." dialogue_id="busy-afternoon-community-centre" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Da får jeg Emil til å rydde raskere, og så lager jeg kaffe.
:::

The scene involves three different kinds of influence. Sara wants Jonas to cause Emil to act. She allows Nora to continue her work. Jonas’s help changes Sara’s emotional state. Bokmål uses different patterns for these meanings:

::: examples
- no: få noen til å gjøre noe
- en: get someone to do something
- no: la noen gjøre noe
- en: let someone do something
- no: gjøre noen rolig, glad eller sint
- en: make someone calm, happy, or angry
:::

The key choice is not simply how to translate the English verb make. You must first decide what kind of result you mean: causing an action, allowing an action, or causing a state.

## Getting someone to act: få noen til å + infinitive {#getting-someone-to-act role=model objectives=obj-causative-constructions-01}

Use **få + object + til å + infinitive** when one person causes, persuades, encourages, or arranges for another person to do something.

Here the **object (objekt)** is the person who performs the action. The final verb appears in the **infinitive (infinitiv)**.

::: examples
- no: Sara får Emil til å rydde.
- en: Sara gets Emil to tidy up.
:::

Sara causes or persuades Emil to do the tidying. Emil is the object of får, but he is also the person who performs rydde.

The pattern is:

**person causing the action + få + person acting + til å + action**

Compare the roles:

::: examples
- no: Jonas rydder rommet.
- en: Jonas tidies the room.
- no: Sara får Jonas til å rydde rommet.
- en: Sara gets Jonas to tidy the room.
:::

In the first sentence, Jonas acts independently. In the second, Sara causes or persuades him to act.

The verb få changes form normally according to tense:

::: examples
- no: Jeg får henne til å ringe kunden.
- en: I get her to call the customer.
- no: Jeg fikk henne til å ringe kunden.
- en: I got her to call the customer.
- no: Jeg skal få henne til å ringe kunden.
- en: I will get her to call the customer.
:::

The person who performs the action may be a noun or an object pronoun:

::: examples
- no: Vi fikk naboen til å åpne døra.
- en: We got the neighbour to open the door.
- no: Vi fikk ham til å åpne døra.
- en: We got him to open the door.
- no: Læreren fikk oss til å lese teksten høyt.
- en: The teacher got us to read the text aloud.
- no: Barna fikk meg til å le.
- en: The children made me laugh.
:::

Common object pronouns include meg, deg, ham, henne, oss and dem.

**What kind of pressure does få express?**

Få noen til å gjøre noe tells us that someone’s influence produced an action. It does not, by itself, say exactly how. The person may ask, persuade, encourage, or arrange matters so that the action happens.

::: examples
- no: Hun fikk broren sin til å kjøre henne til stasjonen.
- en: She got her brother to drive her to the station.
:::

This could mean that she asked him and he agreed. It does not necessarily imply force.

::: examples
- no: Sjefen fikk alle til å bli igjen etter møtet.
- en: The manager got everyone to stay after the meeting.
:::

The situation may involve authority, but the grammar itself focuses on the result: everyone stayed because of the manager’s influence.

**A common mistake: missing til**

Do not place the second infinitive directly after the object with få.

Incorrect pattern:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Hun fikk meg ringe kunden.
- en: She got me to call the customer.
:::

Standard Bokmål uses til å:

::: examples
- no: Hun fikk meg til å ringe kunden.
- en: She got me to call the customer.
:::

Remember the complete link: **få noen til å gjøre noe**.

## Letting someone act: la noen + infinitive {#letting-someone-act role=model objectives=obj-causative-constructions-01}

Use **la + object + infinitive** when someone permits an action or does not prevent it.

::: examples
- no: Sara lar Nora gjøre ferdig plakatene.
- en: Sara lets Nora finish the posters.
:::

Nora performs the action. Sara allows it to happen.

The infinitive after the object appears **without å**, sometimes called the **bare infinitive (infinitiv uten å)**:

::: examples
- no: La ham bestemme.
- en: Let him decide.
- no: Vi lot barna leke ute.
- en: We let the children play outside.
- no: De lar meg bruke kjøkkenet.
- en: They let me use the kitchen.
:::

The pattern is:

**person allowing the action + la + person acting + infinitive without å**

The present-tense form is lar, and the past-tense form is lot:

::: examples
- no: Hun lar meg låne sykkelen.
- en: She lets me borrow the bicycle.
- no: Hun lot meg låne sykkelen.
- en: She let me borrow the bicycle.
- no: Vi skal la dem velge.
- en: We will let them choose.
:::

## Compare få and la {#compare-faa-and-la role=contrast objectives=obj-causative-constructions-01}

These constructions can describe the same two people and the same action, but they show different relationships.

::: examples
- no: Sara fikk Jonas til å flytte bordet.
- en: Sara got Jonas to move the table.
- no: Sara lot Jonas flytte bordet.
- en: Sara let Jonas move the table.
:::

In the first sentence, Sara’s influence causes Jonas to move it. Perhaps she asks him or persuades him.

In the second sentence, Jonas wants or intends to move it, and Sara permits him to do so.

Another contrast:

::: examples
- no: Moren fikk barnet til å spise suppa.
- en: The mother got the child to eat the soup.
- no: Moren lot barnet spise suppa.
- en: The mother let the child eat the soup.
:::

The first sentence suggests that the child needed encouragement or persuasion. The second focuses on permission.

**A common mistake: adding å after la**

English learners may expect an infinitive marker before every infinitive. After la + object, Bokmål does not use å.

Incorrect pattern:

::: examples
- no: ✗ De lot oss å vente inne.
- en: They let us wait inside.
:::

Correct pattern:

::: examples
- no: De lot oss vente inne.
- en: They let us wait inside.
:::

The contrast is worth learning as two complete frames:

::: examples
- no: få dem til å vente
- en: get them to wait
- no: la dem vente
- en: let them wait
:::

## Causing a state: gjøre noen + adjective {#causing-a-state role=model objectives=obj-causative-constructions-01}

Sometimes the result is not an action performed by the object. Instead, something causes a person or thing to enter a state. Bokmål commonly uses **gjøre + object + adjective**.

An **adjective (adjektiv)** describes the resulting state.

::: examples
- no: Hjelpen gjør Sara roligere.
- en: The help makes Sara calmer.
- no: Nyheten gjorde henne glad.
- en: The news made her happy.
- no: Den høye musikken gjør meg sliten.
- en: The loud music makes me tired.
- no: Spørsmålet gjorde alle usikre.
- en: The question made everyone uncertain.
:::

The pattern is:

**cause + gjøre + affected person or thing + resulting adjective**

Compare an ordinary description with a caused state:

::: examples
- no: Sara er rolig.
- en: Sara is calm.
- no: Jonas gjør Sara rolig.
- en: Jonas makes Sara calm.
:::

The first sentence describes Sara’s state. The second introduces a cause.

The subject does not have to be a person:

::: examples
- no: Kaffen gjorde meg våken.
- en: The coffee made me alert.
- no: Ventetiden gjør barna utålmodige.
- en: The wait makes the children impatient.
- no: Det varme lyset gjorde rommet hyggelig.
- en: The warm light made the room pleasant.
:::

The adjective describes the object, not the subject. In the following sentence, trøtt describes meg:

::: examples
- no: Reisen gjorde meg trøtt.
- en: The journey made me tired.
:::

It does not describe reisen.

**Agreement in the resulting adjective**

The adjective normally agrees with the person or thing whose resulting state it describes.

::: examples
- no: Beskjeden gjorde ham nervøs.
- en: The message made him nervous.
- no: Beskjeden gjorde henne nervøs.
- en: The message made her nervous.
- no: Beskjeden gjorde barna nervøse.
- en: The message made the children nervous.
- no: Malingen gjorde rommet lysere.
- en: The paint made the room brighter.
:::

The central causative pattern remains gjøre + object + adjective. Familiar adjective agreement still applies inside that pattern.

## Do not copy English make mechanically {#do-not-copy-english-make role=contrast objectives=obj-causative-constructions-01}

English uses make before both actions and adjectives:

en: She made me call.

en: She made me nervous.

Bokmål normally separates these meanings:

::: examples
- no: Hun fikk meg til å ringe.
- en: She got me to call.
- no: Hun gjorde meg nervøs.
- en: She made me nervous.
:::

Do not use gjøre + object + infinitive as a general translation of English make someone do something.

Incorrect pattern:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Hun gjorde meg ringe.
- en: She made me call.
:::

Use få + object + til å + infinitive for the caused action:

::: examples
- no: Hun fikk meg til å ringe.
- en: She made me call.
:::

Use gjøre + object + adjective when the result is a state:

::: examples
- no: Hun gjorde meg nervøs.
- en: She made me nervous.
:::

## Three patterns, three relationships {#three-patterns-three-relationships role=recap objectives=obj-causative-constructions-01}

The object occupies a similar position in all three constructions, but what follows it tells us what kind of result is involved.

**Meaning:** Cause or get someone to act.

**Bokmål pattern:** få + object + til å + infinitive.

**Example:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg fikk ham til å vente.
- en: I got him to wait.
:::

**Meaning:** Permit someone to act.

**Bokmål pattern:** la + object + bare infinitive.

**Example:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg lot ham vente.
- en: I let him wait.
:::

**Meaning:** Cause someone to be in a state.

**Bokmål pattern:** gjøre + object + adjective.

**Example:**

::: examples
- no: Ventetiden gjorde ham irritert.
- en: The wait made him irritated.
:::

Notice how ham has a different relationship to each final element:

::: examples
- no: Jeg fikk ham til å vente.
- en: I got him to wait.
:::

He waits because of my influence.

::: examples
- no: Jeg lot ham vente.
- en: I let him wait.
:::

He waits because I permit or do not prevent it.

::: examples
- no: Ventetiden gjorde ham irritert.
- en: The wait made him irritated.
:::

He does not perform irritert as an action. Irritert describes his resulting state.

A useful decision sequence is:

1. Does the object perform an action because someone causes or arranges it? Use få noen til å gjøre noe.
2. Does the object perform an action because someone permits it? Use la noen gjøre noe.
3. Does the object enter a state described by an adjective? Use gjøre noen glad, rolig, sint, and similar expressions.

:::

::: {.reading translation="They looked better near the window." dialogue_id="community-centre-follow-up" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}

:::

::: {.reading translation="I agree. The light makes the room much more pleasant." dialogue_id="community-centre-follow-up" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Jeg er enig. Lyset gjør rommet mye hyggeligere.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And the coffee made everyone a little more alert." dialogue_id="community-centre-follow-up" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Og kaffen gjorde alle litt mer våkne.
:::

The roles have shifted. Emil was originally the person whom Jonas influenced, but later Emil gets Jonas to act. This shows why word order matters: the object after få identifies the person who performs the infinitive action.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jonas fikk Emil til å flytte bordet.
- en: Jonas got Emil to move the table.
- no: Emil fikk Jonas til å flytte bordet.
- en: Emil got Jonas to move the table.
:::

The vocabulary is almost identical, but the people exchange roles.
