## At the cabin door {#at-the-cabin-door role=orient objectives=obj-possessive-placement-01}

Sara, Jonas, and Emil are packing after a weekend at a cabin. Several bags, jackets, and sets of keys are lying near the door.

::: {.reading translation="Is this your jacket?" dialogue_id="cabin" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Er dette jakka di?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, that is my jacket. But where is my backpack?" dialogue_id="cabin" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Ja, det er jakka mi. Men hvor er sekken min?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Is that your backpack by the door?" dialogue_id="cabin" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Er det sekken din ved døra?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, that is Jonas’s backpack. My backpack is blue." dialogue_id="cabin" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Nei, det er sekken til Jonas. Min sekk er blå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Here it is. I put your phone in your backpack." dialogue_id="cabin" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Her er den. Jeg la telefonen din i sekken din.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thanks. And these must be your keys?" dialogue_id="cabin" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Takk. Og dette er vel nøklene dine?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, my keys are in my pocket. Those are her keys, I think." dialogue_id="cabin" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nei, mine nøkler ligger i lomma mi. De der er nøklene hennes, tror jeg.
:::

Everyone is talking about ownership, but the possessives do not always appear in the same place. Sara says both sekken min and min sekk. Jonas says both sekken din and mine nøkler. In the regular common-noun phrases taught in this lesson, the choice changes which part of the message receives attention and pairs with a change in the form of the noun.

## Two possible positions {#two-possible-positions role=model objectives=obj-possessive-placement-01}

A Norwegian possessive (possessiv) can normally come after or before its noun. This lesson practices the regular pattern with common nouns.

In that pattern, when the possessive follows the noun, the noun takes the **definite form**:

::: examples
- no: sekken min
- en: my backpack
:::

When the possessive comes before the noun in that pattern, the noun takes the **indefinite form**:

::: examples
- no: min sekk
- en: my backpack
:::

Both phrases identify the same owner. Their usual focus differs.

**Possessive after the noun: the neutral pattern**

The most common everyday pattern is:

**definite noun + possessive**

Use this when ownership is ordinary information and no special contrast is needed.

::: examples
- no: jakka mi
- en: my jacket
- no: huset mitt
- en: my house
- no: vennene våre
- en: our friends
- no: Telefonen din ligger på bordet.
- en: Your phone is on the table.
- no: Jeg finner ikke billetten min.
- en: I cannot find my ticket.
:::

In the cabin dialogue, Sara first asks:

::: examples
- no: Hvor er sekken min?
- en: Where is my backpack?
:::

She is simply looking for it. She does not need to contrast her backpack with anyone else’s yet.

**Possessive before the noun: contrast or emphasis**

The other pattern is:

**possessive + indefinite noun**

This order often highlights the owner. It is useful when correcting someone, distinguishing between owners, or making a contrast.

::: examples
- no: min sekk
- en: my backpack
- no: mitt hus
- en: my house
- no: våre venner
- en: our friends
- no: Dette er min plass, ikke din.
- en: This is my seat, not yours.
- no: Våre venner kommer i kveld, men naboens venner kommer i morgen.
- en: Our friends are coming this evening, but the neighbor’s friends are coming tomorrow.
:::

At the cabin, Emil points to the wrong backpack. Sara then says:

::: examples
- no: Min sekk er blå.
- en: My backpack is blue.
:::

Here, min receives extra attention: her backpack, not Jonas’s backpack.

Preposed possessives can also occur for stylistic reasons, especially in more formal language. At this level, the most useful distinction is simple: use the postposed pattern as the everyday neutral choice, and use the preposed pattern when the owner needs contrast or emphasis.

## The noun form in the regular pattern {#noun-form-in-regular-pattern role=model objectives=obj-possessive-placement-01}

For the regular common-noun phrases taught in this lesson, moving the possessive is not enough. The noun changes form too.

Each row below lists Neutral placement first, then Emphatic or contrastive placement.

::: examples
- no: boka mi
- en: my book
- no: mi bok
- en: my book
- no: huset mitt
- en: my house
- no: mitt hus
- en: my house
- no: sykkelen hans
- en: his bicycle
- no: hans sykkel
- en: his bicycle
- no: foreldrene våre
- en: our parents
- no: våre foreldre
- en: our parents
:::

This regular common-noun pattern works with the singular and plural nouns practiced here.

::: examples
- no: rommet hennes
- en: her room
- no: hennes rom
- en: her room
- no: kollegaene deres
- en: their colleagues
- no: deres kollegaer
- en: their colleagues
:::

With feminine nouns, this chapter uses forms such as boka mi and mi bok. Bokmål also permits common-gender forms such as boken min and min bok. With either gender pattern, the regular placement pairing taught here remains the same.

**Compact rule for the regular common-noun pattern taught here**

- After the noun: use the noun’s definite form.
- Before the noun: use the noun’s indefinite form.

In this taught pattern, the possessive already makes a preposed phrase definite in meaning, so the noun itself does not take the definite ending.

## A common mistake {#a-common-mistake role=contrast objectives=obj-possessive-placement-01}

English places possessive determiners such as my, your, and our before the noun. This can lead learners to produce combinations outside the regular Norwegian common-noun pattern taught here, such as an English-like possessive position with a definite noun.

**Incorrect:**

::: examples
- no: ✗ min sekken
- en: my backpack
:::

**Correct:**

::: examples
- no: min sekk
- en: my backpack
:::

Also correct:

::: examples
- no: sekken min
- en: my backpack
:::

In the regular common-noun pattern taught here, the opposite error is leaving the noun indefinite when the possessive follows it.

**Incorrect:**

::: examples
- no: ✗ sekk min
- en: my backpack
:::

**Correct:**

::: examples
- no: sekken min
- en: my backpack
:::

Do not think of one placement as “more possessive” than the other. Both express ownership. The main differences are noun form and information focus.

## Choosing placement from the message {#choosing-placement-from-the-message role=model objectives=obj-possessive-placement-01}

Compare these two exchanges.

**Neutral question**

::: {.reading translation="Where are your glasses?" dialogue_id="glasses-neutral" speaker_id="lina" speaker_name="Lina" character_id="lina"}
Hvor er brillene dine?
:::

::: {.reading translation="My glasses are in the kitchen." dialogue_id="glasses-neutral" speaker_id="aron" speaker_name="Aron" voice_profile="masculine"}
Brillene mine ligger på kjøkkenet.
:::

Aron is only giving their location. The neutral postposed pattern is natural.

**Correction and contrast**

::: {.reading translation="Are these your glasses?" dialogue_id="glasses-contrast" speaker_id="lina" speaker_name="Lina" character_id="lina"}
Er dette brillene dine?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, my glasses have black frames." dialogue_id="glasses-contrast" speaker_id="aron" speaker_name="Aron" voice_profile="masculine"}
Nei, mine briller har svarte innfatninger.
:::

Now Aron is distinguishing his glasses from another pair. Placing mine first highlights the owner.

The same contrast appears with other noun types:

::: examples
- no: Katten vår sover på sofaen.
- en: Our cat is sleeping on the sofa.
- no: Vår katt er inne, men naboens katt er ute.
- en: Our cat is indoors, but the neighbor’s cat is outside.
- no: Jeg har lagt passet ditt i skuffen.
- en: I have put your passport in the drawer.
- no: Dette er ditt pass; det andre er mitt.
- en: This is your passport; the other one is mine.
:::

The preposed order does not automatically mean that the speaker is angry or speaking loudly. It means that the owner is prominent in the message. Context supplies any stronger emotion.

## Correcting mismatched forms {#correcting-mismatched-forms role=contrast objectives=obj-possessive-placement-01}

In the regular common-noun pattern taught here, possessive placement and noun form operate as a pair. When checking one of these phrases, ask two questions:

1. Is the possessive before or after the noun?
2. Does the noun have the form taught for that position?

Consider these corrected examples. In each pair, the first sentence can answer a routine ownership question. For the second sentence, imagine that someone has proposed the wrong key owner or compared where two families live:

::: examples
- no: Det er nøklene hennes.
- en: Those are her keys.
- no: Hennes nøkler ligger ikke her.
- en: Her keys are not here.
- no: Vi besøker foreldrene våre på søndag.
- en: We are visiting our parents on Sunday.
- no: Våre foreldre bor i Bergen, men deres foreldre bor i Tromsø.
- en: Our parents live in Bergen, but their parents live in Tromsø.
:::

In the first sentence of each pair, ownership is presented neutrally and the noun is definite. In the supplied correction or comparison context, the second sentence highlights or contrasts owners and follows the taught regular pattern with the noun’s indefinite form.

## Back at the cabin {#back-at-the-cabin role=orient objectives=obj-possessive-placement-01}

The group has found most of its belongings, but Emil is still holding a scarf.

::: {.reading translation="Who owns this scarf?" dialogue_id="cabin-return" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Hvem eier dette skjerfet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="My scarf is green. That one is not mine." dialogue_id="cabin-return" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Skjerfet mitt er grønt. Det der er ikke mitt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="My scarf is already in my backpack." dialogue_id="cabin-return" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Mitt skjerf ligger allerede i sekken min.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then we still do not know who owns it." dialogue_id="cabin-return" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Da vet vi fortsatt ikke hvem som eier det.
:::

Sara begins with the neutral phrase skjerfet mitt while describing her scarf. Jonas chooses mitt skjerf because he is distinguishing his scarf from the unidentified one. In the same sentence, he uses sekken min neutrally because the backpack’s owner does not need contrast.

One sentence can therefore contain both placements. Imagine Emil has mistakenly said that Jonas’s jacket is in the car, and Sara corrects him:

::: examples
- no: Min jakke ligger i bilen, men telefonen min ligger her.
- en: My jacket is in the car, but my phone is here.
:::

In this correction context, the first phrase highlights whose jacket is being discussed. The second simply identifies the phone.
