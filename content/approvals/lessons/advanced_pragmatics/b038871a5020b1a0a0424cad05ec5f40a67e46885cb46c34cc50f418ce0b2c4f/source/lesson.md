## When a possibility becomes a plan {#possibility-becomes-plan role=orient objectives=obj-advanced-pragmatics-01}

Lea and Jonas are organizing a workshop. They still need a room.

::: {.reading translation="We might be able to use the large meeting room, but I haven't asked yet." dialogue_id="room_misunderstanding" speaker_id="lea" speaker_name="Lea" character_id="lea"}
Vi kan kanskje bruke det store møterommet, men jeg har ikke spurt ennå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great, then I'll write in the invitation that we'll be there." dialogue_id="room_misunderstanding" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Flott, da skriver jeg i invitasjonen at vi skal være der.
:::

Lea mentioned a **possibility**. Jonas treated it as a **confirmed arrangement**.
If Lea only says *ja* or changes the subject, the invitation may contain false
information. She needs to repair the shared plan before they act on it.

::: examples
- no: Vent litt, rommet er ikke bekreftet ennå. Jeg spør i dag, og så gir jeg deg beskjed før du sender invitasjonen.
- en: Wait a moment, the room hasn't been confirmed yet. I'll ask today, and then I'll let you know before you send the invitation.
:::

This response does three jobs:

1. It shows which assumption needs attention.
2. It restores the correct status: possible, not confirmed.
3. It establishes what happens next and when Jonas can act.

## What different replies do {#response-consequences role=contrast objectives=obj-advanced-pragmatics-01}

Several replies can be grammatical without solving the conversational problem.

::: examples
- no: Ja, det blir sikkert bra.
- en: Yes, it will probably be good.
- no: Forresten, hvem bestiller kaffe?
- en: By the way, who is ordering coffee?
- no: Jeg mente bare at rommet kanskje er ledig. Jeg sjekker det før vi sender invitasjonen.
- en: I only meant that the room might be available. I'll check before we send the invitation.
:::

The first reply allows the mistaken plan to stand. The second introduces a side
topic. **Forresten** can make that topic shift clear, but a clear topic shift still
does not repair the misunderstanding. The third reply corrects the degree of
certainty and protects the next action.

A repair does not need to sound dramatic. Short phrases such as *vent litt*, *ikke
helt*, *jeg mente bare at ...*, or *det er ikke bestemt ennå* can interrupt the
inference without blaming the other person.

## Acknowledge, clarify, continue {#acknowledge-clarify-continue role=model objectives=obj-advanced-pragmatics-01}

A useful repair often follows this sequence:

- **Acknowledge** the relevant goal or concern: *Det hadde vært praktisk.*
- **Clarify** what is intended, known, or confirmed: *Men rommet er ikke bestilt.*
- **Continue** with a suitable next step: *Jeg ringer resepsjonen, og så oppdaterer jeg deg.*

Acknowledgement is useful, but it does not mean agreement with the mistaken
inference. It keeps the exchange cooperative while the clarification changes the
shared understanding.

The exact wording can vary:

::: examples
- no: Det hadde vært praktisk, men jeg vet ikke om rommet er ledig. Jeg sjekker det nå.
- en: That would be practical, but I don't know whether the room is available. I'll check now.
- no: Egentlig foreslo jeg bare at vi kunne undersøke møterommet. Kan du vente med invitasjonen til jeg har fått svar?
- en: What I actually suggested was only that we could check the meeting room. Can you wait to send the invitation until I have an answer?
- no: Du forstår det altså som en bekreftet reservasjon? Det er det ikke ennå. Jeg sier fra når det er avklart.
- en: So you understand it as a confirmed reservation? It isn't one yet. I'll let you know when it has been clarified.
:::

**Egentlig** can help recover the underlying intention. Interactional **altså** can
check the partner's inference. They are optional tools, not requirements. Plain
language may be clearer, especially when the practical consequence matters more
than the tone of the repair.

## Preserve what is still uncertain {#preserve-uncertainty role=model objectives=obj-advanced-pragmatics-01}

A good repair changes only what needs changing. It should not replace one unsupported
claim with another.

::: {.reading translation="The client is interested in Tuesday, but she needs to speak with her manager first." dialogue_id="meeting_date" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Kunden er interessert i tirsdag, men hun må snakke med lederen sin først.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then I'll book everyone's tickets for Tuesday." dialogue_id="meeting_date" speaker_id="erik" speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Da bestiller jeg billetter til alle på tirsdag.
:::

Amina should not answer *Hun kan ikke på tirsdag*, because that is also unknown.
The accurate repair is that Tuesday is **under consideration**, not accepted or
rejected. A next step can be to wait for the client's answer or set a deadline for
checking again.

This matters at B2 because conversational misunderstandings often concern force and
commitment rather than vocabulary: a suggestion becomes a decision, interest becomes
agreement, or an estimate becomes a promise.

## Adapt after new information {#adapt-after-clarification role=model objectives=obj-advanced-pragmatics-01}

A repair can invite the other person to clarify in return. The next response must
then use the new information instead of repeating the original correction.

::: {.reading translation="I thought we could send the draft today." dialogue_id="draft_clarification" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Jeg tenkte at vi kunne sende utkastet i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Okay, then I'll send it to the client now." dialogue_id="draft_clarification" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Greit, da sender jeg det til kunden nå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Do you mean that it isn't ready?" dialogue_id="draft_clarification" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Mener du at det ikke er klart?
:::

Nora can now explain the precise status and agree on a revised action. Her reply
should answer Jonas's question directly; simply repeating *ikke send det* would leave
the reason and next step unclear.

## Transfer: protect the arrangement {#protect-arrangement-transfer role=recap objectives=obj-advanced-pragmatics-01}

In a new situation, begin from the exact mismatch rather than searching for a
particular marker. Ask:

- What was actually proposed or confirmed?
- What has the other person assumed?
- What could go wrong if nobody repairs it?
- What next step would make the arrangement safe and clear?
