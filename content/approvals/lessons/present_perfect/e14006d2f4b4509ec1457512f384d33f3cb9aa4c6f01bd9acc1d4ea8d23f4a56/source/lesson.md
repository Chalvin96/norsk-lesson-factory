## A new flat, but not everything is ready {#sec-new-flat role=orient objectives=obj-present-perfect-01}

Maja has recently moved into a new flat. Her friend Sander arrives shortly before Maja’s parents are due to visit.

::: {.reading translation="Have you unpacked everything?" dialogue_id="maja-flat-opening" speaker_id="sander" speaker_name="Sander"}
Har du pakket ut alt?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I have unpacked the clothes, but I haven’t opened the boxes of books." dialogue_id="maja-flat-opening" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jeg har pakket ut klærne, men jeg har ikke åpnet eskene med bøker.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Have you found the coffee maker?" dialogue_id="maja-flat-opening" speaker_id="sander" speaker_name="Sander"}
Har du funnet kaffetrakteren?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but I have forgotten to buy coffee." dialogue_id="maja-flat-opening" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Ja, men jeg har glemt å kjøpe kaffe.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s all right. I have bought coffee and bread on the way." dialogue_id="maja-flat-opening" speaker_id="sander" speaker_name="Sander"}
Det går bra. Jeg har kjøpt kaffe og brød på veien.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Perfect. My parents are coming soon, and I haven’t tidied the kitchen." dialogue_id="maja-flat-opening" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Perfekt. Foreldrene mine kommer snart, og jeg har ikke ryddet kjøkkenet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I have already cleaned the counter. Now we’ll deal with the boxes together." dialogue_id="maja-flat-opening" speaker_id="sander" speaker_name="Sander"}
Jeg har allerede vasket benken. Nå tar vi eskene sammen.
:::

The conversation is about actions in the past, but the speakers care about their results now. The clothes are out of their boxes, the books are still packed, and there is now coffee for the visitors. This connection between a past action and the present is the central meaning of the Norwegian present perfect.

## The form: har + past participle {#sec-form-har-past-participle role=model objectives=obj-present-perfect-01}

The present perfect (presens perfektum) consists of two parts:

har + past participle (perfektum partisipp)

Har is the present-tense form of å ha, to have. The past participle is a special form of the main verb.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har pakket.
- en: I have packed.
- no: Hun har kjøpt kaffe.
- en: She has bought coffee.
- no: Vi har funnet nøklene.
- en: We have found the keys.
:::

The whole expression is used for completed actions or experiences connected to now, and for situations that began earlier and continue now. Har does not change according to the subject:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har ryddet.
- en: I have tidied.
- no: Sander har ryddet.
- en: Sander has tidied.
- no: Vi har ryddet.
- en: We have tidied.
:::

The past participle also stays the same in this construction:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har kjøpt brød.
- en: I have bought bread.
- no: De har kjøpt brød.
- en: They have bought bread.
:::

Norwegian uses **har** with every subject; it has no different forms corresponding to English has and have. A common mistake is to use the infinitive **ha** instead of the present form **har**.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Hun ha kjøpt kaffe.
- en: She has bought coffee.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Hun har kjøpt kaffe.
- en: She has bought coffee.
:::

## Recognizing past participles {#sec-recognizing-past-participles role=model objectives=obj-present-perfect-01}

Many past participles are easy to recognize once you have met the verb’s main forms. However, there is no single ending that works for every Norwegian verb. Learn the past participle as part of the verb.

Some common verbs have participles ending in -et:

::: examples
- no: å snakke → har snakket
- en: to speak → has spoken
- no: å pakke → har pakket
- en: to pack → has packed
- no: å vaske → har vasket
- en: to wash → has washed
- no: å åpne → har åpnet
- en: to open → has opened
- no: å rydde → har ryddet
- en: to tidy → has tidied
:::

Other common verbs have shorter participles:

::: examples
- no: å kjøpe → har kjøpt
- en: to buy → has bought
- no: å prøve → har prøvd
- en: to try → has tried
- no: å lese → har lest
- en: to read → has read
:::

Several frequent verbs have forms that must be learned individually:

::: examples
- no: å gjøre → har gjort
- en: to do → has done
- no: å skrive → har skrevet
- en: to write → has written
- no: å se → har sett
- en: to see → has seen
- no: å være → har vært
- en: to be → has been
- no: å gå → har gått
- en: to go → has gone
- no: å spise → har spist
- en: to eat → has eaten
- no: å ta → har tatt
- en: to take → has taken
- no: å finne → har funnet
- en: to find → has found
:::

The infinitive ending alone does not always tell you the participle. For example, å kjøpe becomes kjøpt, not ✗ kjøpet, and å skrive becomes skrevet, not ✗ skrivet.

When a dictionary gives forms such as kjøpe – kjøpte – kjøpt, the final form, kjøpt, is the one used after har.

## Why use the present perfect? {#sec-why-present-perfect role=contrast objectives=obj-present-perfect-01}

The present perfect connects earlier time with the present. In the patterns taught here, it presents completed actions or experiences connected to now, or situations that began earlier and still continue. It often answers a practical question such as: What is ready now? What has happened so far? What experience does someone have now?

## A completed action with a result now {#sec-completed-action-result-now role=contrast objectives=obj-present-perfect-01}

Maja says:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har pakket ut klærne.
- en: I have unpacked the clothes.
:::

The unpacking happened earlier. Its result matters now: the clothes are no longer packed.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Sander har kjøpt kaffe.
- en: Sander has bought coffee.
- no: Jeg har mistet nøkkelen.
- en: I have lost the key.
- no: Vi har laget middag.
- en: We have made dinner.
:::

The important result is that they have coffee now.

The loss happened earlier, but the current problem is that the speaker does not have the key.

The dinner is ready now.

The action does not need to have happened only a few minutes ago. What matters is the present connection.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har lest boka, så du kan låne den.
- en: I have read the book, so you can borrow it.
- no: De har solgt bilen og tar bussen nå.
- en: They have sold the car and take the bus now.
:::

## Experience up to now {#sec-experience-up-to-now role=contrast objectives=obj-present-perfect-01}

The present perfect can also describe experience within a period that reaches the present.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har vært i Bergen tre ganger.
- en: I have been to Bergen three times.
- no: Hun har aldri sett nordlyset.
- en: She has never seen the northern lights.
- no: Har du spist norsk brunost?
- en: Have you eaten Norwegian brown cheese?
:::

The exact occasion is not the point. The speaker is asking or stating what belongs to someone’s experience up to now.

Words such as allerede, already, aldri, never, and ennå, yet, often fit this meaning:

::: examples
- no: Vi har allerede sett filmen.
- en: We have already seen the film.
- no: Jeg har aldri prøvd dette før.
- en: I have never tried this before.
- no: Han har ikke svart ennå.
- en: He hasn’t answered yet.
:::

These words do not create the present perfect. They help show how the past event is viewed from the present.

## A situation that began earlier and still continues {#sec-continuing-situation role=contrast objectives=obj-present-perfect-01}

Norwegian also uses the present perfect for a situation that started in the past and is still true now.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har bodd her i to år.
- en: I have lived here for two years.
- no: Vi har kjent hverandre lenge.
- en: We have known each other for a long time.
- no: Hun har jobbet på hotellet siden mai.
- en: She has worked at the hotel since May.
:::

The time expressions support the present connection:

- i to år means for two years: a length of time.
- siden mai means since May: a starting point.

The situation has not been presented as finished. Jeg har bodd her i to år normally means that I still live here.

A likely mistake is to use only the present tense because the situation is still true. Norwegian uses har + past participle when the speaker includes the period from the past up to now.

Incorrect for the intended meaning:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg bor her i to år.
- en: I live here for two years.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har bodd her i to år.
- en: I have lived here for two years.
:::

## Present relevance versus a finished past occasion {#sec-present-relevance-finished-occasion role=contrast objectives=obj-present-perfect-01}

The present perfect normally does not locate an event at a specific, finished past time such as yesterday or last year. If the speaker reports what happened during a finished past occasion, Norwegian generally uses the past tense (preteritum).

Compare the viewpoints:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har kjøpt kaffe.
- en: I have bought coffee.
- no: Jeg kjøpte kaffe i går.
- en: I bought coffee yesterday.
- no: Har du sett den filmen?
- en: Have you seen that film?
- no: Så du den filmen på fredag?
- en: Did you see that film on Friday?
- no: Hun har vært i Tromsø mange ganger.
- en: She has been to Tromsø many times.
- no: Hun var i Tromsø i fjor.
- en: She was in Tromsø last year.
:::

The coffee is available now. No finished past time is specified.

The speaker places the event yesterday.

This asks about experience up to now.

This asks about a particular finished occasion.

The visits belong to her experience up to now.

The visit is located in a finished year.

This chapter does not require you to produce all past-tense forms. The contrast helps you make the present-perfect choice: use har + past participle when the past event is viewed through its result, experience, or continuation now.

A common error is to combine the present perfect with a finished-time expression:

Incorrect in ordinary use:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg har kjøpt boka i går.
- en: I have bought the book yesterday.
:::

For present relevance, remove the finished time:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har kjøpt boka.
- en: I have bought the book.
:::

If yesterday is the point, the sentence needs the past-time viewpoint:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kjøpte boka i går.
- en: I bought the book yesterday.
:::

## Negative statements and questions {#sec-negative-statements-questions role=model objectives=obj-present-perfect-01}

The central verb form remains har + past participle in statements, negatives, and questions.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har åpnet esken.
- en: I have opened the box.
- no: Jeg har ikke åpnet esken.
- en: I have not opened the box.
- no: Har du åpnet esken?
- en: Have you opened the box?
- no: Hun har svart.
- en: She has answered.
- no: Hun har ikke svart ennå.
- en: She has not answered yet.
- no: Har hun svart?
- en: Has she answered?
:::

Do not replace the participle with the infinitive after har.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg har åpne esken.
- en: I have open the box.
:::

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg har åpner esken.
- en: I have opens the box.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har åpnet esken.
- en: I have opened the box.
:::

## Transfer: preparing for a weekend trip {#sec-trip-preparation-transfer role=recap objectives=obj-present-perfect-01}

Now change the situation. Amir and Nora are leaving for a cabin this afternoon. They need to check what is ready before they go.

Relevant statements might include:

::: examples
- no: Nora har pakket varme klær.
- en: Nora has packed warm clothes.
- no: Amir har funnet kartet.
- en: Amir has found the map.
- no: De har ikke kjøpt mat ennå.
- en: They have not bought food yet.
- no: Har Nora tatt med nøklene?
- en: Has Nora brought the keys?
- no: Amir har vært på hytta før.
- en: Amir has been to the cabin before.
:::

The same construction now serves several closely related present-relevant purposes: completed preparations with results now, something still not done, and relevant experience.
