## A change of plans {#change-of-plans role=orient objectives=obj-subordinating-conjunctions-01}

Maja and Jonas are preparing to spend Saturday at a cabin outside town. Maja calls Jonas on Friday evening because the weather forecast has changed.

::: {.reading translation="I think that there will be a lot of rain tomorrow." dialogue_id="change-of-plans" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jeg tror at det blir mye regn i morgen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Should we cancel the trip because the weather is going to be bad?" dialogue_id="change-of-plans" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Skal vi avlyse turen fordi været blir dårlig?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, we can go if the road is open." dialogue_id="change-of-plans" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Nei, vi kan dra hvis veien er åpen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="When should we decide?" dialogue_id="change-of-plans" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Når skal vi bestemme oss?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’ll check the weather forecast before I go to bed, and I’ll send you a message when I wake up." dialogue_id="change-of-plans" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jeg sjekker værmeldingen før jeg legger meg, og jeg sender deg en melding når jeg våkner.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. I’ll pack my rain jacket even though I hope that we won’t need it." dialogue_id="change-of-plans" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Bra. Jeg pakker regnjakka selv om jeg håper at vi ikke trenger den.
:::

::: {.reading translation="While you’re packing, I can check whether the shop near the cabin is open." dialogue_id="change-of-plans" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Mens du pakker, kan jeg sjekke om butikken ved hytta er åpen.
:::

The conversation is mainly about one decision: will Maja and Jonas go to the cabin? To discuss that decision, they connect ideas in several ways. Maja reports what she thinks, Jonas gives a reason, they state a condition, and they place actions in time.

The small connecting words make those relationships clear:

::: examples
- no: at
- en: that
- no: fordi
- en: because
- no: hvis
- en: if
- no: når
- en: when
- no: før
- en: before
- no: selv om
- en: even though
- no: mens
- en: while
- no: om
- en: whether
:::

These words are subordinating conjunctions (subjunksjoner). A subordinating conjunction introduces a dependent clause (leddsetning): a group of words containing a subject and a verb that functions as part of a larger sentence.

In this sentence, the first part can stand as the main message:

::: examples
- no: Vi kan dra hvis veien er åpen.
- en: We can go if the road is open.
:::

The dependent clause begins with hvis:

::: examples
- no: hvis veien er åpen
- en: if the road is open
:::

It contains a subject, veien, and a verb, er. However, by itself it leaves an obvious question: what happens if the road is open? The main clause supplies that information.

Your task in this chapter is to choose a conjunction that expresses the intended relationship and to recognize the dependent clause that it introduces.

## Reporting a thought or statement with at {#reporting-a-thought-or-statement-with-at role=model objectives=obj-subordinating-conjunctions-01}

Use at when one clause gives the content of a thought, belief, statement, or piece of information. It often corresponds to English that.

::: examples
- no: Maja tror at det blir regn.
- en: Maja thinks that it will rain.
- no: Jonas sier at han er klar.
- en: Jonas says that he is ready.
- no: Vi vet at veien er åpen.
- en: We know that the road is open.
- no: Jeg håper at butikken ikke er stengt.
- en: I hope that the shop is not closed.
:::

In each example, the words after at tell us what someone thinks, says, knows, or hopes.

Compare the two parts:

::: examples
- no: Maja tror.
- en: Maja thinks.
- no: at det blir regn
- en: that it will rain
:::

The first part is grammatically possible, but it does not tell us what Maja thinks. The at-clause provides the content.

A compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use at to introduce the content of a thought, statement, belief, hope, or known fact.
:::

Do not choose at merely because English can sometimes omit that. Norwegian may also omit at in some informal contexts, but at is common, clear, and useful at this level.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror at bussen kommer snart.
- en: I think that the bus will arrive soon.
:::

The meaning is not a reason or a condition. The clause tells us what the speaker thinks.

## Giving a reason with fordi {#giving-a-reason-with-fordi role=model objectives=obj-subordinating-conjunctions-01}

Use fordi when the dependent clause explains why something happens or why someone makes a choice. It corresponds to because.

::: examples
- no: De vurderer å avlyse turen fordi været blir dårlig.
- en: They are considering cancelling the trip because the weather is going to be bad.
- no: Maja tar med støvler fordi det har regnet.
- en: Maja is bringing boots because it has rained.
- no: Jonas ringer fordi han har et spørsmål.
- en: Jonas calls because he has a question.
- no: Vi blir inne fordi det blåser mye.
- en: We are staying inside because it is very windy.
:::

The fordi-clause answers a why-question.

::: examples
- no: Hvorfor tar Maja med støvler?
- en: Why is Maja bringing boots?
- no: Fordi det har regnet.
- en: Because it has rained.
:::

In conversation, a fordi-clause can be used as a short answer when the missing main idea is already understood. In a full sentence, it belongs to a larger message:

::: examples
- no: Maja tar med støvler fordi det har regnet.
- en: Maja is bringing boots because it has rained.
:::

A compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use fordi when the dependent clause gives a reason.
:::

A common mistake is to choose fordi whenever two events are connected. The connection must specifically be one of cause or explanation.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Vi drar hvis veien er åpen.
- en: We will go if the road is open.
- no: Vi drar fordi veien er åpen.
- en: We are going because the road is open.
:::

The first sentence states a condition: the trip depends on the road being open. The second gives the open road as a reason for going.

## Stating a condition with hvis {#stating-a-condition-with-hvis role=contrast objectives=obj-subordinating-conjunctions-01}

Use hvis when an event or decision depends on a condition. It corresponds to if.

::: examples
- no: Vi drar hvis veien er åpen.
- en: We will go if the road is open.
- no: Jeg kjører hvis du er trøtt.
- en: I’ll drive if you are tired.
- no: Vi kan handle på veien hvis butikken ved hytta er stengt.
- en: We can shop on the way if the shop near the cabin is closed.
- no: Hvis det regner, blir vi inne.
- en: If it rains, we will stay inside.
:::

The hvis-clause can come after or before the main clause. Its function remains the same: it gives the condition under which the main situation applies.

::: examples
- no: Jonas tar med ved hvis det blir kaldt.
- en: Jonas will bring firewood if it gets cold.
- no: Hvis det blir kaldt, tar Jonas med ved.
- en: If it gets cold, Jonas will bring firewood.
:::

A compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use hvis when the main event depends on a possible condition.
:::

Do not confuse a condition with a reason:

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar med mat hvis butikken er stengt.
- en: I’ll bring food if the shop is closed.
:::

The speaker does not yet present the closure as a fact. It is a possibility that affects the plan.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar med mat fordi butikken er stengt.
- en: I’m bringing food because the shop is closed.
:::

Here, the closure is presented as the reason for the decision.

## Placing events in time with når, før, and mens {#placing-events-in-time role=model objectives=obj-subordinating-conjunctions-01}

Several common subordinating conjunctions tell us how two events relate in time.

**Når: when**

Use når to connect an event with a time or recurring situation.

::: examples
- no: Jeg sender deg en melding når jeg våkner.
- en: I’ll send you a message when I wake up.
- no: Når vi kommer fram, lager vi middag.
- en: When we arrive, we will make dinner.
- no: Jonas blir glad når han er på hytta.
- en: Jonas is happy when he is at the cabin.
- no: Vi spiller kort når det regner.
- en: We play cards when it rains.
:::

In the dialogue, Maja has not woken up yet. Når introduces the future time at which she will send the message.

A compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use når when one event occurs at the time another event occurs, especially in present, future, or repeated situations.
:::

A question beginning with når is not automatically a dependent clause:

::: examples
- no: Når skal vi bestemme oss?
- en: When should we decide?
:::

This is a complete question. Compare it with:

::: examples
- no: Vi bestemmer oss når Maja har sjekket værmeldingen.
- en: We will decide when Maja has checked the weather forecast.
:::

Here, når introduces the dependent clause:

::: examples
- no: når Maja har sjekket værmeldingen
- en: when Maja has checked the weather forecast
:::

To recognize a dependent clause, look at its job in the sentence, not only at the word når. In the second example, the når-clause gives the time for the main decision.

**Før: before**

Use før when the event in the dependent clause happens later than the main event.

::: examples
- no: Maja sjekker værmeldingen før hun legger seg.
- en: Maja checks the weather forecast before she goes to bed.
- no: Vi kjøper mat før vi kjører til hytta.
- en: We buy food before we drive to the cabin.
- no: Jonas fyller bensin før han henter Maja.
- en: Jonas gets petrol before he picks up Maja.
:::

In the first example, checking the forecast happens first. Going to bed happens afterward, although it appears inside the før-clause.

A compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use før to mark the later event and show that the main event happens before it.
:::

**Mens: while**

Use mens when two situations happen during the same period.

::: examples
- no: Mens Jonas pakker, sjekker Maja butikken.
- en: While Jonas is packing, Maja checks the shop.
- no: Vi hører på radio mens vi kjører.
- en: We listen to the radio while we drive.
- no: Maja lager kaffe mens Jonas tenner i ovnen.
- en: Maja makes coffee while Jonas lights the stove.
:::

A compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use mens when two events or situations overlap in time.
:::

Compare these time relationships:

::: examples
- no: Jeg sjekker kartet før vi kjører.
- en: I check the map before we drive.
- no: Jeg sjekker kartet når vi stopper.
- en: I check the map when we stop.
- no: Jeg ser på kartet mens du kjører.
- en: I look at the map while you drive.
:::

Før places one event before another. Når identifies the time at which something happens. Mens presents two actions as overlapping.

## Expressing an unexpected contrast with selv om {#expressing-an-unexpected-contrast-with-selv-om role=contrast objectives=obj-subordinating-conjunctions-01}

Use selv om when the main situation is true despite something that might normally lead to a different result. It corresponds to even though or although.

::: examples
- no: Jonas pakker regnjakka selv om han håper på sol.
- en: Jonas packs his rain jacket even though he hopes for sunshine.
- no: De drar på tur selv om været er dårlig.
- en: They go on the trip even though the weather is bad.
- no: Maja er trøtt selv om hun har sovet lenge.
- en: Maja is tired even though she has slept for a long time.
- no: Selv om det regner, går de en liten tur.
- en: Even though it is raining, they go for a short walk.
:::

The dependent clause introduces information that could create an expectation. The main clause shows that the expected result does not follow.

Bad weather might make us expect Maja and Jonas to stay home. Instead:

::: examples
- no: De drar selv om været er dårlig.
- en: They go even though the weather is bad.
:::

A compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use selv om when the main event happens despite the situation in the dependent clause.
:::

Do not use selv om for an ordinary reason:

::: examples
- no: De blir inne fordi det regner.
- en: They stay inside because it is raining.
:::

The rain explains the decision.

::: examples
- no: De går ut selv om det regner.
- en: They go outside even though it is raining.
:::

Going outside contrasts with the expectation created by the rain.

## Asking indirectly with om {#asking-indirectly-with-om role=model objectives=obj-subordinating-conjunctions-01}

In the opening dialogue, Maja says:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan sjekke om butikken ved hytta er åpen.
- en: I can check whether the shop near the cabin is open.
:::

Here, om means whether. It introduces something that is uncertain and can be checked, asked, known, or discovered.

::: examples
- no: Jonas spør om veien er åpen.
- en: Jonas asks whether the road is open.
- no: Vi vet ikke om det blir regn.
- en: We do not know whether it will rain.
- no: Maja undersøker om bussen går på lørdager.
- en: Maja checks whether the bus runs on Saturdays.
- no: Jeg lurer på om hytta er varm.
- en: I wonder whether the cabin is warm.
:::

A compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use om to introduce an indirect yes-or-no question after expressions such as ask, know, check, or wonder.
:::

Compare at and om:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vet at butikken er åpen.
- en: I know that the shop is open.
- no: Jeg vet ikke om butikken er åpen.
- en: I do not know whether the shop is open.
:::

With at, the speaker presents the information as known. With om, the question of whether the shop is open remains uncertain.

This distinction is about the relationship between the clauses:

- at introduces content presented as a statement or fact;
- om introduces an unresolved yes-or-no question.

## Returning to the cabin trip {#returning-to-the-cabin-trip role=model objectives=obj-subordinating-conjunctions-01}

On Saturday morning, Maja checks the forecast again. The rain will arrive later than expected, and the road is open. She sends Jonas a message.

::: {.reading translation="I can see that the road is open." dialogue_id="cabin-trip-return" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jeg ser at veien er åpen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="We can go if you still want to." dialogue_id="cabin-trip-return" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Vi kan dra hvis du fortsatt har lyst.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I want to go even though it may rain this evening." dialogue_id="cabin-trip-return" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jeg vil dra selv om det kanskje blir regn i kveld.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Can you check whether we need to bring firewood?" dialogue_id="cabin-trip-return" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Kan du sjekke om vi trenger å ta med ved?
:::

Jonas replies:

::: {.reading translation="Yes. I’ll call the owner before I leave home." dialogue_id="cabin-trip-return" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. Jeg ringer eieren før jeg kjører hjemmefra.
:::

::: {.reading translation="While I’m speaking with her, I can also ask whether the shop is open." dialogue_id="cabin-trip-return" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Mens jeg snakker med henne, kan jeg også spørre om butikken er åpen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’ll send you a message when I know more." dialogue_id="cabin-trip-return" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg sender deg en melding når jeg vet mer.
:::

The conjunctions let Maja and Jonas build one plan from several connected ideas. They report information with at, state a condition with hvis, accept an unexpected difficulty with selv om, investigate uncertainty with om, and organize actions with før, mens, and når.
