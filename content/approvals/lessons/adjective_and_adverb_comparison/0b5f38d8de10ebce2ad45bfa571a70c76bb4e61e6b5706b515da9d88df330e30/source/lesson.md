## Two routes to the cabin {#two-routes-to-the-cabin role=orient objectives=obj-adjective-and-adverb-comparison-01}

Mina and Jonas are renting bicycles for a weekend trip. They must choose between two routes and several bikes.

::: {.reading translation="This bicycle is lighter than the red one." dialogue_id=two-routes-to-the-cabin speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Denne sykkelen er lettere enn den røde.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but the red one looks more comfortable." dialogue_id=two-routes-to-the-cabin speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, men den røde ser mer komfortabel ut.
:::

::: {.reading translation="The short route is steeper. We must cycle more slowly there." dialogue_id=two-routes-to-the-cabin speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Den korte ruta er brattere. Vi må sykle saktere der.
:::

::: {.reading translation="The long route is less steep, but it is more complicated." dialogue_id=two-routes-to-the-cabin speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Den lange ruta er mindre bratt, men den er mer komplisert.
:::

::: {.reading translation="You cycle faster than I do, so you can wait if necessary." dialogue_id=two-routes-to-the-cabin speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Du sykler fortere enn meg, så du kan vente hvis det er nødvendig.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Of course. Which bicycle is best for you?" dialogue_id=two-routes-to-the-cabin speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Selvfølgelig. Hvilken sykkel er best for deg?
:::

::: {.reading translation="The blue one is lightest, but the red one has the best seat." dialogue_id=two-routes-to-the-cabin speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Den blå er lettest, men den røde har det beste setet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then you take the red one. If we cycle carefully, we’ll arrive safely." dialogue_id=two-routes-to-the-cabin speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Da tar du den røde. Hvis vi sykler forsiktig, kommer vi trygt fram.
:::

Mina and Jonas compare both things and actions. A bicycle can be **lettere** “lighter,” while a person can cycle **fortere** “faster.” They also choose a highest degree: one bicycle is **lettest** “lightest,” and one seat is **best** “best.”

## What comparison forms do {#what-comparison-forms-do role=model objectives=obj-adjective-and-adverb-comparison-01}

Norwegian uses three degrees of comparison:

- the base form (**positiv**): no comparison
- the comparative (**komparativ**): comparing two people, things, or actions
- the superlative (**superlativ**): identifying the highest or lowest degree in a group

Compare these three sentences:

::: examples
- no: Sykkelen er lett.
- en: The bicycle is light.
- no: Den blå sykkelen er lettere enn den røde.
- en: The blue bicycle is lighter than the red one.
- no: Den blå sykkelen er lettest.
- en: The blue bicycle is lightest.
:::

The same three-way distinction applies to adverbs describing actions:

::: examples
- no: Jonas sykler fort.
- en: Jonas cycles fast.
- no: Jonas sykler fortere enn Mina.
- en: Jonas cycles faster than Mina.
- no: Jonas sykler fortest.
- en: Jonas cycles fastest.
:::

The compact meaning rule is:

- Use the base form when you are not comparing.
- Use the comparative when one side has more or less of a quality than another.
- Use the superlative for the highest or lowest degree within a group.

## Regular comparison with *-ere* and *-est* {#regular-comparison role=model objectives=obj-adjective-and-adverb-comparison-01}

Many short adjectives and adverbs form the comparative with **-ere** and the superlative with **-est**.

- **lett** — **lettere** — **lettest**
- **rask** — **raskere** — **raskest**
- **billig** — **billigere** — **billigst**
- **sakte** — **saktere** — **saktest**
- **fort** — **fortere** — **fortest**

**Comparing people and things**

::: examples
- no: Den blå sykkelen er lett.
- en: The blue bicycle is light.
- no: Den blå sykkelen er lettere enn den røde.
- en: The blue bicycle is lighter than the red one.
- no: Den blå sykkelen er lettest.
- en: The blue bicycle is lightest.
- no: Jonas er raskere enn Mina.
- en: Jonas is faster than Mina.
- no: Mina valgte den billigste sykkelen.
- en: Mina chose the cheapest bicycle.
:::

The word **enn** normally introduces the second side of a comparative:

::: examples
- no: Denne ruta er kortere enn den andre.
- en: This route is shorter than the other one.
- no: Sykkelen er billigere enn scooteren.
- en: The bicycle is cheaper than the scooter.
:::

Do not use **som** after an ordinary comparative.

::: examples
- no: Correct: Ruta er brattere enn veien.
- en: Correct: The route is steeper than the road.
- no: ✗ Common mistake: Ruta er brattere som veien.
- en: ✗ Common mistake: The route is steeper than the road.
:::

**Comparing actions**

An adjective describes a person or thing. An adverb describes how an action happens.

::: examples
- no: Jonas er raskere enn Mina.
- en: Jonas is faster than Mina.
:::

Here **raskere** describes Jonas.

::: examples
- no: Jonas sykler fortere enn Mina.
- en: Jonas cycles faster than Mina.
:::

Here **fortere** describes the cycling.

Another contrast:

::: examples
- no: Mina er roligere i dag.
- en: Mina is calmer today.
- no: Mina sykler roligere i dag.
- en: Mina cycles more calmly today.
:::

Some Norwegian forms can function in both roles, so meaning and sentence position matter:

::: examples
- no: Den raske syklisten kom først.
- en: The fast cyclist arrived first.
- no: Syklisten kjørte raskere på flat vei.
- en: The cyclist rode faster on flat ground.
:::

**Form behavior in comparisons**

Regular comparative forms do not change for gender or number:

::: examples
- no: en raskere sykkel
- en: a faster bicycle
- no: et raskere tog
- en: a faster train
- no: raskere busser
- en: faster buses
:::

A superlative used after the verb can stand in its short form:

::: examples
- no: Denne sykkelen er raskest.
- en: This bicycle is fastest.
:::

Before a definite noun, the superlative normally takes **-e**:

::: examples
- no: den raskeste sykkelen
- en: the fastest bicycle
- no: det billigste alternativet
- en: the cheapest alternative
- no: de letteste sekkene
- en: the lightest backpacks
:::

## Longer forms with *mer* and *mest* {#longer-forms-with-mer-and-mest role=contrast objectives=obj-adjective-and-adverb-comparison-01}

Longer adjectives, especially many borrowed or structurally complex words, often use **mer** “more” and **mest** “most” rather than adding **-ere** and **-est**.

- **komfortabel** — **mer komfortabel** — **mest komfortabel**
- **komplisert** — **mer komplisert** — **mest komplisert**
- **moderne** — **mer moderne** — **mest moderne**
- **effektiv** — **mer effektiv** — **mest effektiv**

::: examples
- no: Den røde sykkelen er mer komfortabel enn den blå.
- en: The red bicycle is more comfortable than the blue one.
- no: Dette er den mest komfortable sykkelen i butikken.
- en: This is the most comfortable bicycle in the shop.
- no: Den lange ruta er mer komplisert.
- en: The long route is more complicated.
- no: Kartet viser den minst kompliserte ruta.
- en: The map shows the least complicated route.
:::

For the lower degree, use **mindre** “less” and **minst** “least”:

::: examples
- no: Denne veien er mindre komplisert enn skogsstien.
- en: This road is less complicated than the forest path.
- no: Det er den minst kompliserte løsningen.
- en: It is the least complicated solution.
:::

**Long-form adverbs**

The same pattern can compare how actions happen:

::: examples
- no: Jonas forklarer ruta presist.
- en: Jonas explains the route precisely.
- no: Mina forklarer den mer presist.
- en: Mina explains it more precisely.
- no: Guiden forklarer den mest presist.
- en: The guide explains it most precisely.
- no: Vi planlegger turen effektivt.
- en: We plan the trip efficiently.
- no: Med kartet planlegger vi mer effektivt.
- en: With the map, we plan more efficiently.
:::

Here **presist** and **effektivt** describe actions. The words following **mer** and **mest** remain in their appropriate adjective or adverb form.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Et mer komfortabelt sete koster mer.
- en: A more comfortable seat costs more.
- no: Hun sitter mer komfortabelt på dette setet.
- en: She sits more comfortably on this seat.
:::

In the first sentence, **komfortabelt** agrees with the neuter noun **et sete**. In the second, it describes how she sits.

A common error is to apply **mer** to every adjective:

::: examples
- no: Correct: Denne sykkelen er raskere.
- en: Correct: This bicycle is faster.
- no: ✗ Not normally: Denne sykkelen er mer rask.
- en: ✗ Not normally: This bicycle is more fast.
:::

Use **-ere/-est** with ordinary short regular forms such as **rask**, **lett**, and **billig**. Use **mer/mest** with forms such as **komfortabel**, **komplisert**, and **moderne**.

## Irregular comparison {#irregular-comparison role=contrast objectives=obj-adjective-and-adverb-comparison-01}

Several frequent words have irregular comparison forms. These must be learned as complete sets.

- **god / godt** — **bedre** — **best**
- **dårlig** — **verre** — **verst**
- **stor** — **større** — **størst**
- **liten / lite** — **mindre** — **minst**
- **lang / langt** — **lengre** — **lengst**
- **mye** — **mer** — **mest**

**God, bedre, best**

::: examples
- no: Dette er en god sykkel.
- en: This is a good bicycle.
- no: Den røde sykkelen er bedre enn den gamle.
- en: The red bicycle is better than the old one.
- no: Dette er den beste sykkelen for lange turer.
- en: This is the best bicycle for long trips.
:::

The adverb form **godt** uses the same irregular comparative and superlative:

::: examples
- no: Mina sykler godt.
- en: Mina cycles well.
- no: Hun sykler bedre etter pausen.
- en: She cycles better after the break.
- no: Av alle i gruppa sykler hun best.
- en: Of everyone in the group, she cycles best.
:::

Do not form these as *godere* or *godest*.

**Dårlig, verre, verst**

::: examples
- no: Været er dårlig.
- en: The weather is bad.
- no: Været er verre i fjellet.
- en: The weather is worse in the mountains.
- no: Søndagens vær blir verst.
- en: Sunday’s weather will be worst.
:::

The same forms can compare actions:

::: examples
- no: Jonas ser dårlig uten brillene.
- en: Jonas sees poorly without his glasses.
- no: Han ser verre i mørket.
- en: He sees worse in the dark.
:::

**Size, amount, and distance**

::: examples
- no: Den røde sekken er stor.
- en: The red backpack is big.
- no: Den blå sekken er større.
- en: The blue backpack is bigger.
- no: Den svarte sekken er størst.
- en: The black backpack is biggest.
- no: Mina har en liten sekk.
- en: Mina has a small backpack.
- no: Jonas har en mindre sekk.
- en: Jonas has a smaller backpack.
- no: Guiden har den minste sekken.
- en: The guide has the smallest backpack.
- no: Den andre ruta er lengre enn denne.
- en: The other route is longer than this one.
- no: Vi syklet lengst på lørdag.
- en: We cycled farthest on Saturday.
- no: Jonas trener mye.
- en: Jonas trains a lot.
- no: Mina trener mer enn Jonas.
- en: Mina trains more than Jonas.
- no: Guiden trener mest.
- en: The guide trains most.
:::

Notice that **mer** and **mest** have two related jobs. They can be comparison forms of **mye**, or they can stand before another word:

::: examples
- no: Mina trener mer.
- en: Mina trains more.
- no: Denne sykkelen er mer komfortabel.
- en: This bicycle is more comfortable.
:::

In the first sentence, **mer** carries the main meaning “more.” In the second, it helps compare **komfortabel**.

## Returning to Mina and Jonas {#returning-to-mina-and-jonas role=model objectives=obj-adjective-and-adverb-comparison-01}

Mina chooses the red bicycle, and the two riders try the shorter route.

::: {.reading translation="The seat is actually more comfortable than the seat on the blue bicycle." dialogue_id=returning-to-mina-and-jonas speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Setet er faktisk mer komfortabelt enn setet på den blå sykkelen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. How is it going on the hill?" dialogue_id=returning-to-mina-and-jonas speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Bra. Hvordan går det i bakken?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’m cycling more slowly here, but the bicycle works better than I expected." dialogue_id=returning-to-mina-and-jonas speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Jeg sykler saktere her, men sykkelen fungerer bedre enn jeg forventet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="This section is steepest. After the bend, the road becomes easier." dialogue_id=returning-to-mina-and-jonas speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Denne delen er brattest. Etter svingen blir veien lettere.
:::

::: {.reading translation="You explain the route more precisely than the map does!" dialogue_id=returning-to-mina-and-jonas speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Du forklarer ruta mer presist enn kartet gjør!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Perhaps, but the map knows the shortest way home." dialogue_id=returning-to-mina-and-jonas speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Kanskje, men kartet kjenner den korteste veien hjem.
:::

The conversation now combines all three patterns:

- **saktere, brattest, lettere, korteste**: regular forms
- **mer komfortabelt, mer presist**: comparison with **mer**
- **bedre**: irregular comparison
