## A weekend invitation {#a-weekend-invitation role=orient objectives=obj-da-vs-nar-01}

Mina has recently moved to Bergen. On Monday morning, she tells her colleague Jonas about the weekend.

::: {.reading translation='When I came home on Friday, there was a message from Aunt Liv on my phone.' dialogue_id='weekend-invitation' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Da jeg kom hjem på fredag, lå det en melding fra tante Liv på telefonen.
:::

::: {.reading translation='What did she want?' dialogue_id='weekend-invitation' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hva ville hun?
:::

::: {.reading translation='She invited me to dinner. When she has visitors, she always makes far too much food.' dialogue_id='weekend-invitation' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Hun inviterte meg på middag. Når hun får besøk, lager hun alltid altfor mye mat.
:::

::: {.reading translation='That sounds nice.' dialogue_id='weekend-invitation' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Det høres hyggelig ut.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes. When I arrived there on Saturday, there were four dishes on the table.' dialogue_id='weekend-invitation' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja. Da jeg kom dit på lørdag, sto det fire retter på bordet.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Four dishes for two people?' dialogue_id='weekend-invitation' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Fire retter for to personer?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Of course! When we eat together, she always sends leftovers home with me.' dialogue_id='weekend-invitation' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Selvfølgelig! Når vi spiser sammen, sender hun alltid rester med meg hjem.
:::

::: {.reading translation='So now you have dinner for the whole week?' dialogue_id='weekend-invitation' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Så nå har du middag hele uka?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Almost. And when she comes to my place next month, I’m going to make dinner for her.' dialogue_id='weekend-invitation' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Nesten. Og når hun kommer til meg neste måned, skal jeg lage middag til henne.
:::

Mina uses English when several times in her story, but Norwegian divides these situations between **da** and **når**. The important question is not simply whether the sentence is in the past, present, or future. You must ask what kind of time Mina means.

She uses **da** for one specific occasion in the past: coming home on Friday and arriving at her aunt’s home on Saturday. She uses **når** for things that happen repeatedly and for her aunt’s future visit.

## One English word, two Norwegian choices {#one-english-word-two-norwegian-choices role=model objectives=obj-da-vs-nar-01}

In this lesson, **da** and **når** introduce a time clause: a part of a sentence that tells us when something happens.

Use this compact rule:

- Use **da** for one specific time or event in the past.
- Use **når** for future time or repeated time.

A traditional memory aid is:

::: examples
- no: Den gang da, hver gang når.
- en: That one time: da. Every time: når.
:::

This means roughly:

**That one time: da. Every time: når.**

The memory aid is useful, but the real decision is about meaning. Is the speaker pointing to one completed past occasion, or to a future or recurring situation?

## One specific past occasion: da {#one-specific-past-occasion-da role=model objectives=obj-da-vs-nar-01}

Look again at Mina’s first sentence:

::: examples
- no: Da jeg kom hjem på fredag, lå det en melding på telefonen.
- en: When I came home on Friday, there was a message on my phone.
- no: Da jeg kom dit på lørdag, sto det fire retter på bordet.
- en: When I arrived there on Saturday, there were four dishes on the table.
:::

Friday’s arrival happened once. Mina is describing one definite point in her past, so she uses **da**.

The same applies to her arrival at Aunt Liv’s home:

The expressions på fredag and på lørdag make the specific occasions especially clear. However, a sentence does not need an exact date to use **da**. The wider context may already identify one particular past event.

::: examples
- no: Da bussen stoppet, gikk vi av.
- en: When the bus stopped, we got off.
- no: Jeg ble overrasket da jeg så henne.
- en: I was surprised when I saw her.
- no: Da møtet var ferdig, ringte jeg pappa.
- en: When the meeting was over, I called Dad.
:::

Each sentence presents one completed situation. There was one bus stop in the event being described, one sight of the woman, and one end to the meeting.

The **da** clause can come before or after the other part of the sentence:

::: examples
- no: Da filmen begynte, slo alle av telefonene.
- en: When the film started, everyone turned off their phones.
- no: Alle slo av telefonene da filmen begynte.
- en: Everyone turned off their phones when the film started.
:::

The position changes what comes first in the sentence, but it does not change the choice of **da**. The film began on one specific past occasion.

**Checkpoint request: notice-specific-past-da**

**Purpose:** Notice why **da** belongs with a single completed past occasion before producing the form independently.

**Context:** Short continuations of Mina’s Saturday visit and similar everyday stories about one identified day.

**Learner action:** Identify the time clause in each short sentence and explain which single past event makes **da** appropriate.

**Success meaning:** A successful response shows that the learner connects **da** with one particular completed occasion, rather than choosing it mechanically whenever a past-tense verb appears.

{{exercise: notice_specific_past_da}}

## Repeated time: når {#repeated-time-nar role=contrast objectives=obj-da-vs-nar-01}

Now compare Mina’s description of her aunt’s usual behavior:

::: examples
- no: Når hun får besøk, lager hun alltid altfor mye mat.
- en: When she has visitors, she always makes far too much food.
- no: Når vi spiser sammen, sender hun alltid rester med meg hjem.
- en: When we eat together, she always sends leftovers home with me.
:::

Mina is not talking about only last Saturday. She means that this happens regularly. Every time Aunt Liv has visitors, she prepares too much food. Therefore Mina uses **når**.

Again, this is a recurring pattern. Words such as alltid, ofte, vanligvis and hver gang frequently help reveal repeated meaning.

::: examples
- no: Når jeg tar bussen, leser jeg nyhetene.
- en: When I take the bus, I read the news.
- no: Vi går ofte en tur når været er fint.
- en: We often go for a walk when the weather is nice.
- no: Når Erik lager middag, hører han på musikk.
- en: When Erik makes dinner, he listens to music.
- no: Barna blir glade når bestefar kommer på besøk.
- en: The children are happy when Grandpa comes to visit.
:::

In each example, the speaker describes what normally happens in a recurring situation.

Repeated time can also be in the past. This is an important boundary because **da** does not simply mean past and **når** does not simply mean present or future.

Compare these two sentences:

::: examples
- no: Da jeg besøkte bestemor på søndag, bakte hun vafler.
- en: When I visited Grandma on Sunday, she made waffles.
- no: Når jeg besøkte bestemor, bakte hun alltid vafler.
- en: When I visited Grandma, she always made waffles.
:::

The first sentence refers to one Sunday visit, so it uses **da**. The second describes a repeated pattern across several visits, so it uses **når**.

Another contrast:

::: examples
- no: Da toget kom i går, sto Anna på perrongen.
- en: When the train arrived yesterday, Anna was standing on the platform.
- no: Når toget kom om morgenen, sto Anna vanligvis på perrongen.
- en: When the train arrived in the morning, Anna was usually standing on the platform.
:::

The verb forms are in the past in both pairs. The choice changes because the meaning changes: one occasion versus a repeated situation.

**Checkpoint request: distinguish-single-and-repeated-past**

**Purpose:** Separate the time meaning from the verb tense and prevent the misconception that every past sentence requires **da**.

**Context:** Paired memories about family visits, journeys, school days, and work routines. Each pair contrasts one named past occasion with something that happened repeatedly in the past.

**Learner action:** Choose the appropriate time word for each sentence and briefly identify whether the context describes one occasion or a recurring pattern.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates accurate choice between **da** and **når** inside past-time contexts. Repeated past meaning is the scored dimension; other sentence details provide context only.

{{exercise: distinguish_single_and_repeated_past}}

## Future time: når {#future-time-nar role=model objectives=obj-da-vs-nar-01}

Mina ends her story by looking ahead:

::: examples
- no: Når hun kommer til meg neste måned, skal jeg lage middag til henne.
- en: When she comes to my place next month, I’m going to make dinner for her.
- no: Når jeg kommer hjem, skal jeg ringe deg.
- en: When I get home, I’ll call you.
- no: Vi spiser når de andre kommer.
- en: We’ll eat when the others arrive.
- no: Når kurset er ferdig, skal Amir ta en ferie.
- en: When the course is finished, Amir is going to take a holiday.
- no: Hun sender oss adressen når hun finner et hotell.
- en: She’ll send us the address when she finds a hotel.
:::

The visit has not happened yet. Mina is talking about a future situation, so she uses **når**.

Notice that the Norwegian time clause often uses a present-tense form even though the meaning is future:

::: examples
- no: Når hun kommer, skal jeg lage middag.
- en: When she comes, I’m going to make dinner.
:::

The future meaning comes from the whole sentence and its context. For the choice in this lesson, the key fact is simple: the event is expected later, so use **når**, not **da**.

**Checkpoint request: select-nar-for-future-time**

**Purpose:** Establish **når** as the choice for future when before mixing future and repeated meanings.

**Context:** Plans connected to Aunt Liv’s next visit, along with ordinary plans involving travel, work, meals, and returning home.

**Learner action:** Complete or reformulate future statements by supplying **når** in the time clause, then state which later event sets the time for the main event.

**Success meaning:** A successful response shows that the learner recognizes future reference even when the verb inside the time clause has a present-tense form.

{{exercise: select_nar_for_future_time}}

## Compare the three meanings {#compare-the-three-meanings role=contrast objectives=obj-da-vs-nar-01}

The following sentences use similar vocabulary but describe different time relationships.

One specific past dinner

::: examples
- no: Da gjestene kom i går, serverte Liv suppa.
- en: When the guests arrived yesterday, Liv served the soup.
:::

There was one arrival yesterday. Use **da**.

A repeated dinner routine

::: examples
- no: Når gjestene kommer, serverer Liv ofte suppe først.
- en: When guests arrive, Liv often serves soup first.
:::

This describes Liv’s usual routine. Use **når**.

A future dinner

::: examples
- no: Når gjestene kommer i morgen, skal Liv servere suppe.
- en: When the guests arrive tomorrow, Liv is going to serve soup.
:::

This refers to tomorrow. Use **når**.

The compact decision is therefore:

**One specific completed past occasion → da**

**Future or repeated situation → når**

**Checkpoint request: interpret-time-meaning-before-choice**

**Purpose:** Make the learner decide what the sentence means before selecting a form.

**Context:** Three related versions of everyday events, such as receiving visitors, catching a bus, finishing work, or meeting a friend. Each group contains one specific past occasion, one routine, and one future plan.

**Learner action:** Read the time information in each sentence, determine whether it describes a single past event, repeated time, or future time, and then supply the correct form.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates meaning-based selection across all approved categories. The scored goal is the choice of **da** or **når**; naming the time category supports that decision.

{{exercise: interpret_time_meaning_before_choice}}

## A common mistake: choosing from tense alone {#common-mistake-choosing-from-tense-alone role=contrast objectives=obj-da-vs-nar-01}

A learner may see a past-tense verb and automatically choose **da**:

::: examples
- no: Da jeg var liten, gikk jeg til skolen hver dag.
- en: When I was little, I walked to school every day.
:::

This sentence can describe childhood as one overall period, so **da** is natural: during that specific period of my life.

But consider a sentence about repeated occasions inside childhood:

::: examples
- no: Når det snødde, tok vi alltid bussen til skolen.
- en: When it snowed, we always took the bus to school.
:::

Here the meaning is every time it snowed. The repeated situations require **når**, even though both snødde and tok are past-tense forms.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Da det snødde på mandag, tok vi bussen.
- en: When it snowed on Monday, we took the bus.
- no: Når det snødde, tok vi alltid bussen.
- en: When it snowed, we always took the bus.
:::

The first sentence identifies one snowy Monday. The second describes a recurring response to snow.

Do not ask only, Is this sentence in the past? Ask:

**Is this one particular past occasion, or something that happened repeatedly?**

**Checkpoint request: repair-tense-based-choices**

**Purpose:** Correct choices made from verb tense alone.

**Context:** Brief learner-like sentences about childhood, commuting, weather, and family routines. Some describe one dated memory; others describe repeated past conditions.

**Learner action:** Review each use of **da** or **når**, decide whether it matches the intended meaning, and revise only the time word when necessary. Give a short meaning-based reason.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the learner can detect and repair the specific misconception that past tense automatically requires **da**.

{{exercise: repair_tense_based_choices}}

## Returning to Mina and Aunt Liv {#returning-to-mina-and-aunt-liv role=model objectives=obj-da-vs-nar-01}

Later that day, Jonas asks Mina what she plans to cook for her aunt.

::: {.reading translation='What are you going to make when Aunt Liv comes?' dialogue_id='returning-to-aunt-liv' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hva skal du lage når tante Liv kommer?
:::

::: {.reading translation='I don’t know yet. When I ate at her place on Saturday, we had fish soup.' dialogue_id='returning-to-aunt-liv' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Jeg vet ikke ennå. Da jeg spiste hos henne på lørdag, fikk vi fiskesuppe.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Does she always make fish soup when you visit her?' dialogue_id='returning-to-aunt-liv' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Lager hun alltid fiskesuppe når du besøker henne?
:::

::: {.reading translation='No, she makes something different every time. When I decide, I’ll send her the menu.' dialogue_id='returning-to-aunt-liv' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Nei, hun lager noe forskjellig hver gang. Når jeg bestemmer meg, sender jeg henne menyen.
:::

Jonas’s first question concerns a future visit, so he uses **når**. Mina then refers back to one particular Saturday meal and uses **da**. Jonas asks about a possible routine and uses **når**. Finally, Mina refers to a future decision and again uses **når**.

The same people and topic can therefore require both words. What matters is the time relationship intended in each sentence.

**Checkpoint request: complete-the-visit-story**

**Purpose:** Use both forms coherently inside one connected context rather than in isolated sentences.

**Context:** A short continuation of Mina’s plans, preparations, and memories connected to Aunt Liv’s visit.

**Learner action:** Complete the connected account with **da** or **når**, using details such as last Saturday, every visit, and next month to interpret each time clause.

**Success meaning:** A successful response shows consistent choices across a short narrative containing specific past, repeated, and future references. Coherence supports the task, but accurate form choice remains the single scored goal.

{{exercise: complete_the_visit_story}}

## Building your own meaning contrast {#building-your-own-meaning-contrast role=contrast objectives=obj-da-vs-nar-01}

A small change in context can change the correct word.

::: examples
- no: Da Nora åpnet døra i går, sto det en pakke utenfor.
- en: When Nora opened the door yesterday, there was a package outside.
- no: Når Nora åpner døra om morgenen, slipper hun katten ut.
- en: When Nora opens the door in the morning, she lets the cat out.
- no: Når Nora åpner døra i morgen, vil hun se den nye navneplaten.
- en: When Nora opens the door tomorrow, she will see the new nameplate.
:::

The physical action is similar in all three examples. The time meaning is different:

- yesterday points to one past occasion;
- in the morning describes a routine;
- tomorrow points to the future.

You can create similar contrasts with common events:

::: examples
- no: Da jeg gikk av bussen, begynte det å regne.
- en: When I got off the bus, it started to rain.
- no: Når jeg går av bussen, kjøper jeg ofte kaffe.
- en: When I get off the bus, I often buy coffee.
- no: Når jeg går av bussen senere, skal jeg ringe deg.
- en: When I get off the bus later, I’ll call you.
:::

**Practice request: transform-the-time-context**

**Purpose:** Show that the learner can preserve a basic event while changing the time meaning and the required connector.

**Context:** Everyday actions such as arriving home, opening a shop, meeting a friend, or leaving work.

**Learner action:** Take a sentence about one specific past occasion and rewrite its context first as a repeated situation and then as a future situation, changing **da** or **når** as required.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates control of the form-meaning contrast when time context changes. Natural tense adjustments support the transformation, but the scored evidence goal is correct selection of **da** and **når**.

{{exercise: transform_the_time_context}}

## Cumulative transfer: a new everyday situation {#cumulative-transfer-new-everyday-situation role=recap objectives=obj-da-vs-nar-01}

Imagine that Sander is preparing for a driving lesson tomorrow. He remembers his first lesson, thinks about what normally happens during lessons, and makes plans for the next one.

He might need meanings such as these:

::: examples
- no: Da jeg kjørte for første gang, var jeg veldig nervøs.
- en: When I drove for the first time, I was very nervous.
- no: Når jeg blir nervøs, snakker kjørelæreren rolig.
- en: When I get nervous, the driving instructor speaks calmly.
- no: Når timen begynner i morgen, skal jeg huske å puste rolig.
- en: When the lesson starts tomorrow, I’ll remember to breathe calmly.
:::

The topic has changed, but the decision remains the same. One first lesson in the past takes **da**. A recurring reaction takes **når**. Tomorrow’s lesson also takes **når**.

**Cumulative request: narrate-memory-routine-and-plan**

**Purpose:** Retrieve the complete distinction and transfer it to a new situation.

**Context:** Sander’s driving lessons: one specific earlier lesson, something that normally happens during lessons, and tomorrow’s plan.

**Learner action:** Produce a short connected account by completing or writing one sentence for each supplied time context. Use **da** for the identified past occasion and **når** for the routine and future plan.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates independent, meaning-based choice of **da** and **når** across the full approved scope without copying Mina’s dialogue.

{{exercise: narrate_memory_routine_and_plan}}

## Short review {#short-review role=recap objectives=obj-da-vs-nar-01}

Complete a final three-part retrieval task about a new everyday event: one thing that happened on a specific past day, one thing that happens regularly, and one thing that will happen later. Choose **da** or **når** in each time clause and briefly state whether the meaning is specific past, repeated, or future.

Remember:

**One specific past occasion: da. Future or repeated time: når.**
