## At the community room {#community-room role=orient objectives=obj-imperative-01}

Mina and Jonas are preparing a community room for a small birthday party. Jonas has brought the food, while Mina is arranging the tables. Their friend Sara arrives to help.

::: {.reading translation="Hi, Sara! Come in!" dialogue_id="party-opening" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Hei, Sara! Kom inn!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thanks. What should I do?" dialogue_id="party-opening" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Takk. Hva skal jeg gjøre?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Take this box and put it on the table." dialogue_id="party-opening" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ta denne esken, og sett den på bordet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Wait a moment! Put the glasses here first." dialogue_id="party-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Vent litt! Sett glassene her først.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Here by the window?" dialogue_id="party-opening" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Her ved vinduet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Open the box and find the blue napkins." dialogue_id="party-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. Åpne esken og finn de blå serviettene.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Jonas, come here and look. Is this table in the right position?" dialogue_id="party-opening" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Jonas, kom hit og se. Står dette bordet riktig?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No. Move it a little to the left." dialogue_id="party-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nei. Flytt det litt til venstre.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Like this?" dialogue_id="party-opening" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Sånn?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, good. Sara, put the napkins beside the glasses." dialogue_id="party-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, fint. Sara, legg serviettene ved siden av glassene.
:::

::: {.reading translation="All right. What now?" dialogue_id="party-opening" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Greit. Hva nå?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Take a break!" dialogue_id="party-opening" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ta en pause!
:::

The three speakers are cooperating, so short instructions are useful. Mina and Jonas do not repeatedly say “You should take the box” or “Can you move the table?” They use direct verb forms: **kom**, **ta**, **sett**, **vent**, **åpne**, **finn**, **se**, **flytt**, and **legg**.

These forms tell another person what to do.

## The imperative {#imperative-meaning role=model objectives=obj-imperative-01}

The command or instruction form is called the **imperative (imperativ)**. Norwegian uses it for short instructions, directions, invitations, warnings, and requests.

Compare:

**Statement or question**

::: examples
- no: Du kommer inn.
- en: You come inside.
- no: Du tar denne esken.
- en: You take this box.
- no: Du setter glassene her.
- en: You put the glasses here.
:::

**Instruction**

::: examples
- no: Kom inn!
- en: Come in!
- no: Ta denne esken!
- en: Take this box!
- no: Sett glassene her!
- en: Put the glasses here!
:::

In an imperative sentence, Norwegian normally leaves out the subject pronoun **du** or **dere**. The listener is understood, and a name may be used to address someone directly. Mina says:

::: examples
- no: Ta denne esken!
- en: Take this box!
:::

She does not need to say **du**, because the situation already makes it clear that she is speaking to Sara.

The imperative therefore usually begins directly with the verb:

::: examples
- no: Vent litt!
- en: Wait a moment!
- no: Kom hit!
- en: Come here!
- no: Se på bordet!
- en: Look at the table!
:::

**Request: notice-imperatives-in-party-setup**

This activity should return to Mina, Jonas, and Sara in the community room. The learner should identify which lines give instructions and connect each instruction with the action expected in the scene. Its purpose is to establish meaning before requiring verb formation. A successful response would demonstrate that the learner recognizes an imperative as a direct instruction rather than as a statement or question.

{{exercise: notice_imperatives_in_party_setup}}

## Making the imperative from verbs ending in unstressed -e {#remove-unstressed-e role=model objectives=obj-imperative-01}

The basic dictionary form of a verb is the **infinitive (infinitiv)**. It is often shown with **å**:

- **å vente** — *to wait*
- **å komme** — *to come*
- **å flytte** — *to move*
- **å finne** — *to find*
- **å legge** — *to put, to lay*

For many common verbs whose infinitive ends in an unstressed **-e**, remove that final **-e** to make the imperative.

::: examples
- no: Vent litt!
- en: Wait a moment!
- no: Kom hit!
- en: Come here!
- no: Flytt bordet!
- en: Move the table!
- no: Finn serviettene!
- en: Find the napkins!
- no: Legg dem her!
- en: Put them here!
- no: Lukk døra!
- en: Close the door!
- no: Les meldingen!
- en: Read the message!
:::

- **å vente → vent**
- **å komme → kom**
- **å flytte → flytt**
- **å finne → finn**
- **å legge → legg**
- **å lukke → lukk**
- **å lese → les**

Some forms keep the spelling at the end of the stem, but **å komme → kom** is an important boundary: the doubled **mm** is reduced to **m** in this common imperative. Learn this form as **kom**, not *komm*.

- **å finne → finn**
- **å legge → legg**

Do not add a present-tense **-r**. Compare the present tense with the imperative:

**Present tense**

::: examples
- no: Sara venter.
- en: Sara is waiting.
- no: Jonas kommer.
- en: Jonas is coming.
- no: Mina flytter bordet.
- en: Mina is moving the table.
:::

**Imperative**

::: examples
- no: Vent, Sara!
- en: Wait, Sara!
- no: Kom, Jonas!
- en: Come, Jonas!
- no: Flytt bordet, Mina!
- en: Move the table, Mina!
:::

A common mistake is to use the present-tense form as an instruction:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Kommer hit!
- en: Come here!
:::

This is not the normal way to say *Come here!*

Use:

::: examples
- no: Kom hit!
- en: Come here!
:::

::: examples
- no: ✗ Venter litt!
- en: Wait a moment!
:::

This is not the normal way to say *Wait a moment!*

Use:

::: examples
- no: Vent litt!
- en: Wait a moment!
:::

The compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
With many common infinitives ending in unstressed **-e**, remove **-e**: **å vente → vent**, **å komme → kom**.
:::

**Request: form-regular-imperatives-for-room-setup**

This activity should use familiar preparations in the community room: moving furniture, finding objects, closing a door, and placing items on a table. The learner should change common **-e** infinitives into imperative forms and use each form in a short instruction. The primary scored goal is accurate imperative formation by removing the final unstressed **-e**. A successful response would retain the correct form, including doubled consonants where the taught form keeps them, while remembering the boundary **å komme → kom**.

{{exercise: form_regular_imperatives_for_room_setup}}

**Request: distinguish-present-and-imperative-forms**

This checkpoint should present short statements and direct instructions from the same party-preparation context. The learner should determine whether each highlighted verb describes an action or tells someone to perform it. The primary scored goal is distinguishing present-tense forms in **-r** from imperative forms. A successful response would show that the learner does not use forms such as **kommer** or **venter** as simple commands.

## Verbs that keep their infinitive form {#unchanged-imperatives role=contrast objectives=obj-imperative-01}

Some common infinitives do not have an extra unstressed **-e** to remove. Their imperative looks the same as the infinitive without **å**.

::: examples
- no: Gå inn!
- en: Go inside!
- no: Ta denne stolen!
- en: Take this chair!
- no: Se her!
- en: Look here!
- no: Stå her!
- en: Stand here!
- no: Bli her!
- en: Stay here!
:::

- **å gå → gå**
- **å ta → ta**
- **å se → se**
- **å stå → stå**
- **å bli → bli**

Compare the two main patterns:

- **å vente**: Remove final unstressed **-e**. Imperative: **vent**.
- **å komme**: Remove final unstressed **-e**. Imperative: **kom**.
- **å gå**: Nothing is removed. Imperative: **gå**.
- **å ta**: Nothing is removed. Imperative: **ta**.
- **å se**: Nothing is removed. Imperative: **se**.

The one-syllable verb **å se** ends in the letter **e**, but **e** is part of the whole verb form. Its imperative is therefore **se**, not **s**.

::: examples
- no: Se på bildet!
- en: Look at the picture!
- no: Se her!
- en: Look here!
:::

**Request: choose-unchanged-common-imperatives**

This activity should move briefly from party preparation to simple everyday directions, such as entering a room, taking an object, looking at a sign, or staying in one place. The learner should form instructions from **gå**, **ta**, **se**, **stå**, and **bli**. The primary scored goal is preserving the complete verb when no final unstressed **-e** should be removed. A successful response would produce forms such as **gå**, **ta**, and **se** without adding **-r** or cutting the verb too short.

{{exercise: choose_unchanged_common_imperatives}}

## More examples of removing final unstressed -e {#frequent-forms-boundaries role=contrast objectives=obj-imperative-01}

Several very frequent verbs follow the taught pattern: remove the final unstressed **-e** to form the imperative.

::: examples
- no: Vær forsiktig!
- en: Be careful!
- no: Gjør dette først!
- en: Do this first!
- no: Sett koppen her!
- en: Put the cup here!
- no: Sitt her!
- en: Sit here!
:::

- **å være → vær**
- **å gjøre → gjør**
- **å sette → sett**
- **å sitte → sitt**

Do not confuse **sett** with **sitt**. The two imperatives have different meanings:

::: examples
- no: Sett koppen på bordet!
- en: Put the cup on the table!
- no: Sitt på stolen!
- en: Sit on the chair!
:::

**Sett** means “put” and comes from **å sette**. **Sitt** means “sit” and comes from **å sitte**. The present-tense forms **sitter** and **setter** are different forms and are not the imperative forms taught in this contrast.

One more highly useful form is:

::: examples
- no: Åpne døra!
- en: Open the door!
:::

Although many **-e** verbs lose their final **-e**, **åpne** keeps it in the usual imperative. Learn **åpne** as the everyday instruction form.

The main lesson is not that every Norwegian verb follows one perfectly mechanical spelling rule. The useful strategy is:

1. Remove the final unstressed **-e** from many common verbs.
2. Keep short forms such as **gå**, **ta**, and **se** unchanged.
3. Learn **åpne** as an important boundary: unlike many verbs ending in unstressed **-e**, it keeps the final **-e**.

**Request: retrieve-frequent-imperative-forms**

This activity should ask the learner to complete short, meaningful instructions using the frequent verbs **være**, **gjøre**, **sette**, **sitte**, and **åpne**. The context can include setting up the room and welcoming arriving guests. The primary scored goal is forming **vær**, **gjør**, **sett**, and **sitt** by removing final unstressed **-e**, while recalling that **åpne** keeps its final **-e**. A successful response would apply the taught pattern accurately and preserve the learned boundary form **åpne**.

{{exercise: retrieve_frequent_imperative_forms}}

## Building a useful instruction {#useful-instructions role=model objectives=obj-imperative-01}

An imperative can stand alone:

::: examples
- no: Vent!
- en: Wait!
- no: Kom!
- en: Come!
:::

It often has extra information telling the listener what, where, or when:

::: examples
- no: Ta esken!
- en: Take the box!
- no: Kom inn!
- en: Come in!
- no: Sett glassene her!
- en: Put the glasses here!
- no: Gjør dette først!
- en: Do this first!
- no: Vent litt!
- en: Wait a moment!
:::

The imperative verb usually comes first. A name may come first when the speaker addresses someone directly. The extra words complete the practical meaning.

Compare these instructions from the opening scene:

- **Kom inn!** invites Sara into the room.
- **Ta denne esken!** tells her which object to take.
- **Sett den på bordet!** tells her where to put it.
- **Vent litt!** tells her to pause briefly.
- **Finn de blå serviettene!** tells her what to look for.
- **Flytt bordet til venstre!** gives a direction.

Two instructions can be joined with **og**:

::: examples
- no: Åpne esken og finn serviettene!
- en: Open the box and find the napkins!
- no: Ta glassene og sett dem på bordet!
- en: Take the glasses and put them on the table!
:::

Each verb is an imperative because each one names an action for the listener to perform.

**Request: understand-complete-everyday-instructions**

This activity should give short instructions containing familiar objects and locations from the community room. The learner should show which action and destination or object each instruction specifies. The primary scored goal is comprehension of complete imperative sentences. A successful response would distinguish, for example, taking an object from putting it somewhere, even when both instructions mention the same object.

{{exercise: understand_complete_everyday_instructions}}

**Request: build-two-step-instructions**

This activity should ask the learner to produce short two-step instructions joined with **og**, using the party setup as context. The primary scored goal is forming both verbs as imperatives. Word choice about objects and locations provides context but is not the scored grammar dimension. A successful response would use an appropriate imperative verb for each action and communicate a sensible sequence. The imperative usually comes first, while a name may come first when addressing someone.

{{exercise: build_two_step_instructions}}

## Direct does not always mean unfriendly {#imperative-tone role=model objectives=obj-imperative-01}

Norwegian imperatives are common in cooperative situations, especially when the context makes the instruction helpful and expected. Mina can say:

::: examples
- no: Kom inn!
- en: Come in!
:::

This sounds like a welcome, not an angry command.

Jonas can say:

::: examples
- no: Vent litt!
- en: Wait a moment!
:::

The tone depends on the situation and the speaker’s voice. Short words can also make an instruction warmer:

::: examples
- no: Kom inn, takk!
- en: Come in, please!
- no: Sett esken her, takk!
- en: Put the box here, please!
:::

The imperative itself does not automatically tell you whether the speaker is friendly, impatient, or worried. Context does that work.

## Common mistakes {#common-imperative-errors role=contrast objectives=obj-imperative-01}

**Adding du as if making a statement**

A simple Norwegian instruction normally does not need **du**.

::: examples
- no: Ta en stol!
- en: Take a chair!
:::

Do not build the instruction as though it were the statement **Du tar en stol**.

**Using present tense**

Present tense often ends in **-r**, but the imperative does not.

::: examples
- no: Du venter her.
- en: You are waiting here.
- no: Vent her!
- en: Wait here!
:::

**Removing too much**

Keep the complete verb when the imperative is unchanged.

::: examples
- no: Se her!
- en: Look here!
:::

Not a shortened form made by removing the **e** from **se**.

**Applying one rule to every verb**

The remove-**e** pattern correctly produces forms such as **vent** and **vær**, while unchanged verbs and the boundary form **åpne** must remain complete:

- **å vente → vent**
- **å ta → ta**
- **å være → vær**
- **å åpne → åpne**

**Request: repair-common-imperative-errors**

This activity should present short instructions containing one formation error at a time: an unnecessary present-tense **-r**, an over-shortened verb, or an incorrectly formed frequent imperative. The learner should rewrite each instruction correctly. The primary scored goal is correction of imperative verb form. A successful response would preserve the intended meaning while changing only what is needed to produce a natural command.

{{exercise: repair_common_imperative_errors}}
