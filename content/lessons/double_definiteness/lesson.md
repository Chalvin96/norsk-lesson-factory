## A choice at the second-hand shop {#a-choice-at-the-second-hand-shop role=orient objectives=obj-double-definiteness-01}

Jonas has recently moved into a new flat. His friend Nora is helping him choose a few things from a second-hand shop. Several similar objects are on display, so they need adjectives to identify the ones they mean.

::: {.reading translation="What do you think about the green chair?" dialogue_id="choice-at-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Hva synes du om den grønne stolen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I like it, but the old chair is cheaper." dialogue_id="choice-at-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg liker den, men den gamle stolen er billigere.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s true. Do you also need a table?" dialogue_id="choice-at-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det er sant. Trenger du også et bord?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, the round table fits well in the kitchen." dialogue_id="choice-at-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, det runde bordet passer godt på kjøkkenet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And what about the blue lamps?" dialogue_id="choice-at-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Og hva med de blå lampene?
:::

::: {.reading translation="They’re nice, but I’d rather have the small lamps by the door." dialogue_id="choice-at-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
De er fine, men jeg vil heller ha de små lampene ved døra.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then we’ll take the old chair, the round table, and the small lamps." dialogue_id="choice-at-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Da tar vi den gamle stolen, det runde bordet og de små lampene.
:::

Nora and Jonas are talking about specific objects that both of them can see. They do not mean any green chair, any round table, or any small lamps. They mean particular ones: the green chair, the round table, and the small lamps.

Norwegian marks this meaning in three places:

::: examples
- no: den grønne stolen
- en: the green chair
:::

The phrase contains:

- den before the adjective
- grønne, the adjective with an -e ending
- stolen, the noun in its definite form

This pattern is called **double definiteness**. The noun has its normal definite ending, but the phrase also has den, det, or de before the adjective.

## Choosing den, det, or de {#choosing-den-det-or-de role=model objectives=obj-double-definiteness-01}

The word at the beginning agrees with the noun:

- Use **den** with a singular masculine or feminine noun.
- Use **det** with a singular neuter noun.
- Use **de** with a plural noun.

The noun itself keeps its definite ending.

**Singular masculine and feminine nouns: den**

::: examples
- no: en stol → stolen
- en: a chair → the chair
- no: den gamle stolen
- en: the old chair
- no: en jakke → jakken
- en: a jacket → the jacket
- no: den varme jakken
- en: the warm jacket
:::

A feminine noun can also use den:

::: examples
- no: ei dør → døra
- en: a door → the door
- no: den åpne døra
- en: the open door
:::

The essential pattern is:

**den + adjective in -e + definite singular noun**

**Singular neuter nouns: det**

::: examples
- no: et bord → bordet
- en: a table → the table
- no: det runde bordet
- en: the round table
- no: et hus → huset
- en: a house → the house
- no: det store huset
- en: the large house
:::

The pattern is:

**det + adjective in -e + definite singular neuter noun**

**Plural nouns: de**

In the plural, use de regardless of the noun’s gender.

::: examples
- no: lamper → lampene
- en: lamps → the lamps
- no: de små lampene
- en: the small lamps
- no: sko → skoene
- en: shoes → the shoes
- no: de svarte skoene
- en: the black shoes
:::

The pattern is:

**de + adjective in -e + definite plural noun**

Here is the complete contrast:

::: examples
- no: den gamle stolen
- en: the old chair
- no: det gamle bordet
- en: the old table
- no: de gamle stolene
- en: the old chairs
:::

The adjective is gamle in all three definite phrases. The beginning changes according to gender or number, and the noun has its definite form.

## Why the noun is still definite {#why-the-noun-is-still-definite role=model objectives=obj-double-definiteness-01}

Without an adjective, Norwegian usually places definiteness at the end of the noun:

::: examples
- no: stolen
- en: the chair
- no: bordet
- en: the table
- no: lampene
- en: the lamps
:::

When an adjective comes before a specific definite noun, Norwegian does not remove this ending. Instead, it adds den, det, or de before the adjective.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: stolen
- en: the chair
- no: den grønne stolen
- en: the green chair
- no: bordet
- en: the table
- no: det runde bordet
- en: the round table
- no: lampene
- en: the lamps
- no: de små lampene
- en: the small lamps
:::

English normally has only one visible marker, the. Norwegian uses the opening word and the definite noun form together. This is why the structure is called double definiteness.

A common mistake is to copy the English structure too closely:

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ den grønne stol
- en: ✗ the green chair
:::

The noun stol is indefinite. The target phrase needs the definite noun stolen:

::: examples
- no: den grønne stolen
- en: the green chair
:::

The opposite mistake is to use only the definite noun and omit den:

::: examples
- no: ✗ grønne stolen
- en: ✗ the green chair
:::

In the standard pattern taught here, include den:

::: examples
- no: den grønne stolen
- en: the green chair
:::

## The adjective takes -e {#the-adjective-takes-e role=model objectives=obj-double-definiteness-01}

In the target structure, the adjective normally takes the **-e form**:

::: examples
- no: grønn → den grønne stolen
- en: green → the green chair
- no: rund → det runde bordet
- en: round → the round table
- no: svart → de svarte skoene
- en: black → the black shoes
:::

This differs from an indefinite phrase:

::: examples
- no: en grønn stol
- en: a green chair
- no: den grønne stolen
- en: the green chair
- no: et rundt bord
- en: a round table
- no: det runde bordet
- en: the round table
- no: svarte sko
- en: black shoes
- no: de svarte skoene
- en: the black shoes
:::

The compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
To describe a specific definite noun, use den, det, or de + adjective in -e + noun in the definite form.
:::

Do not let the gender of a singular noun remove the adjective’s -e in this pattern.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ det stort huset
- en: ✗ the large house
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: det store huset
- en: the large house
:::

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ den grønn jakken
- en: ✗ the green jacket
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: den grønne jakken
- en: the green jacket
:::

## Building the whole phrase {#building-the-whole-phrase role=model objectives=obj-double-definiteness-01}

The three parts work as a unit:

- Opening word: den; Adjective: nye; Definite noun: sofaen; Complete meaning: the new sofa
- Opening word: det; Adjective: gamle; Definite noun: skapet; Complete meaning: the old cupboard
- Opening word: de; Adjective: røde; Definite noun: gardinene; Complete meaning: the red curtains

More examples:

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker den nye sofaen.
- en: I like the new sofa.
- no: Det gamle skapet står på soverommet.
- en: The old cupboard is in the bedroom.
- no: De røde gardinene passer ikke her.
- en: The red curtains do not suit this place.
- no: Kan du flytte det lille bordet?
- en: Can you move the small table?
- no: Vi trenger de lange hyllene.
- en: We need the long shelves.
:::

Each phrase refers to a particular object or group. The speaker expects the listener to know which sofa, cupboard, curtains, table, or shelves are meant.

:::

::: {.reading translation="Are you taking the small lamps too?" dialogue_id="return-to-shop" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Skal dere ha de små lampene også?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but not the blue lamps in the window. We mean the small lamps by the door." dialogue_id="return-to-shop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}

::: {.reading translation="All right. I’ll put the selected things by the checkout." dialogue_id="return-to-shop" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Greit. Jeg setter de valgte tingene ved kassa.
:::

The adjectives help distinguish between similar objects. Because everyone is discussing identifiable objects in the shop, the phrases use the definite pattern.

Notice how Jonas distinguishes two groups:

::: examples
- no: de blå lampene i vinduet
- en: the blue lamps in the window
- no: de små lampene ved døra
- en: the small lamps by the door
:::

Both phrases use de because lampene is plural. Both adjectives are in the definite form, and both nouns are definite.

## Choosing between indefinite and definite meaning {#choosing-between-indefinite-and-definite-meaning role=contrast objectives=obj-double-definiteness-01}

The target construction is used when the noun is definite and has an adjective. Compare the meanings:

::: examples
- no: Jonas kjøper en gammel stol.
- en: Jonas is buying an old chair.
- no: Jonas kjøper den gamle stolen.
- en: Jonas is buying the old chair.
- no: Nora ser et rundt bord.
- en: Nora sees a round table.
- no: Nora ser på det runde bordet.
- en: Nora is looking at the round table.
- no: De trenger små lamper.
- en: They need small lamps.
- no: De kjøper de små lampene.
- en: They are buying the small lamps.
:::

In the first sentence, the chair is introduced as one old chair. In the second, the speaker means a particular old chair.

The definite meaning controls the complete structure. It is not enough to add den, det, or de to an otherwise indefinite phrase.

## Common mistakes to check {#common-mistakes-to-check role=contrast objectives=obj-double-definiteness-01}

**Missing den, det, or de**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ store huset
- en: ✗ the large house
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: det store huset
- en: the large house
:::

**Indefinite noun after the adjective**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ de røde gardiner
- en: ✗ the red curtains
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: de røde gardinene
- en: the red curtains
:::

**Adjective without -e**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ den ny sofaen
- en: ✗ the new sofa
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: den nye sofaen
- en: the new sofa
:::

**Wrong opening word**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ den store huset
- en: ✗ the large house
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: det store huset
- en: the large house
:::

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ det svarte skoene
- en: ✗ the black shoes
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: de svarte skoene
- en: the black shoes
:::

A useful final check is to inspect the phrase from left to right:

1. Is the noun singular masculine or feminine, singular neuter, or plural?
2. Have you selected den, det, or de?
3. Does the adjective have -e?
4. Is the noun definite?
