## A change of plan {#a-change-of-plan role=orient objectives=obj-sentence-adverbs-01}

Maja and Henrik are arranging a Saturday walk with their friend Sara. Rain is expected, and Sara has not answered their latest message yet.

::: {.reading translation='Sara isn’t coming at ten. She has to work a little first.' dialogue_id='change-of-plan' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Sara kommer ikke klokka ti. Hun må jobbe litt først.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Do you know whether she is coming later?' dialogue_id='change-of-plan' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Vet du om hun kommer senere?
:::

::: {.reading translation='She wrote that she might come at twelve.' dialogue_id='change-of-plan' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Hun skrev at hun kanskje kommer klokka tolv.
:::

::: {.reading translation='I don’t usually go for a walk when it rains.' dialogue_id='change-of-plan' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Jeg går vanligvis ikke på tur når det regner.
:::

::: {.reading translation='But perhaps it won’t rain all day.' dialogue_id='change-of-plan' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Men det regner kanskje ikke hele dagen.
:::

::: {.reading translation='If it isn’t raining at twelve, we can meet Sara at the station.' dialogue_id='change-of-plan' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Hvis det ikke regner klokka tolv, kan vi møte Sara ved stasjonen.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes. I’ll send her a message, but she never answers immediately.' dialogue_id='change-of-plan' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Ja. Jeg sender henne en melding, men hun svarer aldri med en gang.
:::

::: {.reading translation='When she finally answers, perhaps we’ll know more.' dialogue_id='change-of-plan' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Når hun endelig svarer, vet vi kanskje mer.
:::

The practical problem is simple: Maja and Henrik need to say what is not happening, what perhaps will happen, and what usually or never happens. To do that naturally, they must place words such as **ikke**, **kanskje**, **aldri**, and **vanligvis** in the correct position.

These words are often called **sentence adverbs (setningsadverb)** because they affect the meaning of the whole statement or show the speaker’s attitude toward it. In this chapter, the important question is not which adverb to choose, but where to put it.

## The central contrast {#the-central-contrast role=model objectives=obj-sentence-adverbs-01}

In the neutral declarative patterns taught here, word order changes according to the type of clause.

A **main clause (hovedsetning)** can normally stand alone:

::: examples
- no: Sara kommer ikke klokka ti.
- en: Sara isn’t coming at ten.
:::

A **subordinate clause (leddsetning)** is introduced by a word such as **at**, **fordi**, **hvis**, **når**, or **om** and forms part of a larger sentence:

::: examples
- no: Maja sier at Sara ikke kommer klokka ti.
- en: Maja says that Sara isn’t coming at ten.
:::

Compare the position of **ikke**:

- Main clause: subject **Sara** + finite verb **kommer** + sentence adverb **ikke**
- Subordinate clause: subject **Sara** + sentence adverb **ikke** + finite verb **kommer**

For the neutral declarative patterns taught here, the compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
In a subject-first main clause, put the sentence adverb **after the finite verb**. In a subordinate clause of the modeled type, put the sentence adverb **before the finite verb**.
:::

The **finite verb** is the verb carrying tense. In **kommer**, the finite verb is **kommer**. In **kan komme**, the finite verb is **kan**, while **komme** is the infinitive.

## Sentence adverbs in ordinary main clauses {#sentence-adverbs-in-ordinary-main-clauses role=model objectives=obj-sentence-adverbs-01}

In a main clause beginning with the subject, the usual order is:

**subject + finite verb + sentence adverb + rest**

::: examples
- no: Sara kommer ikke klokka ti.
- en: Sara isn’t coming at ten.
- no: Hun svarer aldri med en gang.
- en: She never answers immediately.
- no: Vi går vanligvis langs elva.
- en: We usually walk along the river.
- no: Det regner kanskje i ettermiddag.
- en: Perhaps it will rain this afternoon.
:::

When there is more than one verb, the sentence adverb still follows the finite verb and comes before the remaining verb form:

::: examples
- no: Sara kan ikke komme klokka ti.
- en: Sara cannot come at ten.
- no: Henrik vil kanskje vente på henne.
- en: Henrik might wait for her.
- no: Vi har aldri gått denne veien før.
- en: We have never walked this way before.
:::

The useful pattern is therefore:

**subject + finite verb + sentence adverb + other verb + rest**

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Sara kommer ikke.
- en: Sara isn’t coming.
- no: Sara kan ikke komme.
- en: Sara cannot come.
:::

In the second sentence, **kan** is finite. That is why **ikke** comes after **kan**, not after **komme**.

**A common mistake**

Learners sometimes copy English order and put **ikke** before the finite verb in an ordinary main clause.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Sara ikke kommer klokka ti.
- en: Sara isn’t coming at ten.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Sara kommer ikke klokka ti.
- en: Sara isn’t coming at ten.
:::

The incorrect order is not repaired by thinking only about the subject. First identify the finite verb; then place the sentence adverb after it.

## Sentence adverbs in subordinate clauses {#sentence-adverbs-in-subordinate-clauses role=contrast objectives=obj-sentence-adverbs-01}

Now return to Maja’s report:

::: examples
- no: Hun skrev at hun kanskje kommer klokka tolv.
- en: She wrote that she might come at twelve.
:::

The subordinate clause begins with **at**:

::: examples
- no: at hun kanskje kommer klokka tolv
- en: that she might come at twelve
:::

Its normal order is:

**subordinator + subject + sentence adverb + finite verb + rest**

::: examples
- no: Maja vet at Sara ikke kommer klokka ti.
- en: Maja knows that Sara isn’t coming at ten.
- no: Henrik tror at det kanskje regner senere.
- en: Henrik thinks that perhaps it will rain later.
- no: De blir hjemme fordi de vanligvis ikke går ut i kraftig regn.
- en: They are staying home because they do not usually go out in heavy rain.
- no: Maja spør om Sara aldri tar bussen.
- en: Maja asks whether Sara never takes the bus.
:::

Again, in the neutral subordinate-clause pattern taught here, place the sentence adverb before the finite verb when the clause contains two verbs:

::: examples
- no: Maja vet at Sara ikke kan komme klokka ti.
- en: Maja knows that Sara cannot come at ten.
- no: Henrik tror at de kanskje må vente.
- en: Henrik thinks that they may have to wait.
- no: Sara sier at hun aldri har gått den veien.
- en: Sara says that she has never walked that way.
:::

Compare the same idea in a main clause and a subordinate clause:

::: examples
- no: Sara kan ikke komme tidlig.
- en: Sara cannot come early.
- no: Maja vet at Sara ikke kan komme tidlig.
- en: Maja knows that Sara cannot come early.
:::

In the main clause, **ikke** follows **kan**. In the subordinate clause, it precedes **kan**.

**Why the clause boundary matters**

A long sentence can contain both clause types. Do not choose one adverb position for the entire sentence. Each clause has its own structure.

::: examples
- no: Henrik blir hjemme fordi han ikke liker regnet.
- en: Henrik is staying home because he does not like the rain.
:::

The main clause is:

::: examples
- no: Henrik blir hjemme.
- en: Henrik is staying home.
:::

The subordinate clause is:

::: examples
- no: fordi han ikke liker regnet
- en: because he does not like the rain
:::

The position of **ikke** is controlled by the subordinate clause in which it appears.

## More than one sentence adverb {#more-than-one-sentence-adverb role=model objectives=obj-sentence-adverbs-01}

Sometimes a clause contains two relevant adverbs, as in Henrik’s statement:

::: examples
- no: Jeg går vanligvis ikke på tur når det regner.
- en: I don’t usually go for a walk when it rains.
:::

Here **vanligvis** describes what is usual, while **ikke** makes the activity negative. In this meaning, the natural order is:

::: examples
- no: Jeg går vanligvis ikke på tur i regnet.
- en: I don’t usually go for a walk in the rain.
:::

In a subordinate clause, both come before the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: Henrik sier at han vanligvis ikke går på tur i regnet.
- en: Henrik says that he does not usually go for a walk in the rain.
:::

Compare the clause frames:

::: examples
- no: Han går vanligvis ikke alene.
- en: He does not usually walk alone.
- no: fordi han vanligvis ikke går alene
- en: because he does not usually walk alone
:::

The lesson contrast remains the same within these neutral patterns: sentence adverbs occur after the finite verb in the modeled main clauses and before it in the modeled subordinate clauses.

Do not infer that sentence adverbs can be placed in any order without changing the message. For now, learn common combinations as meaningful units, such as **vanligvis ikke**.

## Compound sentences: check each clause separately {#compound-sentences-check-each-clause-separately role=contrast objectives=obj-sentence-adverbs-01}

A **compound sentence** joins coordinated main clauses, often with **og**, **men**, or **for**. In the neutral subject-first examples here, each part follows the modeled main-clause word order.

Return to Maja:

::: examples
- no: Jeg sender henne en melding, men hun svarer aldri med en gang.
- en: I’ll send her a message, but she never answers immediately.
:::

The clause after **men** is a new main clause:

::: examples
- no: hun svarer aldri med en gang
- en: she never answers immediately
:::

It therefore has finite verb + sentence adverb:

**svarer + aldri**

More examples:

::: examples
- no: Sara kommer ikke klokka ti, men hun kommer kanskje klokka tolv.
- en: Sara isn’t coming at ten, but she may come at twelve.
- no: Vi tar ikke bussen, og Henrik kjører heller ikke.
- en: We aren’t taking the bus, and Henrik isn’t driving either.
- no: Maja har aldri gått denne ruten, men Henrik kjenner den godt.
- en: Maja has never walked this route, but Henrik knows it well.
:::

A conjunction does not automatically create a subordinate clause. After **men** and **og**, a complete second main clause keeps main-clause order.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Sara kommer ikke fordi hun må jobbe.
- en: Sara isn’t coming because she has to work.
:::

Here **fordi hun må jobbe** is subordinate.

Now add a sentence adverb there:

::: examples
- no: Sara kommer ikke fordi hun ikke har tid.
- en: Sara isn’t coming because she does not have time.
:::

The first **ikke** follows the main-clause verb **kommer**. The second **ikke** precedes the subordinate-clause verb **har**.

**Common misconception: every linking word works alike**

It is tempting to see **men** and **fordi** as the same kind of connector. They are not.

::: examples
- no: Hun kommer ikke, men hun ringer kanskje.
- en: She isn’t coming, but she may call.
- no: Hun kommer ikke fordi hun kanskje må jobbe.
- en: She isn’t coming because she may have to work.
:::

After **men**, the second clause is a main clause, so **kanskje** follows **ringer**. After **fordi**, the subordinate clause places **kanskje** before **må**.

## Fronted elements in main clauses {#fronted-elements-in-main-clauses role=model objectives=obj-sentence-adverbs-01}

A main clause does not always begin with the subject. A time, place, or other element can be moved to the front:

::: examples
- no: I dag kommer Sara ikke klokka ti.
- en: Today Sara isn’t coming at ten.
:::

When something other than the subject occupies the first position, the finite verb comes next. With an unstressed pronoun subject, the neutral pattern taught here places the subject before the sentence adverb. With a full noun subject, sentence-adverb placement relative to the subject can vary with information structure; the examples below model one common order without excluding supported alternatives:

**fronted element + finite verb + unstressed pronoun subject + sentence adverb + rest**

::: examples
- no: I dag kommer Sara ikke klokka ti.
- en: Today Sara isn’t coming at ten.
- no: På lørdager går Henrik vanligvis langs elva.
- en: On Saturdays Henrik usually walks along the river.
- no: Etter jobb kan Sara kanskje møte dem.
- en: After work, Sara might be able to meet them.
- no: Denne veien har Maja aldri gått før.
- en: Maja has never walked this way before.
:::

Compare the ordinary and fronted versions:

::: examples
- no: Sara kommer ikke klokka ti i dag.
- en: Sara isn’t coming at ten today.
- no: I dag kommer Sara ikke klokka ti.
- en: Today Sara isn’t coming at ten.
:::

The sentence adverb remains connected to the finite-verb area, but fronting changes the positions of the finite verb and subject.

A frequent mistake is to keep subject-first order after a fronted element:

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ I dag Sara kommer ikke klokka ti.
- en: Today Sara isn’t coming at ten.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: I dag kommer Sara ikke klokka ti.
- en: Today Sara isn’t coming at ten.
:::

With the full noun subject **Sara**, both of these orders can be accepted, depending on information structure and context:

::: examples
- no: I dag kommer ikke Sara klokka ti.
- en: Today Sara isn’t coming at ten.
- no: I dag kommer Sara ikke klokka ti.
- en: Today Sara isn’t coming at ten.
:::

This lesson models the second order but does not treat the first as necessarily contrastive or incorrect. For deterministic placement practice, use an unstressed pronoun subject, as in **I dag kommer hun ikke klokka ti**.

## Fronted subordinate clauses {#fronted-subordinate-clauses role=contrast objectives=obj-sentence-adverbs-01}

A subordinate clause can itself come first:

::: examples
- no: Hvis det ikke regner klokka tolv, kan vi møte Sara ved stasjonen.
- en: If it isn’t raining at twelve, we can meet Sara at the station.
:::

This sentence contains two word-order decisions.

Inside the fronted subordinate clause:

::: examples
- no: hvis det ikke regner klokka tolv
- en: if it isn’t raining at twelve
:::

The order remains:

**subordinator + subject + sentence adverb + finite verb**

Then comes the main clause:

::: examples
- no: kan vi møte Sara ved stasjonen
- en: we can meet Sara at the station
:::

Because the subordinate clause occupies the first position of the whole sentence, the following main clause begins with its finite verb:

**fronted subordinate clause + finite verb + subject + rest**

If the following main clause contains an unstressed pronoun subject and a sentence adverb, the neutral pattern taught here places the adverb after that subject:

::: examples
- no: Hvis det ikke regner, kan vi kanskje gå langs elva.
- en: If it isn’t raining, perhaps we can walk along the river.
:::

Notice both placements:

- **ikke** comes before **regner** inside the subordinate clause.
- **kanskje** comes after **kan vi** in the following main clause.

More examples:

::: examples
- no: Når Sara endelig svarer, vet vi kanskje mer.
- en: When Sara finally answers, perhaps we’ll know more.
- no: Fordi Henrik ikke liker kraftig regn, går han vanligvis ikke langt.
- en: Because Henrik does not like heavy rain, he does not usually walk far.
- no: Hvis Sara ikke kan komme, blir Maja kanskje hjemme.
- en: If Sara cannot come, Maja may stay home.
- no: Når de ikke tar bussen, går de vanligvis fra stasjonen.
- en: When they do not take the bus, they usually walk from the station.
:::

Do not move the subordinate-clause adverb merely because the whole subordinate clause has been moved. Its internal order stays subordinate:

::: examples
- no: fordi Henrik ikke liker regn
- en: because Henrik does not like rain
- no: Fordi Henrik ikke liker regn, blir han hjemme.
- en: Because Henrik does not like rain, he stays home.
:::

Fronting changes the relationship between the opening clause and the following main clause. It does not turn the opening subordinate clause into a main clause.

## Returning to Maja, Henrik, and Sara {#returning-to-maja-henrik-and-sara role=model objectives=obj-sentence-adverbs-01}

Sara finally answers.

::: {.reading translation='Unfortunately, I can’t come at twelve.' dialogue_id='new-plan' speaker_id='sara' speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Jeg kan dessverre ikke komme klokka tolv.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Might you come later?' dialogue_id='new-plan' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Kommer du kanskje senere?
:::

::: {.reading translation='I don’t know whether I can leave work early.' dialogue_id='new-plan' speaker_id='sara' speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Jeg vet ikke om jeg kan dra tidlig fra jobb.
:::

::: {.reading translation='If you can’t come today, perhaps we can go for a walk tomorrow.' dialogue_id='new-plan' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Hvis du ikke kan komme i dag, kan vi kanskje gå på tur i morgen.
:::

::: {.reading translation='That works well. On Sundays I don’t usually work.' dialogue_id='new-plan' speaker_id='sara' speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Det passer bra. På søndager jobber jeg vanligvis ikke.
:::

The same placement rules allow the group to negotiate a new plan:

- In **Jeg kan dessverre ikke komme**, the sentence adverbs follow the finite verb **kan** in a main clause.
- In **om jeg kan dra**, there is no sentence adverb inside the subordinate clause. If Sara said **om jeg ikke kan dra**, **ikke** would precede **kan**.
- In **Hvis du ikke kan komme**, **ikke** precedes the finite verb in the subordinate clause.
- In **kan vi kanskje gå**, **kanskje** follows the finite verb and subject in the main clause after the fronted subordinate clause.
- In **På søndager jobber jeg vanligvis ikke**, the main clause begins with a fronted time expression, followed by the finite verb, subject, and sentence adverbs.

## A practical method {#a-practical-method role=recap objectives=obj-sentence-adverbs-01}

For the neutral declarative patterns practiced in this lesson, work through these decisions:

1. Find the clause containing the adverb.
2. Find that clause’s finite verb.
3. Decide whether the clause is a main clause or a subordinate clause.
4. In the modeled subordinate-clause pattern, place the sentence adverb before the finite verb.
5. In the modeled subject-first main-clause pattern, place it after the finite verb.
6. If another element or a subordinate clause comes first, keep main-clause verb-second order. With an unstressed pronoun subject, use finite verb + subject + sentence adverb; with a full noun subject, allow supported variation according to information structure.

Use the contrast as your memory aid:

::: examples
- no: Sara kan ikke komme.
- en: Sara cannot come.
- no: fordi Sara ikke kan komme
- en: because Sara cannot come
- no: I dag kan Sara ikke komme.
- en: Today Sara cannot come.
- no: Hvis Sara ikke kan komme, kan Maja kanskje møte Henrik.
- en: If Sara cannot come, perhaps Maja can meet Henrik.
:::
