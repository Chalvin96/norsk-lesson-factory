## A message before the meeting {#a-message-before-the-meeting role=orient objectives=obj-word-order-subordinate-clauses-01}

Maja and Henrik are preparing a short report for a residents’ meeting. Anna has the latest figures, but she may arrive late.

::: {.reading translation="Has Anna said whether she is coming to the meeting?" dialogue_id="a-message-before-the-meeting" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Har Anna sagt om hun kommer på møtet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but she wrote that she might be late because the bus is not running." dialogue_id="a-message-before-the-meeting" speaker_id="henrik" speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Ja, men hun skrev at hun kanskje blir forsinket fordi bussen ikke går.
:::

::: {.reading translation="If she does not arrive in time, we can begin without her." dialogue_id="a-message-before-the-meeting" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Hvis hun ikke kommer tidsnok, kan vi begynne uten henne.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I need the document that she has on her computer." dialogue_id="a-message-before-the-meeting" speaker_id="henrik" speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Jeg trenger dokumentet som hun har på datamaskinen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Can you ask whether she can send it before the meeting begins?" dialogue_id="a-message-before-the-meeting" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Kan du spørre om hun kan sende det før møtet begynner?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’ll do that. I hope that she sees the message when she gets off the bus." dialogue_id="a-message-before-the-meeting" speaker_id="henrik" speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Det gjør jeg. Jeg håper at hun ser meldingen når hun går av bussen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="The neighbour who usually opens the meeting room is not coming today either." dialogue_id="a-message-before-the-meeting" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Naboen som vanligvis åpner møterommet, kommer heller ikke i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then we must find someone who can unlock the door before the others arrive." dialogue_id="a-message-before-the-meeting" speaker_id="henrik" speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Da må vi finne noen som kan låse opp før de andre kommer.
:::

Maja and Henrik are joining several pieces of information: what Anna wrote, why she may be late, what they can do if she is late, and which document or person they mean. Norwegian uses **subordinate clauses (leddsetninger)** for these purposes.

A subordinate clause depends on another part of the sentence. It often begins with a linker such as **at**, **fordi**, **hvis**, **om**, **når**, or **før**, or with the relative word **som**.

The important word-order difference becomes especially visible with **ikke** and other sentence adverbs.

## Main clauses and subordinate clauses {#main-clauses-and-subordinate-clauses role=model objectives=obj-word-order-subordinate-clauses-01}

You already know main-clause word order:

::: examples
- no: Hun kommer ikke på møtet.
- en: She is not coming to the meeting.
:::

Here, the subject **hun** comes before the conjugated verb **kommer**, and **ikke** follows the verb.

Inside a subordinate clause, **ikke** normally comes before the conjugated verb:

::: examples
- no: Henrik sier at hun ikke kommer på møtet.
- en: Henrik says that she is not coming to the meeting.
:::

Compare the two patterns:

Main clause: **subject + verb + ikke**

Example:

::: examples
- no: Hun kommer ikke.
- en: She is not coming.
:::

Subordinate clause: **linker + subject + ikke + verb**

Example:

::: examples
- no: fordi hun ikke kommer
- en: because she is not coming
:::

The **conjugated verb**, also called the **finite verb (finitte verb)**, is the verb marked for tense. In **hun ikke kommer**, the finite verb is **kommer**.

::: {.rule}
In a subordinate clause, place the subject before sentence adverbs such as ikke, kanskje, vanligvis, aldri, ofte, and then place the finite verb.
:::

::: examples
- no: fordi bussen ikke går
- en: because the bus is not running
- no: at hun kanskje blir forsinket
- en: that she might be late
- no: når han vanligvis kommer hjem
- en: when he usually comes home
- no: hvis de aldri svarer
- en: if they never answer
:::

Without an adverb, subordinate-clause order may look similar to ordinary subject–verb order:

::: examples
- no: Hun sier at bussen går klokka åtte.
- en: She says that the bus leaves at eight.
:::

The presence of **ikke** makes the contrast easier to see:

::: examples
- no: Bussen går ikke klokka åtte.
- en: The bus does not leave at eight.
- no: Hun sier at bussen ikke går klokka åtte.
- en: She says that the bus does not leave at eight.
:::

A common mistake is to copy main-clause order after a subordinate linker:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Hun blir hjemme fordi hun kommer ikke tidsnok.
- en: She is staying home because she is not arriving in time.
:::

Use subordinate-clause order instead:

::: examples
- no: Hun blir hjemme fordi hun ikke kommer tidsnok.
- en: She is staying home because she is not arriving in time.
:::

**Activity request: Notice-the-word-order-signal**

**Purpose:** Notice how **ikke** reveals whether a clause has main-clause or subordinate-clause word order.

**Context:** Short statements and explanations about Anna’s journey and the meeting.

**Learner action:** Identify the subordinate clause in each sentence and mark the order of its subject, **ikke**, and finite verb.

**Success demonstrates:** Recognition of the pattern **linker + subject + ikke + finite verb**. This activity scores word-order recognition; the meaning of each linker is supporting context.

## Building clauses with common linkers {#building-clauses-with-common-linkers role=model objectives=obj-word-order-subordinate-clauses-01}

Different linkers express different relationships, but the internal word order remains the same.

**Reporting information with at**

**At** introduces information, thoughts, statements, or hopes. English often omits *that*, but Norwegian normally keeps **at** in examples like these.

::: examples
- no: Anna skriver at hun ikke finner dokumentet.
- en: Anna writes that she cannot find the document.
- no: Henrik tror at møtet kanskje begynner sent.
- en: Henrik thinks that the meeting might begin late.
- no: Maja håper at naboen snart svarer.
- en: Maja hopes that the neighbour answers soon.
:::

Notice that **ikke**, **kanskje**, and **snart** come before the finite verb in these subordinate clauses.

**Giving a reason with fordi**

::: examples
- no: Anna tar en taxi fordi bussen ikke går.
- en: Anna takes a taxi because the bus is not running.
- no: Vi begynner senere fordi dokumentet ennå ikke er klart.
- en: We are beginning later because the document is not ready yet.
- no: Henrik ringer fordi Anna vanligvis ikke leser meldinger på bussen.
- en: Henrik calls because Anna does not usually read messages on the bus.
:::

When more than one sentence adverb appears, they remain before the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: fordi dokumentet ennå ikke er klart
- en: because the document is not ready yet
:::

The lesson’s main concern is the position before the finite verb. Exact ordering among several adverbs depends on meaning, so begin by building clear clauses with one adverb.

**Expressing a condition with hvis**

::: examples
- no: Hvis Anna ikke kommer, begynner vi uten henne.
- en: If Anna does not come, we will begin without her.
- no: Hvis Henrik allerede har dokumentet, trenger han ikke å ringe.
- en: If Henrik already has the document, he does not need to call.
:::

The **hvis** clause has subordinate-clause order:

::: examples
- no: hvis Anna ikke kommer
- en: if Anna does not come
:::

When that clause comes first, the following main clause begins with its verb:

::: examples
- no: Hvis Anna ikke kommer, begynner vi uten henne.
- en: If Anna does not come, we will begin without her.
:::

Do not apply subordinate-clause order to the following main clause. The two clauses follow different rules:

Subordinate clause:

::: examples
- no: Anna ikke kommer
- en: Anna does not come
:::

Following main clause:

::: examples
- no: begynner vi
- en: we begin
:::

A comma normally separates an initial subordinate clause from the main clause.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Vi begynner uten henne hvis Anna ikke kommer.
- en: We will begin without her if Anna does not come.
- no: Hvis Anna ikke kommer, begynner vi uten henne.
- en: If Anna does not come, we will begin without her.
:::

The condition is the same. Moving it to the beginning changes the main-clause opening, not the internal order of the **hvis** clause.

**Activity request: Build-a-linked-explanation**

**Purpose:** Produce subordinate-clause word order after a given linker.

**Context:** Reasons, reports, and conditions connected with the delayed meeting.

**Learner action:** Complete short sentences by building one subordinate clause from supplied meanings, placing a subject, one sentence adverb, and a finite verb in natural order.

**Success demonstrates:** Accurate production of **subject + adverb + finite verb** after **at**, **fordi**, or **hvis**. Linker selection is provided and is not the scored dimension.

{{exercise: build_a_linked_explanation}}

**Activity request: Move-the-condition**

**Purpose:** Keep subordinate-clause order stable while changing clause position.

**Context:** Meeting plans that depend on whether Anna or another neighbour arrives.

**Learner action:** Rewrite a sentence so its **hvis** clause moves from the end to the beginning, then adjust the following main clause.

**Success demonstrates:** Preservation of subordinate word order and correct verb-first opening in the following main clause. The primary scored goal is clause linking after an initial subordinate clause.

{{exercise: move_the_condition}}

## Questions and time relationships inside larger sentences {#questions-and-time-relationships-inside-larger-sentences role=contrast objectives=obj-word-order-subordinate-clauses-01}

**Indirect yes–no questions with om**

A direct yes–no question begins with the verb:

::: examples
- no: Kommer Anna på møtet?
- en: Is Anna coming to the meeting?
:::

When the question becomes part of a larger sentence, use **om** and subordinate-clause order:

::: examples
- no: Henrik spør om Anna kommer på møtet.
- en: Henrik asks whether Anna is coming to the meeting.
:::

With **ikke**, the order becomes especially clear:

::: examples
- no: Kommer Anna ikke på møtet?
- en: Is Anna not coming to the meeting?
- no: Henrik spør om Anna ikke kommer på møtet.
- en: Henrik asks whether Anna is not coming to the meeting.
:::

Do not keep direct-question order after **om**:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Henrik spør om kommer Anna på møtet.
- en: Henrik asks whether Anna is coming to the meeting.
:::

Use a subject before the verb:

::: examples
- no: Henrik spør om Anna kommer på møtet.
- en: Henrik asks whether Anna is coming to the meeting.
:::

**Time with når and før**

::: examples
- no: Hun leser meldingen når hun går av bussen.
- en: She reads the message when she gets off the bus.
- no: Vi må åpne døra før de andre kommer.
- en: We must open the door before the others arrive.
- no: Anna ringer når hun ikke finner inngangen.
- en: Anna calls when she cannot find the entrance.
- no: Henrik skriver ut rapporten før skriveren vanligvis slår seg av.
- en: Henrik prints the report before the printer usually switches itself off.
:::

The same internal pattern applies:

- **når + subject + adverb + finite verb**
- **før + subject + adverb + finite verb**
- **om + subject + adverb + finite verb**

**Activity request: Turn-a-direct-question-into-an-indirect-question**

**Purpose:** Replace direct-question order with subordinate-clause order after **om**.

**Context:** Henrik sends Maja a message asking about people, documents, and meeting arrangements.

**Learner action:** Embed a short yes–no question after a reporting phrase such as **Han spør om ...**, including some items with **ikke** or **allerede**.

**Success demonstrates:** Correct use of **om + subject + adverb + finite verb**. The activity scores embedded-question word order, not punctuation in direct questions.

{{exercise: turn_a_direct_question_into_an_indirect_question}}

## Relative clauses with som {#relative-clauses-with-som role=contrast objectives=obj-word-order-subordinate-clauses-01}

A **relative clause (relativsetning)** identifies or describes a person, thing, or place already mentioned. Norwegian commonly introduces it with **som**.

In the dialogue, Maja and Henrik mention:

::: examples
- no: dokumentet som hun har på datamaskinen
- en: the document that she has on her computer
- no: naboen som vanligvis åpner møterommet
- en: the neighbour who usually opens the meeting room
:::

The relative clause follows the noun it describes.

**Som as the subject**

In this example, **som** represents the person who performs the action:

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger en nabo som kan låse opp.
- en: We need a neighbour who can unlock the door.
:::

There is no extra subject after **som**. Compare:

::: examples
- no: naboen som kommer tidlig
- en: the neighbour who arrives early
- no: naboen som ikke kommer i dag
- en: the neighbour who is not coming today
:::

The negative word still precedes the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: som ikke kommer
- en: who is not coming
:::

Do not add another subject referring to the same neighbour:

::: examples
- no: ✗ naboen som han kommer tidlig
- en: the neighbour who he arrives early
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: naboen som kommer tidlig
- en: the neighbour who arrives early
:::

**Som as the object**

In other relative clauses, the noun is the object of the action, and a separate subject appears after **som**:

::: examples
- no: dokumentet som Anna sender
- en: the document that Anna sends
- no: dokumentet som Anna ikke sender
- en: the document that Anna does not send
:::

Here, **Anna** is the subject of **sender**, while **dokumentet** is what she sends.

Compare the two relative patterns:

::: examples
- no: personen som sender dokumentet
- en: the person who sends the document
- no: dokumentet som personen sender
- en: the document that the person sends
:::

With **ikke**:

::: examples
- no: personen som ikke sender dokumentet
- en: the person who does not send the document
- no: dokumentet som personen ikke sender
- en: the document that the person does not send
:::

Both are subordinate clauses, so **ikke** comes before the finite verb. The difference is whether **som** itself represents the subject.

**Describing a place with der**

For a place where something happens, **der** is also useful:

::: examples
- no: møterommet der vi vanligvis møtes
- en: the meeting room where we usually meet
- no: bygningen der døra ikke åpner automatisk
- en: the building where the door does not open automatically
:::

The clause after **der** also uses subordinate-clause order.

**Activity request: Identify-the-role-of-som**

**Purpose:** Distinguish relative clauses where **som** represents the subject from clauses with a separate subject after **som**.

**Context:** Descriptions of neighbours, documents, keys, and rooms connected with the meeting.

**Learner action:** Match each described noun with a suitable relative clause, then indicate whether the clause needs an explicit subject after **som**.

**Success demonstrates:** Correct construction of the relative-clause subject pattern. Placement of **ikke** appears in the material but serves as supporting context.

{{exercise: identify_the_role_of_som}}

**Activity request: Place-ikke-in-a-relative-clause**

**Purpose:** Apply subordinate word order inside relative clauses.

**Context:** Maja clarifies which person, document, or room she means by adding negative information.

**Learner action:** Expand a noun phrase with a **som** or **der** clause containing **ikke**, keeping the intended subject clear.

**Success demonstrates:** Accurate placement of **ikke** before the finite verb in a relative clause. The primary score concerns adverb placement, not the choice between people and things.

## More than one subordinate clause {#more-than-one-subordinate-clause role=model objectives=obj-word-order-subordinate-clauses-01}

Written Norwegian often places one subordinate clause inside or beside another. This creates **nested structure**: a clause contains another clause.

Consider Henrik’s message:

::: examples
- no: Jeg håper at Anna ser meldingen når hun går av bussen.
- en: I hope that Anna sees the message when she gets off the bus.
:::

The sentence has three layers:

1. Main clause: **Jeg håper**
2. **at** clause: **at Anna ser meldingen**
3. **når** clause: **når hun går av bussen**

Each subordinate clause keeps its own subject and finite verb.

Add sentence adverbs:

::: examples
- no: Jeg håper at Anna snart ser meldingen når hun ikke lenger sitter på bussen.
- en: I hope that Anna soon sees the message when she is no longer sitting on the bus.
:::

Inside the **at** clause:

::: examples
- no: at Anna snart ser meldingen
- en: that Anna soon sees the message
:::

Inside the **når** clause:

::: examples
- no: når hun ikke lenger sitter på bussen
- en: when she is no longer sitting on the bus
:::

Do not treat the entire sentence as one long word-order pattern. Build and check each clause separately.

Another example combines a report, a condition, and a relative clause:

::: examples
- no: Maja sier at de trenger en nabo som kan åpne døra hvis Henrik ikke finner nøkkelen.
- en: Maja says that they need a neighbour who can open the door if Henrik cannot find the key.
:::

The structure is:

- **Maja sier** — main clause
- **at de trenger en nabo** — reported information
- **som kan åpne døra** — description of the neighbour
- **hvis Henrik ikke finner nøkkelen** — condition

The sentence is long, but every clause carries one manageable piece of meaning.

A further sentence may contain one report inside another:

::: examples
- no: Henrik skriver at Anna sier at hun ikke kan sende dokumentet før hun kommer hjem.
- en: Henrik writes that Anna says that she cannot send the document before she gets home.
:::

Work from the inside outward:

::: examples
- no: før hun kommer hjem
- en: before she gets home
- no: at hun ikke kan sende dokumentet før hun kommer hjem
- en: that she cannot send the document before she gets home
- no: at Anna sier at hun ikke kan sende dokumentet før hun kommer hjem
- en: that Anna says that she cannot send the document before she gets home
:::

Then attach the main clause:

::: examples
- no: Henrik skriver at Anna sier at hun ikke kan sende dokumentet før hun kommer hjem.
- en: Henrik writes that Anna says that she cannot send the document before she gets home.
:::

Dense structure does not require complicated vocabulary. It requires clear links and correct local word order.

**A common misconception: one ikke belongs to the whole sentence**

The position of **ikke** shows which verb or idea is negated.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Henrik sier ikke at Anna kommer.
- en: Henrik does not say that Anna is coming.
- no: Henrik sier at Anna ikke kommer.
- en: Henrik says that Anna is not coming.
:::

Here, **ikke** negates **sier** in the main clause.

Here, **ikke** belongs inside the **at** clause and negates **kommer**.

One small change creates a different meaning. First decide which action is negative; then place **ikke** in that clause.

**Activity request: Mark-clause-boundaries**

**Purpose:** Make nested sentences manageable by separating them into clauses.

**Context:** Messages exchanged after Anna reports a new delay.

**Learner action:** Divide each longer sentence into its main clause, linker-led subordinate clauses, and relative clauses. Mark the subject, sentence adverb, and finite verb within each subordinate clause.

**Success demonstrates:** Accurate recognition of local clause structure in a sentence containing multiple subordinate clauses. This activity scores boundary recognition rather than sentence production.

{{exercise: mark_clause_boundaries}}

**Activity request: Build-from-the-inside-outward**

**Purpose:** Produce clear nested written structure without losing subordinate word order.

**Context:** Henrik summarizes what Anna said about sending the document and arriving at the meeting.

**Learner action:** Begin with the innermost time, reason, or condition clause; place it inside an **at** clause; then attach that clause to a short main clause.

**Success demonstrates:** Construction of a sentence with at least two levels of subordination, with correct word order inside each subordinate clause.

{{exercise: build_from_the_inside_outward}}

**Activity request: Put-negation-in-the-intended-clause**

**Purpose:** Connect the position of **ikke** with sentence meaning.

**Context:** Contrasts between not reporting an event and reporting that an event will not happen.

**Learner action:** Write a sentence that negates the main reporting action, then a parallel sentence that negates the action inside the subordinate clause.

**Success demonstrates:** Deliberate placement of **ikke** in the clause whose meaning is negative. Other subordinate clauses may appear as context, but negation scope is the scored goal.

## Back at the meeting room {#back-at-the-meeting-room role=contrast objectives=obj-word-order-subordinate-clauses-01}

Anna eventually sends a message. Henrik reads it aloud to Maja.

::: {.reading translation="Anna writes that she will not arrive before the meeting begins." dialogue_id="back-at-the-meeting-room" speaker_id="henrik" speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Anna skriver at hun ikke kommer før møtet begynner.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Does she explain why she is still delayed?" dialogue_id="back-at-the-meeting-room" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Forklarer hun hvorfor hun fortsatt er forsinket?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. She says that the road the bus usually uses is closed." dialogue_id="back-at-the-meeting-room" speaker_id="henrik" speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Ja. Hun sier at veien som bussen vanligvis bruker, er stengt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="If she does not make it to the meeting, we must send her the notes afterwards." dialogue_id="back-at-the-meeting-room" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Hvis hun ikke rekker møtet, må vi sende henne notatene etterpå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="She has sent the document that we need, so at least we can begin." dialogue_id="back-at-the-meeting-room" speaker_id="henrik" speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Hun har sendt dokumentet som vi trenger, så vi kan i hvert fall begynne.
:::

The situation has changed, but the same forms organize the information:

- **at hun ikke kommer** reports information.
- **før møtet begynner** gives a time limit.
- **som bussen vanligvis bruker** identifies the road.
- **hvis hun ikke rekker møtet** gives a condition.
- **som vi trenger** identifies the document.

## Cumulative transfer: a changed everyday situation {#cumulative-transfer-a-changed-everyday-situation role=recap objectives=obj-word-order-subordinate-clauses-01}

Imagine that you are arranging a repair visit at home. A technician may arrive late, a neighbour has a spare key, and you need to tell another resident what will happen.

**Purpose:** Transfer subordinate-clause word order, linkers, relative clauses, and nested structure to a new everyday context.

**Context:** A written update about the technician, the key, the neighbour, and a possible delay.

**Learner action:** Write a short connected message containing a reported fact with **at**, a reason or condition, one relative clause identifying a person or object, and one sentence in which a subordinate clause is nested inside another structure. **Success demonstrates:** Integrated construction of linked subordinate clauses with correct local word order and a clear relationship between ideas.

## Short retrieval review {#short-retrieval-review role=recap objectives=obj-word-order-subordinate-clauses-01}

Without looking back, state the subordinate-clause pattern used with **ikke**. Then write:

1. one sentence reporting information with **at**;
2. one sentence beginning with a condition introduced by **hvis**;
3. one noun phrase expanded by a **som** clause;
4. one longer sentence containing two subordinate clauses.

A successful review keeps the subject and sentence adverb before the finite verb inside every subordinate clause, while preserving main-clause word order outside them.
