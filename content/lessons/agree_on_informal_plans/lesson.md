## A plan for Saturday {#a-plan-for-saturday role=orient objectives=obj-agree-on-informal-plans-01}

Mina and Jonas want to meet at the weekend. Mina suggests an activity, but her first proposed time does not work for Jonas.

::: {.reading translation='Shall we go to a café on Saturday?' dialogue_id='opening-plan' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Skal vi gå på kafé på lørdag?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, I’d love to! When works for you?' dialogue_id='opening-plan' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, gjerne! Når passer det for deg?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Does two o’clock work?' dialogue_id='opening-plan' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Passer det klokka to?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Unfortunately, I can’t at two o’clock. I’m going to help my sister.' dialogue_id='opening-plan' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Dessverre, jeg kan ikke klokka to. Jeg skal hjelpe søsteren min.
:::

::: {.reading translation='How about four o’clock?' dialogue_id='opening-plan' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Hva med klokka fire?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, that works well.' dialogue_id='opening-plan' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, det passer fint.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Great! Then we’ll meet at Café Sol at four o’clock on Saturday.' dialogue_id='opening-plan' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Flott! Da møtes vi på Kafé Sol klokka fire på lørdag.
:::

::: {.reading translation='We will. See you!' dialogue_id='opening-plan' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Det gjør vi. Vi ses!
:::

Mina and Jonas complete four useful steps:

1. Mina proposes an activity.
2. Jonas responds positively but asks about the time.
3. Jonas cannot manage the first time, so Mina offers an alternative.
4. They state the final plan clearly.

An informal plan often takes only a few lines, but both people need to know what they are doing and when they are meeting.

## Proposing an activity {#proposing-an-activity role=model objectives=obj-agree-on-informal-plans-01}

A common way to suggest doing something together is:

::: examples
- no: Skal vi + activity?
- en: Shall we + activity?
:::

In this expression, skal vi does not sound like a formal obligation. It is a friendly proposal.

::: examples
- no: Skal vi ta en kaffe?
- en: Shall we have a coffee?
- no: Skal vi gå en tur etter jobb?
- en: Shall we go for a walk after work?
- no: Skal vi se en film på fredag?
- en: Shall we watch a film on Friday?
- no: Skal vi lage middag sammen?
- en: Shall we make dinner together?
:::

You can include a day or time in the proposal:

::: examples
- no: Skal vi spise lunsj sammen i morgen?
- en: Shall we have lunch together tomorrow?
- no: Skal vi møtes på søndag formiddag?
- en: Shall we meet on Sunday morning?
:::

Another natural proposal begins with hva med, meaning how about:

::: examples
- no: Hva med en tur i parken?
- en: How about a walk in the park?
- no: Hva med å dra på kino på lørdag?
- en: How about going to the cinema on Saturday?
:::

Use **hva med + noun** for a thing or event:

::: examples
- no: Hva med en kaffe?
- en: How about a coffee?
:::

Use **hva med å + verb** for an activity:

::: examples
- no: Hva med å ta en kaffe?
- en: How about having a coffee?
:::

In the opening dialogue, Mina first uses skal vi to propose the main activity. Later, she uses hva med to suggest a different time.

**Checkpoint: Recognize the proposal**

Read a short exchange between two friends and identify the sentence that proposes their activity. Then say what activity they may do and whether a day or time has already been mentioned.

This belongs here because the learner must first recognize the proposal before responding to it. A successful response demonstrates understanding of the suggested activity and any scheduling information, without adding a new plan.

## Responding to a proposal {#responding-to-a-proposal role=model objectives=obj-agree-on-informal-plans-01}

If you like the proposal, you can respond briefly and warmly:

::: examples
- no: Ja, gjerne!
- en: Yes, I’d love to!
- no: Det høres hyggelig ut.
- en: That sounds nice.
- no: God idé!
- en: Good idea!
- no: Ja, det kan vi gjøre.
- en: Yes, we can do that.
:::

These responses accept the activity, but they do not always settle the time. Compare:

::: examples
- no: Ja, gjerne!
- en: Yes, I’d love to!
:::

This accepts the activity.

::: examples
- no: Ja, gjerne! Klokka tre passer fint.
- en: Yes, I’d love to! Three o’clock works well.
:::

This accepts both the activity and the proposed time.

You can also accept the activity while showing that the proposed time is difficult:

::: examples
- no: Jeg blir gjerne med, men jeg kan ikke på fredag.
- en: I’d be happy to come along, but I can’t on Friday.
- no: Det høres hyggelig ut, men klokka seks passer ikke.
- en: That sounds nice, but six o’clock doesn’t work.
:::

This distinction is important. You may want to meet even when you cannot accept the first suggested time.

## Saying when you are available {#saying-when-you-are-available role=model objectives=obj-agree-on-informal-plans-01}

The verb passe is very useful when agreeing on a time. In scheduling, it means to suit or to work.

::: examples
- no: Passer det klokka to?
- en: Does two o’clock work?
- no: Ja, det passer fint.
- en: Yes, that works well.
- no: Nei, det passer dessverre ikke.
- en: No, unfortunately that doesn’t work.
:::

You can ask about a day in the same way:

::: examples
- no: Passer det på torsdag?
- en: Does Thursday work?
- no: Passer lørdag for deg?
- en: Does Saturday work for you?
:::

To ask more generally, say:

::: examples
- no: Når passer det for deg?
- en: When works for you?
:::

To state your availability, kan is often enough:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan etter jobb.
- en: I’m available after work.
- no: Jeg kan på søndag.
- en: I’m available on Sunday.
- no: Jeg kan ikke før klokka fem.
- en: I’m not available before five o’clock.
:::

Here kan means that the time is possible for you in this situation. It does not describe a learned ability.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan møtes klokka fem.
- en: I can meet at five o’clock.
- no: Jeg kan ikke møtes klokka fem.
- en: I can’t meet at five o’clock.
:::

The second response is clear, but an informal conversation often sounds more cooperative if you add an alternative.

**Checkpoint: Respond to availability**

Return to Mina and Jonas. Imagine that Mina proposes the café and suggests two o’clock. Give Jonas a response that shows he likes the activity but cannot meet at that time.

This activity belongs here because it separates willingness from availability. A successful response demonstrates that Jonas accepts the idea while clearly rejecting only the proposed time.

## Offering one alternative {#offering-one-alternative role=contrast objectives=obj-agree-on-informal-plans-01}

When the first time does not work, offer another possible time. This keeps the conversation moving toward an agreement.

A simple pattern is:

::: examples
- no: Hva med + alternative time?
- en: How about + alternative time?
- no: Hva med klokka fire?
- en: How about four o’clock?
- no: Hva med søndag i stedet?
- en: How about Sunday instead?
- no: Hva med litt senere?
- en: How about a little later?
:::

You can also state your availability directly:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan klokka fire.
- en: I’m available at four o’clock.
- no: Jeg kan ikke på lørdag, men jeg kan på søndag.
- en: I can’t on Saturday, but I’m available on Sunday.
- no: Fredag passer ikke. Kan vi møtes på torsdag?
- en: Friday doesn’t work. Can we meet on Thursday?
:::

The word i stedet means instead and makes the contrast explicit:

::: examples
- no: Kan vi møtes klokka seks i stedet?
- en: Can we meet at six o’clock instead?
- no: Jeg kan ikke i kveld. Hva med i morgen i stedet?
- en: I can’t tonight. How about tomorrow instead?
:::

For this lesson, one clear alternative is enough. Avoid producing a long list of possible times before the other person has had a chance to respond.

**An aligned contrast**

**Rejecting without helping**

::: examples
- no: Nei, jeg kan ikke.
- en: No, I can’t.
:::

This is understandable, but the other person must make another suggestion.

**Rejecting and offering an alternative**

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan ikke klokka to, men jeg kan klokka fire.
- en: I can’t at two, but I’m available at four.
:::

This response helps both people reach an agreement.

**Offering the alternative as a question**

::: examples
- no: Klokka to passer ikke. Hva med klokka fire?
- en: Two o’clock doesn’t work. How about four?
:::

This sounds friendly and gives the other person space to accept or reject the new time.

**Checkpoint: Supply one alternative**

Use a situation in which a friend suggests going for a walk on Saturday morning, but you are busy then. Respond positively to the activity, say that Saturday morning does not work, and offer one alternative time.

This belongs here because it combines the first three stages of the conversation: proposal, availability response, and alternative. A successful response remains cooperative and gives exactly one usable alternative.

## Agreeing and stating the final plan {#agreeing-and-stating-the-final-plan role=model objectives=obj-agree-on-informal-plans-01}

Once both people accept the activity and time, state the agreed plan. This prevents confusion.

In the opening dialogue, Mina says:

::: examples
- no: Da møtes vi på Kafé Sol klokka fire på lørdag.
- en: Then we’ll meet at Café Sol at four o’clock on Saturday.
:::

Here da means then or in that case. It shows that the plan follows from the agreement.

Useful confirmation patterns include:

::: examples
- no: Da møtes vi klokka seks.
- en: Then we’ll meet at six o’clock.
- no: Da tar vi en kaffe etter jobb.
- en: Then we’ll have a coffee after work.
- no: Flott, da sees vi på søndag.
- en: Great, then we’ll see each other on Sunday.
- no: Supert! Da går vi på kino fredag kveld.
- en: Great! Then we’ll go to the cinema on Friday evening.
:::

A complete confirmation usually repeats the activity and the agreed time:

::: examples
- no: Da spiser vi lunsj sammen klokka ett på torsdag.
- en: Then we’ll have lunch together at one o’clock on Thursday.
:::

This is clearer than a response such as OK or flott on its own.

The other person can confirm with:

::: examples
- no: Ja, det gjør vi.
- en: Yes, we will.
- no: Det passer fint. Vi ses da!
- en: That works well. See you then!
- no: Flott, vi ses på lørdag!
- en: Great, see you on Saturday!
:::

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-agree-on-informal-plans-01}

**Treating a time problem as rejection of the whole invitation**

::: examples
- no: Nei, jeg vil ikke.
- en: No, I don’t want to.
:::

This rejects the activity. Do not use it if you want to join but are unavailable at the proposed time.

Use:

::: examples
- no: Jeg blir gjerne med, men jeg kan ikke klokka tre.
- en: I’d be happy to come along, but I can’t at three o’clock.
:::

**Giving an unclear alternative**

::: examples
- no: Kanskje en annen gang.
- en: Maybe another time.
:::

This does not provide a usable time.

Use a specific alternative:

::: examples
- no: Hva med søndag ettermiddag?
- en: How about Sunday afternoon?
:::

**Accepting without confirming what was agreed**

::: examples
- no: Ja, fint.
- en: Yes, fine.
:::

This may be enough in a very short conversation, but it can leave the plan unclear.

A clearer ending is:

::: examples
- no: Ja, fint. Da møtes vi utenfor kinoen klokka sju.
- en: Yes, fine. Then we’ll meet outside the cinema at seven.
:::

**Mixing two different times in the confirmation**

If one person rejects two o’clock and both accept four o’clock, the final sentence must use four o’clock. The confirmation should repeat the latest agreed time, not the original proposal.

## Back to Mina and Jonas {#back-to-mina-and-jonas role=model objectives=obj-agree-on-informal-plans-01}

On Saturday afternoon, Jonas sends Mina a message.

::: {.reading translation='Hi! I’ll be finished at my sister’s at half past three. Does it still work for us to meet at four o’clock?' dialogue_id='follow-up-plan' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hei! Jeg er ferdig hos søsteren min klokka halv fire. Passer det fortsatt at vi møtes klokka fire?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, it does. We’ll meet at Café Sol at four o’clock.' dialogue_id='follow-up-plan' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja, det gjør det. Vi møtes på Kafé Sol klokka fire.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Great. See you soon!' dialogue_id='follow-up-plan' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Flott. Vi ses snart!
:::

::: {.reading translation='See you!' dialogue_id='follow-up-plan' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Vi ses!
:::

Mina repeats the place and time, so Jonas knows that nothing has changed. The short exchange closes the same plan introduced at the beginning of the chapter.

**Checkpoint: Build the complete exchange**

Create a short message exchange between two named friends. One person proposes an informal activity and a time. The other person wants to join but is unavailable then, so they offer one alternative. The first person accepts and states the final activity and time.

This activity belongs here because it rehearses the complete communicative sequence from proposal to agreement. A successful exchange contains one coherent plan, one availability problem, one alternative, and an unambiguous final confirmation.

## The same pattern in new situations {#the-same-pattern-in-new-situations role=contrast objectives=obj-agree-on-informal-plans-01}

The structure works with many informal activities.

**Lunch with a colleague**

::: examples
- no: Skal vi spise lunsj sammen på tirsdag klokka tolv?
- en: Shall we have lunch together on Tuesday at twelve?
- no: Gjerne, men jeg kan ikke klokka tolv. Hva med klokka ett?
- en: I’d love to, but I can’t at twelve. How about one?
- no: Ja, det passer. Da møtes vi i kantina klokka ett.
- en: Yes, that works. Then we’ll meet in the cafeteria at one.
:::

**A walk with a friend**

::: examples
- no: Skal vi gå en tur i kveld?
- en: Shall we go for a walk tonight?
- no: Det høres hyggelig ut, men jeg kan ikke før klokka åtte.
- en: That sounds nice, but I can’t before eight.
- no: Hva med klokka åtte, da?
- en: How about eight, then?
- no: Ja, fint. Da møtes vi ved parken klokka åtte.
- en: Yes, fine. Then we’ll meet by the park at eight.
:::

**A film at the weekend**

::: examples
- no: Hva med å se en film på lørdag?
- en: How about watching a film on Saturday?
- no: God idé! Jeg kan ikke på lørdag, men jeg kan på søndag.
- en: Good idea! I can’t on Saturday, but I’m available on Sunday.
- no: Søndag passer fint. Da ser vi filmen på søndag.
- en: Sunday works well. Then we’ll watch the film on Sunday.
:::

## Final retrieval and review {#final-retrieval-and-review role=recap objectives=obj-agree-on-informal-plans-01}

Without looking back, produce a four-part informal planning exchange: propose an activity, respond to the suggested time, offer one alternative, and state the agreed plan. Use a new activity and make the final sentence clear enough that both people know what they are doing and when.
