## A busy afternoon {#a-busy-afternoon role=orient objectives=obj-rekke-aa-or-ha-tid-til-aa-01}

Nora and Emil work in the same office. They are due at a customer meeting across town, but Nora also needs to send a revised budget before they leave.

::: {.reading translation='The meeting starts at half past two. Will we manage to catch the bus that leaves in twenty minutes?' dialogue_id='busy-afternoon' speaker_id='nora' speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Møtet begynner klokka halv tre. Rekker vi å ta bussen som går om tjue minutter?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, if we leave in ten minutes. But will you manage to send the budget first?' dialogue_id='busy-afternoon' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ja, hvis vi går om ti minutter. Men rekker du å sende budsjettet først?
:::

::: {.reading translation='I think so. I only need to check two figures. Do you have time to look at the last page?' dialogue_id='busy-afternoon' speaker_id='nora' speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det tror jeg. Jeg må bare kontrollere to tall. Har du tid til å se på den siste siden?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, I do. I don’t have any other tasks right now.' dialogue_id='busy-afternoon' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ja, det har jeg. Jeg har ingen andre oppgaver akkurat nå.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Great. If we work together, we’ll manage to finish before the bus leaves.' dialogue_id='busy-afternoon' speaker_id='nora' speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Flott. Hvis vi samarbeider, rekker vi å bli ferdige før bussen går.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Send me the document. I have time to read the page now, but not to rewrite the entire text.' dialogue_id='busy-afternoon' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Send meg dokumentet. Jeg har tid til å lese siden nå, men ikke til å skrive om hele teksten.
:::

Two different questions about time appear here. Nora asks whether they can complete actions before particular cutoffs: sending the budget before leaving and finishing before the bus departs. For those questions, she uses **rekke å**. She also asks whether Emil has some time available to help. For that, she uses **ha tid til å**.

## The central distinction {#the-central-distinction role=model objectives=obj-rekke-aa-or-ha-tid-til-aa-01}

Use **rekke å + infinitive** when successful completion before a cutoff matters.

::: examples
- no: Rekker du å sende budsjettet før vi går?
- en: Will you manage to send the budget before we leave?
:::

There is a limited period, and the result must be achieved within it. The cutoff may be stated directly, as in før vi går, or it may be clear from the situation.

Use **ha tid til å + infinitive** when you are asking or stating whether time is available for an activity.

::: examples
- no: Har du tid til å se på den siste siden?
- en: Do you have time to look at the last page?
:::

Here the main issue is Emil’s availability. Nora wants to know whether his schedule or workload leaves room for the activity.

Both expressions can correspond to English questions with have time, so a word-for-word translation is not enough. Ask what the speaker means:

- Is the key question whether the action can be completed in time? Use **rekke å**.
- Is the key question whether there is time available for the activity? Use **ha tid til å**.

## Completion before a cutoff: rekke å {#completion-before-a-cutoff-rekke-aa role=model objectives=obj-rekke-aa-or-ha-tid-til-aa-01}

The basic frame is:

**rekke å + infinitive**

The verb after å names the action that must happen within the available period.

::: examples
- no: Jeg rekker å sende e-posten før lunsj.
- en: I’ll manage to send the email before lunch.
- no: Vi rekker å kjøpe billetter før filmen begynner.
- en: We’ll manage to buy tickets before the film starts.
- no: Hun rakk å låse døra før hun løp til bussen.
- en: She managed to lock the door before she ran to the bus.
:::

A cutoff often appears with expressions such as før møtet, innen fredag, før butikken stenger, or før toget går.

::: examples
- no: Rekker dere å bli ferdige innen fredag?
- en: Will you manage to finish by Friday?
- no: Jeg rekker ikke å handle før butikken stenger.
- en: I won’t manage to shop before the store closes.
- no: Rakk han å ringe legen før kontoret stengte?
- en: Did he manage to call the doctor before the office closed?
:::

Sometimes the cutoff remains unstated because everyone already knows it.

At a station, someone might ask:

::: examples
- no: Rekker vi å kjøpe kaffe?
- en: Will we have enough time to buy coffee?
:::

The likely cutoff is the train’s departure. The question is not simply whether coffee buying fits somewhere in the day. It is whether the speakers can buy it and still catch the train.

At work, a colleague might say:

::: examples
- no: Jeg rekker ikke å lese alt.
- en: I won’t manage to read everything in time.
:::

The deadline could be a meeting that is about to begin or a document that must be returned soon. Even without an explicit før phrase, rekke presents the activity as a race against limited time.

**Checkpoint: identify the hidden cutoff**

Read several short situations involving a departing bus, a closing shop, a meeting that is about to start, and a report due at the end of the day. For each situation, explain in a few words what event limits the available time.

This activity belongs here because **rekke å** often depends on a cutoff that is understood rather than stated. A successful response would identify the relevant endpoint and show why completing the action before that endpoint matters.

## Available time: ha tid til å {#available-time-ha-tid-til-aa role=model objectives=obj-rekke-aa-or-ha-tid-til-aa-01}

The basic frame is:

**ha tid til å + infinitive**

Use it when time is treated as part of someone’s availability or capacity.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har tid til å hjelpe deg etter lunsj.
- en: I have time to help you after lunch.
- no: Har du tid til å snakke litt?
- en: Do you have time to talk for a bit?
- no: Vi har ikke tid til å besøke museet i dag.
- en: We don’t have time to visit the museum today.
- no: Hun hadde tid til å lage middag hjemme.
- en: She had time to make dinner at home.
:::

The activity may take ten minutes or several hours. The important point is whether the person’s schedule leaves room for it.

::: examples
- no: På søndag har jeg tid til å vaske leiligheten.
- en: On Sunday I have time to clean the apartment.
:::

This says that Sunday offers enough available time for cleaning. It does not necessarily present cleaning as a task that must be completed before a particular deadline.

Questions with **har du tid til å** are also common requests.

::: examples
- no: Har du tid til å lese gjennom søknaden min?
- en: Do you have time to read through my application?
:::

The speaker is asking about the other person’s availability. The application may eventually have a deadline, but that is not what the expression itself highlights.

**Checkpoint: focus on availability**

Use short descriptions of people’s schedules to write questions with **har tid til å**. The contexts should include Nora asking Emil for help, a friend checking whether someone can meet after work, and a family member asking for help with dinner.

This practice belongs here because it keeps attention on free time and workload rather than on racing toward an endpoint. A successful response would use **ha tid til å** when the context asks whether an activity fits into someone’s schedule.

## Compare the same activity {#compare-the-same-activity role=contrast objectives=obj-rekke-aa-or-ha-tid-til-aa-01}

The difference becomes clearer when the action stays the same but the time perspective changes.

**Reading a document**

::: examples
- no: Har du tid til å lese rapporten i dag?
- en: Do you have time to read the report today?
:::

The question concerns available time in the person’s day.

::: examples
- no: Rekker du å lese rapporten før møtet?
- en: Will you manage to read the report before the meeting?
:::

The question concerns completion before the meeting begins.

**Calling someone**

::: examples
- no: Jeg har tid til å ringe kunden etter lunsj.
- en: I have time to call the customer after lunch.
:::

The speaker has an available period after lunch.

::: examples
- no: Jeg rekker å ringe kunden før lunsj.
- en: I’ll manage to call the customer before lunch.
:::

The call can be completed before the stated cutoff.

**Shopping**

::: examples
- no: Vi har tid til å handle i ettermiddag.
- en: We have time to go shopping this afternoon.
:::

Shopping fits into the afternoon schedule.

::: examples
- no: Vi rekker å handle før butikken stenger.
- en: We’ll manage to shop before the store closes.
:::

The store’s closing time creates a cutoff.

**Finishing work**

::: examples
- no: Nora har tid til å arbeide med budsjettet nå.
- en: Nora has time to work on the budget now.
:::

She has room in her schedule to do some work on it.

::: examples
- no: Nora rekker å gjøre ferdig budsjettet før hun går.
- en: Nora will manage to finish the budget before she leaves.
:::

Finishing the budget must happen before her departure.

Notice how **rekke å** naturally combines with actions that lead to a result: send, finish, catch, submit, buy, or arrive. **Ha tid til å** can also be used with these verbs, but it presents the activity as something that does or does not fit into the speaker’s available time.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har tid til å skrive ferdig rapporten i kveld.
- en: I have time to finish writing the report this evening.
:::

This means that the evening provides sufficient available time.

::: examples
- no: Jeg rekker å skrive ferdig rapporten før fristen.
- en: I’ll manage to finish writing the report before the deadline.
:::

This emphasizes achieving the result before the deadline.

**Checkpoint: choose the intended perspective**

Work with paired situations that contain the same action but different intentions. In one version, a person’s schedule is the main issue. In the other, an approaching departure, closing time, meeting, or deadline is the main issue. Supply a complete sentence with the expression that matches each intention.

This controlled contrast belongs here because vocabulary alone cannot determine the choice. A successful response would preserve the action while changing between **ha tid til å** and **rekke å** according to whether availability or timely completion is central.

{{exercise: checkpoint_intended_perspective}}

## Negative statements reveal the difference {#negative-statements-reveal-the-difference role=contrast objectives=obj-rekke-aa-or-ha-tid-til-aa-01}

Negative forms are especially useful because they show what the speaker says is impossible.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har ikke tid til å spise frokost.
- en: I don’t have time to eat breakfast.
:::

Breakfast does not fit into the speaker’s available time.

::: examples
- no: Jeg rekker ikke å spise frokost før bussen går.
- en: I won’t manage to eat breakfast before the bus leaves.
:::

The speaker may have a few free minutes, but not enough to complete breakfast before the bus departs.

Compare another pair:

::: examples
- no: Emil har ikke tid til å kontrollere hele dokumentet.
- en: Emil doesn’t have time to check the entire document.
:::

His available time or workload does not allow it.

::: examples
- no: Emil rekker ikke å kontrollere hele dokumentet før Nora må sende det.
- en: Emil won’t manage to check the entire document before Nora has to send it.
:::

The sending time is the cutoff. He may begin checking, but he cannot complete the whole task in time.

This distinction matters because beginning an activity is not the same as completing it before an endpoint.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har ti minutter, så jeg har tid til å begynne. Men jeg rekker ikke å bli ferdig.
- en: I have ten minutes, so I have time to start. But I won’t manage to finish.
:::

The first clause describes available time. The second describes failure to reach a result before time runs out.

**Checkpoint: explain what fails**

For each negative sentence, state whether the problem is lack of room in the schedule or failure to complete the action before a cutoff. Use contexts from Nora and Emil’s afternoon, including checking figures, rewriting text, leaving the office, and catching the bus.

This checkpoint belongs here because negative sentences can sound similar in English. A successful response would identify precisely what the speaker lacks: general available time or enough time to reach the required result.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-rekke-aa-or-ha-tid-til-aa-01}

**Using ha tid til å when the deadline is the point**

Less suitable for the intended meaning:

::: examples
- no: Har vi tid til å bli ferdige før bussen går?
- en: Do we have time to finish before the bus leaves?
:::

This sentence is understandable, but if the intended question is whether the speakers will successfully finish before the bus leaves, **rekker vi å** expresses that perspective more directly.

Preferred for the chapter’s target meaning:

::: examples
- no: Rekker vi å bli ferdige før bussen går?
- en: Will we manage to finish before the bus leaves?
:::

**Using rekke å for ordinary availability**

Less suitable when asking about someone’s schedule:

::: examples
- no: Rekker du å hjelpe meg en gang i ettermiddag?
- en: Will you manage to help me sometime this afternoon?
:::

This can suggest an implicit limit or a crowded afternoon. If the speaker simply wants to know whether the other person is available, use:

::: examples
- no: Har du tid til å hjelpe meg i ettermiddag?
- en: Do you have time to help me this afternoon?
:::

**Copying English have time mechanically**

English often uses have time for both ideas.

Do you have time to send it before the meeting?

Norwegian choice depends on the intended focus.

::: examples
- no: Rekker du å sende den før møtet?
- en: Will you manage to send it before the meeting?
:::

The meeting is an endpoint, and successful sending before that endpoint is the central issue.

By contrast:

::: examples
- no: Har du tid til å arbeide med den i dag?
- en: Do you have time to work on it today?
:::

Here the speaker asks whether the activity fits into the day.

**Leaving out parts of the frame**

Both target expressions introduce another verb with **å**.

::: examples
- no: Jeg rekker å sende den.
- en: I’ll manage to send it in time.
- no: Jeg har tid til å sende den.
- en: I have time to send it.
:::

Keep **til** in **ha tid til å**. The complete frame is not *ha tid å*.

## Back at the office {#back-at-the-office role=model objectives=obj-rekke-aa-or-ha-tid-til-aa-01}

Emil has checked the final page. Nora is preparing to send the document.

::: {.reading translation='I have time to check one more figure.' dialogue_id='back-at-office' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Jeg har tid til å kontrollere ett tall til.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Do it. Will we manage to send the document before we have to leave?' dialogue_id='back-at-office' speaker_id='nora' speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Gjør det. Rekker vi å sende dokumentet før vi må gå?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes. But we don’t have time to discuss the wording now.' dialogue_id='back-at-office' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ja. Men vi har ikke tid til å diskutere formuleringene nå.
:::

::: {.reading translation='That’s fine. The customer needs the figures first.' dialogue_id='back-at-office' speaker_id='nora' speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det går bra. Kunden trenger tallene først.
:::

A minute later, Nora sends the budget.

::: {.reading translation='It’s sent now. Will we manage to walk to the bus stop?' dialogue_id='back-at-office' speaker_id='nora' speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Nå er det sendt. Rekker vi å gå til holdeplassen?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, if we leave immediately. We have time to talk about the rest on the bus.' dialogue_id='back-at-office' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ja, hvis vi går med en gang. Vi har tid til å snakke om resten på bussen.
:::

The two expressions divide the situation neatly. Sending the document and reaching the stop must happen before cutoffs, so Nora uses **rekke å**. Discussing the wording and talking on the bus depend on whether time is available, so Emil uses **ha tid til å**.

## Guided practice in new situations {#guided-practice-in-new-situations role=contrast objectives=obj-rekke-aa-or-ha-tid-til-aa-01}

**From schedule to deadline**

Take a sentence stating that someone has time available for an activity. Add a clear cutoff and rewrite the message so that successful completion before that cutoff becomes central.

Use situations such as reading a message during the afternoon, buying food before a guest arrives, and completing an online form before the system closes.

This activity belongs here because it requires more than replacing one phrase with another. The whole time perspective must change. A successful response would use **rekke å**, retain a natural infinitive after **å**, and make the cutoff relevant to completing the action.

{{exercise: guided_schedule_to_deadline}}

**From deadline to availability**

Take a sentence about managing an action before an endpoint. Remove the race against time and rewrite it as a statement or question about available time.

Use situations such as calling a colleague, preparing lunch, reviewing a document, and visiting a friend.

This activity belongs here because it tests whether the learner can deliberately shift away from completion and toward schedule capacity. A successful response would use **ha tid til å** and produce a context in which availability, not a deadline, is the main concern.

{{exercise: guided_deadline_to_availability}}

**Complete Nora and Emil’s plan**

Continue the office story with short written lines about what Nora and Emil can do before the bus leaves and what they can do during the journey. Include one positive and one negative use of each target expression.

This practice returns to the chapter anchor while requiring independent choices. A successful response would connect bus-related cutoffs with **rekke å** and periods of availability with **ha tid til å**, without treating the expressions as interchangeable.

{{exercise: guided_nora_emil_plan}}
