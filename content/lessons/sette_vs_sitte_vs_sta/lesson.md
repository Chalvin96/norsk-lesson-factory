## Getting the room ready {#getting-the-room-ready role=orient objectives=obj-sette-vs-sitte-vs-sta-01}

Marius and Amina are preparing a room for a neighborhood meeting. The guests will arrive soon, but cups, chairs, and people are not yet where they should be.

::: {.reading translation="Where should I put the coffee pots?" dialogue_id="getting-the-room-ready" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Hvor skal jeg sette kaffekannene?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Put them on the long table. The cups can stand next to them." dialogue_id="getting-the-room-ready" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Sett dem på det lange bordet. Koppene kan stå ved siden av.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Should this chair stand by the door?" dialogue_id="getting-the-room-ready" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Skal denne stolen stå ved døra?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Nora will sit there and welcome the guests." dialogue_id="getting-the-room-ready" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Ja. Nora sitter der og tar imot gjestene.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’m happy to sit down by the door, but the table is standing a little too far away." dialogue_id="getting-the-room-ready" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Jeg setter meg gjerne ved døra, men bordet står litt for langt unna.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then I’ll put the table closer to the chair." dialogue_id="getting-the-room-ready" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Da setter jeg bordet nærmere stolen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Can you also put the flowers on the table?" dialogue_id="getting-the-room-ready" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Flott. Kan du også sette blomstene på bordet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but the vase isn’t standing securely here." dialogue_id="getting-the-room-ready" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Ja, men vasen står ikke støtt her.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Put it in the middle of the table. It stands better there." dialogue_id="getting-the-room-ready" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Sett den midt på bordet. Der står den bedre.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Now Nora can sit by the door, and everything is ready for the meeting." dialogue_id="getting-the-room-ready" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Nå kan Nora sitte ved døra, og alt er klart til møtet.
:::

Three closely related verbs organize this scene:

- **sette** describes seating someone or placing the upright or base-resting objects taught here somewhere.
- **sitte** describes someone or an animal being seated.
- **stå** describes someone standing or an object being upright in its normal position.

For the constructions taught here, the main question is whether you are describing a seating or object-placing action or a position that already exists.

## First checkpoint: notice action and position {#first-checkpoint-notice-action-and-position role=contrast objectives=obj-sette-vs-sitte-vs-sta-01}

Read the dialogue again and identify each use of **sette**, **sitte**, and **stå**. For every example, decide whether someone is moving or placing something, is seated, or is upright in a location.

This activity belongs here because the dialogue already provides a complete physical setting: people arrange objects, then describe their resulting positions. A successful response would distinguish the placing actions from the positions that follow, rather than translating each verb mechanically into English.

## Sette: placing someone or something {#sette-placing-someone-or-something role=model objectives=obj-sette-vs-sitte-vs-sta-01}

In the constructions taught here, use **sette** when a person seats someone or places an upright or base-resting object in a particular place. The verb focuses on the placing action.

::: examples
- no: Amina setter kaffekannene på bordet.
- en: Amina puts the coffee pots on the table.
:::

Amina performs an action. The coffee pots change location because she places them on the table.

::: examples
- no: Marius setter stolen ved døra.
- en: Marius puts the chair by the door.
- no: Nora setter vasen midt på bordet.
- en: Nora puts the vase in the middle of the table.
- no: De setter gjestene ved to lange bord.
- en: They seat the guests at two long tables.
:::

With a person as the object, **sette** can mean placing or seating that person somewhere.

The present-tense form is **setter**. The imperative, used for direct instructions, is **sett**.

::: examples
- no: Sett koppene ved siden av kaffekannene.
- en: Put the cups next to the coffee pots.
- no: Sett barnet på stolen.
- en: Put the child on the chair.
:::

In these sentences, there is normally someone or something being placed: **koppene**, **barnet**, **stolen**, or **vasen**. This direct participant helps signal that **sette** is the placing verb.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Amina setter koppen på bordet.
- en: Amina puts the cup on the table.
- no: Koppen står på bordet.
- en: The cup is standing on the table.
:::

The first sentence describes Amina’s action. The second describes the cup’s position after that action.

## Sitte: being seated {#sitte-being-seated role=model objectives=obj-sette-vs-sitte-vs-sta-01}

Use **sitte** when a person or animal is in a seated position. It describes a state, not the act of placing another object.

::: examples
- no: Nora sitter ved døra.
- en: Nora is sitting by the door.
- no: Gjestene sitter rundt bordet.
- en: The guests are sitting around the table.
- no: Katten sitter på stolen.
- en: The cat is sitting on the chair.
:::

English often uses a form of *be* plus *sitting*. Norwegian commonly uses the single verb **sitte**.

A person can also **sette seg**, literally place oneself, when moving into a seated position. The reflexive word **seg** is the object of **sette**.

::: examples
- no: Nora setter seg ved døra.
- en: Nora sits down by the door.
- no: Nå sitter Nora ved døra.
- en: Now Nora is sitting by the door.
:::

The contrast is the same as with the cup:

::: examples
- no: Nora setter seg på stolen.
- en: Nora sits down on the chair.
- no: Nora sitter på stolen.
- en: Nora is sitting on the chair.
:::

**Setter seg** describes the movement into the seated position. **Sitter** describes the position after the movement. This remains the same central distinction: placing versus existing position.

## Stå: being upright or standing {#sta-being-upright-or-standing role=model objectives=obj-sette-vs-sitte-vs-sta-01}

Use **stå** for a person who is standing.

::: examples
- no: Marius står ved vinduet.
- en: Marius is standing by the window.
- no: To gjester står utenfor døra.
- en: Two guests are standing outside the door.
:::

Use **stå** for many objects that are upright, rest on their usual base, or occupy a normal standing position. Cups, bottles, vases, chairs, tables, boxes, and similar objects commonly **står** somewhere.

::: examples
- no: Kaffekannene står på bordet.
- en: The coffee pots are standing on the table.
- no: Vasen står mellom koppene.
- en: The vase is standing between the cups.
- no: Stolen står ved døra.
- en: The chair is standing by the door.
- no: Bordene står langs veggen.
- en: The tables are standing along the wall.
:::

English does not always use *stand* for these objects. It might say that a cup, chair, or table *is* somewhere. Norwegian often specifies the object’s position with **stå**.

That makes this pair especially useful:

::: examples
- no: Hvor skal jeg sette stolen?
- en: Where should I put the chair?
- no: Stolen skal stå ved døra.
- en: The chair should stand by the door.
:::

The question asks about the placing action, so it uses **sette**. The answer describes the intended position, so it uses **stå**.

## A practical decision {#a-practical-decision role=contrast objectives=obj-sette-vs-sitte-vs-sta-01}

When choosing among the three verbs, ask two questions.

First, is someone actively seating a person or placing an upright or base-resting object of the kind shown here somewhere? In these constructions, use **sette**.

::: examples
- no: Vi setter blomstene på bordet.
- en: We put the flowers on the table.
:::

If not, are you describing a seated person or animal? Use **sitte**.

::: examples
- no: Nora sitter ved døra.
- en: Nora is sitting by the door.
:::

Otherwise, if a person is standing or an object is upright in its normal position, use **stå**.

::: examples
- no: Marius står ved vinduet.
- en: Marius is standing by the window.
- no: Blomstene står på bordet.
- en: The flowers are standing on the table.
:::

Here are the contrasts aligned:

**Placing action**

::: examples
- no: Amina setter koppen på bordet.
- en: Amina puts the cup on the table.
:::

**Resulting position**

::: examples
- no: Koppen står på bordet.
- en: The cup is standing on the table.
:::

**Placing action**

::: examples
- no: Marius setter stolen ved døra.
- en: Marius puts the chair by the door.
:::

**Resulting position**

::: examples
- no: Stolen står ved døra.
- en: The chair is standing by the door.
:::

**Placing action**

::: examples
- no: De setter barnet på stolen.
- en: They put the child on the chair.
:::

**Resulting position**

::: examples
- no: Barnet sitter på stolen.
- en: The child is sitting on the chair.
:::

**Placing action**

::: examples
- no: Nora setter seg ved døra.
- en: Nora sits down by the door.
:::

**Resulting position**

::: examples
- no: Nora sitter ved døra.
- en: Nora is sitting by the door.
:::

## Do not translate English word for word {#do-not-translate-english-word-for-word role=contrast objectives=obj-sette-vs-sitte-vs-sta-01}

A common mistake is to choose a verb only because it resembles an English word.

**Mistake: using sit for objects**

A learner may think that an object “sits” on a table in English and therefore choose **sitte** in Norwegian. For an upright cup or vase, Norwegian normally uses **stå**.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Vasen sitter på bordet.
- en: ✗ The vase is sitting on the table.
:::

Natural Norwegian:

::: examples
- no: Vasen står på bordet.
- en: The vase is standing on the table.
:::

**Mistake: using sette for an existing position**

**Sette** needs a placing action. It does not normally describe where an object already is.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Kaffekannen setter på bordet.
- en: ✗ The coffee pot puts on the table.
:::

Correct action:

::: examples
- no: Amina setter kaffekannen på bordet.
- en: Amina puts the coffee pot on the table.
:::

Correct position:

::: examples
- no: Kaffekannen står på bordet.
- en: The coffee pot is standing on the table.
:::

Notice that the action sentence includes the person performing the action, Amina. The position sentence makes the coffee pot the subject.

**Mistake: using stå for a seated person**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Nora står på stolen og tar imot gjestene.
- en: ✗ Nora is standing on the chair and welcoming the guests.
:::

If Nora is seated, use:

::: examples
- no: Nora sitter på stolen og tar imot gjestene.
- en: Nora is sitting on the chair and welcoming the guests.
:::

The incorrect sentence is grammatically possible, but it means that Nora is physically standing on top of the chair. Choosing the wrong position verb changes the scene.

## The guests arrive {#the-guests-arrive role=model objectives=obj-sette-vs-sitte-vs-sta-01}

A few minutes later, the room is ready.

::: {.reading translation="Now the first guests are sitting by the window." dialogue_id="the-guests-arrive" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Nå sitter de første gjestene ved vinduet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. I’m putting some extra cups on the table." dialogue_id="the-guests-arrive" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Bra. Jeg setter noen ekstra kopper på bordet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="The coffee pots are already standing there." dialogue_id="the-guests-arrive" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Kaffekannene står allerede der.
:::

::: {.reading translation="A guest is standing by the door and waiting. Where should we seat him?" dialogue_id="the-guests-arrive" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
En gjest står ved døra og venter. Hvor skal vi sette ham?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Seat him next to Ingrid. She is sitting alone." dialogue_id="the-guests-arrive" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Sett ham ved siden av Ingrid. Hun sitter alene.
:::

This continuation shows all three verbs working together. The waiting guest **står** because he is standing. Ingrid **sitter** because she is seated. Marius asks where they should **sette** the guest because someone must place or seat him.

Compare the same person at three moments:

::: examples
- no: Gjesten står ved døra.
- en: The guest is standing by the door.
- no: Nora setter gjesten ved siden av Ingrid.
- en: Nora seats the guest next to Ingrid.
- no: Gjesten sitter ved siden av Ingrid.
- en: The guest is sitting next to Ingrid.
:::

The person first has one position, then someone places him in a new position, and finally he has a seated position.

This activity belongs here because it asks you to track a change through time without introducing another grammar goal. A successful response would keep the roles clear: **sette** names the placing event, while **sitte** or **stå** names the resulting position.

## Choosing the subject correctly {#choosing-the-subject-correctly role=model objectives=obj-sette-vs-sitte-vs-sta-01}

The verb choice often affects which participant becomes the subject.

::: examples
- no: Amina setter vasen på bordet.
- en: Amina puts the vase on the table.
:::

Amina is the subject because she performs the action.

::: examples
- no: Vasen står på bordet.
- en: The vase is standing on the table.
:::

The vase is the subject because its position is being described.

The same pattern applies to people:

::: examples
- no: Marius setter Nora ved døra.
- en: Marius seats Nora by the door.
- no: Nora sitter ved døra.
- en: Nora is sitting by the door.
:::

Before writing these taught constructions, decide what the sentence is mainly about. If it is about the person seating someone or placing one of the demonstrated upright or base-resting objects, that person is normally the subject of **sette**. If it is about someone or something’s current seated or upright position, that person or object is the subject of **sitte** or **stå**.

Imagine a small café opening for the morning. Write a connected paragraph describing an employee placing cups and a vase on tables, the resulting positions of those objects, one customer standing near the counter, and two customers seated by a window.

This activity transfers the same verb choices from the meeting room to a fresh but similar setting. A successful paragraph would use **sette** only for placing actions, **sitte** for seated customers, and **stå** for the standing customer and upright objects.
