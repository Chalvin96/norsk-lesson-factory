## A missed meeting {#a-missed-meeting role=orient objectives=obj-indirect-speech-01}

Sara arrives at the office after a delayed bus. She has missed the morning meeting, and Jonas tells her what the others said.

**Sara**

::: {.reading translation="I didn’t make it to the meeting. What did Liv say?" dialogue_id="office" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Jeg rakk ikke møtet. Hva sa Liv?
:::

**Jonas**

::: {.reading translation="She said that the train to Bergen was cancelled, so she was working from home." dialogue_id="office" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hun sa at toget til Bergen var innstilt, så hun jobbet hjemmefra.
:::

**Sara**

::: {.reading translation="Did she ask whether we could move the presentation?" dialogue_id="office" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Spurte hun om vi kunne flytte presentasjonen?
:::

**Jonas**

::: {.reading translation="Yes. She asked whether we could do it tomorrow." dialogue_id="office" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. Hun spurte om vi kunne ta den i morgen.
:::

**Jonas**

::: {.reading translation="She also asked me to send the clients a message today." dialogue_id="office" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hun ba meg også om å sende kundene en melding i dag.
:::

**Sara**

::: {.reading translation="What did Amir say?" dialogue_id="office" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Hva sa Amir?
:::

**Jonas**

::: {.reading translation="He said that he had already spoken to them." dialogue_id="office" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Han sa at han allerede hadde snakket med dem.
:::

**Jonas**

::: {.reading translation="He also asked when Liv was coming back to the office." dialogue_id="office" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Han spurte også når Liv kom tilbake til kontoret.
:::

**Sara**

::: {.reading translation="She was going to come back today, wasn’t she?" dialogue_id="office" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Hun skulle komme tilbake i dag, ikke sant?
:::

**Jonas**

::: {.reading translation="Yes. When she called yesterday, she said that she would come back here today." dialogue_id="office" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. Da hun ringte i går, sa hun at hun skulle komme tilbake hit i dag.
:::

**Sara**

::: {.reading translation="Can you ask her to call me when she arrives?" dialogue_id="office" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Kan du be henne om å ringe meg når hun er framme?
:::

**Jonas**

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I’ll say that you want her to call you." dialogue_id="office" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, jeg skal si at du vil at hun skal ringe deg.
:::

Sara needs more than Liv’s exact words. She needs to know who said what, whether something was a statement, a question, or a request, and whether the message still applies now. Jonas therefore uses **indirect speech (indirekte tale)**: he reports the content of someone’s words without repeating the original conversation exactly.

Notice the three main patterns in the scene:

::: examples
- no: Hun sa at toget var innstilt.
- en: She said that the train was cancelled.
- no: Hun spurte om vi kunne flytte presentasjonen.
- en: She asked whether we could move the presentation.
- no: Hun ba meg om å sende kundene en melding.
- en: She asked me to send the clients a message.
:::

The type of original message determines how the report begins, and the resulting subordinate clause structure determines word order. The reporting viewpoint guides tense, pronouns, and expressions such as i dag and her.

**Request: anchor-message-map**

This activity checks whether the learner can connect each report in the office scene to its communicative function. The learner should identify who originally spoke, whether the reported content was a statement, a question, or a command or request, and, only when the dialogue states it explicitly, who received the message. It belongs here because understanding the message relationship must come before changing its grammar. A successful response demonstrates that the learner can recognize what kind of report must be formed without inferring an unstated recipient.

{{exercise: anchor-message-map}}

## Reporting statements with at {#reporting-statements-with-at role=model objectives=obj-indirect-speech-01}

A reported statement normally uses a reporting verb followed by **at**, meaning that.

::: examples
- no: Liv sier at toget er innstilt.
- en: Liv says that the train is cancelled.
- no: Liv sa at toget var innstilt.
- en: Liv said that the train was cancelled.
- no: Amir forklarte at kundene allerede visste om forsinkelsen.
- en: Amir explained that the clients already knew about the delay.
- no: Sara fortalte at hun ikke rakk møtet.
- en: Sara said that she didn’t make it to the meeting.
:::

Common reporting verbs include si, fortelle, forklare and svare. Their grammar is not identical.

::: examples
- no: Liv sa at hun jobbet hjemmefra.
- en: Liv said that she was working from home.
- no: Liv fortalte Jonas at hun jobbet hjemmefra.
- en: Liv told Jonas that she was working from home.
:::

With si, the listener is commonly introduced with til.

::: examples
- no: Liv sa til Jonas at hun jobbet hjemmefra.
- en: Liv said to Jonas that she was working from home.
:::

With fortelle, the listener can follow the verb directly.

::: examples
- no: Liv fortalte Jonas at hun jobbet hjemmefra.
- en: Liv told Jonas that she was working from home.
:::

The content after at is a **subordinate clause (leddsetning)**. It uses **subordinate word order (leddsetningsordstilling)**. In particular, sentence adverbs such as ikke, aldri and allerede normally come before the finite verb.

Compare the direct statement and the report:

**Original statement**

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobber ikke på kontoret i dag.
- en: I’m not working at the office today.
:::

**Reported statement**

::: examples
- no: Liv sa at hun ikke jobbet på kontoret den dagen.
- en: Liv said that she was not working at the office that day.
:::

The key contrast is:

::: examples
- no: Hun jobbet ikke på kontoret.
- en: She was not working at the office.
- no: Han sa at hun ikke jobbet på kontoret.
- en: He said that she was not working at the office.
:::

In the independent sentence, ikke follows the finite verb jobbet. In the subordinate clause after at, ikke comes before jobbet.

The same pattern applies to other sentence adverbs:

::: examples
- no: Amir hadde allerede ringt kundene.
- en: Amir had already called the clients.
- no: Jonas sa at Amir allerede hadde ringt kundene.
- en: Jonas said that Amir had already called the clients.
- no: Liv kommer kanskje senere.
- en: Liv may come later.
- no: Sara sa at Liv kanskje kom senere.
- en: Sara said that Liv might come later.
:::

For the neutral reported-statement pattern taught here, use subordinate word order after at:

::: examples
- no: Jonas sa at Liv ikke jobbet hjemme.
- en: Jonas said that Liv was not working at home.
:::

A compact rule for this lesson’s at-report patterns is: place ikke and similar sentence adverbs before the finite verb.

**Request: statement-word-order**

This controlled activity focuses only on subordinate word order in reported statements. It uses short updates from Liv, Amir, and Sara, including statements with ikke, allerede and kanskje. The learner should turn each statement into a report beginning with an appropriate reporting clause. Tense and perspective changes may appear as context, but the primary scored evidence is placement of the sentence adverb before the finite verb. A successful response demonstrates natural word order after at.

## Reporting yes-or-no questions with om {#reporting-yes-or-no-questions-with-om role=contrast objectives=obj-indirect-speech-01}

A yes-or-no question has no question word such as hva, hvor or når. In indirect speech, it normally begins with **om**, meaning whether or if.

**Original question**

::: examples
- no: Kan vi flytte presentasjonen?
- en: Can we move the presentation?
:::

**Reported question**

::: examples
- no: Liv spurte om vi kunne flytte presentasjonen.
- en: Liv asked whether we could move the presentation.
:::

The original question uses verb–subject order:

::: examples
- no: Kommer Liv tilbake i dag?
- en: Is Liv coming back today?
:::

The reported question is a subordinate clause. It therefore uses subject–verb order:

::: examples
- no: Amir spurte om Liv kom tilbake den dagen.
- en: Amir asked whether Liv was coming back that day.
:::

Compare the structures:

::: examples
- no: Har Amir ringt kundene?
- en: Has Amir called the clients?
- no: Sara spurte om Amir hadde ringt kundene.
- en: Sara asked whether Amir had called the clients.
- no: Jobber ikke Liv hjemme i dag?
- en: Isn’t Liv working from home today?
- no: Sara spurte om Liv ikke jobbet hjemme den dagen.
- en: Sara asked whether Liv was not working from home that day.
:::

The report is not written as a direct question. It has no question mark when the whole sentence is a statement, and it does not retain question inversion.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Sara spurte om hadde Amir ringt kundene.
- en: 'Incorrect: Sara asked whether had Amir called the clients.'
:::

Natural Bokmål:

::: examples
- no: Sara spurte om Amir hadde ringt kundene.
- en: Sara asked whether Amir had called the clients.
:::

The compact rule is: use om for a reported yes-or-no question, then use subordinate word order.

**Request: yes-no-question-report**

This activity isolates the form of reported yes-or-no questions. In the office context, colleagues have asked about the train, the presentation, and the clients. The learner should report those questions using om and remove direct-question inversion. The primary scored evidence is correct use of om followed by subject–verb subordinate order. A successful response demonstrates that the learner can distinguish a reported question from both a direct question and an at-clause.

{{exercise: yes-no-question-report}}

## Reporting questions with a question word {#reporting-questions-with-a-question-word role=contrast objectives=obj-indirect-speech-01}

When the original question already contains a question word, keep that word. In the basic indirect-question pattern, do not add om before it. If the question word is not the subject, the reported question uses ordinary subordinate word order.

**Original question**

::: examples
- no: Når kommer Liv tilbake?
- en: When is Liv coming back?
:::

**Reported question**

::: examples
- no: Amir spurte når Liv kom tilbake.
- en: Amir asked when Liv was coming back.
:::

Here, Liv is the subject and når refers to time. The original question uses inversion, but the reported question uses subject–verb subordinate order.

::: examples
- no: Hvor jobber Liv i dag?
- en: Where is Liv working today?
- no: Sara spurte hvor Liv jobbet den dagen.
- en: Sara asked where Liv was working that day.
:::

When hvem or hva is itself the subject of the reported question, add som after the question word.

::: examples
- no: Hvem kommer tilbake i morgen?
- en: Who is coming back tomorrow?
- no: Sara spurte hvem som kom tilbake dagen etter.
- en: Sara asked who was coming back the following day.
- no: Hva skjedde på møtet?
- en: What happened at the meeting?
- no: Liv spurte hva som skjedde på møtet.
- en: Liv asked what happened at the meeting.
:::

Do not add som when another word is the subject.

::: examples
- no: Hva kjøpte Liv?
- en: What did Liv buy?
- no: Sara spurte hva Liv kjøpte.
- en: Sara asked what Liv bought.
:::

::: examples
- no: Hvorfor har toget blitt innstilt?
- en: Why has the train been cancelled?
- no: Jonas spurte hvorfor toget hadde blitt innstilt.
- en: Jonas asked why the train had been cancelled.
:::

With ikke, subordinate order remains visible:

::: examples
- no: Hvorfor kommer hun ikke til kontoret?
- en: Why isn’t she coming to the office?
- no: Sara spurte hvorfor hun ikke kom til kontoret.
- en: Sara asked why she wasn’t coming to the office.
:::

A common mistake is to keep the original inversion.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Amir spurte når kom Liv tilbake.
- en: 'Incorrect: Amir asked when did Liv come back.'
:::

Natural Bokmål:

::: examples
- no: Amir spurte når Liv kom tilbake.
- en: Amir asked when Liv was coming back.
:::

Om before a question word can instead be read as the preposition selected by spørre, but that reading is less direct and outside the basic indirect-question pattern taught here:

::: examples
- no: Sara spurte om hvorfor Liv jobbet hjemme.
- en: Sara asked about why Liv was working from home.
:::

Preferred basic indirect-question form:

::: examples
- no: Sara spurte hvorfor Liv jobbet hjemme.
- en: Sara asked why Liv was working from home.
:::

As an indirect-question marker in this lesson’s target pattern, use om only when the original question has no question word.

**Request: question-word-order**

This activity checks reported questions beginning with hvem, hva, hvor, hvorfor, hvordan or når. The learner should preserve the relevant question word, decide whether hvem or hva is the clause subject, and use som when it is. Otherwise, the learner should change the question to subordinate word order without adding som. The primary scored evidence is the correct choice between subject-question hvem/hva + som and non-subject question-word order. A successful response demonstrates that the learner can report an information question without adding om.

{{exercise: question-word-order}}

## Reporting commands and requests {#reporting-commands-and-requests role=model objectives=obj-indirect-speech-01}

Commands and requests are often reported with be noen om å followed by an infinitive. The person after be is the person expected to perform the action.

**Original request from Liv to Jonas**

::: examples
- no: Send kundene en melding.
- en: Send the clients a message.
:::

**Jonas’s report**

::: examples
- no: Liv ba meg om å sende kundene en melding.
- en: Liv asked me to send the clients a message.
:::

The original message addresses Jonas as du, but Jonas reports his own role with meg.

::: examples
- no: Vent på meg utenfor stasjonen.
- en: Wait for me outside the station.
- no: Liv ba Jonas om å vente på henne utenfor stasjonen.
- en: Liv asked Jonas to wait for her outside the station.
:::

For a negative command in the be noen om å construction, this lesson uses the pattern om ikke å + infinitive:

::: examples
- no: Ikke ring kundene ennå.
- en: Don’t call the clients yet.
- no: Amir ba meg om ikke å ringe kundene ennå.
- en: Amir asked me not to call the clients yet.
:::

A stronger instruction can also be reported with si at someone must or should do something.

::: examples
- no: Du må sende meldingen før lunsj.
- en: You must send the message before lunch.
- no: Liv sa at jeg måtte sende meldingen før lunsj.
- en: Liv said that I had to send the message before lunch.
:::

These patterns present the same instruction from slightly different angles:

::: examples
- no: Liv ba meg om å sende meldingen.
- en: Liv asked me to send the message.
- no: Liv sa at jeg måtte sende meldingen.
- en: Liv said that I had to send the message.
:::

The first reports the request through be om å. The second reports the obligation as a statement with måtte.

Do not normally report a command by placing its imperative form after at.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Liv sa at send kundene en melding.
- en: 'Incorrect: Liv said that send the clients a message.'
:::

Natural alternatives:

::: examples
- no: Liv ba meg om å sende kundene en melding.
- en: Liv asked me to send the clients a message.
- no: Liv sa at jeg måtte sende kundene en melding.
- en: Liv said that I had to send the clients a message.
:::

**Request: command-recipient-action**

This activity focuses on who must perform the reported action. It uses instructions exchanged among Liv, Jonas, Sara, and Amir. The learner should report each instruction with be noen om å and choose the correct person form after be. Negative instructions should retain negation in a natural position. The primary scored evidence is a correct be + person + om å + infinitive structure. A successful response demonstrates that the learner can preserve both the requested action and its intended performer.

{{exercise: command-recipient-action}}

## Choosing tense in a report {#choosing-tense-in-a-report role=contrast objectives=obj-indirect-speech-01}

Norwegian does not require an automatic tense change in every report. The tense depends on the reporting time, whether the situation is still current, and how the speaker views it.

If the reporting verb is present and the information is still current, the original present tense often remains present.

::: examples
- no: Liv sier at hun er på toget.
- en: Liv says that she is on the train.
- no: Jonas sier at presentasjonen begynner klokka ett.
- en: Jonas says that the presentation starts at one.
:::

A past reporting verb often creates a past viewpoint.

::: examples
- no: Liv sa at hun var på toget.
- en: Liv said that she was on the train.
- no: Jonas sa at presentasjonen begynte klokka ett.
- en: Jonas said that the presentation started at one.
:::

However, present tense can remain when the reported information is still valid or is presented as a general fact.

::: examples
- no: Amir sa at kontoret stenger klokka fem.
- en: Amir said that the office closes at five.
- no: Læreren forklarte at norsk har to offisielle skriftformer.
- en: The teacher explained that Norwegian has two official written forms.
:::

The report does not mechanically turn every present tense into past tense. Compare:

::: examples
- no: Liv sa at hun var syk.
- en: Liv said that she was ill.
:::

This presents her illness from the earlier time.

::: examples
- no: Liv sa at hun er syk.
- en: Liv said that she is ill.
:::

This is possible when the speaker presents her illness as still true now.

An event completed before another past reporting point commonly uses the **past perfect (pluskvamperfektum)**: hadde plus the past participle.

**Original words from Amir**

::: examples
- no: Jeg har allerede snakket med kundene.
- en: I have already spoken to the clients.
:::

**Jonas’s later report**

::: examples
- no: Amir sa at han allerede hadde snakket med kundene.
- en: Amir said that he had already spoken to the clients.
:::

The conversation with the clients happened before Amir’s statement, and both are viewed from the past.

A future action viewed from a past reporting point often uses skulle.

**Original plan**

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal komme tilbake i morgen.
- en: I’m going to come back tomorrow.
:::

**Later report**

::: examples
- no: Liv sa at hun skulle komme tilbake dagen etter.
- en: Liv said that she was going to come back the following day.
:::

Use tense to preserve the time relationship, not to perform a blind word-for-word conversion.

**Request: report-time-viewpoint**

This activity presents short messages together with clear reporting times, such as reporting immediately, later the same day, or several days afterward. The learner should choose a tense that shows whether the information remains current, belongs to an earlier time, or was already completed before the report. The primary scored evidence is preservation of the intended time relationship. Pronoun and word-order changes provide context but are not the scored dimension. A successful response demonstrates a meaningful tense choice rather than automatic backshifting.

## Shifting pronouns, time, and place {#shifting-pronouns-time-and-place role=contrast objectives=obj-indirect-speech-01}

Indirect speech is told from the reporter’s point of view. Pronouns and references to time or place may therefore change.

Consider Liv speaking to Jonas. Liv is working from her home in Grünerløkka, while Jonas later reports from the office in Majorstuen, so the two speakers are in different places:

::: examples
- no: Jeg sender deg filen herfra i morgen.
- en: I’ll send you the file from here tomorrow.
:::

Jonas reports this to Sara two days later from the office:

::: examples
- no: Liv sa at hun skulle sende meg filen derfra dagen etter.
- en: Liv said that she would send me the file from there the following day.
:::

Several references have shifted:

- jeg becomes hun because Jonas is speaking about Liv.
- deg becomes meg because Jonas was the original listener.
- herfra becomes derfra because Jonas is now somewhere else.
- i morgen becomes dagen etter because the original tomorrow is now in the past.

Useful time shifts include:

::: examples
- no: Hun sa at hun ikke kunne komme den dagen.
- en: She said that she could not come that day.
- no: Han forklarte at han hadde ringt dagen før.
- en: He explained that he had called the day before.
- no: De sa at de skulle reise dagen etter.
- en: They said that they were going to leave the following day.
- no: Liv sa at hun måtte dra da.
- en: Liv said that she had to leave then.
:::

Useful place shifts include:

::: examples
- no: Hun sa at hun ventet der.
- en: She said that she was waiting there.
- no: Jonas fortalte at Liv skulle komme tilbake dit senere.
- en: Jonas said that Liv was going to return there later.
:::

These changes are not compulsory when the reporter still shares the original time or place. Jonas may report Liv’s morning message later on the same day:

::: examples
- no: Liv sa at hun kommer hit i dag.
- en: Liv said that she is coming here today.
:::

Here, i dag still means the same day, and hit still points to the office where Jonas is speaking.

The important question is not “Which words always change?” but “Do these words still point to the same person, day, and place from the reporter’s position?”

A common mistake is to preserve a pronoun even though its speaker has changed.

Original words from Liv:

::: examples
- no: Jeg ringer deg senere.
- en: I’ll call you later.
:::

Jonas reporting to Sara:

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Liv sa at jeg skulle ringe deg senere.
- en: 'Not: Liv said that I would call you later. (for the intended reported perspective)'
:::

Natural report:

::: examples
- no: Liv sa at hun skulle ringe meg senere.
- en: Liv said that she would call me later.
:::

The incorrect version makes Jonas, rather than Liv, the person who will call.

**Request: perspective-shift**

This activity uses messages sent between the four people in the office scene and asks the learner to report them from a new speaker’s position at a later time or different place. The learner should adjust personal pronouns and any time or place expressions whose reference has changed. The primary scored evidence is consistent perspective: every reference must still identify the intended person, day, and location. A successful response demonstrates that the learner can preserve meaning while changing viewpoint.

## Back at the office {#back-at-the-office role=model objectives=obj-indirect-speech-01}

Later, Sara phones Liv. She now has to pass on what Jonas told her.

**Liv**

::: {.reading translation="Did you find out what Jonas said about me?" dialogue_id="phone-relay" speaker_id="liv" speaker_name="Liv" character_id="liv"}
Fikk du vite hva Jonas sa om meg?
:::

**Sara**

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Jonas said that your train was cancelled and that you were working from home." dialogue_id="phone-relay" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Ja. Jonas sa at toget ditt var innstilt, og at du jobbet hjemmefra.
:::

**Sara**

::: {.reading translation="He also said that you had asked him to send the clients a message." dialogue_id="phone-relay" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Han fortalte også at du hadde bedt ham om å sende kundene en melding.
:::

**Liv**

::: {.reading translation="Did he say that Amir had already spoken to them?" dialogue_id="phone-relay" speaker_id="liv" speaker_name="Liv" character_id="liv"}
Sa han at Amir allerede hadde snakket med dem?
:::

**Sara**

::: {.reading translation="Yes, he said that Amir had already done so." dialogue_id="phone-relay" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Ja, han sa at Amir allerede hadde gjort det.
:::

**Liv**

::: {.reading translation="Great. Did Amir ask when I was coming back?" dialogue_id="phone-relay" speaker_id="liv" speaker_name="Liv" character_id="liv"}
Flott. Spurte Amir når jeg kom tilbake?
:::

**Sara**

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Jonas said that Amir had asked when you were coming back to the office." dialogue_id="phone-relay" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Ja. Jonas sa at Amir hadde spurt når du kom tilbake til kontoret.
:::

The same information has now passed through more than one person. Pronouns change again because Sara is speaking directly to Liv: Jonas’s hun becomes Sara’s du. The time relationships also become more layered: Amir asked first, Jonas reported his question later, and Sara is now reporting Jonas’s account.

Even in a longer chain, the core decisions remain the same:

- Use at to report a statement.
- Use om for a yes-or-no question.
- Keep the question word in an information question; use hvem or hva + som when the question word is the clause subject.
- Use subordinate word order inside the report.
- Report commands and requests with a structure such as be noen om å.
- Choose tense, pronouns, and time or place expressions from the current reporting viewpoint.

**Request: office-message-relay**

This activity returns to the original people and asks the learner to relay selected parts of the meeting story to a different participant. The learner should produce complete indirect reports rather than repeat the original dialogue. The primary scored evidence is preservation of message type and meaning across a changed speaker perspective. A successful response demonstrates coordinated use of the lesson’s reporting forms in the established context.

## Transfer: a delivery at the wrong address {#transfer-a-delivery-at-the-wrong-address role=contrast objectives=obj-indirect-speech-01}

Now move to a new everyday situation. Nora receives a message from a delivery driver while her neighbour Emil is away. The driver gives information, asks questions, and leaves an instruction. Later, Nora must tell Emil what happened.

The learner should report:

- what the driver said about the parcel;
- whether the driver asked a yes-or-no question;
- what the driver wanted to know;
- what the driver asked Nora or Emil to do;
- when and where the events were supposed to happen from Nora’s later viewpoint.

**Request: delivery-report-statements**

This activity asks the learner to report the driver’s factual statements about the parcel. The primary scored evidence is natural at-clause word order, especially with negation or a sentence adverb. Successful responses show that statements remain clear after being moved into indirect speech.

{{exercise: delivery-report-statements}}

**Request: delivery-report-questions**

This activity asks the learner to report the driver’s questions. The source material includes both yes-or-no questions and questions with a question word. The learner should use om as an indirect-question marker only for yes-or-no questions. In question-word reports, the learner should use hvem or hva + som when the question word is the clause subject and otherwise maintain subordinate word order without som. The primary scored evidence is correct indirect-question structure. Successful responses distinguish yes-or-no, subject question-word, and non-subject question-word forms without preserving direct-question inversion.

{{exercise: delivery-report-questions}}

**Request: delivery-report-instruction**

This activity asks the learner to report one instruction or request concerning the parcel. The learner should make clear who was asked to perform the action and use a natural infinitive construction. The primary scored evidence is accurate reporting of recipient and action through be noen om å. Successful responses preserve the practical responsibility assigned by the driver.

{{exercise: delivery-report-instruction}}

**Request: delivery-report-shifts**

This activity changes the reporting time and location: Nora speaks to Emil the following evening from another place. The learner should revise tense, pronouns, and time or place expressions where the new viewpoint requires it. The primary scored evidence is coherent reference across the changed situation. Successful responses make it unambiguous who did what, on which day, and at which place.

{{exercise: delivery-report-shifts}}

## Short retrieval review {#short-retrieval-review role=recap objectives=obj-indirect-speech-01}

Without looking back, explain when a report uses at, when it uses om, what happens after a non-subject question word, and when subject hvem or hva requires som. Then report one statement, one question, and one command from a recent imaginary conversation, changing pronouns and time or place expressions wherever the new viewpoint requires it.

**Request: final-indirect-speech-retrieval**

This final activity asks the learner to retrieve the reporting decisions without copying the chapter examples. The learner should produce a compact set of connected reports in one new situation. The primary scored evidence is integrated control of indirect speech: message type, subordinate word order, meaningful tense choice, and consistent perspective. A successful response communicates the original messages naturally and unambiguously from the reporter’s new point of view.

{{exercise: final-indirect-speech-retrieval}}
