## A delayed morning {#a-delayed-morning role=orient}

Mina and Jonas work in the same office. They are preparing for an important meeting, but Mina arrives late and wet from the rain.

::: {.reading translation='You’re soaked! What happened?' dialogue_id='delayed-morning-01' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Du er gjennomvåt! Hva skjedde?
:::

::: {.reading translation='The bus didn’t come because the road was closed.' dialogue_id='delayed-morning-02' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Bussen kom ikke fordi veien var stengt.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Oh no. How did you get here?' dialogue_id='delayed-morning-03' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Å nei. Hvordan kom du deg hit?
:::

::: {.reading translation='I waited for twenty minutes, but no bus came. Therefore, I took a taxi.' dialogue_id='delayed-morning-04' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Jeg ventet i tjue minutter, men det kom ingen buss. Derfor tok jeg taxi.
:::

::: {.reading translation='The meeting starts soon, so we have to go in now.' dialogue_id='delayed-morning-05' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Møtet begynner snart, så vi må gå inn nå.
:::

::: {.reading translation='I haven’t eaten breakfast because I was in a hurry.' dialogue_id='delayed-morning-06' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Jeg har ikke spist frokost fordi jeg hadde det travelt.
:::

::: {.reading translation='There are some bread rolls in the kitchen, so you can take one with you.' dialogue_id='delayed-morning-07' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Det ligger noen rundstykker på kjøkkenet, så du kan ta med deg ett.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Thanks! I’m very hungry; therefore, it’s difficult to concentrate.' dialogue_id='delayed-morning-08' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Takk! Jeg er veldig sulten. Derfor er det vanskelig å konsentrere seg.
:::

The conversation contains three ways of connecting a cause with a result:

- **fordi** introduces the cause: why something happens.
- **derfor** points to the result as a consequence.
- **så** connects a situation directly to its result.

The meaning is closely related, but the sentence structure is not the same.

## Choosing which part to emphasize {#choosing-which-part-to-emphasize role=model objectives=obj-fordi-derfor-sa-01}

Imagine two facts:

::: examples
- no: Veien var stengt. Bussen kom ikke.
- en: The road was closed. The bus didn’t come.
:::

You can connect them in three ways:

::: examples
- no: Bussen kom ikke fordi veien var stengt.
- en: The bus didn’t come because the road was closed.
- no: Veien var stengt. Derfor kom ikke bussen.
- en: The road was closed. Therefore, the bus didn’t come.
- no: Veien var stengt, så bussen kom ikke.
- en: The road was closed, so the bus didn’t come.
:::

All three versions describe the same basic relationship. The choice depends on how you want to organize the information.

In the postposed **fordi** pattern practiced in this lesson, you state a result and then give its cause:

**practiced pattern: result + fordi + cause**

With **derfor**, you first state the cause. You then begin a new main clause by pointing to its consequence:

**cause. Derfor + result**

With result-linking **så**, you state the cause and connect it directly to a following main clause:

**cause, så + result**

Choosing the right connector is only half the task. You must also place the verb correctly.

## Giving the reason with fordi {#giving-the-reason-with-fordi role=model objectives=obj-fordi-derfor-sa-01}

**Fordi** means because. It introduces a subordinate clause (leddsetning): a clause that functions as part of a larger sentence.

::: examples
- no: Mina tok taxi fordi bussen ikke kom.
- en: Mina took a taxi because the bus did not come.
:::

The main information is that Mina took a taxi. The clause beginning with **fordi** explains why.

::: examples
- no: Jeg blir hjemme fordi jeg er syk.
- en: I’m staying home because I’m ill.
- no: Vi lukket vinduet fordi det regnet.
- en: We closed the window because it was raining.
- no: Han kjøper mat på vei hjem fordi kjøleskapet er tomt.
- en: He is buying food on the way home because the refrigerator is empty.
:::

**Word order after fordi**

In a simple positive clause, the word order after **fordi** may look familiar:

::: examples
- no: fordi veien var stengt
- en: because the road was closed
- no: fordi jeg hadde det travelt
- en: because I was in a hurry
:::

The important difference becomes especially visible with a sentence adverb such as **ikke**. In a subordinate clause introduced by **fordi**, **ikke** normally comes before the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: Jeg tok taxi fordi bussen ikke kom.
- en: I took a taxi because the bus did not come.
- no: Vi spiste hjemme fordi restauranten ikke var åpen.
- en: We ate at home because the restaurant was not open.
- no: Sara ringte legen fordi hun ikke følte seg bedre.
- en: Sara called the doctor because she did not feel better.
:::

Compare a main clause and a fordi-clause:

::: examples
- no: Bussen kom ikke.
- en: The bus did not come.
- no: fordi bussen ikke kom
- en: because the bus did not come
:::

The compact pattern is:

**fordi + subject + ikke + finite verb**

A common mistake is to preserve main-clause order after **fordi**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg tok taxi fordi bussen kom ikke.
- en: I took a taxi because the bus did not come.
:::

This is not standard word order here. The corrected version is:

::: examples
- no: Jeg tok taxi fordi bussen ikke kom.
- en: I took a taxi because the bus did not come.
:::

Do not move every element mechanically. The key point is that **fordi** introduces a subordinate clause, and sentence adverbs such as **ikke** come before its finite verb.

**Request: Find-the-cause**

**Purpose:** Check whether the learner recognizes what **fordi** contributes before manipulating word order.

**Context:** Short everyday statements about transport, work, meals, weather, and appointments, each containing a cause and a result.

**Learner action:** Identify the information that answers why the event happened and indicate where a **fordi** clause should begin.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner distinguishes cause from result and chooses **fordi** when the missing part gives a reason. Word order is present as context but is not the primary scored evidence in this request.

{{exercise: find_the_cause}}

**Request: Fordi-word-order**

**Purpose:** Retrieve subordinate-clause word order at the point where **ikke** makes the pattern visible.

**Context:** People explain missed buses, closed shops, changed plans, and unfinished tasks.

**Learner action:** Complete or reformulate each reason with **fordi**, placing the subject, **ikke**, and finite verb correctly.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner produces the pattern **fordi + subject + ikke + finite verb** while preserving the intended cause.

{{exercise: fordi_word_order}}

## Pointing to the result with derfor {#pointing-to-the-result-with-derfor role=model objectives=obj-fordi-derfor-sa-01}

**Derfor** means therefore, for that reason, or that is why. It does not introduce a subordinate clause. It is usually placed at the beginning of a main clause and occupies the first position.

Norwegian main clauses follow the V2 rule: the finite verb comes in the second position. When **derfor** is first, the verb comes before the subject.

::: examples
- no: Veien var stengt. Derfor tok Mina taxi.
- en: The road was closed. Therefore, Mina took a taxi.
- no: Jeg sov dårlig. Derfor er jeg trøtt.
- en: I slept badly. Therefore, I’m tired.
- no: Hotellet var fullt. Derfor bestilte vi et annet rom.
- en: The hotel was full. Therefore, we booked another room.
:::

The pattern is:

**Derfor + finite verb + subject**

Compare an ordinary subject-first main clause:

::: examples
- no: Mina tok taxi.
- en: Mina took a taxi.
:::

When **derfor** takes the first position, the subject moves after the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: Derfor tok Mina taxi.
- en: Therefore, Mina took a taxi.
:::

With **ikke**, main-clause order remains in place: finite verb before **ikke**.

::: examples
- no: Alarmen ringte ikke. Derfor våknet jeg ikke.
- en: The alarm did not ring. Therefore, I did not wake up.
- no: Butikken var stengt. Derfor kunne vi ikke kjøpe mat.
- en: The shop was closed. Therefore, we could not buy food.
:::

A common mistake is to put both **derfor** and the subject before the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: Derfor Mina tok taxi.
- en: Therefore, Mina took a taxi.
:::

The corrected sentence has inversion:

::: examples
- no: Derfor tok Mina taxi.
- en: Therefore, Mina took a taxi.
:::

This is the same V2 pattern used when another element is placed first:

::: examples
- no: I dag tar Mina taxi.
- en: Today Mina is taking a taxi.
- no: Derfor tar Mina taxi.
- en: Therefore Mina is taking a taxi.
:::

**Request: Derfor-inversion**

**Purpose:** Isolate the verb-position decision after sentence-initial **derfor**.

**Context:** Brief cause statements followed by consequences involving daily routines, delays, purchases, and changes of plan.

**Learner action:** Add a result sentence beginning with **derfor** and place the finite verb before the subject.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner consistently produces **derfor + finite verb + subject**. Choosing among all three connectors is not scored yet; each prompt already calls for **derfor**.

{{exercise: derfor_inversion}}

## Connecting a cause and result with så {#connecting-a-cause-and-result-with-sa role=model objectives=obj-fordi-derfor-sa-01}

Result-linking **så** means so. It joins a cause to a result without turning the result into a subordinate clause.

::: examples
- no: Møtet begynner snart, så vi må gå inn.
- en: The meeting starts soon, so we have to go in.
- no: Jeg var sulten, så jeg lagde middag.
- en: I was hungry, so I made dinner.
- no: Toget var forsinket, så vi kom for sent.
- en: The train was delayed, so we arrived late.
:::

After this **så**, the result is commonly expressed with ordinary subject-first main-clause order:

**cause, så + subject + finite verb**

::: examples
- no: Det regnet, så vi tok bussen.
- en: It was raining, so we took the bus.
- no: Han fant ikke nøklene, så han kunne ikke låse døra.
- en: He could not find the keys, so he could not lock the door.
- no: Kaffen var kald, så jeg drakk den ikke.
- en: The coffee was cold, so I did not drink it.
:::

Notice the position of **ikke** in the result clause:

::: examples
- no: så han kunne ikke låse døra
- en: so he could not lock the door
- no: så jeg drakk den ikke
- en: so I did not drink it
:::

These are main clauses. The finite verb comes before **ikke**.

Compare that with a fordi-clause:

::: examples
- no: Han ringte naboen fordi han ikke kunne låse døra.
- en: He called the neighbor because he could not lock the door.
- no: Han fant ikke nøklene, så han kunne ikke låse døra.
- en: He could not find the keys, so he could not lock the door.
:::

After **fordi**, the sequence is **subject + ikke + finite verb**. After result-linking **så**, the sequence is normally **subject + finite verb + ikke**.

A likely mistake is to treat **så** like sentence-initial **derfor** and invert the result clause:

::: examples
- no: Det regnet, så tok vi bussen.
- en: It was raining, so we took the bus.
:::

For the cause-and-result meaning taught here, use:

::: examples
- no: Det regnet, så vi tok bussen.
- en: It was raining, so we took the bus.
:::

Norwegian **så** can have other functions in other contexts, but here it is the connector meaning so. Keep the cause before it and use a complete main clause after it.

**Request: Så-result-order**

**Purpose:** Establish the subject-first result pattern after result-linking **så** and prevent confusion with **derfor**.

**Context:** One event creates an immediate practical consequence: rain changes transport, a closure changes a shopping plan, or a delay changes an appointment.

**Learner action:** Join each cause to its stated result with **så**, retaining subject–verb order in the result clause.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner produces **cause, så + subject + finite verb**, including correct placement of **ikke** when present.

## Three ways to describe the same relationship {#three-ways-to-describe-the-same-relationship role=contrast objectives=obj-fordi-derfor-sa-01}

The three connectors can often describe the same situation, but they organize it differently.

**Starting with the result**

::: examples
- no: Vi ble hjemme fordi det regnet.
- en: We stayed home because it was raining.
:::

The listener first hears what happened: we stayed home. The **fordi** clause then supplies the explanation.

**Starting with the cause and emphasizing its consequence**

::: examples
- no: Det regnet. Derfor ble vi hjemme.
- en: It was raining. Therefore, we stayed home.
:::

The cause is established first. **Derfor** points back to it and presents the next statement as a consequence. Because **derfor** is first, the verb **ble** comes before the subject **vi**.

**Joining the two events directly**

::: examples
- no: Det regnet, så vi ble hjemme.
- en: It was raining, so we stayed home.
:::

The result follows naturally from the first statement. After result-linking **så**, the subject **vi** comes before the verb **ble**.

Here is another aligned set:

::: examples
- no: Nora dro tidlig fordi hun ikke følte seg bra.
- en: Nora left early because she did not feel well.
- no: Nora følte seg ikke bra. Derfor dro hun tidlig.
- en: Nora did not feel well. Therefore, she left early.
- no: Nora følte seg ikke bra, så hun dro tidlig.
- en: Nora did not feel well, so she left early.
:::

The connector changes the direction in which the information is presented:

- In the postposed pattern practiced here, **fordi** presents the result before its cause.
- **derfor** looks back at a cause and introduces its result.
- **så** moves directly from a cause to its result.

**Request: Connector-meaning-choice**

**Purpose:** Test the central meaning choice among **fordi**, **derfor**, and **så** after all three have been explained.

**Context:** Paired cause-and-result facts are presented in different orders. Some sentences begin with the result and ask why; others begin with the cause and continue toward a consequence.

**Learner action:** Choose the connector that fits the information order and intended relationship.

**Success demonstrates:** For the postposed **fordi** pattern practiced here, the learner chooses **fordi** when a following clause gives the cause; the learner chooses **derfor** for a new result clause beginning with the consequence marker and **så** for a directly connected result. Clause-initial **fordi** is outside this request, not treated as invalid. Verb placement appears in completed sentences but is not the primary scored dimension here.

{{exercise: connector_meaning_choice}}

## Connector and word order must work together {#connector-and-word-order-must-work-together role=contrast objectives=obj-fordi-derfor-sa-01}

It is not enough to choose a connector by translating an English word. Each connector creates or signals a particular structure.

Compare these three patterns with **ikke**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg gikk hjem fordi jeg ikke følte meg bra.
- en: I went home because I did not feel well.
- no: Jeg følte meg ikke bra. Derfor gikk jeg hjem.
- en: I did not feel well. Therefore, I went home.
- no: Jeg følte meg ikke bra, så jeg gikk hjem.
- en: I did not feel well, so I went home.
:::

The key sequences are:

- **fordi + subject + ikke + finite verb**
- **derfor + finite verb + subject**
- **så + subject + finite verb + ikke**, when **ikke** belongs in the result clause

Now compare sentences without **ikke**:

::: examples
- no: Vi avlyste turen fordi været var dårlig.
- en: We canceled the trip because the weather was bad.
- no: Været var dårlig. Derfor avlyste vi turen.
- en: The weather was bad. Therefore, we canceled the trip.
- no: Været var dårlig, så vi avlyste turen.
- en: The weather was bad, so we canceled the trip.
:::

Without **ikke**, the difference between main-clause and subordinate-clause order may be less obvious. The **derfor** sentence still clearly shows V2 inversion: **derfor avlyste vi**, not **derfor vi avlyste**.

**Request: Connector-and-verb-position**

**Purpose:** Combine meaning choice with the corresponding word-order pattern.

**Context:** Short messages explain absence, lateness, canceled plans, and practical decisions. Each item establishes whether the missing section is a cause or a result.

**Learner action:** Supply an appropriate connector and complete the clause with the finite verb, subject, and any occurrence of **ikke** in the correct positions.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner coordinates both decisions: connector meaning and verb placement. This request scores the combined approved outcome rather than connector choice or word order in isolation.

## Returning to Mina and Jonas {#returning-to-mina-and-jonas role=model objectives=obj-fordi-derfor-sa-01}

Mina has eaten a bread roll, and the meeting has ended.

::: {.reading translation='It went well!' dialogue_id='mina-jonas-follow-up-01' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Det gikk bra!
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, but I was nervous because I had not read the latest report.' dialogue_id='mina-jonas-follow-up-02' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja, men jeg var nervøs fordi jeg ikke hadde lest den siste rapporten.
:::

::: {.reading translation='You explained the project clearly, so everyone understood the plan.' dialogue_id='mina-jonas-follow-up-03' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Du forklarte prosjektet tydelig, så alle forsto planen.
:::

::: {.reading translation='The client asked many questions. Therefore, the meeting lasted longer than planned.' dialogue_id='mina-jonas-follow-up-04' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Kunden stilte mange spørsmål. Derfor varte møtet lenger enn planlagt.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Now I’m hungry because I didn’t take a bread roll.' dialogue_id='mina-jonas-follow-up-05' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nå er jeg sulten fordi jeg ikke tok noe rundstykke.
:::

::: {.reading translation='The café next door is still open, so we can eat lunch there.' dialogue_id='mina-jonas-follow-up-06' speaker_id='mina' speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Kafeen ved siden av er fortsatt åpen, så vi kan spise lunsj der.
:::

The same forms now help Mina and Jonas explain feelings, evaluate the meeting, and make a new plan.

In Mina’s first sentence, **fordi** introduces the reason for her nervousness:

::: examples
- no: fordi jeg ikke hadde lest den siste rapporten
- en: because I had not read the latest report
:::

In Jonas’s answer, **så** connects Mina’s clear explanation to its result:

::: examples
- no: så alle forsto planen
- en: so everyone understood the plan
:::

In Mina’s next statement, **derfor** begins a result sentence and triggers inversion:

::: examples
- no: Derfor varte møtet lenger.
- en: Therefore, the meeting lasted longer.
:::

**Request: Dialogue-reconstruction**

**Purpose:** Confirm that the target forms remain usable in connected discourse rather than isolated sentence pairs.

**Context:** A shortened follow-up exchange between Mina and Jonas after the meeting, with causes and results preserved but selected connectors and clause structures omitted.

**Learner action:** Restore the missing cause-and-result links, using the surrounding turns to decide whether each speaker is giving a reason, pointing to a consequence, or directly connecting a result.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner chooses the appropriate target connector and maintains its associated verb position across a coherent conversation.

## Transfer to a new situation {#transfer-to-a-new-situation role=recap objectives=obj-fordi-derfor-sa-01}

Emil is hosting dinner for two friends. The supermarket closes earlier than he expected. He cannot buy one ingredient, changes the recipe, and serves dinner later than planned.

This new situation supports all three information patterns:

::: examples
- no: Emil endret oppskriften fordi butikken ikke var åpen.
- en: Emil changed the recipe because the shop was not open.
- no: Butikken var ikke åpen. Derfor endret Emil oppskriften.
- en: The shop was not open. Therefore, Emil changed the recipe.
- no: Butikken var ikke åpen, så Emil endret oppskriften.
- en: The shop was not open, so Emil changed the recipe.
:::

A later event may create another result:

::: examples
- no: Middagen var ikke ferdig, så gjestene måtte vente.
- en: Dinner was not ready, so the guests had to wait.
:::

The same fact can instead be presented as the reason:

::: examples
- no: Gjestene måtte vente fordi middagen ikke var ferdig.
- en: The guests had to wait because dinner was not ready.
:::

Or the consequence can be highlighted in a separate sentence:

::: examples
- no: Middagen var ikke ferdig. Derfor måtte gjestene vente.
- en: Dinner was not ready. Therefore, the guests had to wait.
:::

**Request: Dinner-plan-transfer**

**Purpose:** Transfer the complete target choice to a fresh everyday context.

**Context:** A connected account of Emil’s shopping problem, changed recipe, delayed dinner, and guests’ reactions.

**Learner action:** Complete a sequence of cause-and-result statements, choosing **fordi**, **derfor**, or **så** according to information order and placing verbs correctly.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner applies the approved outcome independently in a changed context: **fordi** with subordinate-clause order, sentence-initial **derfor** with V2 inversion, and result-linking **så** with a subject-first main clause.

## Quick retrieval and review {#quick-retrieval-and-review role=recap objectives=obj-fordi-derfor-sa-01}

Without looking back, recall the three core patterns. Then reformulate one everyday cause-and-result pair in three ways: first with **fordi**, then with sentence-initial **derfor**, and finally with result-linking **så**.

**Request: Final-three-form-retrieval**

**Purpose:** Strengthen retrieval of both connector choice and verb position after a delay.

**Context:** One familiar everyday problem, such as bad weather changing a plan or a transport delay causing late arrival.

**Learner action:** Express the same relationship three times, using the postposed **fordi** pattern practiced here, then **derfor**, and then **så**.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner can retrieve the three practiced target structures and preserve the correct verb position while the communicative relationship remains constant.
