## A first day at the community workshop {#first-day-community-workshop role=orient}

Lea has volunteered to help at a community repair workshop. She knows where she is supposed to work, but she does not know the people or equipment yet. Amir shows her around.

::: {.reading translation="Who is the man who is talking to Nora?" dialogue_id="first-day-workshop" speaker_id="lea" speaker_name="Lea" character_id="lea"}
Hvem er mannen som snakker med Nora?
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s Erik. He’s the one who has the key to the tool room." dialogue_id="first-day-workshop" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Det er Erik. Det er han som har nøkkelen til verktøyrommet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I need a charger that fits this computer." dialogue_id="first-day-workshop" speaker_id="lea" speaker_name="Lea" character_id="lea"}
Jeg trenger en lader som passer til denne PC-en.
:::

::: {.reading translation="There is a charger in the cupboard that stands by the window." dialogue_id="first-day-workshop" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Det ligger en lader i skapet som står ved vinduet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Is it the cupboard that can’t be locked?" dialogue_id="first-day-workshop" speaker_id="lea" speaker_name="Lea" character_id="lea"}
Er det skapet som ikke kan låses?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. And the woman whom you met at the entrance can help you. Her name is Sara." dialogue_id="first-day-workshop" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja. Og kvinnen som du møtte ved inngangen, kan hjelpe deg. Hun heter Sara.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. I’ve also brought a lamp that I want to repair." dialogue_id="first-day-workshop" speaker_id="lea" speaker_name="Lea" character_id="lea"}
Flott. Jeg har også med en lampe som jeg vil reparere.
:::

Lea needs to identify particular people and things. Instead of producing many short sentences, she uses som to connect each person or thing to useful extra information:

::: examples
- no: mannen som snakker med Nora
- en: the man who is talking to Nora
- no: en lader som passer til denne PC-en
- en: a charger that fits this computer
- no: kvinnen som du møtte
- en: the woman whom you met
- no: en lampe som jeg vil reparere
- en: a lamp that I want to repair
:::

The information after som tells Amir exactly which man, charger, woman, or lamp Lea means.

**Activity request: notice-som-connections**

The learner should revisit the workshop dialogue and identify the person or thing described by each som-clause. The activity belongs here because the learner first needs to notice what som achieves in a real exchange before building new sentences. Success would demonstrate recognition that, in the simple patterns practiced here, the clause after som adds identifying information about the noun phrase it follows. This activity scores understanding of the connection created by som, not word-order production.

{{exercise: notice_som_connections}}

## Adding information with a relative clause {#adding-information-relative-clause role=model objectives=obj-relative-clauses-01}

A clause beginning with som can describe a person or thing already mentioned. This is called a **relative clause (relativsetning)**.

Compare two separate sentences with one connected sentence:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kjenner en mann. Mannen reparerer sykler.
- en: I know a man. The man repairs bicycles.
- no: Jeg kjenner en mann som reparerer sykler.
- en: I know a man who repairs bicycles.
:::

The noun en mann introduces the person. The relative clause som reparerer sykler adds information about him.

The same structure works for things:

::: examples
- no: Vi har et skap. Skapet står ved vinduet.
- en: We have a cupboard. The cupboard stands by the window.
- no: Vi har et skap som står ved vinduet.
- en: We have a cupboard that stands by the window.
:::

In English, the connecting word may be who, that, or which. Norwegian normally uses som for both people and things:

::: examples
- no: en frivillig som hjelper nye deltakere
- en: a volunteer who helps new participants
- no: et verktøy som er lett å bruke
- en: a tool that is easy to use
- no: maskiner som lager mye støy
- en: machines that make a lot of noise
:::

Som does not change according to gender or number. It remains som after en-words, et-words, and plural nouns.

::: examples
- no: en lampe som virker
- en: a lamp that works
- no: et lys som virker
- en: a light that works
- no: to lamper som virker
- en: two lamps that work
:::

A compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
In the simple patterns practiced here, keep the som-clause next to the noun phrase being described, then complete the added clause.
:::

## When the person or thing performs the action {#relative-clause-subject-action role=model objectives=obj-relative-clauses-01}

In several lines from the dialogue, the noun before som performs the action in the relative clause:

::: examples
- no: mannen som snakker med Nora
- en: the man who is talking to Nora
:::

The man is talking, so the pattern is:

**noun + som + verb**

::: examples
- no: Det er Sara som leder kurset.
- en: Sara is the one who leads the course.
- no: Jeg trenger en kollega som kan hjelpe meg.
- en: I need a colleague who can help me.
- no: Vi bruker et program som fungerer på gamle PC-er.
- en: We use a program that works on old computers.
- no: Hun har sko som tåler mye regn.
- en: She has shoes that withstand a lot of rain.
:::

The finite verb expresses the intended tense or modal form but does not change for person or number, and som remains unchanged.

::: examples
- no: Jeg kjenner en student som bor i Bergen.
- en: I know a student who lives in Bergen.
- no: Jeg kjenner to studenter som bor i Bergen.
- en: I know two students who live in Bergen.
:::

Do not add another subject pronoun when som already represents the person or thing performing the action.

Incorrect for the intended meaning:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kjenner en mann som han jobber her.
- en: 'Incorrect: I know a man who he works here.'
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kjenner en mann som jobber her.
- en: I know a man who works here.
:::

The word han is unnecessary because som already connects en mann to jobber.

**Activity request: build-subject-relative-clauses**

The learner should combine pairs of short statements about people and things into single sentences with som. In each pair, the repeated person or thing performs the action in the second statement. The workshop provides the initial context, using volunteers, tools, and equipment; later items should use familiar everyday settings. Success would demonstrate production of the pattern noun + som + verb without an extra subject pronoun. This activity scores formation of subject relative clauses.

{{exercise: build_subject_relative_clauses}}

**Activity request: repair-extra-subjects**

The learner should correct sentences in which a pronoun has been incorrectly placed between som and the verb. The activity belongs after initial construction practice because it targets a likely misconception made visible by the pattern just learned. Success would demonstrate that the learner understands when som already represents the person or thing performing the action. Only removal of unnecessary subjects is scored; vocabulary and subordinate-clause word order are supporting context.

{{exercise: repair_extra_subjects}}

## When another person performs the action {#relative-clause-object-action role=contrast objectives=obj-relative-clauses-01}

Now look at Sara in the dialogue:

::: examples
- no: kvinnen som du møtte ved inngangen
- en: the woman whom you met at the entrance
:::

Here, the woman does not perform the action. Du performs the action: you met the woman. Norwegian still uses som, but the acting subject comes after it:

**noun + som + subject + verb**

::: examples
- no: Jeg kjenner kvinnen som du møtte.
- en: I know the woman whom you met.
- no: Dette er boka som Amir anbefalte.
- en: This is the book that Amir recommended.
- no: Hvor er verktøyet som vi brukte i går?
- en: Where is the tool that we used yesterday?
- no: Hun fant lampen som jeg ville reparere.
- en: She found the lamp that I wanted to repair.
:::

Compare the two patterns carefully:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kjenner mannen som reparerer lampen.
- en: I know the man who repairs the lamp.
:::

The man repairs the lamp.

::: examples
- no: Jeg kjenner mannen som Lea møtte.
- en: I know the man whom Lea met.
:::

Lea met the man.

The words immediately after som help show who performs the action:

::: examples
- no: som reparerer
- en: the described person performs the action
- no: som Lea møtte
- en: Lea performs the action
:::

Do not repeat the described noun with a pronoun at the end of the clause.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Dette er boka som jeg kjøpte den.
- en: 'Incorrect: This is the book that I bought it.'
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Dette er boka som jeg kjøpte.
- en: This is the book that I bought.
:::

Boka is already connected to kjøpte by som. Adding den repeats the same object.

**Activity request: distinguish-relative-clause-actors**

The learner should interpret paired relative clauses and identify whether the described person or thing performs the action or receives the action. The pairs should differ only in the words following som so that meaning, rather than vocabulary, drives the decision. Success would demonstrate accurate interpretation of the two patterns. This activity scores recognition of who performs the action; it does not score sentence production.

{{exercise: distinguish_relative_clause_actors}}

**Activity request: build-object-relative-clauses**

The learner should combine two short statements when a named person or pronoun acts on the repeated person or thing. The learner must retain that subject after som while avoiding a repeated object pronoun. Success would demonstrate production of the pattern noun + som + subject + verb. This activity scores formation of object relative clauses.

{{exercise: build_object_relative_clauses}}

**Activity request: repair-repeated-objects**

The learner should revise sentences containing an unnecessary object pronoun after a som-clause. Everyday objects such as a phone, jacket, bicycle, or book should provide the context. Success would demonstrate that the noun before som already fills the relevant role inside the relative clause. This activity scores removal of repeated objects, not broader editing ability.

{{exercise: repair_repeated_objects}}

## Keeping the added information close to the noun phrase {#keeping-information-close role=contrast objectives=obj-relative-clauses-01}

In the simple patterns practiced here, keep a som-clause next to the noun phrase it describes. This helps the listener understand the intended connection.

::: examples
- no: Jeg snakket med læreren som underviser i norsk.
- en: I spoke with the teacher who teaches Norwegian.
- no: Vi fant nøkkelen som lå under stolen.
- en: We found the key that was under the chair.
:::

The relative clause describes læreren.

The relative clause describes nøkkelen.

Placement can affect meaning when a sentence contains several nouns. Return to Amir’s instruction:

::: examples
- no: Det ligger en lader i skapet som står ved vinduet.
- en: There is a charger in the cupboard that stands by the window.
:::

In this simple sentence, the som-clause is placed next to the noun phrase ending in skapet, and the context makes the cupboard the intended referent. The cupboard stands by the window.

If Amir wants to describe the charger instead, he should organize this simple sentence so that the som-clause is clearly connected to the noun phrase for the charger:

::: examples
- no: Laderen som passer til denne PC-en, ligger i skapet ved vinduet.
- en: The charger that fits this computer is in the cupboard by the window.
:::

Clear placement matters more than copying English word for word. First decide which person or thing needs extra information. In these simple patterns, keep the som-clause next to that noun phrase.

**Activity request: attach-clause-to-intended-noun**

The learner should place or rewrite a som-clause so that it clearly describes the intended noun phrase in simple sentences containing more than one person or thing. The workshop scene provides meaningful contrasts between chargers, cupboards, tools, and volunteers. Success would demonstrate clear attachment of the relative clause to the intended noun phrase. This activity scores clause placement; subject and object patterns serve only as context.

{{exercise: attach_clause_to_intended_noun}}

These som patterns assume subordinate-clause word order learned earlier; this lesson practices using som to add information about a person or thing.

**Activity request: complete-workshop-identifications**

The learner should complete short exchanges from the workshop by adding a suitable som-clause to identify a person or thing already present in the situation. Each prompt should make the intended actor clear. Success would demonstrate selection and production of either noun + som + verb or noun + som + subject + verb according to meaning. This activity scores accurate structural choice between the two som patterns.

{{exercise: complete_workshop_identifications}}

**Activity request: describe-workshop-people-and-things**

The learner should turn brief workshop notes into connected descriptions for a newly arrived volunteer. Each response should identify one person or thing by adding a clear som-clause. Success would demonstrate controlled use of som for practical identification without repeating nouns or pronouns unnecessarily. This activity scores complete relative-clause production in context.

{{exercise: describe_workshop_people_and_things}}

## Transfer to a new situation {#transfer-new-situation role=recap objectives=obj-relative-clauses-01}

Imagine that you are meeting a friend at a busy railway station. Several people are waiting, and several bags stand near the entrance. You need to explain whom and what to look for.

Useful models:

::: examples
- no: Jeg venter på en venn som kommer fra Trondheim.
- en: I’m waiting for a friend who is coming from Trondheim.
- no: Hun har på seg en jakke som er lett å kjenne igjen.
- en: She is wearing a jacket that is easy to recognize.
- no: Kofferten som hun har med seg, er blå.
- en: The suitcase that she has with her is blue.
- no: Mannen som står ved billettautomaten, er broren hennes.
- en: The man who is standing by the ticket machine is her brother.
- no: Toget som vi skal ta, går om tjue minutter.
- en: The train that we are going to take leaves in twenty minutes.
:::

**Activity request: transfer-som-to-station-scene**

The learner should create controlled descriptions from station-scene prompts, using som to identify specified people and things. Some described nouns should perform the action; others should be acted on by another person. Success would demonstrate transfer of both target patterns to a new everyday situation while keeping each relative clause clearly connected to the noun phrase it describes in these simple sentences. This activity scores contextual production of clear som-clauses.

{{exercise: transfer_som_to_station_scene}}

## Retrieval and review {#retrieval-and-review role=recap objectives=obj-relative-clauses-01}

Without returning to the explanations, the learner should briefly reconstruct the two core patterns, then use each pattern in a new sentence about a person or thing in a familiar place. One sentence should describe someone or something that performs an action; the other should describe someone or something that another person acts on.

**Activity request: retrieve-core-som-patterns**

The learner should recall and produce both som structures from memory in a fresh everyday context. Success would demonstrate that the learner can use som to add clear relative clauses about people and things, choose the structure according to who performs the action, and avoid unnecessary repeated pronouns.

{{exercise: retrieve_core_som_patterns}}
