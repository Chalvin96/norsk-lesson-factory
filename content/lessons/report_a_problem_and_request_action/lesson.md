## Reporting a Problem and Requesting Action {#reporting-a-problem role=orient objectives=obj-report-a-problem-and-request-action-01}

## A leak in Nora’s kitchen {#noras-kitchen role=model objectives=obj-report-a-problem-and-request-action-01}

Nora has recently moved into a rented flat. On Monday morning, she notices water inside the cupboard under the kitchen sink. She calls Erik, the building caretaker.

::: {.reading translation="Hi, Erik. Water is dripping from the pipe under the kitchen sink." dialogue_id=nora-kitchen-1 speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Hei, Erik. Det drypper fra røret under kjøkkenvasken.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Has much water come out?" dialogue_id=nora-kitchen-1 speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Har det kommet mye vann?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Not very much, but the bottom of the cupboard is getting wet, and I can’t store anything there." dialogue_id=nora-kitchen-1 speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ikke så mye, men bunnen i skapet blir våt, og jeg kan ikke oppbevare noe der.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I understand. Can you put a bucket under the pipe?" dialogue_id=nora-kitchen-1 speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Jeg forstår. Kan du sette en bøtte under røret?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I can. Can you come and look at it today?" dialogue_id=nora-kitchen-1 speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, det kan jeg. Kan du komme og se på det i dag?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. I’ll come between four and five." dialogue_id=nora-kitchen-1 speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Ja. Jeg kommer mellom klokka fire og fem.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. So you’ll come here between four and five, and I’ll put a bucket under the pipe until then?" dialogue_id=nora-kitchen-1 speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Fint. Så du kommer hit mellom klokka fire og fem, og jeg setter en bøtte under røret fram til da?
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s right. Call me again if more water starts to flow." dialogue_id=nora-kitchen-1 speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Det stemmer. Ring meg igjen hvis det begynner å renne mer.
:::

Nora handles the conversation calmly and efficiently. She says what she can observe, explains the practical effect, asks for one reasonable action, and checks what will happen next. These four parts form a useful pattern:

**observable fact → effect → requested remedy → confirmed next step**

You can use this pattern when speaking to a landlord, caretaker, hotel employee, shop worker, delivery company, or service provider.

## Start with what you can observe {#observable-facts role=contrast objectives=obj-report-a-problem-and-request-action-01}

When reporting a problem, begin with something specific that another person can understand or check. An observable fact describes a condition that you directly notice or that another person can check.

Nora says:

::: examples
- no: Det drypper fra røret under kjøkkenvasken.
- en: Water is dripping from the pipe under the kitchen sink.
:::

She does not begin by guessing who caused the leak or why it happened. This keeps the conversation focused on the problem.

Compare these descriptions:

::: examples
- no: Det ligger vann på gulvet.
- en: There is water on the floor.
- no: Jeg ser en sprekk i vinduet.
- en: I can see a crack in the window.
- no: Pakken er åpen på den ene siden.
- en: The parcel is open on one side.
- no: Lyset på badet virker ikke.
- en: The bathroom light does not work.
- no: Bussen kom ikke til holdeplassen.
- en: The bus did not come to the stop.
:::

Each sentence gives information that can be observed or checked.

A judgment or accusation is less useful:

::: examples
- no: Dette er elendig arbeid.
- en: This is terrible work.
:::

A factual description is clearer:

::: examples
- no: Døra går ikke helt igjen.
- en: The door does not close completely.
:::

Similarly:

::: examples
- no: Dere har ødelagt pakken min.
- en: You have damaged my parcel.
:::

may sound accusatory if you do not know what happened. A more precise opening is:

::: examples
- no: Pakken var skadet da jeg fikk den.
- en: The parcel was damaged when I received it.
:::

You do not need to hide a serious problem or sound uncertain about a fact. The aim is simply to separate what you know from what you assume.

**Checkpoint: Find the factual core**

Read Nora and Erik’s first exchange again. Identify the sentence that states the observable problem and the question Erik asks to establish its extent. Then write one short factual sentence for this situation: you arrive at a hotel room and see that the window cannot be closed.

This activity belongs here because a clear factual opening gives the other person enough information to respond. A successful response would describe the window itself without blaming hotel staff or guessing at the cause.

## Explain the effect {#explain-effect role=model objectives=obj-report-a-problem-and-request-action-01}

A problem report becomes more useful when you explain what the problem changes for you. The effect answers a practical question: Why does this need attention?

Nora gives two effects:

::: examples
- no: Bunnen i skapet blir våt.
- en: The bottom of the cupboard is getting wet.
- no: Jeg kan ikke oppbevare noe der.
- en: I can’t store anything there.
:::

Common ways to describe an effect include:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan ikke bruke …
- en: I can’t use …
- no: Det betyr at …
- en: That means that …
- no: Derfor må jeg …
- en: Therefore I have to …
- no: Nå blir …
- en: Now … is becoming …
:::

Here are some complete problem reports with a fact and an effect:

::: examples
- no: Varmeovnen virker ikke, så rommet er veldig kaldt.
- en: The heater does not work, so the room is very cold.
- no: Jeg fikk feil nøkkel, og derfor kommer jeg ikke inn i boden.
- en: I received the wrong key, and therefore I can’t get into the storage room.
- no: Det mangler én vare i leveringen, så jeg kan ikke fullføre bestillingen.
- en: One item is missing from the delivery, so I can’t complete the order.
- no: Vaskemaskinen stopper etter noen minutter. Det betyr at jeg ikke får vasket klærne.
- en: The washing machine stops after a few minutes. That means I can’t wash the clothes.
- no: Internettforbindelsen forsvinner flere ganger om dagen, så jeg kan ikke delta i nettmøtene mine.
- en: The internet connection drops several times a day, so I can’t participate in my online meetings.
:::

Keep the effect concrete. You usually do not need a long account of your frustration.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Dette er utrolig irriterende, og hele dagen min er ødelagt.
- en: This is incredibly annoying, and my whole day is ruined.
:::

with:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan ikke låse døra, så jeg kan ikke forlate leiligheten.
- en: I can’t lock the door, so I can’t leave the flat.
:::

The second sentence tells the listener exactly why action is needed.

**Checkpoint: Connect the problem to its effect**

Write two short sentences about a delivery. The box has arrived, but one bottle inside is broken and liquid has damaged the other items. First state only what you can observe. Then describe one practical effect.

This activity follows factual description because the listener now needs to understand the consequence. A successful response would keep the fact and effect distinct while showing a clear connection between them.

## Request one proportionate remedy {#request-remedy role=model objectives=obj-report-a-problem-and-request-action-01}

After stating the problem and its effect, say what action would help. A proportionate remedy matches the size and urgency of the problem.

Nora asks:

::: examples
- no: Kan du komme og se på det i dag?
- en: Can you come and look at it today?
:::

She does not immediately demand a complete replacement of the kitchen. She asks Erik to inspect a leak that is getting the cupboard wet.

Useful request patterns are:

::: examples
- no: Kan du se på det?
- en: Can you look at it?
- no: Kan dere sende en ny?
- en: Can you send a new one?
- no: Kan dere rette feilen?
- en: Can you correct the error?
- no: Kan jeg få et annet rom?
- en: Can I have another room?
- no: Kan noen komme i dag?
- en: Can someone come today?
:::

Use **du** when speaking to one person directly, including an individual employee. Use **dere** when speaking to several people or addressing an organization collectively.

::: examples
- no: Kan du sjekke låsen?
- en: Can you check the lock?
- no: Kan dere sjekke bestillingen min?
- en: Can you check my order?
:::

A request is more effective when it names one clear action:

::: examples
- no: Kan dere gjøre noe med dette?
- en: Can you do something about this?
:::

is understandable, but vague.

::: examples
- no: Kan dere sende den manglende varen?
- en: Can you send the missing item?
:::

gives the business a specific remedy to consider.

The requested action should also fit the problem:

::: examples
- no: Kaffen er kald. Kan jeg få en ny kopp?
- en: The coffee is cold. Can I have a new cup?
- no: Nøkkelkortet virker ikke. Kan dere aktivere det på nytt?
- en: The key card does not work. Can you activate it again?
- no: Det er en sprekk i frontruta på leiebilen. Kan noen undersøke bilen før jeg kjører videre?
- en: There is a crack in the windscreen of the rental car. Can someone inspect the car before I drive on?
:::

Notice that a proportionate request does not have to be weak. If a problem creates an immediate risk, the requested action can be urgent:

::: examples
- no: Det lukter brent fra sikringsskapet. Kan dere sende noen med en gang?
- en: There is a burnt smell coming from the fuse box. Can you send someone immediately?
:::

The urgency comes from the observable fact, not from dramatic language.

**Checkpoint: Add a suitable request**

Return to the hotel window. You have already described that it cannot be closed. The room is becoming cold. Add one request for a practical remedy that hotel staff could reasonably provide.

This activity belongs after fact and effect because the request should respond directly to both. A successful response would ask for one clear action, such as inspecting the window or providing another room, without adding unrelated demands.

{{exercise: add_suitable_request}}

## Confirm the next step {#confirm-next-step role=model objectives=obj-report-a-problem-and-request-action-01}

Do not end the exchange as soon as the other person says yes. Check what has been agreed: who will act, what they will do, and when it will happen.

Erik says:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kommer mellom klokka fire og fem.
- en: I’ll come between four and five.
:::

Nora confirms:

::: examples
- no: Så du kommer hit mellom klokka fire og fem, og jeg setter en bøtte under røret fram til da?
- en: So you’ll come here between four and five, and I’ll put a bucket under the pipe until then?
:::

Useful confirmation phrases include:

::: examples
- no: Så du kommer i ettermiddag?
- en: So you’re coming this afternoon?
- no: Så dere sender en ny vare i morgen?
- en: So you’re sending a new item tomorrow?
- no: Da venter jeg på en e-post fra dere.
- en: Then I’ll wait for an email from you.
- no: Skal jeg gjøre noe i mellomtiden?
- en: Should I do anything in the meantime?
- no: Når kan jeg forvente svar?
- en: When can I expect an answer?
- no: Har jeg forstått det riktig?
- en: Have I understood correctly?
:::

Confirmation is not unnecessary repetition. It prevents misunderstandings.

Consider this exchange:

::: {.reading translation="Employee: We’ll send someone later." dialogue_id=vague-promise speaker_id=ansatt speaker_name=Ansatt}
Vi sender noen senere.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Customer: Approximately when will they come?" dialogue_id=vague-promise speaker_id=kunde speaker_name=Kunde}
Når kommer de omtrent?
:::

The customer asks for information needed to understand the next step.

A clear confirmation might be:

::: examples
- no: Så en tekniker kommer mellom klokka seks og åtte, og jeg må være hjemme?
- en: So a technician will come between six and eight, and I need to be at home?
:::

This checks both the service provider’s action and the customer’s responsibility.

**Checkpoint: Confirm an agreement**

Imagine that a delivery company has agreed to collect a damaged parcel tomorrow morning. Write one confirmation sentence that checks the action and time. Then add a short question asking whether you need to print a return label.

This activity comes here because agreement alone may leave practical details unclear. A successful response would restate the promised collection and identify what the customer must do next.

## The complete pattern {#complete-pattern role=model objectives=obj-report-a-problem-and-request-action-01}

A strong report can be brief. It needs one useful detail at each stage.

**Observable fact:**

::: examples
- no: Skjermen på den nye telefonen har en mørk flekk.
- en: The screen on the new phone has a dark spot.
:::

**Effect:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan ikke lese teksten i det området.
- en: I can’t read the text in that area.
:::

**Requested remedy:**

::: examples
- no: Kan dere undersøke telefonen og bytte den hvis den er defekt?
- en: Can you inspect the phone and replace it if it is defective?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Shop employee: Bring it to the shop today, and we’ll inspect it and give you an answer by Friday." dialogue_id=phone-report speaker_id=butikkansatt speaker_name=Butikkansatt}
Lever den i butikken i dag, så undersøker vi den og gir deg svar innen fredag.
:::

**Confirmed next step:**

::: examples
- no: Så jeg leverer den i butikken i dag, og dere gir meg svar innen fredag?
- en: So I’ll bring it to the shop today, and you’ll give me an answer by Friday?
:::

The same structure works in a less formal everyday situation:

::: examples
- no: Sykkellåsen sitter fast, så jeg får ikke brukt sykkelen. Kan du hjelpe meg med å åpne den?
- en: The bicycle lock is stuck, so I can’t use the bicycle. Can you help me open it?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Friend: Yes, I’ll meet you by the bicycle rack after lunch." dialogue_id=bicycle-lock speaker_id=venn speaker_name=Venn}
Ja, jeg møter deg ved sykkelstativet etter lunsj.
:::

::: examples
- no: Så møter du meg ved sykkelstativet etter lunsj?
- en: So you’ll meet me by the bicycle rack after lunch?
:::

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-report-a-problem-and-request-action-01}

**Giving only an emotional reaction**

::: examples
- no: Det er helt håpløst!
- en: Incorrect for this task: It’s completely hopeless!
:::

This communicates frustration but not the problem.

Add an observable fact:

::: examples
- no: Billettautomaten tar ikke imot kortet mitt.
- en: The ticket machine does not accept my card.
:::

**Reporting the fact but not the effect**

::: examples
- no: Heisen virker ikke.
- en: The lift does not work.
:::

This may be enough in a very simple situation, but the effect can show why help is needed:

::: examples
- no: Heisen virker ikke, og jeg får ikke båret barnevogna opp trappa alene.
- en: The lift does not work, and I can’t carry the pram up the stairs alone.
:::

**Describing the problem without requesting action**

::: examples
- no: Jeg bestilte størrelse 40, men fikk størrelse 38.
- en: I ordered size 40, but received size 38.
:::

Add the remedy:

::: examples
- no: Kan dere sende riktig størrelse?
- en: Can you send the correct size?
:::

**Making several unrelated demands**

A focused request is easier to act on. If a meal contains the wrong side dish, ask for the correct side dish rather than combining the problem with complaints about the music, waiting time, and room temperature.

::: examples
- no: Jeg bestilte ris, men fikk poteter. Kan jeg få ris i stedet?
- en: I ordered rice, but received potatoes. Can I have rice instead?
:::

**Failing to confirm vague promises**

::: examples
- no: Vi ordner det snart.
- en: Incorrect for this task: We’ll sort it out soon.
:::

Ask what **snart** means in this situation:

::: examples
- no: Når kan jeg forvente at det er ferdig?
- en: When can I expect it to be finished?
:::

## Controlled practice: Build a complete report {#controlled-practice role=model objectives=obj-report-a-problem-and-request-action-01}

Write a four-part message to a landlord about a bedroom radiator that remains cold when it is switched on. State one observable fact, explain one practical effect, and request one appropriate action. The landlord replies:

::: {.reading translation="A technician will come tomorrow between ten and twelve." dialogue_id=radiator-practice speaker_id=landlord speaker_name=Landlord}
En tekniker kommer i morgen mellom klokka ti og tolv.
:::

Then confirm the agreed action and time.

This activity combines the complete pattern in a familiar housing context. It is still controlled because the situation and communicative jobs are given. A successful response would contain all four parts and keep every sentence relevant to resolving the radiator problem.

{{exercise: build_radiator_report}}

Next, use the same pattern for a shop delivery. Your order confirmation lists three lamps, but only two lamps arrived. One room therefore still has no light. Ask the shop to send the missing lamp. The shop replies:

::: {.reading translation="We’ll send the missing lamp tomorrow." dialogue_id=lamp-practice speaker_id=shop speaker_name=Shop}
Vi sender den manglende lampen i morgen.
:::

Then confirm what the shop will send and when it will be dispatched.

This second version belongs here because it reuses the pattern with a different service provider. A successful response would transfer the same four communicative steps without copying housing-specific language.

{{exercise: build_lamp_report}}

## Back in Nora’s kitchen {#back-in-noras-kitchen role=model objectives=obj-report-a-problem-and-request-action-01}

Erik arrives at half past four and examines the pipe.

::: {.reading translation="The seal is broken. I don’t have the correct part with me, but I can replace it tomorrow morning." dialogue_id=nora-kitchen-2 speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Pakningen er ødelagt. Jeg har ikke riktig del med meg, men jeg kan bytte den i morgen tidlig.
:::

::: {.reading translation="The water is still dripping, so I can’t use the cupboard this evening. Can you turn off the water to this tap until tomorrow?" dialogue_id=nora-kitchen-2 speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Vannet drypper fortsatt, så jeg kan ikke bruke skapet i kveld. Kan du stenge vannet til denne krana fram til i morgen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I’ll do that now. I’ll come back at around nine tomorrow." dialogue_id=nora-kitchen-2 speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Ja, det gjør jeg nå. Jeg kommer tilbake rundt klokka ni i morgen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. So the water supply to this tap is shut off until tomorrow, and you’ll come back at around nine to replace the seal?" dialogue_id=nora-kitchen-2 speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Fint. Så vannet til denne krana er stengt fram til i morgen, og du kommer tilbake rundt ni for å bytte pakningen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, exactly." dialogue_id=nora-kitchen-2 speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Ja, nettopp.
:::

Nora uses the same pattern again. The situation has changed, but her communication remains focused:

- The observable fact is that the water is still dripping.
- The effect is that she cannot use the cupboard that evening.
- The proportionate remedy is to turn off the water to that tap temporarily.
- The confirmed next step is that Erik will return at around nine and replace the seal.

She does not introduce a new complaint. She responds to the current facts and checks the practical agreement.
