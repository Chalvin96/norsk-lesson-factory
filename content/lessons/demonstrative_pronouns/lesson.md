## At the second-hand shop {#at-the-second-hand-shop role=orient objectives=obj-demonstrative-pronouns-01}

Maja has recently moved into a new flat. Her friend Jonas is helping her look for a few inexpensive things at a second-hand shop. Some objects are beside them; others are farther away, near the back wall.

::: {.reading translation="Look at this lamp. It fits on my desk." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Se på denne lampa. Den passer på skrivebordet mitt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but what about that lamp by the window?" dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, men hva med den lampa ved vinduet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="It is nice too. And I like this mirror here." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Den er også fin. Og jeg liker dette speilet her.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I prefer that mirror by the door." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg foretrekker det speilet ved døra.
:::

::: {.reading translation="We also need chairs. Are these chairs comfortable?" dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Vi trenger også stoler. Er disse stolene komfortable?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Perhaps, but those chairs at the back are cheaper." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Kanskje, men de stolene bakerst er billigere.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then I’ll buy this lamp and these chairs." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Da kjøper jeg denne lampa og disse stolene.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Not that large mirror?" dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ikke det store speilet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, this small mirror is easier to carry!" dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Nei, dette lille speilet er lettere å bære!
:::

The scene contains one repeated choice. Maja and Jonas identify objects by showing whether they mean something close to them or something farther away. At the same time, the Norwegian form changes according to whether the noun is masculine or feminine, neuter, or plural.

Notice one more feature:

::: examples
- no: denne lampa
- en: this lamp
- no: dette speilet
- en: this mirror
- no: disse stolene
- en: these chairs
- no: den lampa
- en: that lamp
- no: det speilet
- en: that mirror
- no: de stolene
- en: those chairs
:::

The noun after each demonstrative has a definite ending: **lampa**, **speilet**, **stolene**. Norwegian does not normally use the indefinite form in these phrases.

**Request: Shop-distance-noticing**

**Purpose:** Help the learner notice the close-versus-far meaning before producing forms.

**Context:** Maja and Jonas are comparing lamps, mirrors, and chairs in the second-hand shop.

**Learner action:** Identify which noun phrases refer to objects beside the speakers and which refer to objects elsewhere in the shop. Focus on the demonstratives, using location expressions only as supporting clues.

**Successful response demonstrates:** Recognition of the distance contrast between **denne/dette/disse** and **den/det/de**. Noun gender and number are present as context but are not the primary scored evidence in this activity.

{{exercise: shop_distance_noticing}}

## The basic meaning choice {#the-basic-meaning-choice role=model objectives=obj-demonstrative-pronouns-01}

A **demonstrative** points out a particular person or thing. Norwegian demonstratives make two choices relevant here:

1. Is the thing close or farther away?
2. Is the noun masculine or feminine, neuter, or plural?

For something close, use:

- **Noun type:** Masculine or feminine singular. **Demonstrative:** **denne**. **Example:** **denne lampa** — *this lamp*
- **Noun type:** Neuter singular. **Demonstrative:** **dette**. **Example:** **dette speilet** — *this mirror*
- **Noun type:** Plural. **Demonstrative:** **disse**. **Example:** **disse stolene** — *these chairs*

For something farther away, use:

- **Noun type:** Masculine or feminine singular. **Demonstrative:** **den**. **Example:** **den lampa** — *that lamp*
- **Noun type:** Neuter singular. **Demonstrative:** **det**. **Example:** **det speilet** — *that mirror*
- **Noun type:** Plural. **Demonstrative:** **de**. **Example:** **de stolene** — *those chairs*

The close forms correspond roughly to English **this** and **these**. The farther-away forms correspond roughly to **that** and **those**.

Compare the forms in the shop:

::: examples
- no: denne lampa her
- en: this lamp here
- no: den lampa ved vinduet
- en: that lamp by the window
- no: dette speilet her
- en: this mirror here
- no: det speilet ved døra
- en: that mirror by the door
- no: disse stolene her
- en: these chairs here
- no: de stolene bakerst
- en: those chairs at the back
:::

Words such as **her** (*here*), **ved vinduet** (*by the window*), and **bakerst** (*at the back*) make the intended distance especially clear. The demonstrative itself already signals the contrast, but the location phrase helps the listener identify the right object.

## Matching the demonstrative to the noun {#matching-the-demonstrative-to-the-noun role=model objectives=obj-demonstrative-pronouns-01}

After deciding between close and farther away, choose the form that matches the noun.

## Masculine and feminine singular: denne or den {#masculine-and-feminine-singular-denne-or-den role=model objectives=obj-demonstrative-pronouns-01}

Masculine and feminine singular nouns use **denne** for *this* and **den** for *that*.

::: examples
- no: en stol – stolen
- en: a chair – the chair
- no: denne stolen
- en: this chair
- no: den stolen
- en: that chair
- no: ei lampe – lampa
- en: a lamp – the lamp
- no: denne lampa
- en: this lamp
- no: den lampa
- en: that lamp
:::

Bokmål also allows many feminine nouns to be written with masculine forms. For example, you may encounter **en lampe – lampen**. The demonstrative remains **denne** or **den**:

::: examples
- no: denne lampen
- en: this lamp
- no: den lampen
- en: that lamp
:::

The important choice is not between masculine and feminine here. Both use the same demonstratives.

## Neuter singular: dette or det {#neuter-singular-dette-or-det role=model objectives=obj-demonstrative-pronouns-01}

Neuter nouns use **dette** for *this* and **det** for *that*.

::: examples
- no: et bord – bordet
- en: a table – the table
- no: dette bordet
- en: this table
- no: det bordet
- en: that table
- no: et speil – speilet
- en: a mirror – the mirror
- no: dette speilet
- en: this mirror
- no: det speilet
- en: that mirror
:::

Compare a masculine noun and a neuter noun:

::: examples
- no: denne stolen
- en: this chair
- no: dette bordet
- en: this table
- no: den stolen
- en: that chair
- no: det bordet
- en: that table
:::

English uses the same word, *this* or *that*, in both cases. Norwegian marks the difference.

## Plural: disse or de {#plural-disse-or-de role=model objectives=obj-demonstrative-pronouns-01}

All plural nouns use **disse** for *these* and **de** for *those*. Gender no longer changes the demonstrative.

::: examples
- no: stolene
- en: the chairs
- no: disse stolene
- en: these chairs
- no: de stolene
- en: those chairs
- no: bordene
- en: the tables
- no: disse bordene
- en: these tables
- no: de bordene
- en: those tables
- no: lampene
- en: the lamps
- no: disse lampene
- en: these lamps
- no: de lampene
- en: those lamps
:::

The compact pattern is:

- **denne** + masculine or feminine singular
- **dette** + neuter singular
- **disse** + plural
- **den** + masculine or feminine singular
- **det** + neuter singular
- **de** + plural

**Request: Demonstrative-agreement-selection**

**Context:** A shop assistant points to one nearby object or one object across the room. The nouns include masculine or feminine singular, neuter singular, and plural items from the shop.

**Learner action:** Supply the demonstrative that matches each noun’s gender or number while preserving the stated close-or-far meaning.

**Successful response demonstrates:** Correct selection among **denne**, **dette**, and **disse**, or among **den**, **det**, and **de**, based on noun type. Distance is given as context rather than scored as a separate decision.

## The noun must be definite {#the-noun-must-be-definite role=contrast objectives=obj-demonstrative-pronouns-01}

Norwegian uses the **definite noun form (bestemt form)** after these demonstratives. If you already know forms such as **stolen** (*the chair*), **bordet** (*the table*), and **bøkene** (*the books*), use those same forms after the demonstrative.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: en stol
- en: a chair
- no: stolen
- en: the chair
- no: denne stolen
- en: this chair
:::

And:

::: examples
- no: et bord
- en: a table
- no: bordet
- en: the table
- no: det bordet
- en: that table
:::

And in the plural:

::: examples
- no: bøker
- en: books
- no: bøkene
- en: the books
- no: disse bøkene
- en: these books
:::

A common mistake is to copy the English structure too directly:

::: examples
- no: ✗ denne stol
- en: Intended meaning: this chair
:::

The corrected phrase is:

::: examples
- no: denne stolen
- en: this chair
:::

Another likely mistake is:

::: examples
- no: ✗ det bord
- en: Intended meaning: that table
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: det bordet
- en: that table
:::

The demonstrative does not replace the noun’s definite ending. The two appear together:

**demonstrative + definite noun**

This may feel unusual because English says *this chair*, not “this the-chair.” In Norwegian, however, **denne stolen** is the normal structure.

**Request: Definite-form-repair**

**Purpose:** Correct the common error of placing an indefinite noun after a demonstrative.

**Context:** Maja has written short labels for objects she may buy, but some labels use forms such as **stol**, **bord**, or **lamper** where definite forms are required.

**Learner action:** Rewrite each noun phrase so the noun has its correct definite singular or plural ending. The demonstrative is already supplied.

**Successful response demonstrates:** Production of the definite noun form after a demonstrative. Choice of close or far demonstrative is contextual support, not the scored goal.

## Making both choices together {#making-both-choices-together role=model objectives=obj-demonstrative-pronouns-01}

In real use, the learner normally has to make both decisions:

- close or farther away;
- masculine/feminine singular, neuter singular, or plural.

Consider a kitchen table beside you:

::: examples
- no: dette bordet
- en: this table
:::

The table is close, so choose the close set. **Bord** is neuter, so choose **dette**. The noun then appears in the definite form **bordet**.

Now consider several chairs across the room:

::: examples
- no: de stolene
- en: those chairs
:::

The chairs are farther away, so choose the farther-away set. They are plural, so choose **de**. The noun appears in the definite plural, **stolene**.

Here are further aligned contrasts:

::: examples
- no: denne koppen / den koppen
- en: this cup / that cup
- no: dette glasset / det glasset
- en: this glass / that glass
- no: disse tallerkenene / de tallerkenene
- en: these plates / those plates
- no: denne boka / den boka
- en: this book / that book
- no: dette bildet / det bildet
- en: this picture / that picture
- no: disse bildene / de bildene
- en: these pictures / those pictures
:::

The same meaning contrast works outside physical distance. Two people can point to items in a catalogue or on a screen:

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker denne sofaen på side tre.
- en: I like this sofa on page three.
- no: Jeg liker ikke den sofaen på neste side.
- en: I don’t like that sofa on the next page.
:::

The central grammar remains the same: choose a demonstrative and follow it with a definite noun.

**Request: Complete-reference-building**

**Purpose:** Combine distance, noun agreement, and definite noun form in one controlled phrase.

**Context:** The learner helps Maja distinguish nearby objects from objects near the back of the shop.

**Learner action:** Choose the complete set of labels that fits nearby common-gender, neuter, and plural objects. Check both the demonstrative and the noun form in every label.

**Successful response demonstrates:** Accurate coordination of both required choices: correct demonstrative and correct definite noun form.

## Describing a particular object {#describing-a-particular-object role=contrast objectives=obj-demonstrative-pronouns-01}

An adjective may appear between the demonstrative and the noun:

::: examples
- no: denne lille lampa
- en: this small lamp
- no: det store speilet
- en: that large mirror
- no: disse gamle stolene
- en: these old chairs
:::

The important structure is still visible:

- **denne** + **lampa**
- **det** + **speilet**
- **disse** + **stolene**

The adjective adds description, but it does not remove the definite ending from the noun.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: denne lampa
- en: this lamp
- no: denne lille lampa
- en: this small lamp
- no: de stolene
- en: those chairs
- no: de gamle stolene
- en: those old chairs
:::

Do not let the extra word hide the target pattern. In **dette lille bordet**, **dette** matches the neuter noun **bord**, and **bordet** remains definite.

::: examples
- no: dette lille bordet
- en: this small table
- no: denne lille stolen
- en: this small chair
- no: disse små bordene
- en: these small tables
:::

At this level, treat the adjective as useful context. The main task remains choosing the demonstrative and keeping the noun definite.

**Request: Pattern-through-description**

**Purpose:** Ensure that a descriptive word does not cause the learner to lose the demonstrative-plus-definite-noun pattern.

**Context:** Maja distinguishes similar shop items by size or age, such as a small lamp and an old set of chairs.

**Learner action:** Complete short noun phrases containing a supplied adjective, choosing the appropriate demonstrative and producing the definite noun form.

**Successful response demonstrates:** Correct target structure despite an intervening adjective. Adjective form is supporting context rather than a separate scored grammar goal.

{{exercise: pattern_through_description}}

## Back at the counter {#back-at-the-counter role=model objectives=obj-demonstrative-pronouns-01}

Maja and Jonas take their choices to the counter.

::: {.reading translation="Do you want to buy this lamp?" dialogue_id="counter" speaker_id="ekspeditoren" speaker_name="Ekspeditøren"}
Vil dere kjøpe denne lampa?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, and this mirror too." dialogue_id="counter" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Ja, og dette speilet også.
:::

::: {.reading translation="We’re not taking those chairs at the back." dialogue_id="counter" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Vi tar ikke de stolene bakerst.
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, we’re taking these chairs here." dialogue_id="counter" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Nei, vi tar disse stolene her.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. This lamp, this mirror, and these chairs." dialogue_id="counter" speaker_id="ekspeditoren" speaker_name="Ekspeditøren"}
Fint. Denne lampa, dette speilet og disse stolene.
:::

Maja’s final selection uses all three close forms:

::: examples
- no: denne lampa
- en: this lamp
- no: dette speilet
- en: this mirror
- no: disse stolene
- en: these chairs
- no: de stolene bakerst
- en: those chairs at the back
:::

- **denne lampa** because **lampe** is feminine singular;
- **dette speilet** because **speil** is neuter singular;
- **disse stolene** because **stolene** is plural.

Jonas contrasts the selected chairs with **de stolene bakerst**—the chairs farther away.

**Request: Counter-summary**

**Purpose:** Retrieve all three demonstrative agreement forms within one coherent situation.

**Context:** The shop assistant checks Maja’s final purchases and contrasts them with items left in the shop.

**Learner action:** Restate the selected and rejected items using complete demonstrative noun phrases.

**Successful response demonstrates:** Appropriate use of singular common-gender, singular neuter, and plural demonstratives, each followed by a definite noun.

## Transfer to a new situation {#transfer-to-a-new-situation role=recap objectives=obj-demonstrative-pronouns-01}

The next day, Maja is unpacking in her new flat. Jonas stands near several boxes, while Maja points to furniture and objects in different parts of the room.

Use Norwegian phrases to distinguish:

- a box beside Jonas from a box near the door;
- a picture in Maja’s hands from a picture on the far wall;
- several books on the nearby table from several books across the room.

Then place the phrases into short practical statements such as:

::: examples
- no: Denne esken skal på kjøkkenet.
- en: This box is going to the kitchen.
- no: Det bildet skal på soverommet.
- en: That picture is going in the bedroom.
- no: Disse bøkene skal i hylla.
- en: These books are going on the shelf.
:::

The new situation changes the purpose. Maja is no longer choosing what to buy; she is identifying where particular belongings should go. The same grammar lets her make each reference clear.

**Request: Moving-day-transfer**

**Purpose:** Transfer the target pattern from shopping to organizing a home.

**Context:** Maja and Jonas are unpacking nearby and farther-away boxes, pictures, and books.

**Learner action:** Produce complete demonstrative noun phrases inside short placement statements. Use the object’s location, gender, and number to choose the form.

**Successful response demonstrates:** Independent selection of the correct demonstrative and definite noun form in a changed everyday context.

{{exercise: moving_day_transfer}}
