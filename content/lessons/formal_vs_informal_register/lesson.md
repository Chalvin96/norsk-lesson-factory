## A message that sounds wrong {#message-sounds-wrong role=orient objectives=obj-formal-vs-informal-register-01}

Nora works for a small architecture firm in Bergen. Her team is preparing a public meeting in a municipal building. When she discovers that the reserved room may be too small, she writes to the building coordinator, Eirik. Before sending the message, she shows it to her colleague Jonas.

**Nora’s draft**

::: examples
- no: Hei Eirik!
- en: Hi Eirik!
- no: Vi trenger et større rom. Send meg en oversikt over ledige rom i morgen. Det haster!
- en: We need a larger room. Send me an overview of available rooms tomorrow. It is urgent!
- no: Ha det!
- en: Bye!
:::

**At the office**

::: {.reading translation='Can you look at this message before I send it?' dialogue_id=office speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Kan du se på denne meldingen før jeg sender den?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes. It is clear, but it sounds a little abrupt.' dialogue_id=office speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. Den er tydelig, men den høres litt brå ut.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Because I write Send me?' dialogue_id=office speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Fordi jeg skriver Send meg?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Among other things. You do not know Eirik very well, and you are asking him for help at short notice.' dialogue_id=office speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Blant annet. Du kjenner ikke Eirik så godt, og du ber ham om hjelp på kort varsel.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Should I write De instead of du?' dialogue_id=office speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Bør jeg skrive De i stedet for du?
:::

::: {.reading translation='No, it is completely normal to use du, including in professional emails. I would make the request a little softer instead.' dialogue_id=office speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nei, det er helt vanlig å bruke «du», også i profesjonelle e-poster. Jeg ville heller gjort forespørselen litt mykere.
:::

::: {.reading translation='What about Could you send me an overview?' dialogue_id=office speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Hva med Kunne du sende meg en oversikt?
:::

::: {.reading translation='That fits better. Also explain why it is urgent, and finish with thanks or a friendly greeting.' dialogue_id=office speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Det passer bedre. Forklar også hvorfor det haster, og avslutt med takk eller en vennlig hilsen.
:::

Nora revises the message.

**Nora’s revised email**

::: examples
- no: Hei Eirik,
- en: Hi Eirik,
- no: Vi har fått flere påmeldinger enn forventet til møtet på torsdag, og rommet vi har reservert, ser derfor ut til å bli for lite. Kunne du sendt meg en oversikt over eventuelle større rom som er ledige? Hvis det er mulig, trenger vi en avklaring innen i morgen ettermiddag.
- en: We have received more registrations than expected for the meeting on Thursday, so the room we have reserved appears likely to be too small. Could you send me an overview of any larger rooms that are available? If possible, we need clarification by tomorrow afternoon.
- no: På forhånd takk for hjelpen.
- en: Thank you in advance for your help.
- no: Vennlig hilsen
- en: Kind regards
- no: Nora Vik
- en: Nora Vik
:::

Later, Nora sends Jonas a message in the company chat.

::: {.reading translation='The email has been sent now. Thanks for the help!' dialogue_id=company_chat_initial speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Da er e-posten sendt. Takk for hjelpen!
:::

::: {.reading translation='You’re welcome! I hope they have a larger room.' dialogue_id=company_chat_initial speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Bare hyggelig! Satser på at de har et større rom.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, otherwise it’ll be cramped, you know.' dialogue_id=company_chat_initial speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, ellers blir det trangt, altså.
:::

Both messages are appropriate, but they are appropriate in different ways. The email to Eirik is professional without being stiff. The chat with Jonas is shorter, more personal and more conversational.

## Register: choosing language for the situation {#register-situation role=model objectives=obj-formal-vs-informal-register-01}

The level and style of language used in a situation is called **register (språkregister)**. Register is not simply a choice between formal and informal language. It depends on several connected questions:

- Who is the reader or listener?
- How well do the people know each other?
- What kind of text or conversation is this?
- What is its purpose?
- How much pressure or inconvenience does the request create?

Nora is not writing to a close friend. She is contacting someone she knows only through work, and she needs him to solve a problem quickly. Her first draft communicates the basic information, but its commands and exclamation marks make the request sound more demanding than she intends.

Her revised version does not hide the urgency. Instead, it gives a reason, turns the command into a request and states the deadline calmly.

Compare the central request:

::: examples
- no: Send meg en oversikt over ledige rom i morgen.
- en: Send me an overview of available rooms tomorrow.
- no: Kunne du sendt meg en oversikt over eventuelle større rom som er ledige?
- en: Could you send me an overview of any larger rooms that are available?
:::

The first sentence is grammatically correct. It may be suitable when authority, routine or urgency makes a direct instruction appropriate. The second is more suitable when asking a professional contact for assistance.

**Checkpoint: notice the relationship**

Read Nora’s draft and revised email again. Identify the expressions that reveal the relationship between Nora and Eirik, the inconvenience involved and the purpose of the message. Then explain which changes make the revised email sound cooperative rather than commanding.

This activity belongs here because register begins with context, not isolated vocabulary. A successful response would connect specific language choices—such as the greeting, request form, explanation and closing—to Nora’s professional relationship with Eirik.

## Greetings establish the relationship {#greetings-relationship role=model objectives=obj-formal-vs-informal-register-01}

A greeting gives the reader an immediate signal about tone. In contemporary Norwegian, **Hei** is extremely flexible. It is common in private messages, workplace communication and many professional emails.

::: examples
- no: Hei Eirik,
- en: Hi Eirik,
- no: Hei alle sammen,
- en: Hello everyone,
- no: Hei, og takk for sist.
- en: Hello, and thank you for last time.
:::

The greeting does not become professional merely by becoming longer. What matters is whether it suits the relationship and genre.

**Kjære** can sound warm, personal or ceremonial. It is natural in personal letters and certain formal addresses, but it is not automatically the best opening for an ordinary workplace request.

::: examples
- no: Kjære bestemor,
- en: Dear Grandma,
- no: Kjære kolleger,
- en: Dear colleagues,
:::

**God morgen** and **God dag** may suit spoken encounters, customer service or messages where the time and setting make them natural.

::: examples
- no: God morgen. Jeg har en avtale med rådgiveren klokka ni.
- en: Good morning. I have an appointment with the adviser at nine.
:::

In a message to an unknown organization, the recipient can be named by role if no person is known.

::: examples
- no: Til kundeservice,
- en: To customer service,
:::

A bare opening such as **Hei!** can feel friendly and energetic, but repeated exclamation marks may create a tone that is too enthusiastic, impatient or casual for the situation.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Hei Eirik! Vi må bytte rom!
- en: Hi Eirik! We have to change rooms!
- no: Hei Eirik, vi ser ut til å ha behov for et større rom.
- en: Hi Eirik, it appears that we need a larger room.
:::

Neither exclamation marks nor short sentences are wrong. The question is what effect they create.

**Checkpoint: match greeting to context**

Rewrite the opening of one message for three specified recipients: a close colleague, an unfamiliar administrative contact and a group of participants attending a professional event. Keep the practical purpose unchanged.

This controlled comparison belongs here because changing only the audience makes the effect of the greeting easier to observe. A successful response would use natural Bokmål and signal different relationships without making the professional versions unnaturally ceremonial.

## Du is normal in professional Norwegian {#du-professional role=contrast objectives=obj-formal-vs-informal-register-01}

Nora wonders whether she should replace **du** with **De**. In most present-day Norwegian communication, that is unnecessary.

**Du** is the ordinary singular pronoun used with friends, colleagues, customers, officials and many people met for the first time. A professional tone usually comes from the whole sentence, not from replacing **du**.

::: examples
- no: Kan du bekrefte at du har mottatt dokumentene?
- en: Can you confirm that you have received the documents?
- no: Har du anledning til å delta på møtet?
- en: Are you available to attend the meeting?
:::

The capitalized pronoun **De**, with forms such as **Dem** and **Deres**, is now uncommon in ordinary communication. It may occur in older texts, highly traditional service language or deliberately distant and ceremonial contexts. Used casually, it can sound old-fashioned, unnatural or even ironic.

A frequent mistake is to assume that English professional you must become Norwegian **De**. Usually, it remains **du**.

Less natural in an ordinary modern workplace email:

::: examples
- no: Kan De bekrefte at De har mottatt dokumentene?
- en: Can you confirm that you have received the documents?
:::

Natural in most present-day workplaces:

::: examples
- no: Kan du bekrefte at du har mottatt dokumentene?
- en: Can you confirm that you have received the documents?
:::

Professional language can also avoid direct reference to the recipient when the focus is on a process rather than a person.

::: examples
- no: Søknaden må sendes inn innen 1. september.
- en: The application must be submitted by 1 September.
- no: Vi ber om at dokumentasjonen sendes elektronisk.
- en: We ask that the documentation be sent electronically.
:::

These impersonal forms can be useful in regulations, standard notices and official procedures. In a personal email, however, too many of them may sound distant.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Det bes om at vedlagte skjema returneres innen fredag.
- en: It is requested that the attached form be returned by Friday.
- no: Kan du returnere det vedlagte skjemaet innen fredag?
- en: Can you return the attached form by Friday?
:::

The first resembles standardized administrative language. The second addresses a person directly and will often sound clearer and more natural in an email.

**Checkpoint: keep du, adjust the tone**

Take a short, overly direct workplace request containing **du**. Revise it without replacing **du** with **De**. Change the request form and add only the context needed to make the message professional.

This activity belongs here because learners often try to solve register problems by changing the pronoun alone. A successful response would retain **du** while improving tone through directness, explanation and sentence structure.

## Directness: commands, requests and reasons {#directness-requests role=model objectives=obj-formal-vs-informal-register-01}

Norwegian workplace communication is often relatively direct. Professional does not mean vague, excessively apologetic or full of elaborate politeness. Still, a command can sound abrupt when one person is asking another for a favor.

**Direct instructions**

The imperative is useful when the action is routine, urgent or clearly part of the listener’s responsibility.

::: examples
- no: Ring meg når du kommer fram.
- en: Call me when you arrive.
- no: Legg rapporten i prosjektmappen.
- en: Put the report in the project folder.
- no: Evakuer bygningen umiddelbart.
- en: Evacuate the building immediately.
:::

A direct instruction can also be softened with **takk**, but **takk** does not automatically turn every command into a polite request.

::: examples
- no: Send rapporten innen klokka to, takk.
- en: Send the report by two o’clock, please.
:::

Depending on the relationship and tone, this can sound efficient, authoritative or impatient.

**Requests with kan**

**Kan du …?** is common and neutral. It works in both conversational and professional settings.

::: examples
- no: Kan du sende meg den nyeste versjonen?
- en: Can you send me the latest version?
- no: Kan du gi meg beskjed når rommet er bekreftet?
- en: Can you let me know when the room has been confirmed?
:::

Among colleagues, this may be all the softening needed.

**More tentative requests with kunne**

**Kunne du …?** creates a more tentative request. It is especially useful when the task is inconvenient, the relationship is less familiar or the writer wants to avoid sounding demanding.

::: examples
- no: Kunne du sett på dette før møtet?
- en: Could you look at this before the meeting?
- no: Kunne du sendt oss en oppdatert oversikt?
- en: Could you send us an updated overview?
- no: Kunne du sendt meg en oversikt over eventuelle større rom som er ledige?
- en: Could you send me an overview of any larger rooms that are available?
:::

This phrasing fits because Eirik must investigate the options and respond quickly.

**Requests with har du anledning til**

**Har du anledning til å …?** asks whether the person has the opportunity or availability to do something. It often sounds considerate and professional.

::: examples
- no: Har du anledning til å delta på et kort møte i morgen?
- en: Are you available to attend a short meeting tomorrow?
- no: Har du anledning til å sende dokumentene innen torsdag?
- en: Would you be able to send the documents by Thursday?
:::

It is less suitable when the action is a firm requirement rather than an optional request. If a deadline is fixed, state it clearly.

::: examples
- no: Dokumentene må sendes innen torsdag.
- en: The documents must be sent by Thursday.
:::

A request and a requirement should not be confused merely to sound polite.

**Give a useful reason**

A brief reason can make a request feel reasonable rather than arbitrary.

::: examples
- no: Kan du sende meg deltakerlisten?
- en: Can you send me the participant list?
- no: Kan du sende meg deltakerlisten i løpet av dagen? Vi må bestille adgangskort før klokka fire.
- en: Can you send me the participant list during the day? We have to order access cards before four o’clock.
:::

The second version explains both the deadline and its purpose. The reader can understand why prompt action matters.

Useful framing expressions include:

::: examples
- no: Vi har fått flere påmeldinger enn forventet.
- en: We have received more registrations than expected.
- no: For at vi skal rekke fristen, trenger vi svar innen onsdag.
- en: In order for us to meet the deadline, we need an answer by Wednesday.
- no: Hvis det er mulig, ønsker vi en avklaring i løpet av dagen.
- en: If possible, we would like clarification during the day.
- no: Det haster fordi møtet finner sted allerede i morgen.
- en: It is urgent because the meeting is taking place as early as tomorrow.
:::

**Hvis det er mulig** makes a request sound less absolute. Do not use it if the deadline is genuinely non-negotiable.

**Checkpoint: build a professional request**

Using Nora’s room problem, transform a direct command into a short professional request. Include the action, a realistic deadline and the reason for that deadline. Keep the message concise.

This activity belongs here because it combines the main elements of appropriate directness without yet requiring a full email. A successful response would make the requested action unmistakable while presenting Nora as cooperative rather than hesitant or forceful.

## Sentence style and information flow {#sentence-style-flow role=contrast objectives=obj-formal-vs-informal-register-01}

Conversational Norwegian often relies on short clauses, shared context and incomplete sentences. Professional writing usually needs enough context to stand on its own, especially when the recipient cannot immediately ask what the writer means.

In the office chat, Jonas can write:

::: examples
- no: Satser på at de har et større rom.
- en: I hope they have a larger room.
:::

The subject **jeg** is omitted in this conversational expression. Jonas is the understood speaker, and both speakers know what **de** and **et større rom** refer to.

The same compressed style may be unclear in an email to someone outside the conversation.

Too dependent on shared context:

::: examples
- no: Hei, det gjelder torsdag. Trenger noe større. Gi beskjed fort.
- en: Hi, it concerns Thursday. Need something larger. Let me know quickly.
:::

Clearer professional version:

::: examples
- no: Hei, det gjelder romreservasjonen for møtet på torsdag. Vi trenger et større rom enn det vi opprinnelig bestilte. Kan du gi meg beskjed om hvilke alternativer som er ledige?
- en: Hi, this concerns the room reservation for the meeting on Thursday. We need a larger room than the one we originally booked. Can you let me know which alternatives are available?
:::

Professional writing does not have to use long, abstract sentences. In fact, a very formal, noun-heavy style can make a simple request harder to understand.

Heavy administrative style:

::: examples
- no: På bakgrunn av økningen i antallet registrerte deltakere foreligger det et behov for gjennomføring av en endring av romreservasjonen.
- en: On the basis of the increase in the number of registered participants, there is a need for the implementation of a change to the room reservation.
:::

Clear professional style:

::: examples
- no: Fordi flere har meldt seg på, må vi endre romreservasjonen.
- en: Because more people have registered, we need to change the room reservation.
:::

Both are formal in subject matter, but the second is clearer and more direct. At B2 level, good register control includes resisting the idea that professional language must be complicated.

**Aligned contrast: chat and email**

**To a close colleague in chat**

::: examples
- no: Hei! Har du den siste deltakerlista? Trenger den før fire.
- en: Hi! Do you have the latest participant list? Need it before four.
:::

**To an external coordinator by email**

::: examples
- no: Hei, har du mulighet til å sende meg den siste deltakerlisten innen klokka fire? Vi trenger den for å bestille adgangskort.
- en: Hi, would you be able to send me the latest participant list by four o’clock? We need it to order access cards.
:::

**In a standard notice**

::: examples
- no: Oppdatert deltakerliste må sendes inn innen klokka fire for at adgangskort skal kunne bestilles.
- en: An updated participant list must be submitted by four o’clock so that access cards can be ordered.
:::

The practical content is nearly identical. Pronouns, sentence completeness and degree of impersonality change with genre and purpose.

**Checkpoint: change the genre, not the meaning**

Express the same room-change request first as a brief internal chat message and then as a complete email to an external contact. Preserve the facts and deadline.

This activity belongs here because register control requires transferring the same content across genres. A successful response would make the chat efficient and conversational while making the email self-contained, courteous and clear.

## Conversational markers and their effect {#conversational-markers role=model objectives=obj-formal-vs-informal-register-01}

Spoken and informal Norwegian often uses small words that organize the conversation or express the speaker’s attitude. These are **conversational markers (samtalemarkører)**. They can make language sound natural, but their effect depends heavily on context and tone.

**Bare**

**Bare** can make a suggestion or request feel casual and low-pressure.

::: examples
- no: Bare send meg en melding når du vet mer.
- en: Just send me a message when you know more.
- no: Du kan bare legge dokumentene på pulten min.
- en: You can just put the documents on my desk.
:::

In friendly conversation, this can sound relaxed. In some instructions, however, **bare** may minimize a task that is actually difficult.

::: examples
- no: Du kan bare flytte møtet til et annet rom.
- en: You can just move the meeting to another room.
:::

If changing the room involves contacting participants and updating equipment, **bare** may sound dismissive.

**Jo**

**Jo** often signals that information is known, expected or relevant to both speakers.

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger jo plass til alle deltakerne.
- en: We do, after all, need room for all the participants.
- no: Du kjenner jo bygget bedre enn jeg gjør.
- en: You know the building better than I do, after all.
- no: Dette har jeg jo forklart før.
- en: I have explained this before, you know.
:::

Among colleagues, **jo** can create a sense of shared understanding. In a disagreement, it can also imply that the other person should already know or accept something.

That sentence may sound impatient or reproachful. It is risky in a sensitive professional exchange.

**Vel**

**Vel** can make a statement tentative or invite agreement.

::: examples
- no: Vi rekker vel møtet hvis vi går nå.
- en: We’ll probably make the meeting if we leave now.
- no: Det blir vel litt trangt med førti personer.
- en: It will probably be a little cramped with forty people.
:::

It is natural in conversation but can weaken a statement that should be precise.

Less suitable in a firm written requirement:

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger vel svar innen fredag.
- en: We probably need an answer by Friday.
:::

Clearer:

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger svar innen fredag.
- en: We need an answer by Friday.
:::

**Altså**

**Altså** has several conversational uses. It can introduce a conclusion, clarify a point or add emotional emphasis.

::: examples
- no: Det finnes ingen større rom. Vi må altså begrense antallet deltakere.
- en: There are no larger rooms. We must therefore limit the number of participants.
:::

Here, **altså** marks a logical conclusion and can work in careful speech or writing.

::: examples
- no: Ellers blir det trangt, altså.
- en: Otherwise it’ll be cramped, you know.
:::

In Nora’s chat message, it adds emphasis. This use sounds conversational. In a professional email, it may seem too emotional or spoken.

**Faktisk**

**Faktisk** highlights that something is true, often contrary to expectation.

::: examples
- no: Det største rommet er faktisk ledig.
- en: The largest room is actually available.
:::

It can be useful when the contrast matters. Overused, it may sound as if the writer is correcting or challenging the reader.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Vi har faktisk allerede sendt skjemaet.
- en: We have actually already sent the form.
- no: Skjemaet ble sendt på mandag. Jeg legger ved en kopi her.
- en: The form was sent on Monday. I am attaching a copy here.
:::

The second version may be better if the aim is to correct a misunderstanding without sounding defensive.

**Checkpoint: control conversational markers**

Review a short exchange between Nora and Jonas containing **bare**, **jo**, **vel**, **altså** and **faktisk**. Decide what attitude each marker adds. Then rewrite the essential information as a neutral professional update to Eirik.

This activity belongs here because learners need to understand not only dictionary meaning but interpersonal effect. A successful response would preserve the facts while removing or retaining markers according to the new audience and purpose.

## Closings complete the tone {#closings-tone role=model objectives=obj-formal-vs-informal-register-01}

Nora’s first closing, **Ha det!**, is natural in conversation and friendly messages.

::: examples
- no: Ha det!
- en: Bye!
- no: Vi snakkes!
- en: Speak soon!
- no: Takk, vi ses i morgen.
- en: Thanks, see you tomorrow.
:::

In a professional email, common closings include:

::: examples
- no: Vennlig hilsen
- en: Kind regards
- no: Med vennlig hilsen
- en: Kind regards
- no: På forhånd takk for hjelpen.
- en: Thank you in advance for your help.
- no: Takk for at du ser på saken.
- en: Thank you for looking into the matter.
- no: Jeg ser fram til å høre fra deg.
- en: I look forward to hearing from you.
:::

**Vennlig hilsen** is neutral and widely useful. **Med vennlig hilsen** is slightly more traditional but still common. A simple **Hilsen** can sound less formal and may suit familiar professional contacts.

::: examples
- no: Hilsen Nora
- en: Regards, Nora
:::

The closing should match the rest of the message. A highly ceremonial closing after a casual, abbreviated message creates an uneven tone.

Uneven:

::: examples
- no: Hei! Trenger svar fort. Med vennlig hilsen Nora Vik
- en: Hi! Need an answer quickly. Kind regards, Nora Vik
:::

More consistent:

::: examples
- no: Hei, kan du gi meg en avklaring i løpet av dagen? Takk for hjelpen. Vennlig hilsen Nora Vik
- en: Hi, can you give me clarification during the day? Thank you for your help. Kind regards, Nora Vik
:::

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-formal-vs-informal-register-01}

**Making professional Norwegian unnecessarily old-fashioned**

Replacing **du** with **De**, choosing elaborate formulas and avoiding ordinary verbs can make a modern email sound unnatural.

Overly distant:

::: examples
- no: Det bes herved om at De oversender Deres bekreftelse.
- en: You are hereby requested to forward your confirmation.
:::

Natural professional version:

::: examples
- no: Kan du sende meg en bekreftelse?
- en: Can you send me a confirmation?
:::

**Keeping chat abbreviations in an external email**

Fragments work when the context is shared and the relationship is familiar. They can appear careless or unclear elsewhere.

Chat style:

::: examples
- no: Fint om du svarer fort. Gjelder rommet på torsdag.
- en: It would be good if you reply quickly. Concerns the room on Thursday.
:::

Complete version:

::: examples
- no: Det gjelder rommet vi har reservert på torsdag. Fint om du kan svare innen klokka to.
- en: This concerns the room we have reserved on Thursday. It would be good if you could reply by two o’clock.
:::

**Softening so much that the purpose disappears**

Politeness should not hide the requested action or deadline.

Unclear:

::: examples
- no: Det hadde kanskje vært fint om det muligens var anledning til å se litt på romsituasjonen.
- en: It might perhaps be good if there were possibly an opportunity to look a little at the room situation.
:::

Clear and considerate:

::: examples
- no: Kunne du undersøkt om et større rom er ledig på torsdag?
- en: Could you check whether a larger room is available on Thursday?
:::

**Using a friendly marker where precision is needed**

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger vel svar snart.
- en: We probably need an answer soon.
:::

This gives no reliable deadline.

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger svar innen tirsdag klokka tolv.
- en: We need an answer by Tuesday at noon.
:::

**Assuming short language is always rude**

A brief message can be perfectly appropriate when the relationship and context support it.

::: examples
- no: Hei Jonas, kan du sende meg lenken? Takk!
- en: Hi Jonas, can you send me the link? Thanks!
:::

There is no need to expand every ordinary colleague request into a formal letter.

**Assuming indirect language is always polite**

An indirect hint can create confusion.

::: examples
- no: Det hadde vært nyttig å ha deltakerlisten.
- en: It would be useful to have the participant list.
:::

If the recipient is expected to send it, a direct request is clearer:

::: examples
- no: Kan du sende meg deltakerlisten innen klokka tre?
- en: Can you send me the participant list by three o’clock?
:::

## Back to Nora and Eirik {#back-to-nora role=model objectives=obj-formal-vs-informal-register-01}

Eirik replies later that morning.

::: examples
- no: Hei Nora,
- en: Hi Nora,
- no: Takk for beskjed. Sal 3 er ledig og har plass til femti personer. Jeg kan flytte reservasjonen dit, men jeg trenger en bekreftelse fra deg før klokka to.
- en: Thank you for letting me know. Hall 3 is available and has room for fifty people. I can move the reservation there, but I need confirmation from you before two o’clock.
- no: Vennlig hilsen
- en: Kind regards
- no: Eirik
- en: Eirik
:::

Nora replies:

::: examples
- no: Hei Eirik,
- en: Hi Eirik,
- no: Det høres bra ut. Du kan flytte reservasjonen til Sal 3. Takk for raskt svar og god hjelp.
- en: That sounds good. You can move the reservation to Hall 3. Thank you for the quick reply and your help.
- no: Vennlig hilsen
- en: Kind regards
- no: Nora
- en: Nora
:::

Notice that Nora now uses **Du kan flytte reservasjonen** rather than **Kunne du flyttet reservasjonen?** Eirik has offered to move it and asked for confirmation. Nora’s sentence is therefore permission and confirmation, not an abrupt demand. The same grammatical form can have a different effect when the conversational context changes.

She then updates Jonas in the company chat.

::: {.reading translation='We got Hall 3!' dialogue_id=company_chat_update speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Vi fikk Sal 3!
:::

::: {.reading translation='Perfect. Then we won’t have to sit on top of each other.' dialogue_id=company_chat_update speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Perfekt. Da slipper vi å sitte oppå hverandre.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, that would have been a little too social.' dialogue_id=company_chat_update speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, det hadde blitt litt vel sosialt.
:::

The chat includes an exclamation mark, an idiomatic expression and humor. These choices fit Nora and Jonas’s familiar relationship. Sending the same wording to Eirik would be possible in some workplaces, but it would signal greater familiarity and a more playful purpose.

## Integrated practice: one situation, three tones {#integrated-practice role=recap objectives=obj-formal-vs-informal-register-01}

Work with the same practical event: a meeting has attracted more participants than expected, and the room must be changed.

First, write a concise internal chat message to a close colleague who already knows about the meeting. Then write a complete email request to an unfamiliar building coordinator. Finally, write a neutral update for all meeting participants after the room has been changed.

This activity belongs near the end because it requires coordinated control of greeting, pronouns, directness, sentence style, conversational markers and closing. A successful set of texts would preserve the same facts while making each version clearly appropriate to its audience, genre and purpose.

After drafting, compare the three versions. Check whether the chat depends appropriately on shared context, whether the professional request is clear without sounding commanding, and whether the participant update avoids unnecessary conversational detail.

## Short retrieval and review {#short-retrieval-review role=recap objectives=obj-formal-vs-informal-register-01}

Without returning to the explanations, revise a blunt message asking an unfamiliar professional contact for urgent help. Keep **du**, choose a suitable greeting and closing, state the reason and deadline, and use a request form that is clear but not abrupt. Then note which language choices make your version suitable for its audience, genre and purpose.
