## The central choice {#the-central-choice role=model objectives=obj-adjective-agreement-01}

A describing word such as **stor** “large,” **grønn** “green,” or **liten** “small” is an adjective (**adjektiv**). In Norwegian, an adjective often changes form to match the noun it describes. This is called adjective agreement (**samsvarsbøying**).

To choose the form, ask:

1. Is the noun masculine, feminine, or neuter?
2. Is it singular or plural?
3. Is the noun phrase indefinite or definite?

Compare:

::: examples
- no: en stor stol
- en: a large chair
- no: et stort bord
- en: a large table
- no: store stoler
- en: large chairs
- no: den store stolen
- en: the large chair
:::

The basic meaning of **stor** remains “large,” but its ending changes.

## Indefinite singular forms {#indefinite-singular-forms role=model objectives=obj-adjective-agreement-01}

An indefinite noun phrase refers to an object without identifying it as a particular known object: **en stol**, **ei lampe**, or **et bord**.

With many regular adjectives, masculine and feminine nouns use the basic adjective form. Neuter nouns add **-t**.

- Masculine

::: examples
- no: en fin stol
- en: a nice chair
:::

- Feminine

::: examples
- no: ei fin lampe
- en: a nice lamp
:::

- Neuter

::: examples
- no: et fint bord
- en: a nice table
:::

The article and noun tell you which gender you are working with:

::: examples
- no: en kald dag
- en: a cold day
- no: ei kald natt
- en: a cold night
- no: et kaldt rom
- en: a cold room
:::

Notice that the feminine form of a regular adjective is normally the same as the masculine form:

::: examples
- no: en dyr stol
- en: an expensive chair
- no: ei dyr lampe
- en: an expensive lamp
:::

The neuter form normally adds **-t**:

::: examples
- no: et dyrt bord
- en: an expensive table
- no: et grønt skap
- en: a green cupboard
- no: et nytt teppe
- en: a new rug
:::

Some spelling changes can appear when the ending is added, as in **ny → nytt** and **grønn → grønt**. The important decision remains the same: an indefinite singular neuter noun requires the neuter adjective form.

**Compact rule**

For the regular adjectives used here:

- masculine singular: basic form
- feminine singular: basic form
- neuter singular: usually basic form plus **-t**

A common mistake is to add **-t** because the object itself seems neutral or inanimate. Grammatical gender, not real-world meaning, controls the form. **Bord** is neuter, so you say **et stort bord**. **Stol** is masculine, so you say **en stor stol**.

**Context:** Maja is putting temporary labels on newly unpacked objects before deciding where they belong.

**Learner action:** Complete short written noun phrases by changing a supplied adjective to fit the article and noun.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the learner uses the basic adjective form with masculine and feminine nouns and the neuter form with neuter nouns. Gender agreement is the scored evidence; vocabulary recognition is only context.

## Plural agreement {#plural-agreement role=contrast objectives=obj-adjective-agreement-01}

In the plural, gender no longer creates three different adjective choices. Regular adjectives normally take **-e** with all plural nouns.

::: examples
- no: store stoler
- en: large chairs
- no: store lamper
- en: large lamps
- no: store bord
- en: large tables
:::

Compare singular and plural:

::: examples
- no: en grønn stol
- en: a green chair
- no: grønne stoler
- en: green chairs
- no: ei grønn lampe
- en: a green lamp
- no: grønne lamper
- en: green lamps
- no: et grønt skap
- en: a green cupboard
- no: grønne skap
- en: green cupboards
:::

The plural ending depends on number, not gender. This means that **grønne** works with plural masculine, feminine, and neuter nouns.

Back in the flat, Elias first carries one chair:

::: examples
- no: Han bærer en tung stol.
- en: He is carrying a heavy chair.
:::

Later he carries several:

::: examples
- no: Han bærer tunge stoler.
- en: He is carrying heavy chairs.
:::

**Request: unpacking-one-or-more**

**Purpose:** Separate singular agreement from plural agreement.

**Context:** Elias reports whether each moving box contains one object or several objects.

**Learner action:** Rewrite supplied adjective-and-noun phrases when the number changes from singular to plural or from plural to singular.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates selection of **-e** for regular plural adjectives. Number agreement is scored; gender forms appearing in singular prompts provide supporting context.

## Definite adjective phrases {#definite-adjective-phrases role=model objectives=obj-adjective-agreement-02}

A definite noun phrase identifies a particular known object: not simply “a large table,” but “the large table.”

Norwegian normally marks definiteness in three places when an adjective comes before the noun:

- **den**, **det**, or **de**
- the adjective, usually with **-e**
- the definite ending on the noun

Compare:

::: examples
- no: en stor stol
- en: a large chair
- no: den store stolen
- en: the large chair
- no: et stort bord
- en: a large table
- no: det store bordet
- en: the large table
- no: store stoler
- en: large chairs
- no: de store stolene
- en: the large chairs
:::

This pattern is sometimes called double definiteness because both the word before the adjective and the noun ending show that the phrase is definite.

Use:

- **den** with a definite singular masculine or feminine noun
- **det** with a definite singular neuter noun
- **de** with definite plural nouns

The regular adjective takes **-e** in all three patterns:

::: examples
- no: den fine stolen
- en: the nice chair
- no: den fine boka
- en: the nice book
- no: det fine rommet
- en: the nice room
- no: de fine stolene
- en: the nice chairs
:::

The same contrast appears in Maja’s instructions:

::: examples
- no: Ta en grønn stol.
- en: Take a green chair.
- no: Ta den grønne stolen.
- en: Take the green chair.
:::

The first instruction allows any green chair. The second points to a particular green chair that Maja and Elias can identify.

**Compact rule**

::: {.rule}
After **den**, **det**, and **de**, use the definite adjective form, normally ending in **-e**: **den store**, **det store**, **de store**.
:::

Do not keep the indefinite neuter **-t** after **det**. Compare:

::: examples
- no: et stort bord
- en: a large table
- no: det store bordet
- en: the large table
:::

A likely mistake is **det stort bordet**. The noun is still neuter, but definiteness now controls the adjective form, so **store** is required.

**Request: identify-the-known-object**

**Purpose:** Practise the change from indefinite to definite adjective phrases.

**Context:** Maja and Elias can see several similar pieces of furniture, and Maja identifies one particular object.

**Learner action:** Change written phrases meaning “a/an …” into phrases meaning “the …,” adding the appropriate definite word, adjective ending, and noun ending.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates use of adjective **-e** after **den**, **det**, and **de**. Definite adjective agreement is scored; noun definiteness supplies necessary context.

## Before the noun and after er {#before-the-noun-and-after-er role=contrast objectives=obj-adjective-agreement-01}

Compare two ways of describing Maja’s table:

::: examples
- no: Det store bordet står i stua.
- en: The large table is in the living room.
- no: Bordet er stort.
- en: The table is large.
:::

In the first sentence, **store** is inside the definite noun phrase **det store bordet**. It therefore has the definite **-e** form.

In the second sentence, **stort** comes after **er** and describes the neuter noun **bordet**. The noun happens to be definite, but the adjective is not part of the phrase “the table.” It agrees with the noun’s gender and number:

::: examples
- no: Stolen er stor.
- en: The chair is large.
- no: Boka er stor.
- en: The book is large.
- no: Bordet er stort.
- en: The table is large.
- no: Stolene er store.
- en: The chairs are large.
:::

This explains two forms from the opening scene:

::: examples
- no: Stua er stor.
- en: The living room is large.
- no: Kjøkkenet er lite.
- en: The kitchen is small.
:::

A common mistake is to use **-e** after **er** merely because the noun is definite. Do not say **bordet er store** when describing one table. Say **bordet er stort**. The **-e** form in **det store bordet** belongs to the definite phrase before the noun.

**Request: description-position-check**

**Purpose:** Distinguish adjective agreement inside a definite noun phrase from agreement after **er**.

**Context:** Elias writes short notes describing furniture, sometimes identifying an object and sometimes stating what it is like.

**Learner action:** Supply the correct form of a regular adjective in sentences where it either comes before a definite noun or follows **er**.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the learner uses **-e** inside **den/det/de** phrases without automatically carrying that form over to singular descriptions after **er**. Position within the description is the scored evidence.

{{exercise: description-position-check}}

## The irregular adjective liten {#the-irregular-adjective-liten role=model objectives=obj-adjective-agreement-01}

The adjective **liten**, “small” or “little,” is very common, but it does not follow the regular pattern throughout. Its forms need to be learned as a set.

**Indefinite singular**

::: examples
- no: en liten stol
- en: a small chair
- no: ei lita lampe
- en: a small lamp
- no: et lite bord
- en: a small table
:::

The masculine form is **liten**, the feminine form can be **lita** or **liten**, and the neuter form is **lite**. This chapter uses **lita** as the taught feminine model, but **liten** is also accepted with feminine nouns.

Compare **liten** with a regular adjective:

- Masculine — Regular adjective

::: examples
- no: en fin stol
- en: a nice chair
:::

- Masculine — *Liten*

::: examples
- no: en liten stol
- en: a small chair
:::

- Feminine — Regular adjective

::: examples
- no: ei fin lampe
- en: a nice lamp
:::

- Feminine — *Liten*

::: examples
- no: ei lita lampe
- en: a small lamp
- no: ei liten lampe
- en: a small lamp
:::

- Neuter — Regular adjective

::: examples
- no: et fint bord
- en: a nice table
:::

- Neuter — *Liten*

::: examples
- no: et lite bord
- en: a small table
:::

Do not build the neuter form by adding **-t** to **liten**. The correct form is **lite**, not **litent**.

In Bokmål, some speakers and writers use the common-gender alternative **en liten bok** instead of feminine **ei lita bok**. With **ei**, both **lita** and **liten** are accepted:

::: examples
- no: ei lita bok
- en: a small book
- no: ei liten bok
- en: a small book
- no: ei lita dør
- en: a small door
- no: ei liten dør
- en: a small door
:::

This chapter models **lita** with **ei**, but practice must also accept **liten**.

## Plural and definite forms of liten {#plural-and-definite-forms-of-liten role=contrast objectives=obj-adjective-agreement-01}

The plural of **liten** is **små**:

::: examples
- no: små stoler
- en: small chairs
- no: små lamper
- en: small lamps
- no: små bord
- en: small tables
:::

It is not formed as **litene** or **litne**. Use **små** for plural nouns regardless of gender.

The definite singular form is **lille**:

::: examples
- no: den lille stolen
- en: the small chair
- no: den lille boka
- en: the small book
- no: det lille bordet
- en: the small table
:::

The definite plural form returns to **små**:

::: examples
- no: de små stolene
- en: the small chairs
- no: de små lampene
- en: the small lamps
- no: de små bordene
- en: the small tables
:::

The complete pattern is therefore:

- Indefinite masculine singular — **liten**

::: examples
- no: en liten stol
- en: a small chair
:::

- Indefinite feminine singular — **lita** or **liten**

::: examples
- no: ei lita lampe
- en: a small lamp
- no: ei liten lampe
- en: a small lamp
:::

- Indefinite neuter singular — **lite**

::: examples
- no: et lite bord
- en: a small table
:::

- Indefinite plural — **små**

::: examples
- no: små bord
- en: small tables
:::

- Definite singular — **lille**

::: examples
- no: det lille bordet
- en: the small table
:::

- Definite plural — **små**

::: examples
- no: de små bordene
- en: the small tables
:::

After **er**, the gender and number forms also appear:

::: examples
- no: Stolen er liten.
- en: The chair is small.
- no: Lampa er lita.
- en: The lamp is small.
- no: Lampa er liten.
- en: The lamp is small.
- no: Bordet er lite.
- en: The table is small.
- no: Lampene er små.
- en: The lamps are small.
:::

Notice the contrast:

::: examples
- no: Det lille bordet står på kjøkkenet.
- en: The small table is in the kitchen.
- no: Bordet på kjøkkenet er lite.
- en: The table in the kitchen is small.
:::

In **det lille bordet**, the adjective is part of a definite noun phrase. In **bordet er lite**, it follows **er** and agrees with one neuter noun.

The contrast between **et lite bord** and **det lille bordet** is important. The first introduces or describes one small table without identifying it as already known. The second points to the particular small table already under discussion.

Regular **stor** shows a parallel contrast:

::: examples
- no: et stort bord
- en: a large table
- no: det store bordet
- en: the large table
:::

Although **liten** has irregular forms, the meaning decision is the same: gender matters in indefinite singular phrases, plural number requires a plural form, and definite singular phrases require a definite adjective form.

## A practical decision routine {#a-practical-decision-routine role=contrast objectives=obj-adjective-agreement-01}

When choosing an adjective form, use this short routine.

First, look at number. If the noun is plural, a regular adjective normally takes **-e**, while **liten** becomes **små**. This also handles definite plural phrases with **de**.

::: examples
- no: fine lamper
- en: nice lamps
- no: små lamper
- en: small lamps
- no: de fine lampene
- en: the nice lamps
- no: de små lampene
- en: the small lamps
:::

If the noun is singular, check whether the adjective is inside a definite phrase with **den** or **det**. Regular adjectives then normally take **-e**, and singular **liten** becomes **lille**.

::: examples
- no: den fine lampen
- en: the nice lamp
- no: den lille lampen
- en: the small lamp
- no: det fine bordet
- en: the nice table
- no: det lille bordet
- en: the small table
:::

If the phrase is indefinite singular, use gender:

::: examples
- no: en fin stol
- en: a nice chair
- no: ei fin lampe
- en: a nice lamp
- no: et fint bord
- en: a nice table
:::

With **liten**, retrieve the irregular set. For feminine nouns with **ei**, both forms shown are accepted:

::: examples
- no: en liten stol
- en: a small chair
- no: ei lita lampe
- en: a small lamp
- no: ei liten lampe
- en: a small lamp
- no: et lite bord
- en: a small table
:::

Finally, notice whether the adjective follows **er**. In that position, match gender and number rather than copying the definite **-e** form:

::: examples
- no: Bordet er fint.
- en: The table is nice.
- no: Det fine bordet står her.
- en: The nice table is here.
:::
