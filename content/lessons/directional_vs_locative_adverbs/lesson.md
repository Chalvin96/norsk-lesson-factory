## Finding the meeting room {#sec-finding-meeting-room role=orient objectives=obj-directional-vs-locative-adverbs-01}

Maja and Emil are attending an evening meeting at a community centre. Maja has already found the room, but Emil is still outside and calls her.

::: {.reading translation='Where are you?' dialogue_id='finding_meeting_room' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Hvor er du?
:::

::: {.reading translation='I’m already inside. I’m sitting upstairs on the second floor.' dialogue_id='finding_meeting_room' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jeg er allerede inne. Jeg sitter oppe i andre etasje.
:::

::: {.reading translation='I’m outside by the bus stop. Where is the entrance?' dialogue_id='finding_meeting_room' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Jeg står ute ved bussholdeplassen. Hvor er inngangen?
:::

::: {.reading translation='It’s there, next to the café. Can you see the blue sign?' dialogue_id='finding_meeting_room' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Den er der, ved siden av kafeen. Ser du det blå skiltet?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes. Should I go in there?' dialogue_id='finding_meeting_room' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ja. Skal jeg gå inn der?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, go inside and take the stairs up.' dialogue_id='finding_meeting_room' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Ja, gå inn og ta trappa opp.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Should I come over to you?' dialogue_id='finding_meeting_room' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Skal jeg komme bort til deg?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, come here. We’re inside room 204.' dialogue_id='finding_meeting_room' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Ja, kom hit. Vi sitter inne i rom 204.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Great. Then I’ll go there now.' dialogue_id='finding_meeting_room' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Fint. Da går jeg dit nå.
:::

Emil begins outside and moves inside. Maja is already upstairs, while Emil must go upstairs. From Maja’s point of view, Emil should come **hit**, “here, toward me.” From Emil’s point of view, he is going **dit**, “there, toward that place.”

These small words tell us whether someone or something is already in a place or is moving toward it.

## Location or direction? {#sec-location-or-direction role=model objectives=obj-directional-vs-locative-adverbs-01}

Norwegian often distinguishes a **location adverb (stedsadverb)** from a **directional adverb (retningsadverb)**.

A location adverb describes where someone or something is:

::: examples
- no: Maja er inne.
- en: Maja is inside.
:::

A directional adverb describes movement toward a place or a change of location:

::: examples
- no: Emil går inn.
- en: Emil goes inside.
:::

English often uses the same word in both situations. Norwegian frequently changes the form.

**Location: where someone is**

**Direction: where someone goes**

::: examples
- no: her
- en: here
- no: hit
- en: to here, this way
- no: der
- en: there
- no: dit
- en: to there, that way
- no: inne
- en: inside
- no: inn
- en: inside, inward
- no: ute
- en: outside
- no: ut
- en: outside, outward
- no: oppe
- en: upstairs, up there
- no: opp
- en: up, upward
- no: nede
- en: downstairs, down there
- no: ned
- en: down, downward
- no: hjemme
- en: at home
- no: hjem
- en: home, toward home
:::

The compact rule is:

- Use the location form when the action or situation remains in a place.
- Use the directional form when someone or something moves toward that place or changes location.

Compare the forms in matched situations:

::: examples
- no: Hun er inne.
- en: She is inside.
- no: Hun går inn.
- en: She goes inside.
- no: Barna leker ute.
- en: The children are playing outside.
- no: Barna løper ut.
- en: The children run outside.
- no: Jon er oppe.
- en: Jon is upstairs.
- no: Jon går opp.
- en: Jon goes upstairs.
- no: Jeg er hjemme.
- en: I am at home.
- no: Jeg går hjem.
- en: I am going home.
:::

**Purpose:** Help the learner notice the central meaning contrast before producing forms.

**Context:** Short descriptions of Emil’s route through the community centre and Maja’s position in the building.

**Learner action:** Decide whether each highlighted word describes an existing location or movement toward a location.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the learner can identify location versus direction from the whole situation, not merely from an English translation.

## A movement verb does not always require a directional form {#sec-movement-verb-meaning role=contrast objectives=obj-directional-vs-locative-adverbs-01}

The choice depends on meaning, not simply on whether the verb describes movement.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Emil går ut.
- en: Emil goes outside.
:::

Here, **ut** marks a change from inside to outside.

::: examples
- no: Emil går ute.
- en: Emil walks outside.
:::

Here, **ute** describes the place where the walking happens. Emil is already outside.

The same contrast appears elsewhere:

::: examples
- no: Hun løper inn.
- en: She runs inside.
- no: Hun løper inne.
- en: She runs indoors.
- no: De går opp.
- en: They go upstairs.
- no: De går oppe.
- en: They walk around upstairs.
- no: Jeg sykler hjem.
- en: I cycle home.
- no: Jeg sykler hjemme.
- en: I cycle at home.
:::

The final example could refer to using an exercise bike at home. The verb **sykler** does not determine the form by itself. The speaker’s intended meaning does.

A common mistake is to choose **inn**, **ut**, **opp**, or **ned** whenever a sentence contains a movement verb. Ask instead:

- Is the person crossing into or toward a new place?
- Or is the activity happening within a place?

**Purpose:** Prevent the misconception that all movement verbs automatically take directional forms.

**Context:** Paired everyday scenes involving walking, running, playing, and cycling.

**Learner action:** Choose the form that expresses either a change of place or the location of an ongoing activity.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the learner bases the choice on intended meaning rather than verb type. Vocabulary and tense serve only as context.

## Inside and outside: inne, inn, ute, ut {#sec-inside-outside-change role=model objectives=obj-directional-vs-locative-adverbs-01}

Use **inne** and **ute** for location:

::: examples
- no: Vi sitter inne.
- en: We are sitting inside.
- no: Hunden er ute.
- en: The dog is outside.
- no: Det er varmt inne.
- en: It is warm inside.
- no: De spiser ute.
- en: They are eating outside.
:::

Use **inn** and **ut** for direction or change:

::: examples
- no: Vi går inn.
- en: We go inside.
- no: Hunden løper ut.
- en: The dog runs outside.
- no: Kan du komme inn?
- en: Can you come in?
- no: Hun tar søpla ut.
- en: She takes the rubbish out.
:::

Notice the contrast in one continuous situation:

::: examples
- no: Kari står ute, men nå går hun inn. Etterpå sitter hun inne.
- en: Kari is outside, but now she goes inside. Afterwards she sits inside.
:::

The sequence is:

1. **ute**: her current location;
2. **inn**: her change of location;
3. **inne**: her new location.

**Purpose:** Connect location and direction forms across a short sequence rather than treating them as isolated vocabulary.

**Context:** A person arriving at a shop, entering, spending time inside, and leaving again.

**Learner action:** Supply the appropriate forms from the **inne–inn** and **ute–ut** pairs as the person’s location changes.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the learner can track whether each sentence describes a current position or a transition.

{{exercise: track_inside_outside_change}}

## Upstairs and downstairs: oppe, opp, nede, ned {#sec-upstairs-downstairs role=model objectives=obj-directional-vs-locative-adverbs-01}

Use **oppe** and **nede** when describing a location:

::: examples
- no: Maja er oppe i andre etasje.
- en: Maja is upstairs on the second floor.
- no: Kafeen er nede i første etasje.
- en: The café is downstairs on the first floor.
- no: Barna leker oppe.
- en: The children are playing upstairs.
- no: Skoene står nede.
- en: The shoes are downstairs.
:::

Use **opp** and **ned** for movement in a direction:

::: examples
- no: Emil går opp til andre etasje.
- en: Emil goes up to the second floor.
- no: Etter møtet går han ned til kafeen.
- en: After the meeting, he goes down to the café.
- no: Kan du ta boka opp?
- en: Can you bring the book up?
- no: Hun løper ned.
- en: She runs downstairs.
:::

Compare a complete change:

::: examples
- no: Først er Emil nede. Så går han opp. Nå er han oppe.
- en: First Emil is downstairs. Then he goes upstairs. Now he is upstairs.
:::

Again, the directional form describes the transition. The location form describes the position before or after it.

**Purpose:** Strengthen the distinction between vertical location and vertical movement.

**Context:** People moving between floors in the same community centre.

**Learner action:** Complete short descriptions with forms from **oppe–opp** and **nede–ned**.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates correct selection based on whether the person is located on a floor or moving between floors.

{{exercise: follow_vertical_direction}}

## At home and going home: hjemme and hjem {#sec-home-location-direction role=model objectives=obj-directional-vs-locative-adverbs-01}

The location form is **hjemme**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er hjemme.
- en: I am at home.
- no: Jobber du hjemme i dag?
- en: Are you working at home today?
- no: Vi spiser hjemme.
- en: We eat at home.
:::

The directional form is **hjem**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg går hjem.
- en: I am going home.
- no: Når kommer du hjem?
- en: When are you coming home?
- no: De kjører hjem etter møtet.
- en: They drive home after the meeting.
:::

A frequent mistake is to use **hjemme** for movement:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg går hjemme.
- en: I am walking at home, not going home.
:::

**Jeg går hjemme** does not normally mean *I am going home.* It means that the walking takes place at home.

For the intended direction, say:

::: examples
- no: Jeg går hjem.
- en: I am going home.
:::

Norwegian normally uses **hjem** directly after verbs such as **gå**, **reise**, **kjøre**, and **komme**:

::: examples
- no: Hun reiser hjem.
- en: She travels home.
- no: Vi kommer hjem sent.
- en: We come home late.
:::

**Purpose:** Establish the high-frequency contrast between being at home and moving homeward.

**Context:** Evening plans before and after work, school, or a meeting.

**Learner action:** Choose between **hjemme** and **hjem** according to whether the person remains at home or travels there.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates accurate use of the home pair without relying on English, where *home* can cover both meanings.

{{exercise: choose_home_location_or_direction}}

## Here and there: her, hit, der, dit {#sec-here-there-viewpoint role=model objectives=obj-directional-vs-locative-adverbs-01}

The pairs **her–hit** and **der–dit** also express location versus direction. They additionally depend on the speaker’s viewpoint.

**Her and hit**

Use **her** for a location at or near the speaker:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er her.
- en: I am here.
- no: Du kan sitte her.
- en: You can sit here.
- no: Det er stille her.
- en: It is quiet here.
:::

Use **hit** for movement toward the speaker’s location:

::: examples
- no: Kom hit!
- en: Come here!
- no: Kan du ta stolen hit?
- en: Can you bring the chair here?
- no: Når kom dere hit?
- en: When did you come here?
:::

In the opening dialogue, Maja says:

::: examples
- no: Kom hit.
- en: Come here.
:::

She is asking Emil to move toward her location.

**Der and dit**

Use **der** for a location away from the speaker or at a place already indicated:

::: examples
- no: Inngangen er der.
- en: The entrance is there.
- no: Bussen stopper der.
- en: The bus stops there.
- no: Vi kan vente der.
- en: We can wait there.
:::

Use **dit** for movement toward that other location:

::: examples
- no: Jeg går dit.
- en: I am going there.
- no: Kan du kjøre meg dit?
- en: Can you drive me there?
- no: De flytter bordet dit.
- en: They move the table there.
:::

In the dialogue, Emil says:

::: examples
- no: Da går jeg dit nå.
- en: Then I’ll go there now.
:::

Room 204 is Maja’s current location, so she calls it **her** and asks Emil to come **hit**. For Emil, it is another destination, so he calls it **der** or says that he is going **dit**.

Compare the viewpoints:

::: {.reading translation='Maja: I am here. Come here.' dialogue_id='viewpoint_compare' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Maja: Jeg er her. Kom hit.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Emil: You are there. I’m going there.' dialogue_id='viewpoint_compare' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Emil: Du er der. Jeg går dit.
:::

**Purpose:** Teach the viewpoint distinction within the location–direction contrast.

**Context:** Two people speaking by phone from different parts of a building.

**Learner action:** Select among **her**, **hit**, **der**, and **dit** according to the speaker’s location and the direction of movement.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates correct speaker-relative choice. Whether the place is near the speaker is scored; other directional pairs in the surrounding text are context only.

{{exercise: use_speaker_viewpoint}}

## Questions with hvor and statements with her or der {#sec-where-questions-statements role=contrast objectives=obj-directional-vs-locative-adverbs-01}

Use **hvor** to ask for an unknown place:

::: examples
- no: Hvor er Emil?
- en: Where is Emil?
- no: Hvor er inngangen?
- en: Where is the entrance?
- no: Hvor bor Maja?
- en: Where does Maja live?
:::

An answer identifies the place:

::: examples
- no: Emil er ute.
- en: Emil is outside.
- no: Inngangen er der.
- en: The entrance is there.
- no: Maja bor her.
- en: Maja lives here.
:::

Do not use **der** as the question word for *where* in a direct question. Compare:

::: examples
- no: Hvor er kafeen?
- en: Where is the café?
- no: Kafeen er der.
- en: The café is there.
:::

The question contains **hvor** because the place is unknown. The statement contains **der** because the speaker identifies or points to a place.

Norwegian also uses **hvor** when asking about a destination. The verb and context show whether the question concerns location or direction:

::: examples
- no: Hvor er du?
- en: Where are you?
- no: Jeg er hjemme.
- en: I am at home.
- no: Hvor skal du?
- en: Where are you going?
- no: Jeg skal hjem.
- en: I am going home.
- no: Hvor er Nora?
- en: Where is Nora?
- no: Hun er inne.
- en: She is inside.
- no: Hvor går Nora?
- en: Where is Nora going?
- no: Hun går inn.
- en: She is going inside.
:::

There is no separate directional form of **hvor** required here. The answer, however, still distinguishes location from direction.

**Purpose:** Separate the question word **hvor** from the pointing and location words **her** and **der**.

**Context:** Brief exchanges in which one person asks for an unknown place and another identifies it.

**Learner action:** Form or complete direct where-questions with **hvor**, then complete corresponding statements with an appropriate location word.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the learner uses **hvor** to request place information and does not substitute **der** merely because English uses *where* and *there* in related exchanges.

{{exercise: distinguish_where_question_from_there_statement}}

**Purpose:** Connect a single **hvor** question form with different kinds of answers.

**Context:** Questions about present location and immediate destinations during an evening out.

**Learner action:** Respond to each question with the correct location or directional form, using the verb and situation to determine the intended meaning.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates the location–direction choice in the answer. Question formation itself is supporting context rather than the primary scored goal.

## Common mistakes {#sec-common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-directional-vs-locative-adverbs-01}

**Using a location form for a destination**

If someone is moving from outside to inside, use **inn**, not **inne**:

::: examples
- no: Emil går inn.
- en: Emil goes inside.
- no: ✗ Emil går inne.
- en: That sentence means he walks while already indoors.
:::

Not **Emil går inne** if the intended meaning is that he enters. That sentence means he walks while already indoors.

**Using a directional form for a continuing location**

If an activity takes place outside, use **ute**:

::: examples
- no: Barna leker ute.
- en: The children are playing outside.
- no: ✗ Barna leker ut.
- en: It does not express this location meaning.
:::

**Barna leker ut** does not express this location meaning.

**Treating English “there” as one Norwegian word**

English *there* may correspond to either **der** or **dit**:

::: examples
- no: Hun er der.
- en: She is there.
- no: Hun går dit.
- en: She goes there.
:::

Ask whether *there* is a current location or a destination.

**Using der to ask where**

Use **hvor** in a direct question:

::: examples
- no: Hvor er toalettet?
- en: Where is the toilet?
:::

Then answer with a location:

::: examples
- no: Det er der.
- en: It is there.
:::

**Purpose:** Make likely meaning errors visible and correctable.

**Context:** Short statements about entering buildings, staying outdoors, going home, and asking for places.

**Learner action:** Identify the word that conflicts with the intended situation and replace it with the appropriate target form.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the learner can explain the correction through location, direction, or question-versus-statement meaning.

## Returning to Maja and Emil {#sec-returning-maja-emil role=model objectives=obj-directional-vs-locative-adverbs-01}

The meeting is over. Maja and Emil are still in room 204.

::: {.reading translation='The meeting is over now. Are you going home?' dialogue_id='returning_to_maja_emil' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Nå er møtet ferdig. Skal du hjem?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Not yet. I’m going down to the café.' dialogue_id='returning_to_maja_emil' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ikke ennå. Jeg går ned til kafeen.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Is the café downstairs by the entrance?' dialogue_id='returning_to_maja_emil' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Er kafeen nede ved inngangen?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, it’s there. Do you want to come there with me?' dialogue_id='returning_to_maja_emil' speaker_id='emil' speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ja, den er der. Vil du bli med dit?
:::

::: {.reading translation='No, thank you. I’ll stay here a little longer, and then I’ll go home.' dialogue_id='returning_to_maja_emil' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Nei takk. Jeg blir her litt til, og så går jeg hjem.
:::

Emil is currently **oppe**, although he does not need to say it again. He goes **ned**, and the café is **nede**. The café is **der** as a location and **dit** as a destination. Maja remains **her** before going **hjem**.

**Purpose:** Consolidate several target pairs within the original situation.

**Context:** Emil’s route from the bus stop to room 204 and later from room 204 to the café.

**Learner action:** Reconstruct a short written account of his changing locations using supplied sentence frames and the appropriate target forms.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates consistent switching between location forms and directional forms as the route progresses. Exact recall of the dialogue is not required.

## Transfer: arriving at a friend’s flat {#sec-transfer-new-visit role=recap objectives=obj-directional-vs-locative-adverbs-01}

Now change the situation. Nora is visiting Henrik, who lives in a block of flats. She calls him from the street. Henrik is in his flat, but Nora does not know which entrance or floor to use. Later, they plan to leave the building and go to Nora’s home.

The learner should be able to express ideas such as:

::: examples
- no: Nora er ute.
- en: Nora is outside.
- no: Henrik er inne.
- en: Henrik is inside.
- no: Nora går inn.
- en: Nora goes inside.
- no: Henrik sier: «Kom hit.»
- en: Henrik says, “Come here.”
- no: Nora sier: «Jeg kommer dit nå.»
- en: Nora says, “I’m coming there now.”
- no: Henrik er oppe i tredje etasje.
- en: Henrik is upstairs on the third floor.
- no: Nora går opp.
- en: Nora goes upstairs.
- no: Senere går de ned og ut.
- en: Later they go downstairs and outside.
- no: Til slutt går de hjem til Nora.
- en: Finally they go to Nora’s home.
:::

**Purpose:** Apply the target contrast in a new everyday situation rather than repeat the community-centre dialogue.

**Context:** Nora’s arrival at Henrik’s flat, movement through the building, and departure.

**Learner action:** Complete a connected written exchange and short route description using **hvor**, location forms, and corresponding directional forms.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates transfer of the same location–direction system to new speakers and places while maintaining a consistent viewpoint.
