## A film night {#film-night role=orient objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

Maja is organizing a film night at a small community center. Elias has offered to help, and Nora has come early with some food.

::: {.reading translation="Are you coming to the film night tonight, Elias?" dialogue_id=film-night-opening speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Kommer du på filmkvelden i kveld, Elias?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I’m coming at seven." dialogue_id=film-night-opening speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Ja, jeg kommer klokka sju.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Do you drink coffee?" dialogue_id=film-night-opening speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Drikker du kaffe?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, I don’t drink coffee." dialogue_id=film-night-opening speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Nei, jeg drikker ikke kaffe.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Don’t you drink tea either?" dialogue_id=film-night-opening speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Drikker du ikke te heller?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I do drink tea." dialogue_id=film-night-opening speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Jo, jeg drikker te.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I like tea." dialogue_id=film-night-opening speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Jeg liker te.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Me too." dialogue_id=film-night-opening speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Jeg også.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I don’t eat meat. I’ve brought a vegetarian pizza." dialogue_id=film-night-opening speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Jeg spiser ikke kjøtt. Jeg har med en vegetarisk pizza.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Neither do I. That works well." dialogue_id=film-night-opening speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Ikke jeg heller. Det passer bra.
:::

::: {.reading translation="But you aren’t coming late, are you?" dialogue_id=film-night-opening speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Men dere kommer ikke sent?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, we’re coming before seven." dialogue_id=film-night-opening speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Nei, vi kommer før sju.
:::

The three speakers use very short words, but each word does a different job. Elias uses **ja** to agree with an ordinary positive question and **nei** to give a negative answer. When Maja asks a negative question about tea, Elias uses **jo** because he wants to contradict the negative idea. Later, **også** adds Elias to Nora’s positive statement, while **heller** adds him to her negative statement.

## Three ways to answer {#three-ways-to-answer role=model objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

For the simple positive questions and negative questions with **ikke** taught in this lesson, Norwegian distinguishes between three basic responses:

- **ja** gives or confirms a positive answer;
- **nei** gives or confirms a negative answer;
- **jo** gives a positive answer that contradicts a negative question or statement.

For the question patterns taught here, the important choice is not simply between “yes” and “no.” Notice whether the question is a simple positive question or a negative question with **ikke**. If it contains **ikke**, decide whether you agree with its negative idea or contradict it.

## Answering a positive question {#answering-positive-question role=model objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

The simple positive yes/no questions taught here are presented without **ikke**.

::: examples
- no: Kommer du i kveld?
- en: Are you coming tonight?
:::

A positive answer begins with **ja**:

::: examples
- no: Ja, jeg kommer i kveld.
- en: Yes, I’m coming tonight.
:::

A negative answer begins with **nei**:

::: examples
- no: Nei, jeg kommer ikke i kveld.
- en: No, I’m not coming tonight.
:::

The same pattern works with other ordinary questions:

::: examples
- no: Bor du i Bergen?
- en: Do you live in Bergen?
- no: Ja, jeg bor i Bergen.
- en: Yes, I live in Bergen.
- no: Nei, jeg bor ikke i Bergen.
- en: No, I don’t live in Bergen.
- no: Liker Amir norsk kaffe?
- en: Does Amir like Norwegian coffee?
- no: Ja, han liker norsk kaffe.
- en: Yes, he likes Norwegian coffee.
- no: Nei, han liker ikke norsk kaffe.
- en: No, he doesn’t like Norwegian coffee.
:::

In conversation, a single **ja** or **nei** can be enough. A complete sentence is often clearer for learners because it shows exactly what is being confirmed or rejected.

## Compact rule {#positive-question-compact-rule role=contrast objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

For the simple positive questions taught here:

- use **ja** for a positive answer;
- use **nei** for a negative answer.

## Why Norwegian needs jo {#why-norwegian-needs-jo role=model objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

Return to Maja’s question:

::: examples
- no: Drikker du ikke te heller?
- en: Don’t you drink tea either?
:::

The question already contains a negative idea: perhaps Elias does not drink tea. Elias wants to reject that idea. He does drink tea, so he answers:

::: examples
- no: Jo, jeg drikker te.
- en: Yes, I do drink tea.
:::

Here **jo** means something like “yes, actually I do” or “on the contrary, yes.” English normally uses “yes,” sometimes with extra stress. Norwegian marks this contradiction with a separate word.

Compare the positive and negative forms:

- Positive question:

::: examples
- no: Drikker du te?
- en: Do you drink tea?
- no: Ja, jeg drikker te.
- en: Yes, I drink tea.
- no: Nei, jeg drikker ikke te.
- en: No, I don’t drink tea.
:::

- Negative question:

::: examples
- no: Drikker du ikke te?
- en: Don’t you drink tea?
- no: Jo, jeg drikker te.
- en: Yes, I do drink tea.
- no: Nei, jeg drikker ikke te.
- en: No, I don’t drink tea.
:::

The facts in the answers are identical. Only the wording of the question changes the positive response from **ja** to **jo**.

Consider another pair:

::: examples
- no: Skal Ingrid være med?
- en: Is Ingrid going to join us?
- no: Ja, hun skal være med.
- en: Yes, she is going to join us.
- no: Skal ikke Ingrid være med?
- en: Isn’t Ingrid going to join us?
- no: Jo, hun skal være med.
- en: Yes, she is going to join us.
:::

In the second exchange, **jo** corrects the suggestion that Ingrid will not join.

## Agreeing with a negative question {#agreeing-with-negative-question role=contrast objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

A negative question can also receive a negative answer. In that case, use **nei** because the negative idea is correct.

::: examples
- no: Kommer du ikke i morgen?
- en: Aren’t you coming tomorrow?
- no: Nei, jeg kommer ikke i morgen.
- en: No, I’m not coming tomorrow.
- no: Har dere ikke bil?
- en: Don’t you have a car?
- no: Nei, vi har ikke bil.
- en: No, we don’t have a car.
:::

In the film-night dialogue, Maja asks:

::: examples
- no: Men dere kommer ikke sent?
- en: But you aren’t coming late, are you?
:::

Nora replies:

::: examples
- no: Nei, vi kommer før sju.
- en: No, we’re coming before seven.
:::

Her **nei** confirms that they will not be late.

## Compact rule {#negative-question-compact-rule role=contrast objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

For the negative questions with **ikke** taught here:

- use **jo** to contradict the negative idea;
- use **nei** to confirm the negative idea.

## Common mistake: using ja after every “yes” {#common-mistake-ja-after-every-yes role=contrast objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

Learners often translate English “yes” directly as **ja**:

::: examples
- no: Kommer du ikke?
- en: Aren’t you coming?
:::

If the intended meaning is “Yes, I am coming,” the natural answer is:

::: examples
- no: Jo, jeg kommer.
- en: Yes, I am coming.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Ja, jeg kommer.
- en: ✗ Yes, I am coming.
:::

The full sentence makes the intended fact understandable, but **ja** misses the specific Norwegian contrast. Use **jo** when the positive answer pushes back against **ikke**.

## Contradicting a negative statement {#contradicting-negative-statement role=model objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

**Jo** is also useful when another person makes a negative statement and you correct it.

::: {.reading translation="Elias isn’t coming tonight." dialogue_id=correcting-negative-statements speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Elias kommer ikke i kveld.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I am. I’m coming at seven." dialogue_id=correcting-negative-statements speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Jo, jeg kommer klokka sju.
:::

::: {.reading translation="You don’t have tea." dialogue_id=correcting-negative-statements speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Dere har ikke te.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, we do. We have tea in the kitchen." dialogue_id=correcting-negative-statements speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jo, vi har te på kjøkkenet.
:::

The same principle applies: **jo** rejects a negative idea and replaces it with a positive one.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Du liker kaffe.
- en: You like coffee.
- no: Nei, jeg liker ikke kaffe.
- en: No, I don’t like coffee.
:::

Here the first statement is positive, so the correction is negative and begins with **nei**.

::: examples
- no: Du liker ikke te.
- en: You don’t like tea.
- no: Jo, jeg liker te.
- en: Yes, I do like tea.
:::

Here the first statement is negative, so the correction is positive and begins with **jo**.

Do not treat **jo** as a stronger version of **ja** in every situation. Its job here is specific: it contradicts something negative.

## Adding yourself with også {#adding-yourself-with-ogsa role=model objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

Nora says:

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker te.
- en: I like tea.
:::

Elias shares the same positive preference:

::: examples
- no: Jeg også.
- en: Me too.
:::

The word **også** means “also” or “too.” Use it when adding a person or fact to a positive statement.

::: examples
- no: Sara bor i Oslo. Per bor også i Oslo.
- en: Sara lives in Oslo. Per also lives in Oslo.
- no: Jeg kommer klokka sju. Nora kommer også klokka sju.
- en: I’m coming at seven. Nora is also coming at seven.
- no: Vi liker denne filmen. De liker den også.
- en: We like this film. They like it too.
:::

A short personal response is common:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er trøtt.
- en: I’m tired.
- no: Jeg også.
- en: Me too.
:::

A full sentence can express the same addition:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er også trøtt.
- en: I’m tired too.
:::

For this lesson, the central meaning is simple: **også** adds something to an affirmative statement.

## Adding yourself to a negative statement with heller {#adding-yourself-with-heller role=model objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

Nora says:

::: examples
- no: Jeg spiser ikke kjøtt.
- en: I don’t eat meat.
:::

Elias shares that negative fact:

::: examples
- no: Ikke jeg heller.
- en: Neither do I.
:::

In the patterns taught here, **heller** corresponds to “either” and adds a person or fact to a statement with negative meaning.

::: examples
- no: Maja drikker ikke kaffe. Elias drikker ikke kaffe heller.
- en: Maja doesn’t drink coffee. Elias doesn’t drink coffee either.
- no: Nora kommer ikke sent. Vi kommer ikke sent heller.
- en: Nora isn’t coming late. We aren’t coming late either.
- no: Jeg har ikke bil.
- en: I don’t have a car.
- no: Ikke jeg heller.
- en: Neither do I.
:::

In the full-clause negative-addition pattern taught here, retain **ikke** and add **heller**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker ikke kaffe heller.
- en: I don’t like coffee either.
:::

The short response places **ikke** first:

::: examples
- no: Ikke jeg heller.
- en: Neither do I.
:::

## Også and heller side by side {#ogsa-and-heller-side-by-side role=contrast objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

- Positive first statement and natural addition:

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker te.
- en: I like tea.
- no: Jeg også.
- en: Me too.
:::

- Negative first statement and natural addition:

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker ikke kaffe.
- en: I don’t like coffee.
- no: Ikke jeg heller.
- en: Neither do I.
:::

- Positive first statement and complete addition:

::: examples
- no: Nora kommer tidlig.
- en: Nora is coming early.
- no: Elias kommer også tidlig.
- en: Elias is also coming early.
:::

- Negative first statement and complete addition:

::: examples
- no: Nora kommer ikke sent.
- en: Nora isn’t coming late.
- no: Elias kommer ikke sent heller.
- en: Elias isn’t coming late either.
:::

For the matching additions taught here, the choice follows the first statement:

- positive statement → add **også**;
- statement with **ikke** → retain **ikke** and add **heller**, or use the short personal reply **Ikke jeg heller**.

## Common mistake: using også after a negative statement {#common-mistake-ogsa-after-negative role=contrast objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

English learners may try to use “also” everywhere. In Norwegian, this can produce an unnatural or unintended response.

::: examples
- no: Jeg drikker ikke kaffe.
- en: I don’t drink coffee.
:::

To share the negative fact, say:

::: examples
- no: Jeg drikker ikke kaffe heller.
- en: I don’t drink coffee either.
:::

Or:

::: examples
- no: Ikke jeg heller.
- en: Neither do I.
:::

Do not remove the negative meaning. In the full-clause pattern taught here, retain **ikke** and add **heller**.

## Keeping response and addition separate {#keeping-response-and-addition-separate role=contrast objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

Response words and addition words answer different questions.

**Ja**, **nei**, and **jo** respond to a question or correct an assumption:

::: examples
- no: Kommer du? — Ja.
- en: Are you coming? — Yes.
- no: Kommer du ikke? — Jo.
- en: Aren’t you coming? — Yes, I am.
- no: Kommer du? — Nei.
- en: Are you coming? — No.
:::

**Også** and **heller** connect your fact to an earlier statement:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kommer. — Jeg også.
- en: I’m coming. — Me too.
- no: Jeg kommer ikke. — Ikke jeg heller.
- en: I’m not coming. — Neither am I.
:::

Sometimes a conversation contains both jobs, but the words should not be treated as interchangeable.

::: {.reading translation="Aren’t you coming tonight?" dialogue_id=response-and-addition speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Kommer du ikke i kveld?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I am coming. Maja is coming too." dialogue_id=response-and-addition speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Jo, jeg kommer. Maja kommer også.
:::

Here **jo** contradicts Nora’s negative question. **Også** then adds Maja to the positive fact.

Another example:

::: {.reading translation="Do you drink coffee?" dialogue_id=response-and-negative-addition speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Drikker du kaffe?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, I don’t drink coffee. Nora doesn’t drink coffee either." dialogue_id=response-and-negative-addition speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Nei, jeg drikker ikke kaffe. Nora drikker ikke kaffe heller.
:::

Here **nei** gives a negative answer. **Heller** then adds Nora to that negative fact.

## Quick review {#quick-review role=recap objectives=obj-answering-ja-nei-jo-01}

Without looking back, explain when this lesson uses **jo** rather than **ja**. Then respond to one simple positive question, one negative question with **ikke** that you contradict, and one negative question with **ikke** that you confirm. Finally, add yourself to one affirmative statement with **også** and one statement with **ikke** using **Ikke jeg heller**. Successful retrieval shows that you can distinguish the taught response patterns from affirmative and negative addition.
