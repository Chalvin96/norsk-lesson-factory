## A message about Saturday’s event {#a-message-about-saturdays-event role=orient objectives=obj-slippe-aa-or-la-vaere-aa-01}

Nora and Emil are helping at a neighborhood event on Saturday. The organizer, Sigrid, has sent them an update.

::: {.reading translation="Have you received a message from Sigrid?" dialogue_id="saturday-event" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Har du fått melding fra Sigrid?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. We don’t have to meet at seven. The caretaker will open the venue for us." dialogue_id="saturday-event" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ja. Vi slipper å møte klokka sju. Vaktmesteren åpner lokalet for oss.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s good. And we don’t have to carry the tables because they’re being delivered." dialogue_id="saturday-event" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Så bra. Og vi slipper å bære bordene, fordi de blir levert.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Exactly. But I’m choosing not to cycle there. Apparently, it’s going to be very icy." dialogue_id="saturday-event" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Nettopp. Men jeg lar være å sykle dit. Det skal visst bli veldig glatt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Has Sigrid said that you can’t cycle?" dialogue_id="saturday-event" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Har Sigrid sagt at du ikke kan sykle?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, it’s my own choice. I’ll take the bus." dialogue_id="saturday-event" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Nei, det er mitt eget valg. Jeg tar bussen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I was actually going to bake two cakes, but Sigrid has ordered cakes from a bakery." dialogue_id="saturday-event" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Jeg skulle egentlig bake to kaker, men Sigrid har bestilt kaker fra et bakeri.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then you don’t have to bake." dialogue_id="saturday-event" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Da slipper du å bake.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. But I’ll refrain from buying more coffee. We already have enough." dialogue_id="saturday-event" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja. Men jeg lar være å kjøpe mer kaffe. Vi har allerede nok.
:::

Several planned actions are no longer required, while two other actions will not happen by personal choice. Nora and Emil no longer need to arrive early, carry tables, or bake because someone else has made arrangements. By contrast, Emil personally decides not to cycle, and Nora personally decides not to buy more coffee.

In the situations taught here, **slippe å** expresses absent necessity, while **la være å** expresses deliberate non-action.

## When a requirement disappears: slippe å {#when-a-requirement-disappears role=contrast objectives=obj-slippe-aa-or-la-vaere-aa-01}

Use **slippe å** when an action is not necessary or someone is spared a requirement, task, or burden. In the situations practiced here, another person, a rule, an arrangement, or the circumstances remove the need.

A useful English equivalent is **not have to**. Depending on context, it can also mean **be spared having to**.

::: examples
- no: Vi slipper å møte klokka sju.
- en: We don’t have to meet at seven.
:::

The original plan required an early meeting. The caretaker’s help removes that requirement.

::: examples
- no: Nora slipper å bake kakene.
- en: Nora doesn’t have to bake the cakes.
:::

The bakery order means that baking is no longer necessary.

::: examples
- no: Du slipper å betale for billetten.
- en: You don’t have to pay for the ticket.
:::

Someone else may be paying, or the ticket may be free. The important point is that payment is not required from you.

::: examples
- no: Elevene slipper å ta prøven på nytt.
- en: The pupils don’t have to take the test again.
:::

A teacher or another circumstance has removed the requirement.

::: examples
- no: På grunn av regnet slapp vi å vanne hagen.
- en: Because of the rain, we didn’t have to water the garden.
:::

Here, weather—not a personal decision—made the task unnecessary.

In this lesson’s requirement and task examples, the expression tells us that the action is not necessary. That lack of obligation alone does not determine whether the optional action occurs. In the following example, the speaker may still decide to call, but that possibility comes from the context rather than from **slippe å** itself.

::: examples
- no: Jeg slipper å ringe henne, men jeg gjør det kanskje likevel.
- en: I don’t have to call her, but I may do it anyway.
:::

**Checkpoint: finding the removed requirement**

Return to Nora and Emil’s conversation. Identify each use of **slippe å** and state what removed the requirement: the caretaker, the delivery, or the bakery order.

This activity belongs here because these dialogue examples contrast arrangements that remove requirements with personal choices not to act. A successful response would connect each task that is no longer required to the arrangement described in the dialogue rather than claim that Nora or Emil necessarily does not perform it.

## When someone intentionally does nothing: la være å {#when-someone-intentionally-does-nothing role=contrast objectives=obj-slippe-aa-or-la-vaere-aa-01}

Use **la være å** when a person deliberately refrains from an action. The action is possible, but the person chooses not to do it.

Useful English equivalents include **refrain from**, **choose not to**, and sometimes simply **not do**.

::: examples
- no: Emil lar være å sykle fordi veien er glatt.
- en: Emil chooses not to cycle because the road is icy.
:::

Nobody has removed a cycling requirement. Cycling was an available option, and Emil rejects it.

::: examples
- no: Nora lar være å kjøpe mer kaffe.
- en: Nora refrains from buying more coffee.
:::

She could buy coffee, but she decides that it is unnecessary.

::: examples
- no: Jeg lar være å svare når jeg er sint.
- en: I refrain from replying when I’m angry.
:::

The speaker deliberately waits instead of responding.

::: examples
- no: Han lot være å kommentere feilen.
- en: He chose not to comment on the mistake.
:::

He had an opportunity to comment but intentionally remained silent.

::: examples
- no: Vi lot være å bestille dessert.
- en: We decided not to order dessert.
:::

Ordering dessert was possible, but the group chose not to do it.

**Checkpoint: identifying deliberate non-action**

Look again at Emil’s decision about cycling and Nora’s decision about coffee. Explain what each person could have done and why the final non-action counts as a choice.

This checkpoint makes the contrast visible before you begin producing the expressions yourself. A successful response would mention that the actions remain possible and that Emil or Nora intentionally rejects them.

{{exercise: identify_deliberate_non_action}}

## The central contrast {#the-central-contrast role=contrast objectives=obj-slippe-aa-or-la-vaere-aa-01}

Compare the two expressions with exactly the same action:

::: examples
- no: Jeg slipper å kjøre til jobb i morgen.
- en: I don’t have to drive to work tomorrow.
:::

Perhaps a colleague will collect the speaker, or the speaker can work from home. An external arrangement has removed the need to drive.

::: examples
- no: Jeg lar være å kjøre til jobb i morgen.
- en: I’m choosing not to drive to work tomorrow.
:::

Driving is still an option, but the speaker has decided against it.

The same contrast appears here:

::: examples
- no: Sara slipper å lage middag.
- en: Sara doesn’t have to make dinner.
:::

Someone else is cooking, or dinner has already been prepared.

::: examples
- no: Sara lar være å lage middag.
- en: Sara chooses not to make dinner.
:::

Sara intentionally does not cook. Without more context, listeners may wonder what she plans to eat instead.

And here:

::: examples
- no: Vi slipper å sende inn skjemaet.
- en: We don’t have to submit the form.
:::

The form is not required.

::: examples
- no: Vi lar være å sende inn skjemaet.
- en: We choose not to submit the form.
:::

Submitting it is possible, perhaps even expected, but the group deliberately refrains.

A quick question can guide your choice in the situations practiced here:

- Is the action no longer necessary, as in this lesson’s arrangement-based examples? Use **slippe å**.
- Did someone intentionally refrain from an available action? Use **la være å**.

## Form and placement {#form-and-placement role=model objectives=obj-slippe-aa-or-la-vaere-aa-01}

Both expressions are followed by **å** and an infinitive.

::: examples
- no: Hun slipper å vente.
- en: She doesn’t have to wait.
- no: Hun lar være å vente.
- en: She chooses not to wait.
:::

The examples above mostly use **slipper å** and **lar være å**. For recognition, you may also encounter the past forms **slapp å** and **lot være å**.

::: examples
- no: I går slapp jeg å jobbe overtid.
- en: Yesterday I didn’t have to work overtime.
- no: I går lot jeg være å svare på meldingen.
- en: Yesterday I chose not to reply to the message.
:::

No tense choice is practiced here; focus on recognizing absent necessity with **slippe å** and deliberate non-action with **la være å**.

**Checkpoint: controlled contrast**

For a set of short situations, complete one sentence with the fixed present frame **slipper å** or **lar være å**. Each item supplies the following infinitive, so decide only whether the situation presents absent necessity or a personal decision not to act.

This controlled activity assesses only the need-versus-choice distinction. A successful response would select **slipper å** for absent necessity and **lar være å** for deliberate non-action.

{{exercise: complete_fixed_need_or_choice_frames}}

## What the two expressions do not mean {#what-the-two-expressions-do-not-mean role=contrast objectives=obj-slippe-aa-or-la-vaere-aa-01}

**In this lesson’s requirement and task examples, slippe å does not guarantee non-action**

::: examples
- no: Jeg slipper å delta på møtet.
- en: I don’t have to attend the meeting.
:::

This does not necessarily mean that the speaker will stay away. Attendance is optional.

If the speaker has made a deliberate decision not to attend, **la være å** expresses that choice more directly.

::: examples
- no: Jeg lar være å delta på møtet.
- en: I’m choosing not to attend the meeting.
:::

**La være å does not remove a requirement**

Suppose a manager says that a report is no longer necessary.

::: examples
- no: Du slipper å skrive rapporten.
- en: You don’t have to write the report.
:::

Using **la være å** would shift the meaning toward deliberate non-action:

::: examples
- no: Hun lar være å skrive rapporten.
- en: She chooses not to write the report.
:::

This describes a person who intentionally does not write it; it does not make a claim about whether writing remains possible or expected.

**Keep the infinitive marker**

Both frames require **å** before the following infinitive.

::: examples
- no: Vi slipper å rydde.
- en: We don’t have to tidy up.
- no: Vi lar være å rydde.
- en: We choose not to tidy up.
:::

Do not drop **å** after either expression.

## Practice: explain the difference {#practice-explain-the-difference role=model objectives=obj-slippe-aa-or-la-vaere-aa-01}

Consider pairs about everyday tasks such as paying a fee, attending a meeting, cooking dinner, taking a bus, or answering a message. For each pair, use the supplied context to explain which sentence says the task is not required because of an external event and which presents intentional non-action. Add one plausible reason for each interpretation.

This activity develops meaning rather than mechanical substitution. In this bounded practice, a successful response would connect the **slippe å** sentence to the supplied waiver or arrangement and the **la være å** sentence to an intentional choice instead of translating both as a general negative.

For example, a fee might be waived by an organizer, while a person might deliberately refrain from paying because they dispute it. Those situations are not interchangeable, even though payment does not occur in either one.

{{exercise: explain_external_requirement_or_intentional_non_action}}

## Practice: build a cause before choosing the phrase {#practice-build-a-cause-before-choosing-the-phrase role=model objectives=obj-slippe-aa-or-la-vaere-aa-01}

Create short two-sentence situations. In the first sentence, establish either an outside arrangement or a personal decision. In the second, summarize the result with **slippe å** or **la være å**.

Use contexts such as:

- a colleague taking over a work task;
- bad weather influencing a travel decision;
- a shop providing free delivery;
- someone deciding not to send an angry message;
- a canceled appointment;
- someone avoiding sweets for the day.

Do not choose the expression until the assigned context is clear. In these practice situations, a successful response would make the second sentence express either the absent necessity or the deliberate choice established by the first.

## Back to Saturday’s event {#back-to-saturdays-event role=model objectives=obj-slippe-aa-or-la-vaere-aa-01}

On Friday evening, Sigrid sends another message.

::: {.reading translation="The venue has already been decorated, so you don’t have to come early." dialogue_id="saturday-event-follow-up" speaker_id="sigrid" speaker_name="Sigrid" character_id="sigrid"}
Lokalet er allerede pyntet, så dere slipper å komme tidlig.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Then I won’t set the alarm for six." dialogue_id="saturday-event-follow-up" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Flott. Da lar jeg være å sette alarmen på seks.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I also don’t have to collect the cakes. The bakery is delivering them." dialogue_id="saturday-event-follow-up" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Jeg slipper også å hente kakene. Bakeriet kjører dem ut.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Are you going to buy flowers on the way?" dialogue_id="saturday-event-follow-up" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Skal du kjøpe blomster på veien?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, I won’t. We already have enough decorations." dialogue_id="saturday-event-follow-up" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Nei, jeg lar være. Vi har nok pynt allerede.
:::

Notice how this event supports both meanings in the dialogue. Because the venue is ready, Emil does not need to arrive early. He then personally decides not to set an early alarm. Similarly, Nora does not need to collect the cakes because of the delivery, while her decision about flowers remains her own.

**Final contextual practice**

Continue the preparations with several short updates. Include at least one task removed by Sigrid, a delivery service, the weather, or another arrangement, and at least one action that Nora or Emil deliberately decides not to perform.

This final practice returns to the chapter’s original situation while requiring independent use of the contrast. In these updates, a successful response would use **slippe å** for a task that is no longer required and include the assigned arrangement that explains the situation, while using **la være å** for an intentional choice.

## Retrieval and review {#retrieval-and-review role=recap objectives=obj-slippe-aa-or-la-vaere-aa-01}

Without looking back, complete these two ideas in Norwegian and briefly explain the difference:

1. Something changed, so you no longer need to do a task.
2. The task remains possible, but you deliberately choose not to do it.

A successful review uses **slippe å** for the first meaning and **la være å** for the second.
