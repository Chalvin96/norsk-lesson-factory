The conversation contains two common ways of forming the passive in modern Bokmål:

::: examples
- no: Alle søknader registreres automatisk.
- en: All applications are registered automatically.
- no: Først blir søknaden kontrollert.
- en: First, the application is checked.
:::

Both sentences focus on what happens to an application rather than on who performs the action. The choice between the two passive forms depends partly on time and partly on how the speaker presents the action.

## What the passive helps you say {#what-the-passive-helps-you-say role=model objectives=obj-complex-passives-01}

In an active sentence, the person or organization performing an action is normally the subject.

::: examples
- no: En saksbehandler kontrollerer søknaden.
- en: A case officer checks the application.
:::

In the passive voice (passiv), the affected person or thing becomes the subject.

::: examples
- no: Søknaden blir kontrollert av en saksbehandler.
- en: The application is checked by a case officer.
:::

The passive is useful when the performer is unknown, obvious, unimportant, or less important than the procedure or result. Eirik is explaining what happens to Maya’s application, so he often leaves the municipal employees in the background.

Modern Bokmål commonly uses two passive constructions:

- the s-passive (s-passiv), such as behandles
- the bli-passive (bli-passiv), such as blir behandlet

You already know the basic forms. This chapter concentrates on choosing and building them in present, modal, past, and perfect contexts.

## Present time: procedure or event {#present-time-procedure-or-event role=contrast objectives=obj-complex-passives-01}

The s-passive is especially common when describing a general procedure, repeated practice, rule, or established process.

::: examples
- no: Søknader registreres automatisk.
- en: Applications are registered automatically.
- no: Tegningene vurderes av teknisk avdeling.
- en: The drawings are assessed by the technical department.
- no: Vedtaket sendes digitalt.
- en: The decision is sent digitally.
:::

These sentences describe how the system normally works. They do not direct attention to one particular moment of action.

The bli-passive uses a form of bli followed by a past participle (perfektum partisipp):

::: examples
- no: Søknaden blir kontrollert nå.
- en: The application is being checked now.
- no: Vedlegget blir registrert i morgen.
- en: The attachment will be registered tomorrow.
- no: Saken blir avgjort neste uke.
- en: The case will be decided next week.
:::

Here the speaker presents the action more clearly as an event affecting a particular application, attachment, or case.

Compare the framing:

::: examples
- no: Alle vedlegg kontrolleres før saken behandles.
- en: All attachments are checked before the case is processed.
- no: Vedlegget ditt blir kontrollert av Eirik nå.
- en: Your attachment is being checked by Eirik now.
:::

The first sentence gives a general procedure. The second reports a particular event in progress.

The distinction is useful, but it is not absolute. Formal Norwegian often uses an s-passive for a current process as well:

::: examples
- no: Saken behandles nå.
- en: The case is being processed now.
:::

This is natural in status messages, official reports, and administrative language. In ordinary conversation, blir behandlet can make the unfolding event more explicit:

::: examples
- no: Saken blir behandlet nå.
- en: The case is being processed now.
:::

A compact working tendency is:

**The present s-passive is common for routines, rules, procedures, and concise formal descriptions. Blir plus the past participle often foregrounds a particular event or what is happening to a particular subject. Context and style can make either construction natural in some cases.**

## Forming the present constructions {#forming-the-present-constructions role=model objectives=obj-complex-passives-01}

For many verbs ending in unstressed -e, the s-passive is formed by replacing final -e with -es:

::: examples
- no: å behandle → behandles
- en: to process → is/are processed
- no: å kontrollere → kontrolleres
- en: to check → is/are checked
- no: å registrere → registreres
- en: to register → is/are registered
- no: å sende → sendes
- en: to send → is/are sent
:::

For an infinitive without final -e, -s is normally added:

::: examples
- no: å motta → mottas
- en: to receive → is/are received
:::

The present bli-passive uses blir plus the past participle:

::: examples
- no: Søknaden blir behandlet.
- en: The application is being processed.
- no: Vedlegget blir sendt.
- en: The attachment is being sent.
- no: Meldingen blir mottatt av riktig avdeling.
- en: The message is received by the correct department.
:::

Do not combine the two passive markers.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Søknaden blir behandles.
- en: 'Incorrect: The application is being processed.'
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Søknaden blir behandlet.
- en: The application is being processed.
:::

After blir, use the past participle behandlet, not the s-passive behandles.

## Passive forms after modal verbs {#passive-forms-after-modal-verbs role=contrast objectives=obj-complex-passives-01}

Modal verbs such as må, skal, kan, bør, and må not take a present-tense verb after them. They are followed by an infinitive. In passive constructions, the s-passive infinitive is extremely common.

::: examples
- no: Vedlegget må sendes inn i dag.
- en: The attachment must be submitted today.
- no: Søknaden skal behandles innen tre uker.
- en: The application is to be processed within three weeks.
- no: Feilen kan rettes digitalt.
- en: The error can be corrected digitally.
- no: Personopplysninger bør fjernes fra tegningen.
- en: Personal information should be removed from the drawing.
:::

These constructions are concise and natural for requirements, possibilities, recommendations, and scheduled procedures.

A modal verb can also be followed by bli plus a past participle:

::: examples
- no: Søknaden kan bli avslått.
- en: The application may be rejected.
- no: Saken skal bli avgjort neste uke.
- en: The case will be decided next week.
- no: Vedlegget må bli registrert før fredag.
- en: The attachment must be registered before Friday.
:::

The construction with bli often gives more weight to the event or resulting change. This can matter when the speaker is making a prediction, emphasizing an outcome, or assuring someone that a particular event will occur.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Alle søknader skal behandles innen tre uker.
- en: All applications are to be processed within three weeks.
- no: Søknaden din skal bli behandlet innen tre uker.
- en: Your application will be processed within three weeks.
:::

The first fits a general service rule, while the second fits a promise or prediction about one application. Because the subjects also differ, this pair illustrates common contexts rather than isolating the effect of the passive construction.

Another useful contrast is:

::: examples
- no: Opplysningene kan endres på nettsiden.
- en: The information can be changed on the website.
- no: Opplysningene kan bli endret senere.
- en: The information may be changed later.
:::

In the first sentence, kan expresses practical possibility: the website allows the change. In the second, the later time reference supports uncertainty about a future event. Because the complements differ, this pair also does not isolate the passive construction.

To hold the other wording constant, compare:

::: examples
- no: Søknaden din kan behandles i morgen.
- en: Your application can be processed tomorrow.
- no: Søknaden din kan bli behandlet i morgen.
- en: Your application may be processed tomorrow.
:::

Both are natural. The modal s-passive is concise and can sound neutral or procedural; modal plus bli can foreground the possible occurrence more explicitly. This is a viewpoint tendency, not a categorical difference.

After a modal, Norwegian uses an infinitive rather than a present-tense verb. In this sentence, sendes is the passive infinitive:

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Vedlegget må sendes inn.
- en: The attachment must be submitted.
:::

The passive infinitive sendes has the same spelling as the present s-passive used in other contexts, but its function after må is infinitive. Do not add bli merely because English uses be. If bli is used after a modal, it must also be infinitive: må bli, not må blir.

Modal plus s-passive is often concise for a rule, obligation, possibility, or procedure. Modal plus bli plus the participle can foreground a particular event or outcome. These are tendencies, and both constructions may be natural for the same event with a modest difference in framing.

## Past time: use ble plus the past participle {#past-time-use-ble-plus-the-past-participle role=model objectives=obj-complex-passives-01}

For a completed or ongoing passive event in the past, modern Bokmål normally uses the past tense (preteritum) of bli: ble.

::: examples
- no: Søknaden ble mottatt for åtte dager siden.
- en: The application was received eight days ago.
- no: Vedlegget ble registrert i morges.
- en: The attachment was registered this morning.
- no: Tegningene ble kontrollert av teknisk avdeling.
- en: The drawings were checked by the technical department.
- no: Saken ble ikke avgjort på møtet.
- en: The case was not decided at the meeting.
:::

The time expression often makes the past reference clear, but ble itself carries the past tense.

Compare present and past:

::: examples
- no: Søknaden blir kontrollert nå.
- en: The application is being checked now.
- no: Søknaden ble kontrollert i går.
- en: The application was checked yesterday.
:::

Do not use blir for a finished event simply because English uses a form of be.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Søknaden blir kontrollert i går.
- en: 'Incorrect: The application was checked yesterday.'
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Søknaden ble kontrollert i går.
- en: The application was checked yesterday.
:::

Past s-passive forms can occur in older, literary, or highly formal language, but they are not the productive everyday choice you need here. For ordinary modern Bokmål, use ble plus the past participle to report a past passive event.

Eirik does this when he checks Maya’s timeline:

::: examples
- no: Søknaden ble mottatt på mandag.
- en: The application was received on Monday.
- no: Den ble fordelt til en saksbehandler på onsdag.
- en: It was assigned to a case officer on Wednesday.
- no: Tegningene ble vurdert på fredag.
- en: The drawings were assessed on Friday.
:::

The sequence is clear because each passive event is anchored at a past time.

## Perfect contexts: har blitt plus the past participle {#perfect-contexts-har-blitt-plus-the-past-participle role=contrast objectives=obj-complex-passives-01}

The present perfect (presens perfektum) connects a past event with the present. The present-perfect bli-passive practiced here uses har blitt plus the past participle.

::: examples
- no: Vedlegget har blitt registrert.
- en: The attachment has been registered.
- no: Søknaden har blitt kontrollert.
- en: The application has been checked.
- no: Saken har ikke blitt avgjort ennå.
- en: The case has not been decided yet.
- no: Fristen har blitt endret.
- en: The deadline has been changed.
:::

This construction is useful when the current result matters or when the exact past time is not the main point.

Compare the past and the present perfect:

::: examples
- no: Vedlegget ble registrert klokka ni.
- en: The attachment was registered at nine o’clock.
- no: Vedlegget har blitt registrert, så saken kan behandles videre.
- en: The attachment has been registered, so the case can be processed further.
:::

The first sentence locates the event at a finished past time. The second emphasizes its present relevance: the registration is now complete, and the process can continue.

Within this pattern, the three parts retain this relative order:

**har + blitt + past participle**

::: examples
- no: Søknaden har blitt godkjent.
- en: The application has been approved.
:::

A common mistake is to omit blitt.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Søknaden har godkjent.
- en: 'Not: The application has approved. (for the intended passive meaning)'
:::

This changes the grammar and suggests that the application itself has approved something.

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Søknaden har blitt godkjent.
- en: The application has been approved.
:::

Another mistake is to use an s-passive after har.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Søknaden har behandles.
- en: 'Incorrect: The application has been processed.'
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Søknaden har blitt behandlet.
- en: The application has been processed.
:::

In the perfect passive, blitt carries the passive construction and behandlet is the past participle.

## A practical decision path {#a-practical-decision-path role=contrast objectives=obj-complex-passives-01}

When you need a passive sentence, ask three questions.

First, is the sentence a general rule, routine, or concise procedural description in present time? An s-passive is often the natural choice.

::: examples
- no: Dokumentene lagres digitalt.
- en: The documents are stored digitally.
:::

Second, does a modal express a requirement, possibility, recommendation, or scheduled procedure? A modal plus s-passive is normally concise and idiomatic.

::: examples
- no: Dokumentene må lagres sikkert.
- en: The documents must be stored securely.
:::

Third, are you presenting a particular passive event in the present, past, or perfect? The bli-passive lets you mark these time perspectives with the appropriate form of bli plus the past participle.

::: examples
- no: Dokumentet blir kontrollert nå.
- en: The document is being checked now.
- no: Dokumentet ble kontrollert i går.
- en: The document was checked yesterday.
- no: Dokumentet har blitt kontrollert.
- en: The document has been checked.
:::

In present-time formal or administrative language, an s-passive may also describe a particular current event naturally.

Do not treat s-passive and bli-passive as mechanically interchangeable in every sentence. They often describe similar real-world actions, but they can organize the message differently. At B2 level, good control means choosing a form that fits both the time reference and the speaker’s viewpoint.

## Short retrieval review {#short-retrieval-review role=recap objectives=obj-complex-passives-01}

Without returning to the explanations, give four brief passive updates about a new service situation: one general present procedure using an s-passive, one requirement using a modal plus s-passive, one event at a specified past time using ble plus the participle, and one completed event with a present result using har blitt plus the participle. These named constructions are required because this is form retrieval, not a claim that context alone always determines one natural passive.
