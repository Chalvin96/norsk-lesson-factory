## Before the meeting {#before-the-meeting role=orient objectives=obj-adverbs-time-frequency-degree-manner-place-01}

Maja and Erik are meeting at a café before a volunteer-group meeting. Maja wants to practise what she will say.

::: {.reading translation="Do you often come here this early?" dialogue_id=cafe-before-meeting speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Kommer du ofte hit så tidlig?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I usually work at home, but today I’m meeting you here." dialogue_id=cafe-before-meeting speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Ja, jeg jobber vanligvis hjemme, men i dag møter jeg deg her.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. I always speak quite fast in meetings." dialogue_id=cafe-before-meeting speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Bra. Jeg snakker alltid ganske fort på møter.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Today we’ll speak calmly together here first." dialogue_id=cafe-before-meeting speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
I dag snakker vi rolig sammen her først.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. I write the notes very clearly." dialogue_id=cafe-before-meeting speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Fint. Jeg skriver notatene veldig tydelig.
:::

::: {.reading translation="The project leader is coming soon. She usually cycles here, and she cycles very fast." dialogue_id=cafe-before-meeting speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Prosjektlederen kommer snart. Hun sykler vanligvis hit, og hun sykler veldig fort.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then we’ll find a table inside now." dialogue_id=cafe-before-meeting speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Da finner vi et bord inne nå.
:::

The conversation gives us more than information about a meeting. The adverbs tell us when things happen, how often they happen, how they happen, where they happen, and to what degree something is true.

Compare these details:

::: examples
- no: Prosjektlederen kommer.
- en: The project leader is coming.
- no: Prosjektlederen kommer snart.
- en: The project leader is coming soon.
- no: Hun sykler hit.
- en: She cycles here.
- no: Hun sykler vanligvis hit.
- en: She usually cycles here.
- no: Hun sykler fort.
- en: She cycles fast.
- no: Hun sykler veldig fort.
- en: She cycles very fast.
:::

Each adverb adds a particular kind of meaning. Correct position helps the listener connect that meaning to the right part of the sentence.

## What adverbs do {#what-adverbs-do role=model objectives=obj-adverbs-time-frequency-degree-manner-place-01}

An **adverb (adverb)** can describe a verb, an adjective, another adverb, or the situation expressed by a whole clause. In this chapter, we will work with five useful kinds of information:

- Time tells us when: **nå** (*now*), **snart** (*soon*), **i dag** (*today*), **om morgenen** (*in the morning*).
- Frequency tells us how often: **alltid** (*always*), **vanligvis** (*usually*), **ofte** (*often*), **sjelden** (*rarely*), **aldri** (*never*).
- Degree tells us how strong or complete something is: **veldig** (*very*), **ganske** (*quite*), **helt** (*completely*).
- Manner tells us how an action happens: **rolig** (*calmly*), **fort** (*fast*), **tydelig** (*clearly*), **forsiktig** (*carefully*).
- Place tells us where or in which direction: **her** (*here*), **der** (*there*), **hjemme** (*at home*), **inne** (*inside*), **hit** (*to here*).

You do not need to label every adverb before speaking. The practical question is simpler: what detail are you adding, and where does that detail normally go?

## Frequency: after the finite verb {#frequency-after-finite-verb role=model objectives=obj-adverbs-time-frequency-degree-manner-place-01}

In a neutral main clause beginning with the subject, common frequency adverbs usually come after the finite verb—the verb carrying tense.

::: examples
- no: Jeg trener ofte etter jobb.
- en: I often exercise after work.
- no: Maja snakker alltid fort.
- en: Maja always speaks fast.
- no: Erik jobber vanligvis hjemme.
- en: Erik usually works at home.
- no: Vi spiser sjelden ute.
- en: We rarely eat out.
- no: Hun kommer aldri sent.
- en: She never arrives late.
:::

The basic pattern is:

**subject + finite verb + frequency adverb + remaining information**

In **Erik jobber vanligvis hjemme**, **jobber** is the finite verb, **vanligvis** gives the frequency, and **hjemme** gives the place.

This position matters. English permits *I often work at home*, with the frequency adverb before the main verb. Norwegian normally places it after the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobber ofte hjemme.
- en: I often work at home.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg ofte jobber hjemme.
- en: ✗ I often work at home.
:::

With a verb phrase containing an auxiliary or modal verb, the frequency adverb still follows the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan ofte møte tidlig.
- en: I can often meet early.
- no: Hun har alltid jobbet hjemme.
- en: She has always worked at home.
:::

Here **kan** and **har** are finite. The adverb follows them rather than moving beside the final verb.

**Context:** Use short statements about Maja’s, Erik’s, and the project leader’s routines before meetings.

**Learner action:** Complete or repair neutral main clauses so that the stated frequency appears in its normal Norwegian position. Keep the subject first.

**Success meaning:** A successful response shows that the learner can locate the finite verb and place the frequency adverb immediately after it without copying English word order.

## Degree: keep it close to what it strengthens {#degree-close-to-target role=model objectives=obj-adverbs-time-frequency-degree-manner-place-01}

Degree adverbs usually stand directly before the adjective or adverb they modify.

::: examples
- no: Maja er veldig nervøs.
- en: Maja is very nervous.
- no: Kaffen er ganske varm.
- en: The coffee is quite hot.
- no: Hun snakker veldig tydelig.
- en: She speaks very clearly.
- no: Erik arbeider ganske rolig.
- en: Erik works quite calmly.
- no: Rommet er helt stille.
- en: The room is completely quiet.
:::

The degree adverb and the word it modifies form a close unit:

::: examples
- no: veldig + nervøs
- en: very + nervous
- no: ganske + fort
- en: quite + fast
- no: helt + stille
- en: completely + quiet
:::

This explains the order in Maja’s sentence:

::: examples
- no: Jeg snakker alltid ganske fort på møter.
- en: I always speak quite fast in meetings.
:::

**Alltid** describes how often she speaks that way. It follows the finite verb **snakker**. **Ganske** describes the degree of **fort**, so it stands directly before **fort**.

The sentence therefore builds in layers:

::: examples
- no: Jeg | snakker | alltid | ganske fort | på møter.
- en: I | speak | always | quite fast | in meetings.
:::

Do not separate a degree adverb from the adjective or adverb it modifies:

::: examples
- no: Hun sykler veldig fort.
- en: She cycles very fast.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Hun veldig sykler fort.
- en: ✗ She very cycles fast.
:::

A common misconception is that **veldig** should always go directly after the verb because it feels like an important adverb. Its position depends on what it modifies. In **veldig fort**, it strengthens **fort**, so the two words stay together.

**Degree attachment checkpoint**

**Request name: Keep-Degree-Together**

**Learner action:** Add an appropriate degree meaning to supplied sentences while preserving the sentence’s existing time, frequency, or place information.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the degree adverb appears directly before its target word. Other adverbs are context only and are not the scored dimension.

## Manner: usually after the action or its object {#manner-after-action-or-object role=model objectives=obj-adverbs-time-frequency-degree-manner-place-01}

Manner adverbs describe how an action happens. When there is no object, manner commonly follows the verb:

::: examples
- no: Maja snakker tydelig.
- en: Maja speaks clearly.
- no: Erik arbeider rolig.
- en: Erik works calmly.
- no: Prosjektlederen sykler fort.
- en: The project leader cycles fast.
:::

When the verb has an object, the object normally comes before the manner adverb:

::: examples
- no: Maja skriver notatene tydelig.
- en: Maja writes the notes clearly.
- no: Erik leser planen langsomt.
- en: Erik reads the plan slowly.
- no: Hun åpner døren forsiktig.
- en: She opens the door carefully.
:::

The neutral pattern is:

**subject + verb + object + manner**

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Maja skriver notatene tydelig.
- en: Maja writes the notes clearly.
- no: Maja skriver tydelige notater.
- en: Maja writes clear notes.
:::

In the first sentence, **tydelig** describes how she writes. It is an adverb and comes after the object. In the second sentence, **tydelige** describes the noun **notater**. That adjective pattern is not our focus here; the contrast simply shows why **tydelig** belongs after **notatene** in the first sentence.

With degree added, the whole manner expression stays together:

::: examples
- no: Maja skriver notatene veldig tydelig.
- en: Maja writes the notes very clearly.
- no: Erik leser planen ganske langsomt.
- en: Erik reads the plan quite slowly.
:::

**Manner after the object checkpoint**

**Request name: Describe-How-It-Happens**

**Purpose:** Practise putting a manner adverb after an object.

**Context:** Use actions involved in preparing the meeting: writing notes, reading a plan, opening the café door, and carrying cups.

**Learner action:** Expand simple subject–verb–object sentences with a manner detail.

**Success meaning:** A successful response places the object before the manner expression and keeps any degree adverb directly attached to the manner adverb.

## Place and time at the end {#place-and-time-at-end role=model objectives=obj-adverbs-time-frequency-degree-manner-place-01}

Place and time information often appears toward the end of a neutral main clause.

::: examples
- no: Erik jobber hjemme.
- en: Erik works at home.
- no: Vi møtes her.
- en: We are meeting here.
- no: Prosjektlederen kommer snart.
- en: The project leader is coming soon.
- no: Maja øver i dag.
- en: Maja is practising today.
:::

When a sentence contains both place and time, place commonly comes before time:

::: examples
- no: Vi møtes på kafeen i dag.
- en: We are meeting at the café today.
- no: Erik arbeider hjemme om morgenen.
- en: Erik works at home in the morning.
- no: De sitter ute nå.
- en: They are sitting outside now.
- no: Maja øver her hver tirsdag.
- en: Maja practises here every Tuesday.
:::

When manner, place, and time all occur at the end, a useful neutral order is:

**manner + place + time**

::: examples
- no: Vi snakker rolig her nå.
- en: We are speaking calmly here now.
- no: Hun sykler fort til kafeen hver morgen.
- en: She cycles quickly to the café every morning.
- no: Erik leser planen nøye hjemme om kvelden.
- en: Erik reads the plan carefully at home in the evening.
:::

This is a strong default, not a claim that Norwegian sentences can never vary. Speakers may move information to highlight it. For clear A2 main clauses, however, **manner–place–time** gives you a reliable neutral order.

Notice where frequency fits:

::: examples
- no: Erik arbeider vanligvis rolig hjemme om morgenen.
- en: Erik usually works calmly at home in the morning.
:::

The sentence follows a useful full pattern:

**subject + finite verb + frequency + manner + place + time**

If an object is present, it comes before the end information:

::: examples
- no: Erik leser ofte planen nøye hjemme om kvelden.
- en: Erik often reads the plan carefully at home in the evening.
:::

Here the structure is:

::: examples
- no: Erik | leser | ofte | planen | nøye | hjemme | om kvelden.
- en: Erik | reads | often | the plan | carefully | at home | in the evening.
:::

Do not push the object behind a final time expression:

::: examples
- no: Jeg møter henne i morgen.
- en: I’m meeting her tomorrow.
:::

Not normally:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg møter i morgen henne.
- en: ✗ I’m meeting tomorrow her.
:::

**End-position checkpoint**

**Request name: Build-the-Sentence-End**

**Purpose:** Practise arranging manner, place, and time information in a clear neutral order.

**Context:** Describe where and when the group prepares for meetings and how they complete practical tasks.

**Learner action:** Expand short clauses with supplied contextual details, placing any object before the manner, place, and time expressions.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates the neutral sequence of object before end information and manner before place before time when all three are present.

## Time or place first: the verb remains second {#fronted-time-or-place role=contrast objectives=obj-adverbs-time-frequency-degree-manner-place-01}

Time and place expressions can also come first when the speaker wants to establish the setting.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg møter Erik på kafeen i dag.
- en: I’m meeting Erik at the café today.
- no: I dag møter jeg Erik på kafeen.
- en: Today I’m meeting Erik at the café.
:::

Both sentences are correct. The second makes **i dag** the starting point.

Norwegian main clauses follow the verb-second pattern: when time or place comes first, the finite verb comes next, before the subject.

::: examples
- no: I dag møter jeg Erik.
- en: Today I’m meeting Erik.
- no: Om morgenen jobber han hjemme.
- en: In the morning he works at home.
- no: Her snakker vi rolig.
- en: Here we speak calmly.
- no: På kafeen øver Maja ofte.
- en: At the café Maja often practises.
:::

The opening expression occupies the first position. The finite verb occupies the second:

::: examples
- no: I dag | møter | jeg | Erik.
- en: Today | meet | I | Erik.
:::

Avoid keeping English subject–verb order after a fronted time expression:

::: examples
- no: ✗ I dag jeg møter Erik.
- en: ✗ Today I am meeting Erik.
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: I dag møter jeg Erik.
- en: Today I’m meeting Erik.
:::

The same frequency rule still operates later in the clause:

::: examples
- no: På kafeen øver Maja ofte om morgenen.
- en: At the café Maja often practises in the morning.
:::

**Øver** comes second, followed by the subject **Maja**, and then the frequency adverb **ofte**.

**Fronted setting checkpoint**

**Request name: Start-with-Time-or-Place**

**Purpose:** Practise beginning a simple main clause with a time or place expression while keeping the finite verb second.

**Context:** Retell parts of the café scene with a different starting point, such as today, in the morning, at home, or at the café.

**Learner action:** Rewrite neutral subject-first statements so that their existing time or place expression becomes the opening setting.

**Success meaning:** A successful response places the finite verb immediately after the fronted expression and places the subject after that verb. Frequency placement remains supporting context rather than a separate scored goal.

## Returning to Maja and Erik {#returning-to-maja-and-erik role=model objectives=obj-adverbs-time-frequency-degree-manner-place-01}

The project leader, Nora, arrives at the café.

::: {.reading translation="Hi! I usually arrive a little earlier, but the bus went very slowly today." dialogue_id=cafe-return speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Hei! Jeg kommer vanligvis litt tidligere, men bussen kjørte veldig sakte i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s fine. Maja is practising calmly here now." dialogue_id=cafe-return speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Det går bra. Maja øver rolig her nå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, and I’m not speaking so fast today." dialogue_id=cafe-return speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Ja, og jeg snakker ikke så fort i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. At the meeting you always speak clearly." dialogue_id=cafe-return speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Flott. På møtet snakker du alltid tydelig.
:::

Nora’s first sentence contains several different details:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kommer vanligvis litt tidligere.
- en: I usually arrive a little earlier.
:::

**Vanligvis** gives frequency and follows **kommer**. **Litt** gives the degree of **tidligere** and stays directly before it.

In the next clause:

::: examples
- no: Bussen kjørte veldig sakte i dag.
- en: The bus went very slowly today.
:::

**Veldig sakte** gives degree plus manner. **I dag** gives time and comes at the end.

Erik uses the full end pattern:

::: examples
- no: Maja øver rolig her nå.
- en: Maja is practising calmly here now.
:::

**Rolig** gives manner, **her** gives place, and **nå** gives time.

Nora begins with a setting:

::: examples
- no: På møtet snakker du alltid tydelig.
- en: At the meeting you always speak clearly.
:::

Because **på møtet** comes first, **snakker** comes second. The subject **du** follows the verb, and **alltid** follows the subject in this fronted structure.

Compare the two versions:

::: examples
- no: Du snakker alltid tydelig på møtet.
- en: You always speak clearly at the meeting.
- no: På møtet snakker du alltid tydelig.
- en: At the meeting you always speak clearly.
:::

The information is similar, but the second sentence makes the meeting the point of departure.

## Common mistakes to watch for {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-adverbs-time-frequency-degree-manner-place-01}

**Copying English frequency order**

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg ofte møter Erik her.
- en: ✗ I often meet Erik here.
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: Jeg møter ofte Erik her.
- en: I often meet Erik here.
:::

The finite verb **møter** comes before **ofte**.

**Separating degree from manner**

::: examples
- no: ✗ Hun veldig sykler fort.
- en: ✗ She very cycles fast.
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: Hun sykler veldig fort.
- en: She cycles very fast.
:::

**Veldig** strengthens **fort**, so they stay together.

**Putting time before the object**

::: examples
- no: ✗ Maja skriver i dag notatene tydelig.
- en: ✗ Maja writes today the notes clearly.
:::

For a neutral sentence, use:

::: examples
- no: Maja skriver notatene tydelig i dag.
- en: Maja writes the notes clearly today.
:::

The object **notatene** comes before the manner and time information.

**Forgetting verb-second order after a fronted expression**

::: examples
- no: ✗ Om morgenen Erik jobber hjemme.
- en: ✗ In the morning Erik works at home.
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: Om morgenen jobber Erik hjemme.
- en: In the morning Erik works at home.
:::

The finite verb **jobber** must follow the opening time expression.

**Treating every adverb alike**

Adverbs do not all occupy one universal “adverb position.”

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Hun sykler vanligvis veldig fort til jobb om morgenen.
- en: She usually cycles very fast to work in the morning.
:::

- **Vanligvis** follows the finite verb because it gives frequency.
- **Veldig** stands directly before **fort** because it gives degree.
- **Fort** gives manner.
- **Til jobb** gives place or direction.
- **Om morgenen** gives time.

A useful compact guide is:

**Frequency follows the finite verb. Degree stays beside its target. Objects come before end details. Manner, place, and time usually form the end sequence. A fronted time or place expression is followed by the finite verb.**

## Transfer to a new situation {#transfer-to-library role=recap objectives=obj-adverbs-time-frequency-degree-manner-place-01}

The next week, Maja is preparing at the library rather than at the café.

::: examples
- no: Maja arbeider ofte stille på biblioteket om ettermiddagen.
- en: Maja often works quietly at the library in the afternoon.
- no: Hun leser notatene veldig nøye der.
- en: She reads the notes very carefully there.
- no: I dag møter hun Nora på biblioteket.
- en: Today she is meeting Nora at the library.
- no: Nora kommer vanligvis tidlig.
- en: Nora usually arrives early.
- no: De snakker rolig sammen inne nå.
- en: They are speaking calmly together inside now.
:::

The setting has changed, but the placement choices remain the same. The learner should now be able to build similar sentences without depending on wording from the café dialogue.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates accurate overall placement of common time, frequency, degree, manner, and place adverbs while preserving natural Norwegian main-clause order.
