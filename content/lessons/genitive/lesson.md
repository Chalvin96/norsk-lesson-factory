## A key, a bag, and several moving boxes {#sec-a-key-a-bag-and-several-moving-boxes role=orient objectives=obj-genitive-01}

Jonas is helping Nora move into a new apartment. Nora’s brother Emil has also brought some boxes, but nobody has labeled everything clearly.

::: {.reading translation="Where is the key to the apartment?" dialogue_id="move-anchor" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Hvor er nøkkelen til leiligheten?
:::

::: {.reading translation="It may be in Emil’s backpack." dialogue_id="move-anchor" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Den ligger kanskje i Emils sekk.
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, this is Jonas’s backpack. My backpack is by the door." dialogue_id="move-anchor" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Nei, dette er Jonas’ sekk. Sekken min står ved døra.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Wait a moment. Is this Nora’s key?" dialogue_id="move-anchor" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Vent litt. Er dette nøkkelen til Nora?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, it is the apartment’s key. Great!" dialogue_id="move-anchor" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, det er leilighetens nøkkel. Flott!
:::

::: {.reading translation="And where should Nora’s books go?" dialogue_id="move-anchor" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Og hvor skal Noras bøker stå?
:::

::: {.reading translation="In Jonas’s bookcase. He has lent it to me." dialogue_id="move-anchor" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
I bokhylla til Jonas. Han har lånt meg den.
:::

::: {.reading translation="But Emil’s boxes can go in the kitchen." dialogue_id="move-anchor" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Men eskene til Emil kan stå på kjøkkenet.
:::

The three speakers need to identify people’s belongings and explain how objects are connected. Norwegian commonly does this in two ways:

- add **-s** to the owner or other connecting noun;
- place **til** after the person or thing being identified.

These patterns can often express the same basic relationship:

::: examples
- no: Noras bøker
- en: Nora’s books
- no: bøkene til Nora
- en: Nora’s books
:::

The meaning is similar, but the order and form of the nouns change.

## First notice: Who or what is connected to whom? {#sec-first-notice-who-or-what-is-connected-to-whom role=model objectives=obj-genitive-01}

In a phrase such as Noras bøker, Nora is the person connected to the books. In bøkene til Nora, the same two parts appear in the opposite order.

**Owner or connection first; thing first**

::: examples
- no: Noras bøker
- en: Nora’s books
- no: bøkene til Nora
- en: Nora’s books
- no: Emils esker
- en: Emil’s boxes
- no: eskene til Emil
- en: Emil’s boxes
- no: leilighetens nøkkel
- en: the apartment’s key
- no: nøkkelen til leiligheten
- en: the key to the apartment
:::

“Owner” is a convenient word, but these patterns are not limited to literal ownership. A person can own a bag, but an apartment does not own its key in the same way. The pattern also shows a close connection.

::: examples
- no: døra til leiligheten
- en: the door of the apartment
- no: skolens navn
- en: the school’s name
- no: navnet til skolen
- en: the name of the school
- no: Noras bror
- en: Nora’s brother
- no: broren til Nora
- en: Nora’s brother
:::

In each case, ask two simple questions:

1. What person or thing are we identifying?
2. Who or what is it connected to?

In nøkkelen til leiligheten, we are identifying nøkkelen, and it is connected to leiligheten.

## The -s pattern {#sec-the-s-pattern role=model objectives=obj-genitive-01}

To use the **-s pattern**, put the owner or connecting noun first and add **-s** directly to it.

**Compact rule:**

::: {.rule}
**owner or connection + s + thing**
:::

::: examples
- no: Noras bøker
- en: Nora’s books
- no: Emils esker
- en: Emil’s boxes
- no: leilighetens nøkkel
- en: the apartment’s key
- no: skolens adresse
- en: the school’s address
:::

There is normally no apostrophe before the **s**.

::: examples
- no: Noras jakke
- en: Nora’s jacket
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Nora’s jakke
- en: 'Incorrect: Nora’s jacket'
:::

The English translation uses an apostrophe, but ordinary Norwegian **-s** does not.

**Form of the thing after -s**

After an **-s** owner, the thing normally appears without a definite ending. Compare:

::: examples
- no: boka
- en: the book
- no: Noras bok
- en: Nora’s book
- no: sykkelen
- en: the bicycle
- no: Emils sykkel
- en: Emil’s bicycle
- no: rommet
- en: the room
- no: barnets rom
- en: the child’s room
:::

The owner already tells us which book, bicycle, or room we mean. Norwegian therefore does not normally use the definite form of the second noun in this pattern.

A common mistake is to keep that definite ending:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Noras boka
- en: Incorrect: Nora’s the book
:::

This is not the standard pattern. Use:

::: examples
- no: Noras bok
- en: Nora’s book
:::

The same point applies in the plural:

::: examples
- no: eskene
- en: the boxes
- no: Emils esker
- en: Emil’s boxes
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Emils eskene
- en: 'Incorrect: Emil’s the boxes'
:::

## The til pattern {#sec-the-til-pattern role=model objectives=obj-genitive-01}

Norwegian can also put the thing first and introduce the owner or connection with **til**.

**Compact rule:**

::: {.rule}
**definite thing + til + owner or connection**
:::

::: examples
- no: bøkene til Nora
- en: Nora’s books
- no: eskene til Emil
- en: Emil’s boxes
- no: nøkkelen til leiligheten
- en: the key to the apartment
- no: adressen til skolen
- en: the school’s address
:::

Notice that the first noun is normally definite:

::: examples
- no: boka til Nora
- en: Nora’s book
- no: sykkelen til Emil
- en: Emil’s bicycle
- no: rommet til barnet
- en: the child’s room
- no: eskene til Jonas
- en: Jonas’s boxes
:::

This contrasts directly with the **-s** pattern:

::: examples
- no: Noras bok
- en: Nora’s book
- no: boka til Nora
- en: Nora’s book
- no: Emils sykkel
- en: Emil’s bicycle
- no: sykkelen til Emil
- en: Emil’s bicycle
- no: barnets rom
- en: the child’s room
- no: rommet til barnet
- en: the child’s room
:::

Do not copy English word order mechanically. Norwegian does not normally use an indefinite form of the thing for this possessive meaning. When **til** identifies whose particular object you mean, use the definite form of the thing:

::: examples
- no: boka til Nora
- en: Nora’s book
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: bok til Nora
- en: 'Not: book to Nora (for the possessive meaning)'
:::

That last phrase may suggest a book intended for Nora rather than a book connected to or belonging to her. The definite form helps produce the intended ownership reading.

## Choosing between -s and til {#sec-choosing-between-s-and-til role=contrast objectives=obj-genitive-01}

Both patterns are common:

::: examples
- no: Noras sekk
- en: Nora’s backpack
- no: sekken til Nora
- en: Nora’s backpack
:::

The **-s** pattern is short and places the owner or connection first. It works especially neatly with names and short nouns:

::: examples
- no: Emils telefon
- en: Emil’s phone
- no: lærerens kontor
- en: the teacher’s office
- no: butikkens åpningstider
- en: the shop’s opening hours
:::

The **til** pattern places the object first. This is useful when the object is already central to the conversation or when you ask which person it is connected to:

::: examples
- no: Hvor er telefonen til Emil?
- en: Where is Emil’s phone?
- no: Jeg fant jakka til Nora.
- en: I found Nora’s jacket.
- no: Dette er kontoret til læreren.
- en: This is the teacher’s office.
:::

The **til** pattern can also feel convenient when the connecting phrase is longer:

::: examples
- no: sykkelen til den nye naboen
- en: the new neighbor’s bicycle
- no: kontoret til lederen på hotellet
- en: the hotel manager’s office
:::

At this level, the main goal is not to identify a subtle stylistic difference. It is to form both patterns naturally and understand that they can describe the same relationship.

**Same situation, different starting point**

Imagine that Nora sees a coat and wants to know whose it is. She can receive either answer:

::: examples
- no: Det er Emils jakke.
- en: It is Emil’s jacket.
- no: Det er jakka til Emil.
- en: It is Emil’s jacket.
:::

The first answer begins with Emil. The second begins with the jacket. Both identify the same connection.

Likewise:

::: examples
- no: Dette er bygningens inngang.
- en: This is the building’s entrance.
- no: Dette er inngangen til bygningen.
- en: This is the entrance to the building.
:::

The second version may be especially natural when someone is looking for an entrance and inngangen is the topic. The grammar does not change the basic relationship.

## More than personal possessions {#sec-more-than-personal-possessions role=model objectives=obj-genitive-01}

The same patterns show family, institutional, and part–whole connections.

**People and relationships**

::: examples
- no: Noras familie
- en: Nora’s family
- no: familien til Nora
- en: Nora’s family
- no: Emils søster
- en: Emil’s sister
- no: søsteren til Emil
- en: Emil’s sister
:::

These people are connected through a relationship; nobody is being treated as an object that someone owns.

**Places and their parts**

::: examples
- no: husets kjøkken
- en: the house’s kitchen
- no: kjøkkenet til huset
- en: the kitchen of the house
- no: leilighetens balkong
- en: the apartment’s balcony
- no: balkongen til leiligheten
- en: the balcony of the apartment
:::

**Organizations and information**

::: examples
- no: skolens adresse
- en: the school’s address
- no: adressen til skolen
- en: the school’s address
- no: firmaets navn
- en: the company’s name
- no: navnet til firmaet
- en: the company’s name
:::

The same core question remains useful: What are we identifying, and what is it connected to?

## Back at Nora’s apartment {#sec-back-at-noras-apartment role=model objectives=obj-genitive-01}

After carrying the boxes upstairs, Nora, Jonas, and Emil try to put everything in the right place.

::: {.reading translation="Is this Emil’s lamp?" dialogue_id="move-return" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Er dette Emils lampe?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, Emil’s lamp is black. That is my lamp." dialogue_id="move-return" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Nei, lampa til Emil er svart. Det er min lampe.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I have found the table legs, but where are the table’s screws?" dialogue_id="move-return" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Jeg har funnet beina til bordet, men hvor er bordets skruer?
:::

::: {.reading translation="The table’s screws are in the small box." dialogue_id="move-return" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Skruene til bordet ligger i den lille eska.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And this must be the neighbor’s mail." dialogue_id="move-return" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Og dette må være naboens post.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. The neighbor’s mail should not be here. I’ll take it downstairs." dialogue_id="move-return" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja. Posten til naboen skal ikke ligge her. Jeg går ned med den.
:::

The speakers move freely between the two patterns:

- Emils lampe and lampa til Emil
- bordets skruer and skruene til bordet
- naboens post and posten til naboen

Each pair preserves the same connection while changing which part comes first.

## Common mistakes to avoid {#sec-common-mistakes-to-avoid role=contrast objectives=obj-genitive-01}

**1. Do not use an English apostrophe**

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Nora’s bok
- en: 'Incorrect: Nora’s book'
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: Noras bok
- en: Nora’s book
:::

**2. Do not keep the definite ending after an -s owner**

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Noras boka
- en: 'Incorrect: Nora’s the book'
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: Noras bok
- en: Nora’s book
:::

**3. Do not normally leave the object indefinite in an ownership phrase with til**

Not:

::: examples
- no: bok til Nora
- en: 'Not: book to Nora (for the possessive meaning)'
:::

For ownership or connection, use:

::: examples
- no: boka til Nora
- en: Nora’s book
:::

**4. Do not attach -s to the thing when the owner comes first**

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Nora boks
- en: 'Incorrect: Nora book’s'
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: Noras bok
- en: Nora’s book
:::

The **-s** belongs to the owner or connecting noun, not to the object being identified.
