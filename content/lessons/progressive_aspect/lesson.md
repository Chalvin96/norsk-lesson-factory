## At the community hall {#at-community-hall role=orient objectives=obj-progressive-aspect-01}

Jonas is meeting friends at a community hall for their weekly quiz night. He is late, so Ida calls him.

::: {.reading translation='Where are you? We’re waiting for you.' dialogue_id='at-community-hall' speaker_id='ida' speaker_name='Ida'}
Hvor er du? Vi venter på deg.
:::

::: {.reading translation='I’m just outside. I’m locking my bike.' dialogue_id='at-community-hall' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg er rett utenfor. Jeg holder på å låse sykkelen.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Good. Sara is sitting and getting the questions ready, and Amir is standing and making coffee.' dialogue_id='at-community-hall' speaker_id='ida' speaker_name='Ida'}
Bra. Sara sitter og gjør spørsmålene klare, og Amir står og lager kaffe.
:::

::: {.reading translation='I’ll be inside in a minute.' dialogue_id='at-community-hall' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg kommer inn om et minutt.
:::

::: {.reading translation='I called you earlier too. What were you doing then?' dialogue_id='at-community-hall' speaker_id='ida' speaker_name='Ida'}
Jeg ringte deg tidligere også. Hva gjorde du da?
:::

::: {.reading translation='When you called, I was standing and waiting for the bus.' dialogue_id='at-community-hall' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Da du ringte, sto jeg og ventet på bussen.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Did you see my message?' dialogue_id='at-community-hall' speaker_id='ida' speaker_name='Ida'}
Så du meldingen min?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes. On the bus I was sitting and reading the messages, but I forgot to reply.' dialogue_id='at-community-hall' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. På bussen satt jeg og leste meldingene, men jeg glemte å svare.
:::

The friends use ordinary present and past tense, but several sentences place an action under a spotlight. They show what someone is in the middle of doing now or what was happening around a particular past moment.

Norwegian can express this progressive meaning (progressiv betydning) through several activity frames. Unlike English, Norwegian does not need a direct equivalent of be + verb-ing in every such sentence.

## The main meaning choice {#main-meaning-choice role=contrast objectives=obj-progressive-aspect-01}

Compare these two sentences:

::: examples
- no: Jeg låser sykkelen.
- en: I lock the bike. / I’m locking the bike.
- no: Jeg holder på å låse sykkelen.
- en: I’m in the middle of locking the bike.
:::

The simple present can already describe something happening now, especially when the context makes the time clear. The activity frame holder på å gives the action extra focus. Jonas is not reporting a general habit. He is explaining what is occupying him at that exact moment.

The same contrast is possible in the past:

::: examples
- no: Jeg lagde kaffe.
- en: I made coffee. / I was making coffee.
- no: Jeg holdt på å lage kaffe.
- en: I was in the middle of making coffee.
:::

The simple past reports the action. The frame holdt på å highlights the action as ongoing around a past moment.

**Request: notice-the-ongoing-focus**

**Purpose:** Notice why an activity frame is useful before producing one.

**Context:** Short present- and past-time situations involving Jonas and his friends.

**Learner action:** Identify which sentences report an activity neutrally and which add explicit ongoing focus with an activity frame. Use the surrounding time expressions and interruptions as clues; a simple present or past form may still describe an ongoing event.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Recognize progressive meaning in context.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner distinguishes neutral tense reporting from the extra explicit ongoing focus contributed by the frames.

{{exercise: notice_ongoing_focus}}

## Using holde på å {#using-holde-pa-a role=model objectives=obj-progressive-aspect-01}

The most general frame in this lesson is:

**holde på å + infinitive**

The first verb carries the tense. The activity after å remains in the infinitive.

**Present time**

::: examples
- no: Jeg holder på å låse sykkelen.
- en: I’m locking my bike.
- no: Vi holder på å gjøre klart rommet.
- en: We’re getting the room ready.
- no: Sara holder på å skrive spørsmålene.
- en: Sara is writing the questions.
- no: Amir holder på å lage kaffe.
- en: Amir is making coffee.
:::

The present form is holder:

::: examples
- no: Nå holder jeg på å rydde bordet.
- en: I’m clearing the table now.
- no: Hva holder dere på å gjøre?
- en: What are you doing?
:::

**Past time**

For an action in progress around a past moment, use the past form holdt:

::: examples
- no: Klokka sju holdt jeg på å spise middag.
- en: At seven o’clock I was eating dinner.
- no: Vi holdt på å gjøre klart rommet da lyset gikk.
- en: We were getting the room ready when the lights went out.
- no: Sara holdt på å skrive et spørsmål da Ida kom.
- en: Sara was writing a question when Ida arrived.
- no: Hva holdt du på å gjøre da jeg ringte?
- en: What were you doing when I called?
:::

Notice that only the first verb changes:

**Time: now**

::: examples
- no: holder på å lage
- en: is making
:::

**Time: past moment**

::: examples
- no: holdt på å lage
- en: was making
:::

The infinitive lage does not become lagde after å.

**Compact rule**

::: {.rule}
Use **holder på å + infinitive** to highlight an activity happening now. Use **holdt på å + infinitive** to highlight an activity happening around a past moment.
:::

**A common mistake**

Do not copy the English structure by placing er before a present-tense verb.

**Incorrect:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg er lager kaffe.
- en: 'Incorrect: I’m making coffee.'
:::

**Correct:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg lager kaffe.
- en: I’m making coffee.
:::

**More explicitly ongoing:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg holder på å lage kaffe.
- en: I’m in the middle of making coffee.
:::

Norwegian simple present often covers both English simple-present and present-progressive meanings. The activity frame is available when the ongoing nature of the action matters.

**Request: build-holde-pa-a-frame**

**Purpose:** Establish the form before adding freer context.

**Context:** Everyday activities at the community hall, at home, and while travelling.

**Learner action:** Recast simple present and simple past statements with the appropriate form of holde på å. Keep the activity verb in the infinitive after å.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Form holder på å and holdt på å accurately.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner changes holde for present or past time while preserving the infinitive form of the activity verb.

{{exercise: build_holde_pa_a_frame}}

## Showing posture as well as activity {#showing-posture-as-well-as-activity role=model objectives=obj-progressive-aspect-01}

Norwegian can also describe an ongoing scene with a posture verb:

- sitte og + activity
- stå og + activity
- ligge og + activity

These frames say both what position someone is in and what that person is doing.

::: examples
- no: Sara sitter og skriver.
- en: Sara is sitting and writing.
- no: Amir står og lager kaffe.
- en: Amir is standing and making coffee.
- no: Katten ligger og sover under bordet.
- en: The cat is lying and sleeping under the table.
:::

In natural English, the posture may be translated explicitly or left in the background:

::: examples
- no: Sara sitter og gjør spørsmålene klare.
- en: Sara is sitting and getting the questions ready. / Sara is getting the questions ready.
:::

The Norwegian sentence creates a small visual scene. Sara is seated, and getting the questions ready is the activity in progress.

Use a posture frame only when the posture makes sense in the situation. If you do not know or care whether someone is sitting, standing, or lying down, holde på å is more neutral.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Sara holder på å lese spørsmålene.
- en: Sara is reading the questions.
- no: Sara sitter og leser spørsmålene.
- en: Sara is sitting and reading the questions.
:::

Both highlight an ongoing activity. The second also tells us how Sara is positioned.

**Request: choose-meaningful-activity-frame**

**Purpose:** Connect grammar choice to the scene rather than choosing mechanically.

**Context:** Brief descriptions of people reading on a sofa, waiting at a bus stop, preparing food, or doing an activity when posture is unknown.

**Learner action:** Supply a neutral holde på å frame or a posture frame that matches the physical situation.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Select an activity frame whose meaning fits the context.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner distinguishes neutral ongoing focus from an ongoing action presented with visible posture.

{{exercise: choose_meaningful_activity_frame}}

## Posture frames in present and past time {#posture-frames-present-past role=contrast objectives=obj-progressive-aspect-01}

In a posture frame, both verbs are ordinary tense forms joined by og.

**Present**

::: examples
- no: Jeg sitter og leser.
- en: I’m sitting and reading.
- no: Hun står og venter.
- en: She’s standing and waiting.
- no: De ligger og hviler.
- en: They’re lying down and resting.
:::

**Past**

::: examples
- no: Jeg satt og leste.
- en: I was sitting and reading.
- no: Hun sto og ventet.
- en: She was standing and waiting.
- no: De lå og hvilte.
- en: They were lying down and resting.
:::

The pattern changes like this:

**Now**

::: examples
- no: sitter og leser
- en: is sitting and reading
- no: står og venter
- en: is standing and waiting
- no: ligger og sover
- en: is lying and sleeping
:::

**Around a past moment**

::: examples
- no: satt og leste
- en: was sitting and reading
- no: sto og ventet
- en: was standing and waiting
- no: lå og sov
- en: was lying and sleeping
:::

This differs from holde på å:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holdt på å lese.
- en: I was reading.
- no: Jeg satt og leste.
- en: I was sitting and reading.
:::

Here, lese is an infinitive because it follows å.

Here, leste is past tense. Both satt and leste locate the scene in the past.

**Common form errors**

**Incorrect:**

::: examples
- no: I går satt jeg og leser.
- en: 'Incorrect: Yesterday I was sitting and reading.'
:::

**Correct:**

::: examples
- no: I går satt jeg og leste.
- en: Yesterday I was sitting and reading.
:::

**Incorrect:**

::: examples
- no: Da du ringte, sto jeg å ventet.
- en: 'Incorrect: When you called, I was standing and waiting.'
:::

**Correct:**

::: examples
- no: Da du ringte, sto jeg og ventet.
- en: When you called, I was standing and waiting.
:::

Use å in holde på å, but og in the posture frames:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holder på å vente.
- en: I’m waiting.
- no: Jeg står og venter.
- en: I’m standing and waiting.
:::

**Request: match-tense-posture-frame**

**Purpose:** Practise the form contrast between present and past scenes.

**Context:** Paired scenes showing what people are doing now and what they were doing at a stated time yesterday.

**Learner action:** Complete each posture frame with present forms for current scenes and past forms for past scenes.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Use matching present or past tense across both verbs in a posture frame.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner produces forms such as sitter og leser and satt og leste without mixing time references.

{{exercise: match_tense_posture_frame}}

**Request: distinguish-a-from-og**

**Purpose:** Prevent confusion between the two approved activity-frame patterns.

**Context:** Sentences about preparing quiz night, waiting for transport, reading messages, and resting at home.

**Learner action:** Complete each frame with å or og and adjust the activity verb to the form required by that frame.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Distinguish holde på å + infinitive from posture verb + og + tense-marked activity verb.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner links connector choice to the grammar of the following verb.

{{exercise: distinguish_a_from_og}}

## Choosing present or past focus {#choosing-present-or-past-focus role=contrast objectives=obj-progressive-aspect-01}

Time expressions often make the choice clear.

**Happening now**

::: examples
- no: Akkurat nå holder vi på å finne et ledig bord.
- en: Right now we’re looking for a free table.
- no: Se, Ida står og snakker med vakten.
- en: Look, Ida is standing and talking to the attendant.
- no: Jeg kan ikke snakke lenge. Jeg holder på å gå inn.
- en: I can’t talk for long. I’m going inside.
:::

**Happening around a past moment**

::: examples
- no: Klokka halv åtte satt vi og ventet på Jonas.
- en: At half past seven we were sitting and waiting for Jonas.
- no: Da Jonas kom inn, sto Amir og helte kaffe i koppene.
- en: When Jonas came in, Amir was standing and pouring coffee into the cups.
- no: Da telefonen ringte, holdt Sara på å lese det første spørsmålet.
- en: When the phone rang, Sara was reading the first question.
:::

A past activity frame often supplies the background for a shorter event:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holdt på å låse sykkelen da Ida ringte.
- en: I was locking my bike when Ida called.
:::

The lesson focus is the ongoing background activity: holdt på å låse. The call identifies the moment around which that activity was in progress.

**Request: place-activity-right-time**

**Purpose:** Connect tense choice with now and past-moment contexts.

**Context:** Phone calls, arrivals, clock times, and reports of what is happening at the community hall.

**Learner action:** Produce an activity frame for each scene, using present time when the speaker reports the current situation and past time when another event or stated time provides a past reference point.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Choose present or past forms appropriate to the time context.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner can use activity frames for both actions happening now and actions in progress around a past moment.

{{exercise: place_activity_right_time}}

## Back at quiz night {#back-at-quiz-night role=model objectives=obj-progressive-aspect-01}

Jonas enters the hall and sees the scene Ida described.

::: {.reading translation='Now I’m here. What are you doing?' dialogue_id='back-at-quiz-night' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nå er jeg her. Hva holder dere på å gjøre?
:::

::: {.reading translation='We’re dividing into teams.' dialogue_id='back-at-quiz-night' speaker_id='ida' speaker_name='Ida'}
Vi holder på å dele oss i lag.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Where is Sara?' dialogue_id='back-at-quiz-night' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hvor er Sara?
:::

::: {.reading translation='She’s sitting and talking with the new participant. Amir is standing and counting the coffee cups.' dialogue_id='back-at-quiz-night' speaker_id='ida' speaker_name='Ida'}
Hun sitter og snakker med den nye deltakeren. Amir står og teller kaffekoppene.
:::

::: {.reading translation='I can help with the teams now. When you called earlier, I was standing and waiting for the bus, but now I’m ready.' dialogue_id='back-at-quiz-night' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg kan hjelpe med lagene nå. Da du ringte tidligere, sto jeg og ventet på bussen, men nå er jeg klar.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Good. Help with the teams while I help Amir.' dialogue_id='back-at-quiz-night' speaker_id='ida' speaker_name='Ida'}
Fint. Hjelp med lagene mens jeg hjelper Amir.
:::

The scene has moved forward, but the same forms remain useful. Jonas asks about activity in progress now. He also returns to the earlier phone call and describes what he was doing at that past moment.

One detail deserves care: holdt på å works most clearly here with activities that can continue for some time, such as cooking, reading, preparing, or locking something. With certain sudden events, it can instead suggest that something nearly happened:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holdt på å falle.
- en: I nearly fell.
:::

For this chapter’s target meaning, use holde på å with clearly ongoing activities.

**Request: repair-mixed-activity-frames**

**Purpose:** Consolidate form after the two patterns have been compared.

**Context:** A short retelling of Jonas’s trip to quiz night containing tense mismatches, incorrect connectors, and incorrect infinitive forms.

**Learner action:** Rewrite each faulty activity frame while preserving its intended present or past meaning.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Correct malformed activity frames.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner can diagnose whether a frame requires holde + på å + infinitive or matching verbs joined by og.

{{exercise: repair_mixed_activity_frames}}

## Cumulative transfer: preparing for a birthday visit {#cumulative-transfer-preparing-birthday-visit role=recap objectives=obj-progressive-aspect-01}

Imagine a different everyday situation. You arrive at a friend’s flat while several people are preparing a birthday meal. One person calls to ask what everyone is doing. Later, you explain what was happening when the birthday guest arrived unexpectedly.

**Request: report-current-preparations**

**Purpose:** Transfer present-time activity frames to a new situation.

**Context:** People are decorating a room, cooking, waiting by the door, and resting briefly on a sofa.

**Learner action:** Describe what several people are doing now. Use holde på å when posture is irrelevant and a posture frame when sitting, standing, or lying is meaningful.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Produce context-appropriate present-time activity frames.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner can highlight current ongoing actions without copying sentences from the quiz-night dialogue.

{{exercise: report_current_preparations}}

**Request: reconstruct-arrival-moment**

**Purpose:** Transfer past-time activity frames to a connected account.

**Context:** The birthday guest arrived while the preparations were still underway.

**Learner action:** Describe what different people were doing at that moment, using holdt på å or past posture frames. Keep each frame internally consistent.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Produce context-appropriate past-time activity frames around a shared past moment.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner can create a coherent past scene using forms such as holdt på å lage, satt og skrev, and sto og ventet.

{{exercise: reconstruct_arrival_moment}}

## Retrieval and review {#retrieval-and-review role=recap objectives=obj-progressive-aspect-01}

Without looking back, explain the difference between holder på å lage and står og lager. Then change both frames to describe a past moment. Finish by writing one sentence with a taught present-time activity frame about something happening around you now and one sentence with a taught past-time activity frame about what you were doing at a specific time yesterday.
