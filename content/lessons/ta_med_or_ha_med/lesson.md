## A trip to the cabin {#a-trip-to-the-cabin role=orient objectives=obj-ta-med-or-ha-med-01}

Nora and Emil are going to spend the weekend at Nora’s family cabin. Nora is already on the bus to the station when Emil calls her.

::: {.reading translation="I’m leaving home in ten minutes. Shall I bring some food?" dialogue_id="cabin-call" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Jeg går hjemmefra om ti minutter. Skal jeg ta med noe mat?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, bring bread and cheese. There isn’t much food at the cabin." dialogue_id="cabin-call" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, ta med brød og ost. Det er ikke så mye mat på hytta.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Fine. Do you have the key with you?" dialogue_id="cabin-call" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Greit. Har du med nøkkelen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Wait a moment … No, I don’t have the key with me! It’s at home." dialogue_id="cabin-call" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Vent litt … Nei, jeg har ikke med nøkkelen! Den ligger hjemme.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then I can bring it. Where is it?" dialogue_id="cabin-call" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Da kan jeg ta den med. Hvor ligger den?
:::

::: {.reading translation="It’s on the table in the hall. Thanks! Do you have warm clothes with you?" dialogue_id="cabin-call" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Den ligger på bordet i gangen. Takk! Har du med varme klær?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I have a sweater with me, but not a rain jacket." dialogue_id="cabin-call" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Jeg har med en genser, men ikke regnjakke.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Bring the rain jacket too. It’s going to rain all weekend." dialogue_id="cabin-call" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ta med regnjakka også. Det skal regne hele helga.
:::

The conversation turns on one small but important distinction. Nora wants Emil to make several things accompany him when he leaves home: food, the key, and a rain jacket. She therefore uses **ta med**. When Emil asks what Nora already has in her possession on the journey, he uses **ha med**.

## The central distinction {#the-central-distinction role=model objectives=obj-ta-med-or-ha-med-01}

Both expressions can correspond to English phrases such as bring, take along, or have with you. The useful distinction is not simply between English bring and take. Instead, ask whether the sentence presents an action or an existing situation.

## Ta med: make someone or something accompany you {#ta-med-make-someone-or-something-accompany-you role=model objectives=obj-ta-med-or-ha-med-01}

Use **ta med** when a person causes an object or another person to come along from one place to another.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar med kaffe til møtet.
- en: I’m bringing coffee to the meeting.
:::

The coffee will accompany the speaker to the meeting.

::: examples
- no: Kan du ta med passet?
- en: Can you bring your passport?
:::

The listener is being asked to make sure the passport comes along.

::: examples
- no: Mari tar med sønnen sin på festen.
- en: Mari is bringing her son to the party.
:::

Mari causes her son to accompany her to the party. This does not imply force; it simply presents Mari as the person arranging or enabling his presence.

## Ha med: already have someone or something with you {#ha-med-already-have-someone-or-something-with-you role=model objectives=obj-ta-med-or-ha-med-01}

Use **ha med** when the sentence describes what someone has with them in the current situation or during a journey.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har med kaffe.
- en: I have coffee with me.
:::

The sentence describes the speaker’s present situation. The coffee is already with the speaker.

::: examples
- no: Har du med passet?
- en: Do you have your passport with you?
:::

The question checks whether the passport is already among the things accompanying the listener.

::: examples
- no: Mari har med sønnen sin på festen.
- en: Mari has her son with her at the party.
:::

Mari and her son are already there together, or the sentence views his presence as part of her current situation.

A compact way to remember the contrast is:

**ta med** focuses on getting something or someone to accompany you.
**ha med** focuses on having that thing or person with you.

## First checkpoint: identify action and situation {#first-checkpoint-identify-action-and-situation role=contrast objectives=obj-ta-med-or-ha-med-01}

Read the cabin dialogue again. Find every use of **ta med** and **ha med**. For each one, note whether Emil or Nora is arranging for something to come along or checking what is already with someone.

This activity belongs here because the distinction is easiest to see before you produce it yourself. The shared cabin context keeps the meaning concrete: Emil is still at home, while Nora is already travelling. A successful response would connect each form to that difference in situation rather than merely translating both expressions as bring.

## Compare the forms in the same context {#compare-the-forms-in-the-same-context role=contrast objectives=obj-ta-med-or-ha-med-01}

The contrast becomes especially clear when only the expression changes.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar med en paraply.
- en: I’m taking an umbrella with me.
- no: Jeg har med en paraply.
- en: I have an umbrella with me.
:::

The first sentence presents the decision or action of making the umbrella accompany the speaker. The second reports the speaker’s current state: the umbrella is already with them.

Consider a traveller preparing to leave:

::: examples
- no: Før jeg går, tar jeg med billetten.
- en: Before I leave, I take the ticket with me.
:::

Now consider the same traveller during an inspection:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har med billetten.
- en: I have the ticket with me.
:::

The ticket may be in a pocket or bag. **Ha med** does not mean that the person is holding it in their hand. It means that it is available because it accompanied them.

The same distinction works with people:

::: examples
- no: Vi tar med Anna på kino.
- en: We’re taking Anna with us to the cinema.
- no: Vi har med Anna på kino.
- en: We have Anna with us at the cinema.
:::

The first sentence focuses on Anna joining the trip or plan. The second presents her as already accompanying the group.

## Before departure and after arrival {#before-departure-and-after-arrival role=contrast objectives=obj-ta-med-or-ha-med-01}

Time and setting often make the intended expression easier to choose.

Before someone leaves, speakers often discuss what to **ta med**:

::: examples
- no: Hva skal vi ta med på turen?
- en: What shall we take with us on the trip?
- no: Ta med gode sko.
- en: Bring good shoes.
- no: Jeg kan ta med litt frukt.
- en: I can bring some fruit.
:::

During the trip or after arrival, speakers often check what people **har med**:

::: examples
- no: Hva har dere med?
- en: What do you have with you?
- no: Vi har med mat og varme klær.
- en: We have food and warm clothes with us.
- no: Har noen med et kart?
- en: Does anyone have a map with them?
:::

This before-and-after pattern is useful, but it is not an absolute grammar rule. The real distinction remains action versus existing accompaniment. You can describe a habitual action with **ta med**, and you can talk about a future state with **ha med**.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar alltid med vann når jeg går på tur.
- en: I always take water with me when I go hiking.
- no: I morgen har jeg med matpakke på jobb.
- en: Tomorrow I’ll have a packed lunch with me at work.
:::

In the first sentence, the speaker repeatedly makes water accompany them. In the second, the speaker imagines tomorrow’s situation at work and states what they will have with them.

## Second checkpoint: complete the travel sequence {#second-checkpoint-complete-the-travel-sequence role=contrast objectives=obj-ta-med-or-ha-med-01}

Write one appropriate expression in each gap in this short sequence:

::: examples
- no: Før Lea går til flyplassen, må hun ___ passet.
- en: Before Lea goes to the airport, she must ___ her passport.
- no: På flyplassen spør venninnen: ___ du ___ passet?
- en: At the airport her friend asks: ___ you ___ your passport with you?
- no: Lea åpner veska og sier at hun ___ passet.
- en: Lea opens her bag and says that she ___ her passport with her.
:::

The purpose is to follow one object through changing circumstances. First, the passport must be made part of the journey; later, its presence is checked and confirmed. A successful response would change expression when the situation changes, even though the object remains the same.

## Requests and checks {#requests-and-checks role=model objectives=obj-ta-med-or-ha-med-01}

In everyday conversation, **ta med** often appears in requests. The speaker wants another person to bring something or someone.

::: examples
- no: Kan du ta med melk hjem?
- en: Can you bring milk home?
- no: Husk å ta med laderen.
- en: Remember to bring the charger.
- no: Ta med en venn hvis du vil.
- en: Bring a friend if you want.
:::

By contrast, **ha med** often appears in checks and reports.

::: examples
- no: Har du med laderen?
- en: Do you have the charger with you?
- no: Jeg har ikke med kontanter.
- en: I don’t have cash with me.
- no: Hun har med en venn.
- en: She has a friend with her.
:::

Notice the natural sequence:

::: examples
- no: Husk å ta med adgangskortet.
- en: Remember to bring your access card.
- no: Ja, jeg har det med.
- en: Yes, I have it with me.
:::

The first speaker refers to the action needed. The second confirms the resulting situation.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-ta-med-or-ha-med-01}

**Using ha med for an instruction**

A learner may produce:

::: examples
- no: Ha med passet i morgen.
- en: Have the passport with you tomorrow.
:::

This can be understood, and Norwegian sometimes allows such wording when the intended state is strongly emphasized. However, if the ordinary purpose is to tell someone to bring the passport, **ta med** is the clearer and more natural choice:

::: examples
- no: Ta med passet i morgen.
- en: Bring your passport tomorrow.
:::

The instruction concerns making sure the passport comes along.

**Using ta med when checking a current situation**

At the airport, this question does not normally perform the intended check:

::: examples
- no: Tar du med passet?
- en: Are you bringing the passport?
:::

It asks about the person’s plan or action. If you can see that the person has already arrived and want to know whether the passport is with them, ask:

::: examples
- no: Har du med passet?
- en: Do you have your passport with you?
:::

Compare the contexts:

::: examples
- no: Du er fortsatt hjemme. Tar du med passet?
- en: You’re still at home. Are you bringing your passport?
- no: Du er på flyplassen. Har du med passet?
- en: You’re at the airport. Do you have your passport with you?
:::

**Translating one English verb mechanically**

English bring can refer either to the action of bringing or to the result of having brought something. Norwegian often makes the distinction more explicit.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar med kake til festen.
- en: I’m bringing cake to the party.
- no: Jeg har med kake.
- en: I’ve brought cake. / I have cake with me.
:::

In the second example, English may naturally use I’ve brought, but the Norwegian sentence views the cake as already present with the speaker. This is why direct word-for-word translation is less reliable than asking whether the sentence focuses on action or state.

## Third checkpoint: change the viewpoint {#third-checkpoint-change-the-viewpoint role=contrast objectives=obj-ta-med-or-ha-med-01}

Rewrite each statement as a later check or report. Keep the same person and object, but move the situation from preparation to arrival.

::: examples
- no: Sander tar med dokumentene til kontoret.
- en: Sander is bringing the documents to the office.
- no: Vi tar med håndklær til stranda.
- en: We’re taking towels to the beach.
- no: Mina tar med søsteren sin på konserten.
- en: Mina is bringing her sister to the concert.
:::

Then reverse the task. Turn each current report into an earlier plan or request.

::: examples
- no: Sander har med dokumentene.
- en: Sander has the documents with him.
- no: Vi har med håndklær.
- en: We have towels with us.
- no: Mina har med søsteren sin.
- en: Mina has her sister with her.
:::

This activity tests whether you can preserve the people and objects while changing only the viewpoint. A successful response would use **ta med** for preparation or movement and **ha med** for the resulting situation.

## Back at the station {#back-at-the-station role=orient objectives=obj-ta-med-or-ha-med-01}

Emil reaches the station and finds Nora beside the bus.

::: {.reading translation="Hi! Did you manage to bring the key?" dialogue_id="station" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Hei! Fikk du tatt med nøkkelen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I have it with me here." dialogue_id="station" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ja, jeg har den med her.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. And the rain jacket?" dialogue_id="station" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Flott. Og regnjakka?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I have that with me too. But I forgot the cheese." dialogue_id="station" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Den har jeg også med. Men jeg glemte osten.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s fine. I have some food with me, and we can shop on the way." dialogue_id="station" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det går bra. Jeg har med litt mat, og vi kan handle på veien.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Next time I’ll bring a shopping list." dialogue_id="station" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Neste gang tar jeg med en handleliste.
:::

The key first appears as something Emil must **ta med** from Nora’s home. At the station, he can say that he **har den med** because the action has produced a new situation: the key is now with him. His final sentence returns to **ta med** because he is talking about a future action.

## Guided context practice {#guided-context-practice role=recap objectives=obj-ta-med-or-ha-med-01}

Imagine three moments in one day: preparing for a picnic, meeting at the bus stop, and arriving at the park. Write short Norwegian sentences that do the following:

- Before departure, state that one person will bring food.
- At the bus stop, check whether another person has a blanket with them.
- At the park, report that someone has two friends with them.
- While discussing the next picnic, ask someone to bring a ball.

This practice belongs near the end because it requires you to choose the form from context without copying the wording of the cabin dialogue. Keep each sentence focused on whether accompaniment is being caused or already exists. Successful responses would use the same distinction consistently across objects and people.
