## At the cabin {#at-the-cabin role=orient}

Mina and Eirik are packing after a wet weekend at a rented cabin. Several people’s coats, bags, and skis are crowded together near the door, so they need to identify exactly which things belong to whom.

:::

**Mina:**

::: {.reading translation="What about this old backpack?" dialogue_id="cabin-dialogue" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Hva med denne gamle sekken?
:::

**Eirik:**

Den er min. Kan du legge disse våte hanskene mine på benken?
:::

**Mina:**

::: {.reading translation="Yes. And where should I put our large lunch box?" dialogue_id="cabin-dialogue" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja. Og hvor skal jeg sette den store matboksen vår?
:::

**Eirik:**

::: {.reading translation="Put it next to our new skis." dialogue_id="cabin-dialogue" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Sett den ved siden av de nye skiene våre.
:::

**Mina:**

::: {.reading translation="I can’t find my green scarf." dialogue_id="cabin-dialogue" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Jeg finner ikke mitt grønne skjerf.
:::

**Eirik:**

::: {.reading translation="It’s under this heavy jacket of yours." dialogue_id="cabin-dialogue" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Det ligger under denne tunge jakken din.
:::

Mina and Eirik use several long noun phrases because a simple word such as kofferten or hanskene would not identify the right object. They combine demonstratives, possessives, adjectives, and definite noun forms:

::: examples
- no: denne gamle sekken
- en: this old backpack
- no: disse våte hanskene mine
- en: these wet gloves of mine
- no: den store matboksen vår
- en: our large lunch box
- no: mitt grønne skjerf
- en: my green scarf
:::

Norwegian allows all these elements, but their order is not freely interchangeable.

## The basic purpose {#the-basic-purpose role=model objectives=obj-noun-phrase-order-01}

A group of words built around a noun is a **noun phrase (substantivfrase)**. In English, words such as this, my, and old normally stand before the noun:

- this old backpack
- my old backpack

Bokmål uses more than one pattern. The correct pattern depends especially on whether you use:

- a demonstrative such as denne, dette, or disse;
- a possessive before the noun;
- a possessive after the noun.

The adjective and noun forms must fit the selected pattern. This chapter assumes that you already recognize adjective agreement and the **definite form (bestemt form)** of nouns. Here, the main task is putting those familiar forms into the right sequence.

## Demonstrative first, definite noun last {#demonstrative-first-definite-noun-last role=model objectives=obj-noun-phrase-order-01}

When a demonstrative identifies the noun, the usual order is:

**demonstrative + adjective + definite noun**

The adjective takes its definite form, usually ending in **-e**, and the noun also carries its definite ending.

::: examples
- no: denne gamle sekken
- en: this old backpack
- no: dette store huset
- en: this large house
- no: disse våte hanskene
- en: these wet gloves
:::

Compare the forms inside each phrase:

- denne agrees with a masculine or feminine singular noun;
- dette agrees with a neuter singular noun;
- disse is used with plural nouns;
- the adjective has its definite form;
- the noun is definite: sekken, huset, hanskene.

This produces what may look like repeated definiteness to an English speaker. That repetition is normal and necessary in Bokmål.

::: examples
- no: denne røde bilen
- en: this red car
- no: denne røde bil
- en: intended meaning: this red car
:::

The demonstrative does not replace the noun’s definite ending.

The same pattern works with no adjective:

::: examples
- no: denne sekken
- en: this backpack
- no: dette huset
- en: this house
- no: disse hanskene
- en: these gloves
:::

The adjective simply occupies the middle position when one is present.

## Aligned contrast {#aligned-contrast role=contrast objectives=obj-noun-phrase-order-01}

- Demonstrative: denne; Adjective: gamle; Definite noun: sekken; Meaning: this old backpack
- Demonstrative: dette; Adjective: grønne; Definite noun: skjerfet; Meaning: this green scarf
- Demonstrative: disse; Adjective: nye; Definite noun: skiene; Meaning: these new skis

A compact rule is:

**After denne, dette, or disse, use the definite adjective form and the definite noun form.**

## Possessive after the noun {#possessive-after-the-noun role=model objectives=obj-noun-phrase-order-01}

In everyday Bokmål, a possessive often comes after the noun. Without an adjective, the pattern is:

**definite noun + possessive**

::: examples
- no: kofferten min
- en: my suitcase
- no: skjerfet mitt
- en: my scarf
- no: hanskene mine
- en: my gloves
- no: matboksen vår
- en: our lunch box
:::

The noun must be definite. Compare:

::: examples
- no: sekken min
- en: my backpack
- no: sekk min
- en: intended meaning: my backpack
:::

When an adjective is added, Norwegian needs den, det, or de before the adjective:

**den/det/de + definite adjective + definite noun + possessive**

::: examples
- no: den blå kofferten min
- en: my blue suitcase
- no: det grønne skjerfet mitt
- en: my green scarf
- no: de våte hanskene mine
- en: my wet gloves
:::

The adjective comes before the noun, while the possessive remains after it.

In the dialogue, Eirik says:

::: examples
- no: den blå kofferten min står ved døra
- en: my blue suitcase is by the door
:::

The phrase begins with den because koffert is singular and non-neuter. It ends with min because the suitcase belongs to Eirik. The definite noun kofferten sits between the adjective and the possessive.

For a neuter noun:

::: examples
- no: det grønne skjerfet mitt
- en: my green scarf
:::

For a plural noun:

::: examples
- no: de nye skiene våre
- en: our new skis
:::

## The role of den, det, and de {#the-role-of-den-det-and-de role=contrast objectives=obj-noun-phrase-order-01}

In these phrases, den, det, and de introduce an adjective before a definite noun:

::: examples
- no: den store matboksen
- en: the large lunch box
- no: det gamle huset
- en: the old house
- no: de nye skiene
- en: the new skis
:::

Adding a postposed possessive does not change the order already established:

::: examples
- no: den store matboksen vår
- en: our large lunch box
- no: det gamle huset deres
- en: their old house
- no: de nye skiene våre
- en: our new skis
:::

A compact rule is:

**With a postposed possessive, keep the noun definite. If an adjective is present, use den/det/de before it.**

## Possessive before the noun {#possessive-before-the-noun role=model objectives=obj-noun-phrase-order-01}

A possessive can also stand before the noun:

**possessive + adjective + indefinite noun**

::: examples
- no: min gamle sekk
- en: my old backpack
- no: mitt grønne skjerf
- en: my green scarf
- no: mine våte hansker
- en: my wet gloves
- no: vår store matboks
- en: our large lunch box
:::

The adjective still has its definite-looking **-e** form, but the noun itself is indefinite: sekk, skjerf, hansker, matboks.

Compare the two complete patterns:

- Possessive after noun: den gamle sekken min
- Possessive before noun: min gamle sekk
- Possessive after noun: det grønne skjerfet mitt
- Possessive before noun: mitt grønne skjerf
- Possessive after noun: de våte hanskene mine
- Possessive before noun: mine våte hansker
- Possessive after noun: den store matboksen vår
- Possessive before noun: vår store matboks

The two patterns can refer to the same object. The postposed possessive is especially common in ordinary conversation. A preposed possessive can give the owner more prominence or create a contrast.

::: examples
- no: Jeg finner ikke mitt grønne skjerf.
- en: I can’t find my green scarf.
:::

Here, Mina may be distinguishing her scarf from all the other scarves and clothes in the cabin.

The important grammatical contrast is the noun form:

::: examples
- no: den gamle sekken min
- en: my old backpack
- no: min gamle sekk
- en: my old backpack
:::

The noun is definite because the possessive follows it.

The noun is indefinite because the possessive comes first.

Do not mix the two frames:

::: examples
- no: min gamle sekken
- en: intended meaning: my old backpack
:::

This combines a preposed possessive with a definite noun and is not the standard pattern taught here.

A compact rule is:

- **Possessive after noun:** definite noun.
- **Possessive before noun:** indefinite noun.

## Demonstratives and possessives together {#demonstratives-and-possessives-together role=model objectives=obj-noun-phrase-order-01}

Sometimes both the location or identity of an object and its owner matter. A demonstrative can then begin the phrase, and the possessive can follow the definite noun:

**demonstrative + definite adjective + definite noun + possessive**

::: examples
- no: denne tunge jakken din
- en: this heavy jacket of yours
- no: dette gamle kartet vårt
- en: this old map of ours
- no: disse våte hanskene mine
- en: these wet gloves of mine
:::

This is the fullest pattern in the dialogue:

::: examples
- no: disse våte hanskene mine
- en: these wet gloves of mine
:::

The demonstrative points out the gloves. The adjective describes them. The definite noun names them. The final possessive identifies their owner.

Without an adjective:

::: examples
- no: denne jakken din
- en: this jacket of yours
- no: dette kartet vårt
- en: this map of ours
- no: disse hanskene mine
- en: these gloves of mine
:::

The core order remains visible:

**demonstrative → description → definite noun → owner**

Do not put the possessive directly after the demonstrative:

::: examples
- no: denne tunge jakken din
- en: this heavy jacket of yours
- no: denne din tunge jakke
- en: intended meaning: this heavy jacket of yours
:::

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-noun-phrase-order-01}

**Omitting the noun’s definite ending after a demonstrative**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: dette gamle hus
- en: intended meaning: this old house
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: dette gamle huset
- en: this old house
:::

Norwegian normally marks definiteness on the noun as well as using the demonstrative.

**Using a definite noun after a preposed possessive**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: mitt grønne skjerfet
- en: intended meaning: my green scarf
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: mitt grønne skjerf
- en: my green scarf
:::

A preposed possessive requires the indefinite noun form.

**Forgetting den, det, or de with an adjective and postposed possessive**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: blå kofferten min
- en: intended meaning: my blue suitcase
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: den blå kofferten min
- en: my blue suitcase
:::

The introductory den is required in the standard full phrase.

**Putting every element before the noun as in English**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: min blå kofferten
- en: intended meaning: my blue suitcase
:::

Correct alternatives:

::: examples
- no: min blå koffert
- en: my blue suitcase
- no: den blå kofferten min
- en: my blue suitcase
:::

Choose one Norwegian frame and complete it consistently.

::: examples

Each phrase follows one complete frame. None is formed by arranging individual words in English order.
