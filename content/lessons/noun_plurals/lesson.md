## Getting ready for the neighborhood meeting {#getting-ready-for-the-neighborhood-meeting role=orient objectives=obj-noun-plurals-01}

Nora and Amir are preparing a room for a neighborhood meeting. Nora checks the room while Amir brings things in from the hall.

::: {.reading translation="We need more chairs." dialogue_id="getting-ready-for-the-neighborhood-meeting" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Vi trenger flere stoler.
:::

::: {.reading translation="There are four chairs in the hall." dialogue_id="getting-ready-for-the-neighborhood-meeting" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Det står fire stoler i gangen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. Can you put the chairs by the tables?" dialogue_id="getting-ready-for-the-neighborhood-meeting" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Fint. Kan du sette stolene ved bordene?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Where should the cups go?" dialogue_id="getting-ready-for-the-neighborhood-meeting" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja. Hvor skal koppene stå?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Put the cups on the table, and put the apples in the bowl." dialogue_id="getting-ready-for-the-neighborhood-meeting" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Sett koppene på bordet, og legg eplene i skålen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Do we have enough glasses?" dialogue_id="getting-ready-for-the-neighborhood-meeting" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Har vi nok glass?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, the glasses are in the kitchen." dialogue_id="getting-ready-for-the-neighborhood-meeting" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, glassene er på kjøkkenet.
:::

The conversation distinguishes between things mentioned as a quantity and things treated as a known group. Amir first says that there are **fire stoler**, “four chairs.” Nora then calls the same chairs **stolene**, “the chairs,” because both speakers now know which chairs she means.

This chapter focuses on making and recognizing those two plural forms.

## One noun or more than one {#one-noun-or-more-than-one role=model objectives=obj-noun-plurals-01}

A noun can be singular or plural. **Singular (entall)** refers to one person, place, animal, or thing. **Plural (flertall)** refers to more than one.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: en stol
- en: a chair
- no: stoler
- en: chairs
- no: en kopp
- en: a cup
- no: kopper
- en: cups
- no: et eple
- en: an apple
- no: epler
- en: apples
:::

The singular forms use **en** or **et** here. Indefinite plurals normally have no equivalent of English *a* or *an*:

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger en stol.
- en: We need a chair.
- no: Vi trenger stoler.
- en: We need chairs.
:::

A number or quantity expression can come directly before the plural noun:

::: examples
- no: De har fire stoler.
- en: They have four chairs.
- no: Vi trenger to kopper.
- en: We need two cups.
- no: Hun kjøper flere epler.
- en: She is buying more apples.
:::

The plural noun does not keep **en** or **et**:

::: examples
- no: to stoler
- en: two chairs
- no: to en stol
- en: Incorrect: do not keep **en** before a plural noun.
:::

## Indefinite and definite plurals {#indefinite-and-definite-plurals role=model objectives=obj-noun-plurals-01}

Norwegian distinguishes an **indefinite plural (ubestemt flertall)** from a **definite plural (bestemt flertall)**.

Use the indefinite plural when you mean some members of a group without identifying a particular known set:

::: examples
- no: Amir henter stoler.
- en: Amir is getting chairs.
:::

Use the definite plural when the listener can identify the group:

::: examples
- no: Stolene står i gangen.
- en: The chairs are in the hall.
:::

English normally places *the* before a plural noun. Norwegian normally marks “the” with an ending on the noun:

::: examples
- no: stoler
- en: chairs
- no: stolene
- en: the chairs
:::

Do not add a separate **the** before a simple Norwegian noun. The ending already carries that meaning.

## The most common pattern: **-er** and **-ene** {#the-most-common-pattern-er-and-ene role=contrast objectives=obj-noun-plurals-01}

Many common nouns form the indefinite plural with **-er** and the definite plural with **-ene**.

Compare singular, indefinite plural, and definite plural:

::: examples
- no: en stol
- en: a chair
- no: stoler
- en: chairs
- no: stolene
- en: the chairs
- no: en kopp
- en: a cup
- no: kopper
- en: cups
- no: koppene
- en: the cups
- no: en bil
- en: a car
- no: biler
- en: cars
- no: bilene
- en: the cars
- no: et eple
- en: an apple
- no: epler
- en: apples
- no: eplene
- en: the apples
:::

Notice that the definite form is not normally made by adding **-ene** to the complete indefinite form. It is better to learn the two endings as parallel forms:

::: examples
- no: stol + er → stoler
- en: noun stem plus -er gives chairs
- no: stol + ene → stolene
- en: noun stem plus -ene gives the chairs
:::

This prevents forms such as:

::: examples
- no: stolerene
- en: Incorrect: this form adds the definite ending to the complete indefinite plural.
:::

Compare the forms in sentences:

::: examples
- no: Det står biler utenfor.
- en: There are cars outside.
- no: Bilene tilhører gjestene.
- en: The cars belong to the guests.
- no: Nora finner kopper på kjøkkenet.
- en: Nora finds cups in the kitchen.
- no: Koppene er rene.
- en: The cups are clean.
:::

**Compact rule**

::: {.rule}
For many nouns: indefinite plural: noun stem + **-er**; definite plural: noun stem + **-ene**. Use the indefinite form for an unspecified quantity. Use the definite form for an identifiable group.
:::

**Activity request: notice-indefinite-and-definite-groups**

**Purpose:** Notice the meaning difference between indefinite and definite plural nouns before producing the endings.

**Context:** Short descriptions of Nora and Amir preparing the meeting room.

**Learner action:** Identify whether each plural noun refers to an unspecified quantity or to a group already introduced in the situation.

**Success meaning:** The learner can recognize when Norwegian needs an indefinite plural and when it needs a definite plural. This activity scores the meaning choice, not spelling of endings.

{{exercise: notice_indefinite_and_definite_groups}}

## Nouns ending in an unstressed **-e** {#nouns-ending-in-an-unstressed-e role=model objectives=obj-noun-plurals-01}

Many singular nouns already end in an unstressed **-e**. Their common plural forms look slightly different:

- add **-r** for the indefinite plural
- add **-ne** for the definite plural

Compare singular, indefinite plural, and definite plural:

::: examples
- no: en pose
- en: a bag
- no: poser
- en: bags
- no: posene
- en: the bags
- no: ei/en jente
- en: a girl
- no: jenter
- en: girls
- no: jentene
- en: the girls
- no: en lampe
- en: a lamp
- no: lamper
- en: lamps
- no: lampene
- en: the lamps
:::

The existing **-e** remains part of the word:

::: examples
- no: De kjøper poser.
- en: They are buying bags.
- no: Posene ligger på bordet.
- en: The bags are on the table.
- no: Det står lamper i vinduet.
- en: There are lamps in the window.
- no: Lampene er nye.
- en: The lamps are new.
:::

The overall contrast remains unchanged: **poser** means “bags,” while **posene** means “the bags.”

**Activity request: form-regular-indefinite-plurals**

**Purpose:** Retrieve the common indefinite plural endings.

**Context:** An inventory of ordinary items needed for the neighborhood meeting.

**Learner action:** Change given singular nouns into indefinite plurals in short quantity statements. Nouns ending in an unstressed **-e** should be included alongside nouns such as **stol** and **kopp**.

**Success meaning:** The learner can produce **-er** or **-r** as required by the visible singular form. This activity scores indefinite plural formation only.

{{exercise: form_regular_indefinite_plurals}}

**Activity request: form-regular-definite-plurals**

**Purpose:** Separate definite plural formation from indefinite plural formation.

**Context:** Nora checks the same items after Amir has brought them into the room.

**Learner action:** Change given singular nouns into definite plurals in sentences referring to known groups.

**Success meaning:** The learner can produce forms such as **stolene** and **posene** without incorrectly attaching a definite ending to an already completed indefinite plural. This activity scores definite plural formation only.

{{exercise: form_regular_definite_plurals}}

## Short neuter nouns with no indefinite plural ending {#short-neuter-nouns-with-no-indefinite-plural-ending role=model objectives=obj-noun-plurals-01}

Many short neuter nouns—nouns whose singular form uses **et**—have no visible ending in the indefinite plural.

Compare singular, indefinite plural, and definite plural:

::: examples
- no: et bord
- en: a table
- no: bord
- en: tables
- no: bordene
- en: the tables
- no: et glass
- en: a glass
- no: glass
- en: glasses
- no: glassene
- en: the glasses
- no: et hus
- en: a house
- no: hus
- en: houses
- no: husene
- en: the houses
- no: et barn
- en: a child
- no: barn
- en: children
- no: barnene
- en: the children
:::

The singular and indefinite plural forms may therefore look identical. Other words in the sentence reveal the number:

::: examples
- no: Det står et bord i rommet.
- en: There is one table in the room.
- no: Det står tre bord i rommet.
- en: There are three tables in the room.
- no: Amir finner et glass.
- en: Amir finds a glass.
- no: Amir finner seks glass.
- en: Amir finds six glasses.
:::

The definite plural still has a visible ending:

::: examples
- no: Bordene står ved vinduet.
- en: The tables are by the window.
- no: Glassene er på kjøkkenet.
- en: The glasses are in the kitchen.
:::

A common mistake is to assume that every plural must end in **-er**. Forms such as **border** for “tables” and **glasser** for “glasses” are incorrect in standard Bokmål. Here, use **bord** and **glass**.

::: examples
- no: border
- en: Incorrect: the common plural for “tables” here is **bord**.
- no: glasser
- en: Incorrect: the common plural for “glasses” here is **glass**.
:::

Some neuter nouns can have **-a** in the definite plural, especially in informal or regional usage:

::: examples
- no: barna
- en: the children
- no: husa
- en: the houses
:::

Forms with **-ene**, such as **barnene** and **husene**, are also Bokmål. For consistent production at this stage, **-ene** is a dependable pattern; learners should also recognize common **-a** forms.

**Activity request: recognize-zero-ending-neuter-plurals**

**Purpose:** Show that a plural can be expressed without a plural ending.

**Context:** Counts of tables, glasses, houses, and other short neuter nouns.

**Learner action:** Determine from numbers and sentence meaning whether an unchanged noun is singular or plural.

**Success meaning:** The learner recognizes zero-ending indefinite plurals instead of relying only on the final letters of the noun. This activity scores number recognition.

{{exercise: recognize_zero_ending_neuter_plurals}}

**Activity request: produce-zero-ending-neuter-plurals**

**Purpose:** Prevent overuse of **-er**.

**Context:** Amir reports how many tables and glasses are available.

**Learner action:** Complete short quantity statements with the correct indefinite plural form of familiar short neuter nouns.

**Success meaning:** The learner leaves the noun unchanged where the common pattern requires a zero ending. This activity scores formation of indefinite neuter plurals.

{{exercise: produce_zero_ending_neuter_plurals}}

## Some common nouns change inside the word {#some-common-nouns-change-inside-the-word role=model objectives=obj-noun-plurals-01}

Not every frequent noun follows the regular patterns. Some change a vowel or have another irregular plural form. These forms need to be learned with the noun.

Compare singular, indefinite plural, and definite plural:

::: examples
- no: ei/en bok
- en: a book
- no: bøker
- en: books
- no: bøkene
- en: the books
- no: en mann
- en: a man
- no: menn
- en: men
- no: mennene
- en: the men
- no: en fot
- en: a foot
- no: føtter
- en: feet
- no: føttene
- en: the feet
:::

Use the complete plural form rather than adding a regular ending to the singular:

::: examples
- no: Det ligger bøker på bordet.
- en: There are books on the table.
- no: Bøkene tilhører Nora.
- en: The books belong to Nora.
- no: To menn bærer et bord.
- en: Two men are carrying a table.
- no: Mennene setter bordet ved døra.
- en: The men put the table by the door.
:::

These examples still follow the same meaning contrast. **Bøker** is indefinite; **bøkene** is definite. The unusual part is the noun’s shape, not the communicative choice.

**Activity request: recognize-common-irregular-plurals**

**Purpose:** Build recognition of several frequent plurals that cannot be predicted by the main endings alone.

**Context:** Short meeting-room descriptions involving books, people, and ordinary actions.

**Learner action:** Connect each irregular plural form to its singular noun and determine whether the form is indefinite or definite.

**Success meaning:** The learner recognizes the number and definiteness of common irregular forms. This activity scores recognition, not independent recall of every irregular spelling.

{{exercise: recognize_common_irregular_plurals}}

## Following a noun through a conversation {#following-a-noun-through-a-conversation role=contrast objectives=obj-noun-plurals-01}

A natural conversation often introduces a group with an indefinite plural and then refers back to it with a definite plural:

::: examples
- no: Nora kjøper epler. Eplene ligger i en pose.
- en: Nora buys apples. The apples are in a bag.
:::

The first sentence introduces some apples. The second sentence refers to those same apples.

::: examples
- no: Amir finner tre kopper. Koppene står på kjøkkenet.
- en: Amir finds three cups. The cups are in the kitchen.
- no: Det står to bord i gangen. Bordene er tunge.
- en: There are two tables in the hall. The tables are heavy.
:::

This movement from indefinite to definite explains the forms in the opening dialogue:

::: examples
- no: Det står fire stoler i gangen.
- en: There are four chairs in the hall.
- no: Kan du sette stolene ved bordene?
- en: Can you put the chairs by the tables?
:::

Once the chairs have been introduced, **stolene** identifies that known group. **Bordene** also refers to particular tables that Nora and Amir can identify in the room.

Do not choose the definite plural merely because a noun appears for the second time mechanically. Choose it because the speakers can identify the intended group from earlier words or from the shared situation.

**Activity request: track-plural-reference-in-context**

**Purpose:** Practice the shift from introducing a group to referring back to it.

**Context:** Brief two-sentence scenes about arranging the room.

**Learner action:** Supply an indefinite plural when a group is introduced and a definite plural when the same group is mentioned again.

**Success meaning:** The learner selects plural definiteness from connected meaning rather than treating each sentence in isolation. This activity scores the indefinite-versus-definite choice; regular and zero-ending forms provide context.

{{exercise: track_plural_reference_in_context}}

## Common mistakes to avoid {#common-mistakes-to-avoid role=contrast objectives=obj-noun-plurals-01}

**Keeping the singular article before a plural**

Incorrect pattern:

::: examples
- no: en stoler
- en: Incorrect: do not keep the singular article before a plural noun.
:::

Use the plural noun without **en**:

::: examples
- no: stoler
- en: chairs
- no: to stoler
- en: two chairs
:::

**Adding -er to every noun**

Short neuter nouns often remain unchanged:

::: examples
- no: tre bord
- en: three tables
- no: tre border
- en: Incorrect: do not add **-er** to this short neuter noun.
- no: fem glass
- en: five glasses
- no: fem glasser
- en: Incorrect: do not add **-er** to this short neuter noun.
:::

**Building the definite plural from the whole indefinite form**

The correct pair is:

::: examples
- no: stoler — stolene
- en: chairs — the chairs
- no: kopper — koppene
- en: cups — the cups
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: stoler — stolerene
- en: Incorrect: do not add the definite ending to the whole indefinite plural.
:::

**Confusing a known group with an unspecified quantity**

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger kopper.
- en: We need cups.
- no: Koppene er på kjøkkenet.
- en: The cups are in the kitchen.
:::

The first does not identify particular cups. The second refers to identifiable cups.

**Activity request: repair-plural-form-errors**

**Purpose:** Consolidate the main form boundaries by correcting likely A1 errors.

**Context:** Amir has written a quick inventory note containing singular articles before plurals, unnecessary **-er** endings, and doubled definite endings.

**Learner action:** Rewrite each faulty noun phrase with the appropriate plural form while preserving the intended meaning.

**Success meaning:** The learner produces structurally valid plural nouns. This activity scores form repair; the intended definiteness is supplied by context.

{{exercise: repair_plural_form_errors}}

## Back in the meeting room {#back-in-the-meeting-room role=model objectives=obj-noun-plurals-01}

The room is almost ready. Nora checks Amir’s work.

::: {.reading translation="Now the tables are by the window." dialogue_id="back-in-the-meeting-room" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Nå står bordene ved vinduet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, and I have put six glasses on the tables." dialogue_id="back-in-the-meeting-room" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, og jeg har satt seks glass på bordene.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Where are the bags with apples?" dialogue_id="back-in-the-meeting-room" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Hvor er posene med epler?
:::

::: {.reading translation="The bags are in the kitchen. The apples have been washed." dialogue_id="back-in-the-meeting-room" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Posene står på kjøkkenet. Eplene er vasket.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Then we only need cups." dialogue_id="back-in-the-meeting-room" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Flott. Da mangler vi bare kopper.
:::

::: {.reading translation="The cups are here." dialogue_id="back-in-the-meeting-room" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Koppene er her.
:::

The dialogue now combines all three main patterns:

- **kopper, koppene**: regular **-er/-ene**
- **poser, posene**: singular ending in unstressed **-e**
- **glass, glassene**: zero ending in the indefinite plural

It also repeats the central meaning contrast:

::: examples
- no: Da mangler vi bare kopper.
- en: Then we only need cups.
- no: Koppene er her.
- en: The cups are here.
:::

The cups are presented as a needed quantity.

Amir identifies the relevant cups and says where they are.

## Short retrieval review {#short-retrieval-review role=recap objectives=obj-noun-plurals-01}

Without returning to the tables above, write the indefinite and definite plural forms of several familiar nouns from the chapter. Then complete a short two-sentence scene in which the first sentence introduces more than one object and the second refers back to the same objects.

A successful review shows two things: you can form common plural nouns, and you can choose between “some objects” and “the identifiable objects” in context.
