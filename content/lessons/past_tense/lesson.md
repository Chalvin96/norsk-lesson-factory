## A missed phone call {#missed-phone-call role=orient objectives=obj-past-tense-01}

Maja and Jonas meet at work on Monday morning. Maja wants to know why Jonas did not answer his phone the previous evening.

::: {.reading translation="What did you do yesterday?" dialogue_id=missed-phone-call speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Hva gjorde du i går?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I worked until four o’clock. Then I took the bus to the city centre and met Sara." dialogue_id=missed-phone-call speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg jobbet til klokka fire. Så tok jeg bussen til sentrum og møtte Sara.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Did you go to the cinema?" dialogue_id=missed-phone-call speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Gikk dere på kino?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, we ate at a small restaurant. Afterwards, we went for a walk." dialogue_id=missed-phone-call speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nei, vi spiste på en liten restaurant. Etterpå gikk vi en tur.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I called you at nine, but you didn’t answer." dialogue_id=missed-phone-call speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jeg ringte deg klokka ni, men du svarte ikke.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Sorry. I didn’t see the phone." dialogue_id=missed-phone-call speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Beklager. Jeg så ikke telefonen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="When did you come home?" dialogue_id=missed-phone-call speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Når kom du hjem?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I came home at eleven o’clock." dialogue_id=missed-phone-call speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg kom hjem klokka elleve.
:::

The conversation has a clear past-time frame. Maja begins with i går, yesterday, and later mentions klokka ni, at nine o’clock. Jonas describes a sequence of events within that known period: he worked, took the bus, met Sara, ate, walked, and came home.

The verbs therefore appear in the simple past, called the **past tense (preteritum)** in Norwegian.

## Saying what happened at a known past time {#known-past-time role=model objectives=obj-past-tense-01}

Use the preteritum when an action or state belongs to a known past time. The time may be stated directly:

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobbet i går.
- en: I worked yesterday.
- no: Hun kom hjem klokka ti.
- en: She came home at ten o’clock.
- no: Vi bodde i Bergen i 2022.
- en: We lived in Bergen in 2022.
:::

It may also be clear from the conversation. Once Maja has asked Hva gjorde du i går?, Jonas does not need to repeat i går in every answer. The shared context already establishes the time.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tok bussen til sentrum.
- en: I took the bus to the city centre.
- no: Jeg møtte Sara.
- en: I met Sara.
- no: Vi spiste på en restaurant.
- en: We ate at a restaurant.
:::

The preteritum can describe actions, events, and states:

::: examples
- no: Det regnet hele dagen.
- en: It rained all day.
- no: Jeg var trøtt etter jobb.
- en: I was tired after work.
- no: Vi hadde god tid.
- en: We had plenty of time.
:::

**Present or past?**

Compare the present tense with the preteritum:

::: examples
- no: Jonas jobber til klokka fire i dag.
- en: Jonas works until four o’clock today.
- no: Jonas jobbet til klokka fire i går.
- en: Jonas worked until four o’clock yesterday.
- no: Maja ringer Jonas nå.
- en: Maja is calling Jonas now.
- no: Maja ringte Jonas klokka ni i går.
- en: Maja called Jonas at nine yesterday.
- no: De tar bussen hver mandag.
- en: They take the bus every Monday.
- no: De tok bussen forrige mandag.
- en: They took the bus last Monday.
:::

The time frame controls the choice. Nå and i dag can establish a present frame. I går, forrige mandag, and a past date establish a past frame.

**Compact rule:**

::: {.rule}
When the sentence describes what happened or was true at a stated or shared past time, put the verb in the preteritum.
:::

## Forming the preteritum of regular verbs {#regular-preteritum role=model objectives=obj-past-tense-01}

Many Norwegian verbs form the preteritum by adding an ending. These are **regular verbs**. Several common endings occur, so it is useful to learn an infinitive together with its past form.

**Verbs ending in -et**

A large group adds -et:

::: examples
- no: å jobbe – jobbet
- en: to work – worked
- no: å snakke – snakket
- en: to speak – spoke
- no: å vente – ventet
- en: to wait – waited
- no: å hente – hentet
- en: to fetch – fetched
:::

**In sentences:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobbet hjemme i går.
- en: I worked at home yesterday.
- no: Vi snakket med læreren etter timen.
- en: We spoke with the teacher after class.
- no: Bussen kom sent, så jeg ventet i tjue minutter.
- en: The bus arrived late, so I waited for twenty minutes.
:::

**Verbs ending in -te**

Another common group has -te:

::: examples
- no: å kjøpe – kjøpte
- en: to buy – bought
- no: å møte – møtte
- en: to meet – met
- no: å ringe – ringte
- en: to call – called
- no: å spise – spiste
- en: to eat – ate
:::

**In sentences:**

::: examples
- no: Sara kjøpte en kaffe på stasjonen.
- en: Sara bought a coffee at the station.
- no: Jonas møtte Sara i sentrum.
- en: Jonas met Sara in the city centre.
- no: Maja ringte klokka ni.
- en: Maja called at nine o’clock.
- no: De spiste på en liten restaurant.
- en: They ate at a small restaurant.
:::

Some verbs show a spelling adjustment as well as the ending. For example, å møte becomes møtte. Learn the written past form rather than adding letters mechanically.

**Verbs ending in -de**

Some regular verbs form the past with -de:

::: examples
- no: å prøve – prøvde
- en: to try – tried
- no: å leve – levde
- en: to live – lived
:::

**In sentences:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg prøvde å ringe deg.
- en: I tried to call you.
- no: Familien levde i Oslo i mange år.
- en: The family lived in Oslo for many years.
:::

**Short verbs ending in -dde**

Many short infinitives ending in a stressed vowel form the past with -dde:

::: examples
- no: å bo – bodde
- en: to live – lived
- no: å tro – trodde
- en: to think or believe – thought or believed
- no: å nå – nådde
- en: to reach – reached
:::

**In sentences:**

::: examples
- no: Vi bodde nær sentrum i fjor.
- en: We lived near the city centre last year.
- no: Jeg trodde restauranten var stengt.
- en: I thought the restaurant was closed.
- no: De nådde stasjonen klokka åtte.
- en: They reached the station at eight o’clock.
:::

These families help you notice patterns, but they do not let you predict every form with complete certainty. Record new verbs as pairs:

::: examples
- no: å ringe – ringte
- en: to call – called
- no: å svare – svarte
- en: to answer – answered
:::

This is safer than remembering only the infinitive.

## One form for every subject {#one-form-every-subject role=model objectives=obj-past-tense-01}

Norwegian past-tense verbs do not change according to the subject. The same form follows jeg, du, han, hun, vi, dere, and de.

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobbet i går.
- en: I worked yesterday.
- no: Hun jobbet i går.
- en: She worked yesterday.
- no: Vi jobbet i går.
- en: We worked yesterday.
- no: De jobbet i går.
- en: They worked yesterday.
:::

The verb jobbet remains unchanged.

This differs from English verbs such as was and were, but Norwegian still uses one preteritum form:

::: examples
- no: Jeg var hjemme.
- en: I was at home.
- no: Vi var hjemme.
- en: We were at home.
:::

Do not add a new ending because the subject is plural.

## Common irregular verbs {#common-irregular-verbs role=model objectives=obj-past-tense-01}

Some frequent verbs do not form the preteritum with a predictable regular ending. These are **irregular verbs**. Their past forms must be learned, but many occur so often that they soon become familiar.

::: examples
- no: å være – var
- en: to be – was or were
- no: å ha – hadde
- en: to have – had
- no: å gjøre – gjorde
- en: to do – did
- no: å gå – gikk
- en: to go or walk – went or walked
- no: å komme – kom
- en: to come – came
- no: å ta – tok
- en: to take – took
- no: å se – så
- en: to see – saw
- no: å få – fikk
- en: to get or receive – got or received
- no: å dra – dro
- en: to leave or go – left or went
- no: å bli – ble
- en: to become or remain – became or remained
:::

Several of these forms appear in the opening dialogue:

::: examples
- no: Hva gjorde du i går?
- en: What did you do yesterday?
- no: Jeg tok bussen.
- en: I took the bus.
- no: Vi gikk en tur.
- en: We went for a walk.
- no: Jeg så ikke telefonen.
- en: I didn’t see the phone.
- no: Jeg kom hjem klokka elleve.
- en: I came home at eleven o’clock.
:::

Other common examples:

::: examples
- no: Vi var på jobb til klokka fem.
- en: We were at work until five o’clock.
- no: Sara hadde en avtale i sentrum.
- en: Sara had an appointment in the city centre.
- no: Jeg fikk en melding fra Jonas.
- en: I received a message from Jonas.
- no: De dro hjem etter filmen.
- en: They went home after the film.
:::

A common mistake is to attach a regular ending to an irregular past form.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg gikket hjem klokka ti.
- en: I walked home at ten o’clock.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Jeg gikk hjem klokka ti.
- en: I walked home at ten o’clock.
:::

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Vi tokte bussen.
- en: We took the bus.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Vi tok bussen.
- en: We took the bus.
:::

The forms gikk and tok are already complete preteritum forms.

## Negatives and questions in the past {#past-negatives-and-questions role=model objectives=obj-past-tense-01}

The preteritum form remains on the main verb in negative statements.

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobbet ikke på søndag.
- en: I did not work on Sunday.
- no: Maja ringte ikke om morgenen.
- en: Maja did not call in the morning.
- no: Jonas tok ikke toget.
- en: Jonas did not take the train.
- no: De så ikke telefonen.
- en: They did not see the phone.
:::

Norwegian does not use an equivalent of English did not before another infinitive. Use the Norwegian past form directly:

::: examples
- no: Jeg gikk ikke på kino.
- en: I did not go to the cinema.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg gjorde ikke gå på kino.
- en: I did not go to the cinema.
:::

Questions also use the preteritum form directly, normally before the subject:

::: examples
- no: Jobbet du i går?
- en: Did you work yesterday?
- no: Ringte Maja klokka ni?
- en: Did Maja call at nine?
- no: Tok Jonas bussen?
- en: Did Jonas take the bus?
- no: Når kom du hjem?
- en: When did you come home?
:::

English uses did in these questions, but Norwegian does not add a separate past-tense helper. Jobbet, ringte, tok, and kom already express past time.

## Checkpoint request: maintain-preteritum-in-questions-and-negatives {#checkpoint-maintain-preteritum-questions-negatives role=contrast objectives=obj-past-tense-01}

**Purpose:** Prevent English-style use of did from disrupting Norwegian past-tense sentences.

**Context:** Maja asks follow-up questions about Jonas’s evening, and Jonas corrects details she has misunderstood.

**Learner action:** Turn controlled past statements into questions or negative statements while keeping the main verb in its correct preteritum form.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Maintain the Norwegian preteritum form when producing past questions and negatives.

**Success meaning:** The learner writes forms such as Tok du bussen? and Jeg tok ikke bussen without introducing an unnecessary equivalent of did.

## Choosing the tense from the time frame {#choose-tense-time-frame role=contrast objectives=obj-past-tense-01}

Knowing a past form is not enough; you must also decide when to use it. Compare these pairs:

::: examples
- no: Jeg bor i Oslo nå.
- en: I live in Oslo now.
- no: Jeg bodde i Oslo i 2023.
- en: I lived in Oslo in 2023.
- no: Sara går til jobb hver dag.
- en: Sara walks to work every day.
- no: Sara gikk til jobb i går.
- en: Sara walked to work yesterday.
- no: Vi spiser middag klokka seks i dag.
- en: We are eating dinner at six today.
- no: Vi spiste middag klokka seks i går.
- en: We ate dinner at six yesterday.
:::

Do not keep the present tense merely because the verb describes a habit in general. If the sentence refers to a particular known past occasion, use the preteritum.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ I går jobber jeg hjemme.
- en: Yesterday I worked from home.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: I går jobbet jeg hjemme.
- en: Yesterday I worked from home.
:::

The same applies when the time is established in a question:

::: examples
- no: Hva gjorde du på lørdag?
- en: What did you do on Saturday?
- no: Jeg besøkte foreldrene mine.
- en: I visited my parents.
:::

The answer does not repeat på lørdag, but it remains within that past-time frame.

**Success meaning:** The learner uses time and context to make the tense choice, including when the past-time expression appears only in the preceding question. Exact regular and irregular spelling supports the response but is not the main scored dimension here.

## Back to Jonas and Sara {#back-to-jonas-and-sara role=model objectives=obj-past-tense-01}

Later, Sara sends Maja a message and gives her version of the evening.

::: {.reading translation="Jonas met me at the station at five. First we bought coffee. Then we walked to a restaurant and ate dinner. Jonas received a message, but he did not look at his phone. I went home at half past nine, and Jonas took the bus a little later." dialogue_id=saras-message speaker_id=sara speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Jonas møtte meg ved stasjonen klokka fem. Først kjøpte vi kaffe. Så gikk vi til en restaurant og spiste middag. Jonas fikk en melding, men han så ikke på telefonen. Jeg dro hjem klokka halv ti, og Jonas tok bussen litt senere.
:::

Sara combines regular verbs and irregular verbs within one clear time frame.

Regular forms include:

::: examples
- no: møtte, kjøpte
- en: met, bought
:::

Irregular forms include:

::: examples
- no: gikk, fikk, så, dro, tok
- en: went, received, saw, left, took
:::

The sequence words først, så, and litt senere organize the account, but the grammar stays simple: each event happened during the known past evening, so each verb is in the preteritum.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Use correctly formed regular and common irregular preteritum verbs consistently within a known past-time account.

**Success meaning:** The learner keeps the narrative in the preteritum and produces the required forms accurately. Event order is context, not a separate scored language outcome.
