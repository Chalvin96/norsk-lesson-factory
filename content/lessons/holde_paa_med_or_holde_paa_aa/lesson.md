## A room in the middle of a makeover {#room-in-the-middle-of-a-makeover role=orient objectives=obj-holde-paa-med-or-holde-paa-aa-01}

Sara calls her friend Jonas on Saturday morning. He has recently moved into a small apartment and is trying to finish several jobs before friends visit that evening.

::: {.reading translation="Hi, Jonas. What are you doing?" dialogue_id="room-makeover" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Hei, Jonas. Hva holder du på med?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’m painting the bookcase." dialogue_id="room-makeover" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg holder på å male bokhylla.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Are you still working on the renovation?" dialogue_id="room-makeover" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Holder du fortsatt på med oppussingen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. I’m also working on the kitchen, but the bookcase has to be finished first." dialogue_id="room-makeover" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. Jeg holder også på med kjøkkenet, men bokhylla må bli ferdig først.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Can I drop by later?" dialogue_id="room-makeover" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Kan jeg komme innom senere?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Sure, but not right now. I’m moving the bookcase, and it’s very heavy." dialogue_id="room-makeover" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Gjerne, men ikke akkurat nå. Jeg holder på å flytte bokhylla, og den er veldig tung.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Be careful!" dialogue_id="room-makeover" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Gjør det forsiktig!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Wait a moment … I’m moving the heavy bookcase!" dialogue_id="room-makeover" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Vent litt … Jeg holder på å flytte den tunge bokhylla!
:::

Jonas describes the same general situation in two different ways. Sometimes he names an activity or project:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holder på med oppussingen.
- en: I’m working on the renovation.
:::

At other times he describes an action unfolding at that moment:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holder på å male bokhylla.
- en: I’m painting the bookcase.
:::

The choice between **holde på med** and **holde på å** depends on what comes next.

## Naming an activity: holde på med {#naming-an-activity role=contrast objectives=obj-holde-paa-med-or-holde-paa-aa-01}

Use **holde på med** before a noun or a noun-like expression. The noun names the activity, task, subject, or project occupying someone.

The pattern is:

**holde på med + noun or nominal activity**

Here, **nominal activity** simply means an activity expressed as a thing or a named task rather than as an infinitive verb.

::: examples
- no: Jonas holder på med oppussingen.
- en: Jonas is working on the renovation.
- no: Han holder på med kjøkkenet.
- en: He is working on the kitchen.
- no: Sara holder på med leksene.
- en: Sara is doing her homework.
- no: Vi holder på med et nytt prosjekt.
- en: We are working on a new project.
- no: Hva holder dere på med?
- en: What are you doing?
:::

The question **Hva holder du på med?** is extremely useful. Literally, it asks what activity someone is occupied with. In ordinary conversation, it often corresponds to What are you doing? or What are you working on?

The answer can name a broad activity:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holder på med norskleksene.
- en: I’m working on my Norwegian homework.
:::

It can name a physical object that represents the work being done:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holder på med sykkelen.
- en: I’m working on the bicycle.
:::

It can also name a larger project:

::: examples
- no: De holder på med en rapport om kollektivtransport.
- en: They are working on a report about public transport.
:::

With **med**, the listener learns what task, subject, or project occupies the person. The sentence does not necessarily identify the exact action taking place at that second. Jonas may be painting, measuring, cleaning, or moving furniture as part of **oppussingen**.

**Checkpoint: identify the named activity**

Imagine three people during a busy afternoon: one is occupied with a school assignment, one with dinner, and one with repairs to a bicycle. Describe each person using **holde på med** and a suitable noun expression.

This activity belongs here because it tests the first half of the contrast without introducing an infinitive action yet. A successful response will place **med** directly before a noun or named task and will describe what each person is occupied with.

## Showing an unfolding action: holde på å {#showing-an-unfolding-action role=contrast objectives=obj-holde-paa-med-or-holde-paa-aa-01}

Use **holde på å** before an infinitive when you want to show an action unfolding or developing.

The pattern is:

**holde på å + infinitive**

The infinitive marker is **å**, followed by the infinitive form of the verb.

::: examples
- no: Jonas holder på å male bokhylla.
- en: Jonas is painting the bookcase.
- no: Sara holder på å skrive en melding.
- en: Sara is writing a message.
- no: Barna holder på å rydde rommet.
- en: The children are tidying the room.
- no: Vi holder på å lage middag.
- en: We are making dinner.
- no: Hva holder de på å gjøre?
- en: What are they doing?
:::

This construction focuses on the action itself. Compare Jonas’s two descriptions:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holder på med bokhylla.
- en: I’m working on the bookcase.
- no: Jeg holder på å male bokhylla.
- en: I’m painting the bookcase.
:::

The first sentence names the object or task. It leaves the exact action open. The second says precisely what is happening: Jonas is painting.

The same distinction appears in these pairs:

::: examples
- no: Nora holder på med middagen.
- en: Nora is working on dinner.
- no: Nora holder på å kutte grønnsaker.
- en: Nora is cutting vegetables.
- no: Amir holder på med bilen.
- en: Amir is working on the car.
- no: Amir holder på å skifte et dekk.
- en: Amir is changing a tire.
- no: Elevene holder på med en presentasjon.
- en: The pupils are working on a presentation.
- no: Elevene holder på å finne bilder til presentasjonen.
- en: The pupils are finding pictures for the presentation.
:::

In each pair, **med** introduces the named activity or project. **Å** introduces the infinitive action unfolding within that activity.

**Checkpoint: make the action more precise**

Return to the three people from the previous checkpoint. Replace each broad description with a more precise action: the student is answering the final question, the cook is washing vegetables, and the cyclist is tightening a screw. Use **holde på å** followed by the appropriate infinitive.

This step follows the noun practice because it asks you to change perspective while keeping the situation familiar. A successful response will replace the named activity with an infinitive action and will use **å**, not **med**, before that infinitive.

## One situation, two viewpoints {#one-situation-two-viewpoints role=contrast objectives=obj-holde-paa-med-or-holde-paa-aa-01}

The difference is not always a difference in the real-world situation. Often, both constructions can describe the same person at the same time. They simply package the information differently.

Suppose Ingrid is preparing for an exam and is reading chapter six.

::: examples
- no: Ingrid holder på med eksamensforberedelsene.
- en: Ingrid is working on her exam preparation.
- no: Ingrid holder på å lese kapittel seks.
- en: Ingrid is reading chapter six.
:::

The first sentence gives the larger activity. The second zooms in on the action happening within it.

Suppose Marius is preparing the garden and is planting flowers.

::: examples
- no: Marius holder på med hagen.
- en: Marius is working on the garden.
- no: Marius holder på å plante blomster.
- en: Marius is planting flowers.
:::

Again, the choice depends on the speaker’s focus:

- Use **med** to name the activity, task, project, or thing being worked on.
- Use **å** to introduce an infinitive and show the action in progress.

English often uses an -ing form for both meanings, so translating word by word is not enough. First decide whether the Norwegian expression that follows will be a noun or an infinitive.

::: examples
- no: De holder på med ryddingen.
- en: They are doing the tidying.
- no: De holder på å rydde.
- en: They are tidying.
:::

Here, **ryddingen** is a noun: the tidying or the clean-up. **Rydde** is an infinitive: to tidy.

**Checkpoint: move between broad and specific descriptions**

Picture a community hall before a party. Some people are occupied with the decorations, food, and music. Describe these broad activities with **holde på med**. Then zoom in: someone is hanging balloons, someone is baking a cake, and someone is testing the speakers. Describe those actions with **holde på å**.

This paired activity belongs here because it checks whether you can describe one context from both viewpoints. Success means keeping **med + named activity** and **å + infinitive action** distinct while producing descriptions that could naturally belong to the same scene.

{{exercise: move_between_broad_and_specific_descriptions}}

## Questions in conversation {#questions-in-conversation role=model objectives=obj-holde-paa-med-or-holde-paa-aa-01}

The contrast also helps you ask different kinds of questions.

::: examples
- no: Hva holder du på med?
- en: What are you doing?
:::

This open question asks for the activity or situation occupying the person. An answer can be broad:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holder på med en jobbsøknad.
- en: I’m working on a job application.
:::

If you already know the likely action, you can ask about that unfolding action:

::: examples
- no: Holder du på å skrive en jobbsøknad?
- en: Are you writing a job application?
:::

The answer can confirm or correct the action:

::: examples
- no: Ja, jeg holder på å skrive den nå.
- en: Yes, I’m writing it now.
- no: Nei, jeg holder på med CV-en først.
- en: No, I’m working on the CV first.
:::

Notice how the speakers move naturally between the two patterns. The question with **å skrive** proposes a specific action. The answer with **med CV-en** names a different task.

Another exchange might sound like this:

::: {.reading translation="What are you doing?" dialogue_id="meeting-preparations" speaker_id="lina" speaker_name="Lina" character_id="lina"}
Hva holder dere på med?
:::

::: {.reading translation="We’re working on the preparations for the meeting." dialogue_id="meeting-preparations" speaker_id="paal" speaker_name="Pål"}
Vi holder på med forberedelsene til møtet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Are you writing the agenda?" dialogue_id="meeting-preparations" speaker_id="lina" speaker_name="Lina" character_id="lina"}
Holder dere på å skrive dagsordenen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, we’re checking the equipment." dialogue_id="meeting-preparations" speaker_id="paal" speaker_name="Pål"}
Nei, vi holder på å sjekke utstyret.
:::

**Checkpoint: ask, then narrow the focus**

Create a short exchange between two colleagues preparing for a meeting. Begin with the broad question **Hva holder du på med?** Let the answer name a task with **med**. Then ask a second question proposing a specific action with **holde på å + infinitive**.

This activity comes after the basic contrast because it requires you to use both forms as part of a connected conversation. A successful exchange will move naturally from a broad activity to a specific unfolding action.

{{exercise: ask_then_narrow_the_focus}}

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-holde-paa-med-or-holde-paa-aa-01}

**Using med directly before an infinitive**

Do not use the clean **med + noun** pattern and then place a bare infinitive after it.

Incorrect target pattern:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg holder på med lage middag.
- en: ✗ I am making dinner.
:::

Use an infinitive action after **å**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holder på å lage middag.
- en: I am making dinner.
:::

Or replace the verb with a named task after **med**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holder på med middagen.
- en: I am working on dinner.
:::

The key question is not simply whether the general meaning is similar. Look at the form that follows: **lage** is an infinitive, while **middagen** is a noun.

**Using å before a noun**

The infinitive marker **å** must introduce an infinitive, not a noun.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Hun holder på å rapporten.
- en: ✗ She is working on the report.
:::

Correct with a noun:

::: examples
- no: Hun holder på med rapporten.
- en: She is working on the report.
:::

Correct with an infinitive:

::: examples
- no: Hun holder på å skrive rapporten.
- en: She is writing the report.
:::

**Forgetting that the two versions differ in focus**

These sentences are both possible, but they do not provide the same amount of detail:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holder på med vinduene.
- en: I’m working on the windows.
- no: Jeg holder på å vaske vinduene.
- en: I’m washing the windows.
:::

The first does not tell us exactly what is being done to the windows. The second identifies the action.

**Checkpoint: repair the structure**

Take several short descriptions in which **med** has accidentally been placed before an infinitive or **å** before a noun. Repair each one without changing the intended meaning. Where useful, produce two repairs: one with a noun after **med**, and one with an infinitive after **å**.

This correction activity belongs here because recognizing a bad combination is easier after seeing both valid patterns repeatedly. Success means checking the grammatical form after the phrase rather than choosing only from the English translation.

{{exercise: repair_the_structure}}

## Back at Jonas’s apartment {#back-at-jonass-apartment role=model objectives=obj-holde-paa-med-or-holde-paa-aa-01}

Sara arrives later in the afternoon. The room looks better, but the work is not finished.

::: {.reading translation="I’m here now. What are you doing now?" dialogue_id="jonas-apartment-2" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Nå er jeg her. Hva holder du på med nå?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’m working on the lamps." dialogue_id="jonas-apartment-2" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg holder på med lampene.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Are you hanging up the large lamp?" dialogue_id="jonas-apartment-2" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Holder du på å henge opp den store lampen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, I’m changing a light bulb. Can you help me in the meantime?" dialogue_id="jonas-apartment-2" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nei, jeg holder på å bytte en lyspære. Kan du hjelpe meg imens?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. I can work on the bookcase while you finish the lamps." dialogue_id="jonas-apartment-2" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Ja. Jeg kan holde på med bokhylla mens du gjør ferdig lampene.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Now I’m changing the light bulb." dialogue_id="jonas-apartment-2" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Flott. Nå holder jeg på å bytte lyspæra.
:::

The same present-tense contrast appears in both replies:

::: examples
- no: Jeg holder på med lampene.
- en: I’m working on the lamps.
- no: Jeg holder på å bytte en lyspære.
- en: I’m changing a light bulb.
- no: Jeg holder på å bytte en lyspære.
- en: I’m changing a light bulb.
:::

The lesson’s main decision still comes after **på**: choose **med + noun** for the named activity or **å + infinitive** for the unfolding action.

## Final practice in a new situation {#final-practice-in-a-new-situation role=model objectives=obj-holde-paa-med-or-holde-paa-aa-01}

Imagine a family preparing to leave for a weekend trip. One person is occupied with the luggage, another with breakfast, and another with the car. First describe each broad task using **holde på med**. Then make each description more precise by saying that someone is packing clothes, making sandwiches, or checking the tires, using **holde på å**.

Next, turn one pair of descriptions into a brief dialogue. One speaker asks what the other is doing, receives a broad answer, and follows up with a question about a specific action. A successful response will use the two patterns consistently and make the shift from activity to action clear.

Without looking back, complete this rule aloud: use **holde på med** before a named activity or noun, and use **holde på å** before an infinitive action. Then describe what Jonas is working on and what he is doing now, using one present-tense sentence with each pattern.
