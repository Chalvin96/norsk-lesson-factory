## At the second-hand shop {#at-the-second-hand-shop role=orient objectives=obj-noun-gender-01}

Maja has recently moved into a small flat. She and her friend Jonas are looking for inexpensive furniture and household items.

**Maja:**

::: {.reading translation="I need a table and a chair for the kitchen." dialogue_id="at-the-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jeg trenger et bord og en stol til kjøkkenet.
:::

**Jonas:**

::: {.reading translation="Look at this table. It is small but nice." dialogue_id="at-the-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Se på dette bordet. Det er lite, men fint.
:::

**Maja:**

::: {.reading translation="Yes, and that chair fits well." dialogue_id="at-the-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Ja, og den stolen passer godt.
:::

**Jonas:**

::: {.reading translation="Do you need a lamp too?" dialogue_id="at-the-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Trenger du ei lampe også?
:::

**Maja:**

::: {.reading translation="Yes. A lamp for the living room and a rug for the bedroom." dialogue_id="at-the-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Ja. Ei lampe til stua og et teppe til soverommet.
:::

**Jonas:**

::: {.reading translation="What about this picture?" dialogue_id="at-the-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hva med dette bildet?
:::

**Maja:**

::: {.reading translation="It is nice, but I do not need a picture. I need a shelf for my books." dialogue_id="at-the-second-hand-shop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Det er fint, men jeg trenger ikke et bilde. Jeg trenger ei hylle til bøkene mine.
:::

Maja is naming objects she needs. Each singular noun appears with one of three short words: en, ei, or et. To speak naturally, she needs to know which word belongs with each noun.

Notice the useful expressions from the scene:

::: examples
- no: Jeg trenger et bord.
- en: I need a table.
- no: Trenger du ei lampe?
- en: Do you need a lamp?
- no: Jeg trenger ikke et bilde.
- en: I do not need a picture.
:::

The sentence pattern stays simple. The article changes because the noun changes.

## Three grammatical genders {#three-grammatical-genders role=model objectives=obj-noun-gender-01}

Norwegian nouns have grammatical gender. In Norwegian, this is called kjønn. Bokmål traditionally has three genders:

- masculine (hankjønn), normally used with en
- feminine (hunkjønn), normally used with ei
- neuter (intetkjønn), used with et

The words en, ei, and et are indefinite articles. They correspond roughly to English a or an.

::: examples
- no: en stol
- en: a chair
- no: ei lampe
- en: a lamp
- no: et bord
- en: a table
:::

English uses a chair, a lamp, and a table. Norwegian divides these nouns into different grammatical groups, so the article is not always the same.

Gender is a property of the Norwegian noun. It usually cannot be predicted from the meaning. A table is not naturally more “neuter” than a chair, and a lamp is not biologically feminine. Learn the article together with the noun.

Do not learn only bord. Learn et bord.

Do not learn only stol. Learn en stol.

Do not learn only lampe. Learn ei lampe.

This turns the gender into part of the word you remember.

## Masculine nouns with en {#masculine-nouns-with-en role=model objectives=obj-noun-gender-01}

Many common Norwegian nouns are masculine and take en in the indefinite singular.

::: examples
- no: en stol
- en: a chair
- no: en kopp
- en: a cup
- no: en nøkkel
- en: a key
- no: en telefon
- en: a telephone
- no: en butikk
- en: a shop
:::

Use the complete noun phrase in a sentence:

::: examples
- no: Jeg trenger en stol.
- en: I need a chair.
- no: Jonas har en nøkkel.
- en: Jonas has a key.
- no: Det er en butikk i sentrum.
- en: There is a shop in the town centre.
:::

The compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
**Masculine noun → en + noun**
:::

A common mistake is to choose en for every noun because en is frequent and resembles English a in function.

Incorrect for the intended three-gender pattern:

::: examples
- no: en bord
- en: 'Incorrect: a table'
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: et bord
- en: a table
:::

Frequency does not make en a universal article. The noun decides.

## Feminine nouns with ei {#feminine-nouns-with-ei role=model objectives=obj-noun-gender-01}

Feminine nouns can take ei in Bokmål.

::: examples
- no: ei lampe
- en: a lamp
- no: ei bok
- en: a book
- no: ei hylle
- en: a shelf
- no: ei dør
- en: a door
- no: ei avis
- en: a newspaper
:::

In sentences:

::: examples
- no: Maja kjøper ei lampe.
- en: Maja is buying a lamp.
- no: Jeg leser ei bok.
- en: I am reading a book.
- no: Rommet har ei dør.
- en: The room has a door.
:::

The compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
**Feminine noun → ei + noun**
:::

Bokmål also allows many feminine nouns to be treated as common-gender nouns with en. You may therefore encounter both ei lampe and en lampe. Both can be correct Bokmål. In this lesson, using ei makes the traditional three-gender pattern clear and helps you recognize feminine nouns when other speakers and writers use it.

This variation does not mean that ei can be used with any noun. It belongs to feminine nouns.

::: examples
- no: ei bok
- en: a book
:::

not:

::: examples
- no: ei bord
- en: 'Incorrect: a table'
:::

## Neuter nouns with et {#neuter-nouns-with-et role=model objectives=obj-noun-gender-01}

Neuter nouns take et in the indefinite singular.

::: examples
- no: et bord
- en: a table
- no: et bilde
- en: a picture
- no: et teppe
- en: a rug
- no: et vindu
- en: a window
- no: et rom
- en: a room
:::

In sentences:

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger et bord.
- en: We need a table.
- no: Hun kjøper et teppe.
- en: She is buying a rug.
- no: Leiligheten har et stort vindu.
- en: The flat has a large window.
:::

The compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
**Neuter noun → et + noun**
:::

Do not confuse et with the number ett. They sound similar, but the ordinary indefinite article is written et.

::: examples
- no: Jeg trenger et bord.
- en: I need a table.
:::

Here et means a, not specifically one. At this stage, concentrate on learning et as the article used with a neuter noun.

## The central choice {#the-central-choice role=contrast objectives=obj-noun-gender-01}

When you want to name one non-specific person, place, or thing, ask:

1. What is the noun?
2. Which gender belongs to that noun?
3. Use en, ei, or et before it.

Compare the three patterns:

::: examples
- no: Maja kjøper en stol.
- en: Maja is buying a chair.
- no: Maja kjøper ei lampe.
- en: Maja is buying a lamp.
- no: Maja kjøper et bord.
- en: Maja is buying a table.
:::

The situation and sentence structure are the same. Only the noun and its gender article change.

Another aligned contrast:

::: examples
- no: Det står en kopp på bordet.
- en: There is a cup on the table.
- no: Det ligger ei bok på bordet.
- en: There is a book on the table.
- no: Det ligger et bilde på bordet.
- en: There is a picture on the table.
:::

The article gives grammatical information before the listener reaches the noun.

## Learning nouns efficiently {#learning-nouns-efficiently role=model objectives=obj-noun-gender-01}

Because meaning usually does not reveal gender, vocabulary study should preserve the article.

A weak vocabulary note looks like this:

bord — table

A more useful note looks like this:

::: examples
- no: et bord
- en: a table
:::

You can also organize new words in three groups:

**en nouns**

::: examples
- no: en stol
- en: a chair
- no: en kopp
- en: a cup
- no: en nøkkel
- en: a key
:::

**ei nouns**

::: examples
- no: ei lampe
- en: a lamp
- no: ei bok
- en: a book
- no: ei hylle
- en: a shelf
:::

**et nouns**

::: examples
- no: et bord
- en: a table
- no: et teppe
- en: a rug
- no: et vindu
- en: a window
:::

The goal is not to guess cleverly. The goal is to build reliable memory: en stol, ei lampe, et bord.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-noun-gender-01}

**Using one article for everything**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: en stol, en lampe, en bord
- en: 'Incorrect: a chair, a lamp, a table'
:::

Using traditional three-gender Bokmål, write:

::: examples
- no: en stol, ei lampe, et bord
- en: a chair, a lamp, a table
:::

**Translating English a mechanically**

English gives no clue about Norwegian gender.

::: examples
- no: a key → en nøkkel
- en: a key
- no: a book → ei bok
- en: a book
- no: a window → et vindu
- en: a window
:::

You must recall the Norwegian noun phrase, not translate the article separately.

**Guessing from real-world sex or qualities**

Grammatical gender is not a description of an object’s personality, shape, or value. Lampe is feminine because that is its grammatical pattern. Bord is neuter because that is its grammatical pattern.

**Treating feminine variation as random freedom**

Both ei bok and en bok occur in correct Bokmål. This does not make every article interchangeable. Et bok is not standard Bokmål. If you choose the three-gender system used here, keep the feminine pattern with ei for the nouns taught that way.

## Transfer to a new situation {#transfer-to-a-new-situation role=recap objectives=obj-noun-gender-01}

The next day, Jonas is preparing a study room. He needs to tell a colleague what is missing. The setting has changed, but the same gender choice remains useful.

He may need phrases such as:

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger et bord.
- en: We need a table.
- no: Vi trenger ei hylle til bøkene.
- en: We need a shelf for the books.
- no: Vi trenger en stol ved vinduet.
- en: We need a chair by the window.
- no: Det er et bilde på gulvet.
- en: There is a picture on the floor.
- no: Jeg har en nøkkel til rommet.
- en: I have a key to the room.
:::

**Request name:** describe-missing-items-in-the-study-room

**Purpose:** Transfer noun-gender knowledge to a new everyday purpose.

**Context:** Jonas checks a study room and reports which familiar objects are present or missing.

**Learner action:** Produce short Norwegian sentences from contextual prompts, including the correct article with each target noun. The response should use complete phrases such as en stol, ei hylle, and et bord.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Use of known noun genders in new sentence contexts.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the learner can retrieve en, ei, and et while communicating about a different room, rather than merely repeating the shop dialogue.

{{exercise: describe_missing_items_in_the_study_room}}

## Retrieval and review {#retrieval-and-review role=recap objectives=obj-noun-gender-01}

Without looking back, recall the Norwegian for a chair, a lamp, and a table, including each article. Then add the correct article to book, key, rug, shelf, window, and picture. Finally, write three short sentences about objects needed in a room: one containing an en noun, one containing an ei noun, and one containing an et noun.

**Request name:** retrieve-three-gender-patterns

**Purpose:** Consolidate the lesson through unaided retrieval.

**Context:** A quick review of familiar household objects in an unspecified room.

**Learner action:** Recall article–noun phrases and use one example of each gender in a short sentence.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Independent retrieval of all three gender patterns for common Norwegian nouns.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates the lesson outcome: recognizing and using the gender of common Norwegian nouns.
