## A weekend trip {#sec-weekend-trip role=orient objectives=obj-glede-seg-til-or-vaere-glad-for-01}

Sara and Jonas are meeting at Oslo Central Station on Friday afternoon. They are travelling to a cabin near Lillehammer. Sara planned the trip, while Jonas bought the food.

::: {.reading translation="I’m looking forward to the weekend." dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Jeg gleder meg til helgen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Me too. I’m looking forward to getting out of the city." dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg også. Jeg gleder meg til å komme meg ut av byen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And I’m glad that you bought the food. I didn’t have time yesterday." dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Og jeg er glad for at du kjøpte maten. Jeg hadde ikke tid i går.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s fine. I’m glad that you booked the cabin." dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Det går bra. Jeg er glad for at du bestilte hytta.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Are you looking forward to the ski trip tomorrow?" dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Gleder du deg til skituren i morgen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but I’m also glad to have my warm clothes. It’s going to be cold!" dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, men jeg er også glad for det varme tøyet mitt. Det blir kaldt!
:::

::: {.reading translation="The train leaves in ten minutes. I’m glad that we arrived early." dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Toget går om ti minutter. Jeg er glad for at vi kom tidlig.
:::

Two different kinds of positive feeling appear in this conversation. Sara and Jonas feel anticipation about the weekend, leaving the city, and tomorrow’s ski trip. They use **glede seg til**. They also appreciate things that are already true: Jonas bought the food, Sara booked the cabin, Jonas has warm clothes, and they arrived early. For these existing facts, they use **være glad for**.

## Looking ahead: glede seg til {#sec-looking-ahead-glede-seg-til role=model objectives=obj-glede-seg-til-or-vaere-glad-for-01}

Use **glede seg til** when someone feels happy or excited about something that is coming. A natural English translation is often **look forward to**.

**Glede seg** is a reflexive verb. The reflexive pronoun changes with the subject:

::: examples
- no: Jeg gleder meg til turen.
- en: I’m looking forward to the trip.
- no: Du gleder deg til turen.
- en: You’re looking forward to the trip.
- no: Hun gleder seg til turen.
- en: She’s looking forward to the trip.
- no: Vi gleder oss til turen.
- en: We’re looking forward to the trip.
- no: Dere gleder dere til turen.
- en: You’re looking forward to the trip.
- no: De gleder seg til turen.
- en: They’re looking forward to the trip.
:::

The whole expression matters: **glede seg til**. Do not leave out the reflexive pronoun, and do not replace **til** with another preposition.

After **til**, you can use a noun:

::: examples
- no: Jeg gleder meg til konserten.
- en: I’m looking forward to the concert.
- no: Barna gleder seg til ferien.
- en: The children are looking forward to the holiday.
- no: Vi gleder oss til middag.
- en: We’re looking forward to dinner.
:::

You can also use **å** followed by a verb when you are looking forward to doing something:

::: examples
- no: Jeg gleder meg til å møte familien din.
- en: I’m looking forward to meeting your family.
- no: Hun gleder seg til å begynne i den nye jobben.
- en: She’s looking forward to starting the new job.
- no: Vi gleder oss til å se dere igjen.
- en: We’re looking forward to seeing you again.
:::

The event does not need to be far in the future. It only needs to be something viewed as still ahead.

::: examples
- no: Jeg gleder meg til kaffen etter møtet.
- en: I’m looking forward to the coffee after the meeting.
- no: Han gleder seg til å legge seg.
- en: He’s looking forward to going to bed.
- no: Gleder dere dere til filmen i kveld?
- en: Are you looking forward to the film tonight?
:::

## Checkpoint: identify anticipation {#sec-checkpoint-identify-anticipation role=recap objectives=obj-glede-seg-til-or-vaere-glad-for-01}

At this point, the learner should examine short situations involving plans later today, tomorrow, or next week and identify where the speaker expresses anticipation toward an experience. Time helps establish the situation, but the speaker’s stance is the deciding clue for choosing **glede seg til**. The situations should reuse Sara and Jonas’s journey as well as familiar events such as a meal, a visit, or a day off. A successful response would identify the anticipated experience and produce **glede seg til** with the reflexive pronoun that matches the subject.

## Appreciating what is already true: være glad for {#sec-appreciating-what-is-already-true-vaere-glad-for role=model objectives=obj-glede-seg-til-or-vaere-glad-for-01}

Use **være glad for** when someone is satisfied with, appreciates, or feels grateful for something that is already a fact.

In the station dialogue, the food has already been bought:

::: examples
- no: Sara er glad for at Jonas kjøpte maten.
- en: Sara is glad that Jonas bought the food.
:::

The cabin has already been booked:

::: examples
- no: Jonas er glad for at Sara bestilte hytta.
- en: Jonas is glad that Sara booked the cabin.
:::

Sara and Jonas are already at the station:

::: examples
- no: De er glade for at de kom tidlig.
- en: They are glad that they arrived early.
:::

These sentences do not express anticipation. They express a positive reaction to an existing situation.

**Være glad for** can be followed by a noun:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er glad for hjelpen.
- en: I’m grateful for the help.
- no: Hun er glad for den nye leiligheten sin.
- en: She is happy with her new apartment.
- no: Vi er glade for resultatet.
- en: We are pleased with the result.
:::

It can also be followed by **at** and a complete statement:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er glad for at du er her.
- en: I’m glad that you are here.
- no: Han er glad for at bussen kom.
- en: He is glad that the bus arrived.
- no: Vi er glade for at alle har det bra.
- en: We are glad that everyone is doing well.
:::

The English translation depends on the context. **Glad about**, **happy with**, **pleased with**, and **grateful for** may all be appropriate. The Norwegian expression remains **være glad for**.

Compare appreciation of a thing with appreciation of a fact:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er glad for hjelpen.
- en: I’m grateful for the help.
- no: Jeg er glad for at du hjalp meg.
- en: I’m glad that you helped me.
:::

Both refer to help that is real or has already happened. The second sentence states the fact more fully.

## Checkpoint: connect feelings to existing facts {#sec-checkpoint-connect-feelings-to-existing-facts role=recap objectives=obj-glede-seg-til-or-vaere-glad-for-01}

The learner should now work with brief descriptions of completed actions or present circumstances and express why a person appreciates them. This activity follows the explanation because it makes the learner distinguish a fact from a future plan. Contexts can include Jonas’s shopping, Sara’s booking, warm clothing, and arriving at the station on time. A successful response would use **være glad for** before either a relevant noun or an **at** clause, without turning the fact into an anticipated event.

## The key contrast {#sec-the-key-contrast role=contrast objectives=obj-glede-seg-til-or-vaere-glad-for-01}

Both expressions describe positive feelings, but they look in different directions.

**Anticipation: something ahead**

::: examples
- no: Jeg gleder meg til besøket.
- en: I’m looking forward to the visit.
:::

**Appreciation: something already true**

::: examples
- no: Jeg er glad for besøket.
- en: I’m pleased about the visit.
:::

::: examples
- no: Vi gleder oss til at Anna kommer i morgen.
- en: We’re looking forward to Anna coming tomorrow.
:::

::: examples
- no: Vi er glade for at Anna kan komme.
- en: We’re glad that Anna can come.
:::

::: examples
- no: Han gleder seg til den nye jobben.
- en: He’s looking forward to the new job.
:::

::: examples
- no: Han er glad for den nye jobben.
- en: He’s happy with the new job.
:::

The surrounding context determines what the speaker means. Consider the new job:

::: examples
- no: Erik begynner i den nye jobben på mandag. Han gleder seg til den.
- en: Erik starts the new job on Monday. He’s looking forward to it.
:::

The job is still ahead, so **gleder seg til** expresses anticipation.

::: examples
- no: Erik har jobbet der i to måneder. Han er glad for den nye jobben.
- en: Erik has worked there for two months. He is happy with the new job.
:::

The job is now part of his life, so **er glad for** expresses satisfaction.

The same contrast appears with a visit:

::: examples
- no: Bestemor kommer neste helg, og jeg gleder meg til besøket.
- en: Grandma is coming next weekend, and I’m looking forward to the visit.
:::

::: examples
- no: Bestemor kunne komme tidligere enn planlagt, og jeg er glad for det.
- en: Grandma was able to come earlier than planned, and I’m glad about that.
:::

In the first sentence, the visit is anticipated. In the second, the speaker appreciates an existing fact about the visit.

## Checkpoint: choose by time perspective {#sec-checkpoint-choose-by-time-perspective role=recap objectives=obj-glede-seg-til-or-vaere-glad-for-01}

The learner should compare paired mini-contexts about the same topic. One context should make the speaker anticipate experiencing the event; the other should make the speaker express satisfaction, appreciation, or gratitude about an arrangement, result, or current circumstance. The event may be confirmed and still support either expression, so the context must establish the intended stance. A successful response would choose **glede seg til** for anticipation and **være glad for** for appreciation, while keeping the same topic.

## Questions and responses {#sec-questions-and-responses role=model objectives=obj-glede-seg-til-or-vaere-glad-for-01}

These expressions are useful in everyday conversation because they invite different kinds of answers.

To ask about anticipation:

::: examples
- no: Gleder du deg til helgen?
- en: Are you looking forward to the weekend?
- no: Ja, jeg gleder meg til å slappe av.
- en: Yes, I’m looking forward to relaxing.
- no: Gleder barna seg til turen?
- en: Are the children looking forward to the trip?
- no: Ja, de gleder seg til å ta toget.
- en: Yes, they’re looking forward to taking the train.
:::

To ask about appreciation or satisfaction:

::: examples
- no: Er du glad for den nye sykkelen?
- en: Are you happy with the new bicycle?
- no: Ja, jeg er glad for at den er så lett.
- en: Yes, I’m glad that it is so light.
- no: Er dere glade for hjelpen?
- en: Are you grateful for the help?
- no: Ja, vi er glade for at naboene kunne hjelpe oss.
- en: Yes, we’re glad that the neighbours could help us.
:::

Notice that the question often guides the time perspective. **Gleder du deg til …?** asks what someone anticipates. **Er du glad for …?** asks what someone appreciates or feels satisfied with.

## Checkpoint: complete a short exchange {#sec-checkpoint-complete-a-short-exchange role=recap objectives=obj-glede-seg-til-or-vaere-glad-for-01}

The learner should respond to short questions about either anticipation or appreciation, using the question’s subject and situation. This activity adds conversational control after sentence-level contrasts. It should include questions connected to travel and everyday plans, followed by questions about help, successful arrangements, or useful possessions. A successful response would preserve the correct expression, change the reflexive pronoun when necessary, and add a plausible reason or detail without changing the intended time perspective.

## Common mistakes {#sec-common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-glede-seg-til-or-vaere-glad-for-01}

**Using være glad for for a future event when anticipation is intended**

If the intended meaning is **look forward to**, use **glede seg til**.

Less natural for the intended meaning:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er glad for festen i morgen.
- en: I’m glad about the party tomorrow.
:::

Clear anticipation:

::: examples
- no: Jeg gleder meg til festen i morgen.
- en: I’m looking forward to the party tomorrow.
:::

The first sentence could make sense if the speaker appreciates the fact that the party has been arranged. It does not clearly express looking forward to attending it.

**Using glede seg til for gratitude**

**Glede seg til** cannot express gratitude for something that has already happened.

Incorrect for the intended meaning:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg gleder meg til hjelpen du ga meg.
- en: I’m looking forward to the help you gave me.
:::

Natural appreciation:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er glad for hjelpen du ga meg.
- en: I’m grateful for the help you gave me.
:::

The help is already a fact, so **være glad for** is required.

**Forgetting the reflexive pronoun**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Vi gleder til ferien.
- en: We’re looking forward to the holiday.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Vi gleder oss til ferien.
- en: We’re looking forward to the holiday.
:::

The pronoun must match the subject: **jeg–meg**, **du–deg**, **han/hun–seg**, **vi–oss**, **dere–dere**, **de–seg**.

**Using the wrong preposition**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Hun gleder seg for konserten.
- en: She’s looking forward to the concert.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Hun gleder seg til konserten.
- en: She’s looking forward to the concert.
:::

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg er glad til at du kom.
- en: I’m glad that you came.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er glad for at du kom.
- en: I’m glad that you came.
:::

Learn each expression together with its preposition: **glede seg til** and **være glad for**.

## Back at the station {#sec-back-at-the-station role=model objectives=obj-glede-seg-til-or-vaere-glad-for-01}

An announcement says that Sara and Jonas’s train is delayed by fifteen minutes.

::: {.reading translation="I’m glad that we have plenty of time." dialogue_id="back-at-station" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg er glad for at vi har god tid.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Then we can buy coffee. I’m looking forward to drinking a cup of coffee on the train." dialogue_id="back-at-station" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Ja. Da kan vi kjøpe kaffe. Jeg gleder meg til å drikke en kopp kaffe på toget.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’m glad that the kiosk is still open." dialogue_id="back-at-station" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg er glad for at kiosken fortsatt er åpen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And I’m looking forward to skiing tomorrow." dialogue_id="back-at-station" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Og jeg gleder meg til å gå på ski i morgen.
:::

The scene contains both kinds of positive feeling. They appreciate existing facts: they have enough time, and the kiosk is open. They also anticipate what comes next: drinking coffee on the train and skiing tomorrow.

## Final contextual practice {#sec-final-contextual-practice role=recap objectives=obj-glede-seg-til-or-vaere-glad-for-01}

The learner should retell the station situation from Sara’s or Jonas’s perspective, expressing both the anticipation already present in the scene and appreciation of facts that help the journey go well. This activity belongs near the end because it returns to the chapter anchor and requires the learner to select expressions from meaning rather than isolated cues. A successful response would consistently use **glede seg til** for what still lies ahead and **være glad for** for what is already true.

Without looking back, explain the difference between **glede seg til** and **være glad for**. Then produce one Norwegian sentence about something you anticipate and one about an existing fact you appreciate. A successful review uses the correct preposition and, with **glede seg til**, the correct reflexive pronoun.
