## A difficult morning at the community centre {#difficult-morning role=orient objectives=obj-faa-til-or-klare-aa-01}

Mina works at a community centre in Bergen. She and Jonas are preparing an information meeting for new volunteers. Ten minutes before the meeting, the projector stops working.

::: {.reading translation="The projector isn’t working. I can’t manage to display the presentation." dialogue_id="difficult-morning" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Projektoren virker ikke. Jeg får ikke til å vise presentasjonen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Have you tried the other cable?" dialogue_id="difficult-morning" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Har du prøvd den andre kabelen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but I still can’t get it to work." dialogue_id="difficult-morning" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja, men jeg får det fortsatt ikke til.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Let me see. I managed to use the projector yesterday, but it was difficult to start." dialogue_id="difficult-morning" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
La meg se. Jeg klarte å bruke projektoren i går, men den var vanskelig å starte.
:::

::: {.reading translation="We only have ten minutes. Can you manage to fix it before the meeting?" dialogue_id="difficult-morning" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Vi har bare ti minutter. Klarer du å ordne det før møtet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’ll try. There! Now I got it to work." dialogue_id="difficult-morning" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg skal prøve. Der! Nå fikk jeg det til.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great! Then we’ll manage to start on time." dialogue_id="difficult-morning" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Flott! Da klarer vi å begynne i tide.
:::

Both **få til** and **klare å** can correspond to English expressions such as manage to, succeed in, or be able to. The difference is usually one of focus.

When Jonas says **Nå fikk jeg det til**, he points to a successful result: the projector now works. When Mina asks **Klarer du å ordne det?**, she asks whether Jonas has the ability, energy, time, or practical capacity to do the task.

## Focusing on the result with få til {#focusing-on-result-with-faa-til role=model objectives=obj-faa-til-or-klare-aa-01}

Use **få til** when the important point is that something works, succeeds, or reaches a successful result. It is especially common when a task has been difficult, has required several attempts, or has previously failed.

The structure often appears in three frames:

- **få til + a noun**
- **få + object pronoun + til**
- **få til å + infinitive**

The infinitive is the basic verb form. In **å vise**, **å åpne**, and **å reparere**, **å** is the infinitive marker.

::: examples
- no: Jeg fikk det til.
- en: I got it to work. / I managed it.
:::

Here, **det** refers to a task or problem already known from the context.

::: examples
- no: Mina fikk til presentasjonen.
- en: Mina made the presentation work successfully.
- no: Vi fikk til et godt møte.
- en: We managed to make the meeting a success.
:::

With an action, **få til å** can identify what the successful result was.

::: examples
- no: Jeg fikk til å åpne filen.
- en: I managed to open the file.
- no: De fikk til å reparere mikrofonen.
- en: They succeeded in repairing the microphone.
- no: Hun får ikke til å logge inn.
- en: She can’t manage to log in.
:::

The negative form often suggests repeated difficulty: the person tries, but the task does not work.

Compare the present and past forms:

::: examples
- no: Jeg får det til nå.
- en: I’m getting it to work now.
- no: Jeg fikk det til i går.
- en: I got it to work yesterday.
:::

The past form **fikk til** naturally presents the result as completed.

Read short situations about practical problems, such as opening a document, connecting a printer, or preparing a room. Identify which sentence reports that the task eventually worked. Then explain briefly what result was achieved.

This activity belongs here because **få til** is easiest to understand when there is a visible contrast between an earlier problem and a later success. A successful response would recognize the completed result rather than merely identifying who had the skill to attempt the task.

## Focusing on ability or effort with klare å {#focusing-on-ability-or-effort-with-klare-aa role=model objectives=obj-faa-til-or-klare-aa-01}

Use **klare å + infinitive** when the focus is on whether a person can cope with a task or has enough ability, strength, time, concentration, or determination to do it.

::: examples
- no: Klarer du å bære denne esken?
- en: Can you manage to carry this box?
:::

The question is about the person’s capacity. The box may be heavy, awkward, or difficult to carry.

::: examples
- no: Jeg klarer å lese teksten uten ordbok.
- en: I am able to read the text without a dictionary.
:::

This describes ability.

::: examples
- no: Hun klarte å holde seg våken under hele møtet.
- en: She managed to stay awake during the entire meeting.
:::

This reports success, but the wording highlights her effort or endurance.

::: examples
- no: Vi klarte ikke å bli ferdige før klokka fem.
- en: We didn’t manage to finish before five o’clock.
:::

The group did not have enough time or did not overcome the difficulty.

The infinitive marker **å** normally follows **klare** before another verb:

::: examples
- no: Han klarer å forklare problemet.
- en: He is able to explain the problem.
- no: Han klarte å forklare problemet.
- en: He managed to explain the problem.
:::

The present form **klarer å** can describe current ability or ask whether a task is possible. The past form **klarte å** often reports that someone overcame a challenge.

## Checkpoint: connect the task to the person {#task-to-person-checkpoint role=recap objectives=obj-faa-til-or-klare-aa-01}

Work with short descriptions of people under pressure: someone is tired, has little time, carries something heavy, or must concentrate in a noisy room. Complete each description with a natural statement using **klare å**.

This checkpoint follows the ability-and-effort explanation so that the form is connected to human capacity, not simply to a finished product. A successful response would use **klare å** with an infinitive and make the difficulty or effort relevant to the person.

## The central contrast {#central-contrast role=contrast objectives=obj-faa-til-or-klare-aa-01}

The two expressions overlap. Both can describe success, and both may be translated as manage to. The best choice depends on how the speaker views the situation.

**Successful outcome**

::: examples
- no: Endelig fikk jeg til å installere programmet.
- en: I finally succeeded in installing the program.
:::

The installation is now working. The result is central.

**Personal effort or capacity**

::: examples
- no: Jeg klarte å installere programmet selv om instruksjonene var uklare.
- en: I managed to install the program even though the instructions were unclear.
:::

The speaker presents the installation as something they were able to accomplish despite difficulty.

Consider another pair:

::: examples
- no: Vi fikk til en god middag med det vi hadde i kjøleskapet.
- en: We succeeded in making a good dinner with what we had in the refrigerator.
:::

The successful dinner is the focus.

::: examples
- no: Vi klarte å lage middag før gjestene kom.
- en: We managed to make dinner before the guests arrived.
:::

The focus is on completing the action within a difficult time limit.

And another:

::: examples
- no: Teknikeren fikk til lyden etter flere forsøk.
- en: The technician got the sound working after several attempts.
:::

The sound system finally works.

::: examples
- no: Teknikeren klarte å finne feilen på fem minutter.
- en: The technician managed to find the fault in five minutes.
:::

The technician’s ability and achievement under time pressure are prominent.

A useful question to ask is:

- Am I pointing to something that now works or has become successful? Use **få til**.
- Am I emphasizing whether someone can perform or cope with an action? Use **klare å**.

This is a difference in perspective, not an absolute division. In some situations, either expression is possible, but the emphasis changes.

::: examples
- no: Hun fikk til å løse oppgaven.
- en: She succeeded in solving the task.
:::

This draws attention to the successful solution.

::: examples
- no: Hun klarte å løse oppgaven.
- en: She managed to solve the task.
:::

This draws attention to her accomplishment or ability.

## Questions and negative statements {#questions-and-negative-statements role=model objectives=obj-faa-til-or-klare-aa-01}

Questions with **klare å** often ask directly about capacity.

::: examples
- no: Klarer du å komme før ni?
- en: Can you manage to come before nine?
- no: Klarer Sara å jobbe alene i dag?
- en: Can Sara manage to work alone today?
:::

Questions with **få til** often concern whether a task can be made to work successfully.

::: examples
- no: Får du til å skrive ut dokumentet?
- en: Are you managing to print the document?
- no: Får dere til lyden i videomøtet?
- en: Are you getting the sound in the video meeting to work?
:::

Negative statements reveal the contrast clearly.

::: examples
- no: Jeg klarer ikke å konsentrere meg når det er så mye støy.
- en: I can’t manage to concentrate when there is so much noise.
:::

The problem concerns the speaker’s ability to concentrate.

::: examples
- no: Jeg får ikke til den siste delen av oppgaven.
- en: I can’t get the last part of the task right.
:::

The task is not reaching a successful result.

::: examples
- no: Han klarte ikke å løfte bordet alene.
- en: He couldn’t manage to lift the table alone.
:::

He lacked sufficient strength or capacity.

::: examples
- no: Han fikk ikke til å montere bordet.
- en: He couldn’t manage to assemble the table.
:::

The assembly did not work out successfully.

## Checkpoint: diagnose the difficulty {#diagnose-difficulty-checkpoint role=recap objectives=obj-faa-til-or-klare-aa-01}

Read brief statements about unsuccessful attempts. Decide whether each problem is mainly about a person’s capacity or about getting a task to work. Reformulate the statement with **klare å** or **få til**, preserving its meaning.

This activity develops the distinction in negative contexts, where English often uses can’t manage for both expressions. A successful response would connect personal limitations to **klare å** and an unsuccessful practical outcome to **få til**.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-faa-til-or-klare-aa-01}

**Using the neutral target with å after klare**

This lesson uses **klare å** as the neutral target frame before another verb. Bokmål also allows omission of **å**, especially in informal usage.

Accepted variant outside controlled practice:

::: examples
- no: Jeg klarer ikke åpne døra.
- en: I can’t manage to open the door.
:::

Neutral target form:

::: examples
- no: Jeg klarer ikke å åpne døra.
- en: I can’t manage to open the door.
:::

**Using til after klare**

Do not copy **til** from **få til** into the **klare å** pattern.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Hun klarte til å sende søknaden.
- en: She managed to send the application.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Hun klarte å sende søknaden.
- en: She managed to send the application.
:::

**Forgetting til after få**

In this meaning, **få til** is a unit. **Få** alone has other meanings, such as get or receive.

Incorrect for the intended meaning:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg fikk å starte maskinen.
- en: I managed to start the machine.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Jeg fikk til å starte maskinen.
- en: I managed to get the machine started.
:::

An unstressed object pronoun usually comes between **få** and **til**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg fikk det til.
- en: I got it to work.
:::

**Treating the expressions as exact opposites**

**Få til** does not mean only completed success, and **klare å** does not mean only general ability. Both can describe a completed achievement. The distinction lies in what the speaker emphasizes.

::: examples
- no: Etter tre timer fikk vi til å åpne døra.
- en: After three hours, we succeeded in opening the door.
:::

The result is prominent.

::: examples
- no: Etter tre timer klarte vi å åpne døra.
- en: After three hours, we managed to open the door.
:::

Our effort and eventual accomplishment are prominent.

## Checkpoint: repair the expression {#expression-repair-checkpoint role=recap objectives=obj-faa-til-or-klare-aa-01}

Review sentences containing an omitted **å** after **klare**, a missing **til** after **få**, or a mixture of the two patterns. Treat omitted **å** as an accepted variant, but rewrite it with **å** for this lesson’s neutral target frame. Correct the actual pattern errors. Then state whether each resulting sentence focuses on capacity and effort or on a successful result.

This checkpoint belongs after the contrast is established because form and meaning must now be controlled together. A successful response would use the neutral target **klare å + infinitive** or one of the frames **få til + noun**, **få + object pronoun + til**, and **få til å + infinitive** without combining their parts incorrectly.

## Back at the community centre {#back-at-community-centre role=model objectives=obj-faa-til-or-klare-aa-01}

The meeting has started, but Mina and Jonas face one more problem.

::: {.reading translation="The projector works now, but no one can hear the sound from the video." dialogue_id="back-at-community-centre" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Nå virker projektoren, men ingen hører lyden fra videoen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I can’t get the sound to work with these speakers." dialogue_id="back-at-community-centre" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg får ikke til lyden med disse høyttalerne.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Can you manage to speak loudly enough without a microphone?" dialogue_id="back-at-community-centre" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Klarer du å snakke høyt nok uten mikrofon?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I can manage that. Can you show the pictures while I explain?" dialogue_id="back-at-community-centre" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, det klarer jeg. Kan du vise bildene mens jeg forklarer?
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s fine. And perhaps we’ll get the sound working before the last video." dialogue_id="back-at-community-centre" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Det går fint. Og kanskje vi får til lyden før den siste videoen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Wait a moment—now I got it to work!" dialogue_id="back-at-community-centre" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Vent litt — nå fikk jeg det til!
:::

Jonas uses **klarer** when discussing whether he can speak loudly enough. Mina and Jonas use **få til** when discussing the sound system and its successful operation.
