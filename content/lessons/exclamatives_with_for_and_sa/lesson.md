## Arriving at the cabin {#arriving-at-the-cabin role=orient objectives=obj-exclamatives-with-for-and-sa-01}

Maja and Elias have rented a cabin by a fjord. They arrive late in the afternoon and walk through the rooms for the first time.

::: {.reading translation="What a lovely place!" dialogue_id=cabin-arrival speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
For et hyggelig sted!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, and how quiet it is here!" dialogue_id=cabin-arrival speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Ja, og så stille det er her!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Look at the fjord. What a view!" dialogue_id=cabin-arrival speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Se på fjorden. For en utsikt!
:::

::: {.reading translation="How blue the sky is today!" dialogue_id=cabin-arrival speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Så blå himmelen er i dag!
:::

::: {.reading translation="And what large windows the house has!" dialogue_id=cabin-arrival speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Og for noen store vinduer huset har!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, how bright it is inside!" dialogue_id=cabin-arrival speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Ja, så lyst det er inne!
:::

Maja and Elias are not asking for information. They are reacting emotionally to what they see. Norwegian commonly uses **for** or **så** for this kind of exclamation, but the two words focus attention differently:

- Use **for** when the exclamation focuses on a person, thing, place, or other noun.
- Use **så** when it focuses on how strong a quality is.

Compare Maja’s and Elias’s reactions to the same view:

::: examples
- no: For en vakker utsikt!
- en: What a beautiful view!
- no: Så vakker utsikten er!
- en: How beautiful the view is!
:::

The first reaction presents the view as an impressive thing. The second emphasizes the degree of its beauty.

## Noun-focused exclamations with **for** {#noun-focused-exclamations-with-for role=model objectives=obj-exclamatives-with-for-and-sa-01}

Use **for** before a noun phrase when your reaction is mainly about the person or thing itself. A common pattern is:

::: examples
- no: for + en/et/noen + adjective + noun
- en: for + en/et/noen + adjective + noun
:::

The article agrees with the noun in the usual way:

::: examples
- no: For en fin leilighet!
- en: What a nice apartment!
- no: For et stort kjøkken!
- en: What a large kitchen!
- no: For noen vakre blomster!
- en: What beautiful flowers!
:::

The adjective is useful but not always necessary:

::: examples
- no: For en dag!
- en: What a day!
- no: For et vær!
- en: What weather!
- no: For en utsikt!
- en: What a view!
:::

These short exclamations do not need a verb. The situation makes the reaction clear.

A longer **for** exclamation can include a full statement:

::: examples
- no: For en fin leilighet dere har!
- en: What a nice apartment you have!
- no: For et godt måltid du har laget!
- en: What a good meal you have made!
- no: For noen interessante historier hun forteller!
- en: What interesting stories she tells!
:::

Notice the statement word order in the final part:

::: examples
- no: dere har
- en: you have
- no: du har laget
- en: you have made
- no: hun forteller
- en: she tells
:::

The subject comes before the verb. Although the sentence expresses surprise or admiration, it is not a question.

**Request: Noun-focus-in-the-cabin**

This activity returns to Maja and Elias’s first impressions of the cabin. The learner should identify which reactions focus on a concrete person, place, or thing and complete them with the **for** pattern. The primary scored evidence is choosing a noun-focused exclamation. Successful work demonstrates that the learner recognizes when the reaction is organized around a noun rather than around the degree of a quality.

{{exercise: noun_focus_in_the_cabin}}

## Degree-focused exclamations with **så** {#degree-focused-exclamations-with-sa role=model objectives=obj-exclamatives-with-for-and-sa-01}

Use **så** when the main point is how beautiful, cold, fast, quiet, expensive, or otherwise striking something is. In this use, **så** strengthens an adjective or adverb.

A short reaction can end immediately after the description:

::: examples
- no: Så fint!
- en: How lovely!
- no: Så koselig!
- en: How cozy!
- no: Så interessant!
- en: How interesting!
- no: Så dyrt!
- en: How expensive!
:::

When the person or thing being described is named, a common pattern is:

::: examples
- no: så + adjective/adverb + subject + verb
- en: så + adjective/adverb + subject + verb
:::

::: examples
- no: Så fin leiligheten er!
- en: How nice the apartment is!
- no: Så stort kjøkkenet er!
- en: How large the kitchen is!
- no: Så stille barna er!
- en: How quiet the children are!
- no: Så fort hun løper!
- en: How fast she runs!
:::

The focus falls on the strength or degree of the description: the apartment is remarkably nice, the kitchen remarkably large, and the runner remarkably fast.

## The contrast at the heart of the lesson {#for-sa-focus-contrast role=contrast objectives=obj-exclamatives-with-for-and-sa-01}

This is the contrast at the heart of the lesson:

- Noun-focused with **for**:

::: examples
- no: For en rolig kveld!
- en: What a peaceful evening!
- no: For et høyt fjell!
- en: What a high mountain!
- no: For noen raske løpere!
- en: What fast runners!
- no: For en vakker sang!
- en: What a beautiful song!
:::

- Degree-focused with **så**:

::: examples
- no: Så rolig kvelden er!
- en: How peaceful the evening is!
- no: Så høyt fjellet er!
- en: How high the mountain is!
- no: Så fort de løper!
- en: How fast they run!
- no: Så vakkert hun synger!
- en: How beautifully she sings!
:::

The **for** sentence packages the reaction around a noun: *evening, mountain, runners, song*. The **så** sentence highlights the quality or manner: *peaceful, high, fast, beautifully*.

**Request: Shift-the-focus**

The learner should revisit matched situations such as a mountain, a song, or a quiet evening. For each context, the learner produces either a noun-focused reaction with **for** or a degree-focused reaction with **så**, according to the stated communicative focus. The primary scored evidence is selecting the marker that matches the intended focus. Other vocabulary and adjective forms provide context but are not the main scored dimension. Success means the learner can express two different reactions to the same situation without treating **for** and **så** as interchangeable.

{{exercise: shift_the_focus}}

## Exclamations keep statement word order {#exclamations-keep-statement-word-order role=contrast objectives=obj-exclamatives-with-for-and-sa-01}

English exclamations beginning with *how* can make Norwegian learners expect question-like word order. Norwegian **så** exclamations do not use question inversion.

A normal statement has the subject before the verb:

::: examples
- no: Utsikten er vakker.
- en: The view is beautiful.
:::

The corresponding exclamation keeps the subject before the verb:

::: examples
- no: Så vakker utsikten er!
- en: How beautiful the view is!
:::

Do not move the verb in front of the subject:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Så vakker er utsikten?
- en: Is the view that beautiful?
:::

With that word order and question intonation, the sentence is interpreted as a question rather than the intended exclamation.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Så hyggelig naboen er!
- en: How nice the neighbor is!
- no: Er naboen hyggelig?
- en: Is the neighbor nice?
:::

And:

::: examples
- no: Så raskt toget går!
- en: How fast the train goes!
- no: Går toget raskt?
- en: Does the train go fast?
:::

The compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
In a full **så** exclamation, place the description first, then keep the subject before the verb.
:::

This also applies when the subject is a pronoun:

::: examples
- no: Så glad hun er!
- en: How happy she is!
- no: Så trøtte de er!
- en: How tired they are!
- no: Så varmt det er her!
- en: How warm it is here!
:::

Longer **for** exclamations follow the same principle:

::: examples
- no: For en hyggelig nabo vi har!
- en: What a nice neighbor we have!
:::

The ending is **vi har**, not question order.

**Request: Exclamation-not-question**

This activity presents emotional reactions whose words have been placed in either statement order or question order. The learner should form complete **så** exclamations while preserving subject-before-verb order. The primary scored evidence is avoiding question inversion. Choosing between **for** and **så** is held constant as context rather than scored here. A successful response sounds like an exclamation and not a request for information.

{{exercise: exclamation_not_question}}

## Avoid mixing the two patterns {#avoid-mixing-the-two-patterns role=contrast objectives=obj-exclamatives-with-for-and-sa-01}

A common mistake is to begin with **så** and then continue with the noun pattern used after **for**:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Så en fin utsikt!
- en: What a beautiful view!
:::

Intended meaning: *What a beautiful view!*

Use either the noun-focused pattern:

::: examples
- no: For en fin utsikt!
- en: What a beautiful view!
:::

Or the degree-focused pattern:

::: examples
- no: Så fin utsikten er!
- en: How beautiful the view is!
:::

Another mistake is to omit the article after **for** when a singular countable noun needs one:

::: examples
- no: ✗ For fin utsikt!
- en: What a beautiful view!
:::

Intended meaning: *What a beautiful view!*

The usual form is:

::: examples
- no: For en fin utsikt!
- en: What a beautiful view!
:::

Do not choose mechanically according to whether an adjective appears. Both patterns can contain an adjective:

::: examples
- no: For en søt hund!
- en: What a cute dog!
- no: Så søt hunden er!
- en: How cute the dog is!
:::

The difference is focus, not merely the presence of **søt**.

**Request: Repair-mixed-reactions**

The learner receives brief reactions from visitors commenting on homes, meals, animals, and scenery. Each reaction confuses the **for** and **så** patterns or uses question inversion. The learner should rewrite it as the intended noun-focused or degree-focused exclamation. The primary scored evidence is repairing the selected exclamation pattern as a whole. Success demonstrates that the learner does not combine **så** with the **for + article + noun** frame and does not turn a **så** exclamation into a question.

{{exercise: repair_mixed_reactions}}

## Back at the cabin {#back-at-the-cabin role=model objectives=obj-exclamatives-with-for-and-sa-01}

The next morning, the owner, Ingrid, comes by to ask whether everything is satisfactory.

::: {.reading translation="How was the first evening?" dialogue_id=cabin-morning speaker_id=ingrid speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Hvordan var den første kvelden?
:::

::: {.reading translation="What a peaceful evening we had!" dialogue_id=cabin-morning speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
For en rolig kveld vi hadde!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, and how clearly we could see the stars!" dialogue_id=cabin-morning speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Ja, og så klart vi kunne se stjernene!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good! You didn’t have any problems with the fireplace, did you?" dialogue_id=cabin-morning speaker_id=ingrid speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Fint! Dere hadde vel ikke problemer med peisen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No. How easy it was to use!" dialogue_id=cabin-morning speaker_id=maja speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Nei. Så lett den var å bruke!
:::

::: {.reading translation="And what a good breakfast we ate on the terrace!" dialogue_id=cabin-morning speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Og for en god frokost vi spiste på terrassen!
:::

Maja focuses on **en rolig kveld** and **en god frokost**, so she uses **for**. Elias emphasizes how clearly they saw the stars and how easy the fireplace was to use, so he uses **så**. Their longer exclamations still keep statement order:

::: examples
- no: vi hadde
- en: we had
- no: vi kunne se
- en: we could see
- no: den var
- en: it was
- no: vi spiste
- en: we ate
:::

**Request: Complete-the-cabin-conversation**

The learner should complete new turns in Ingrid’s conversation with Maja and Elias. Each turn provides a clear intention: admiration for a named thing or emphasis on the degree of a quality. The learner supplies an appropriate **for** or **så** exclamation and keeps statement word order where a full clause is needed. The primary scored evidence is matching the marker to the intended focus. Success means the learner can apply the contrast within a connected everyday exchange rather than only in isolated sentences.

{{exercise: complete_the_cabin_conversation}}

## Transfer to a new situation {#transfer-to-a-new-situation role=recap objectives=obj-exclamatives-with-for-and-sa-01}

Imagine that Amir and Nora have arrived at a friend’s birthday party. They react to the decorated room, the large cake, the quiet music, a friend who sings beautifully, and guests who dance energetically.

**Request: Birthday-party-reactions**

The learner should create controlled reactions for the party scene from short meaning prompts. Some prompts foreground a noun, such as the cake or decorations; others foreground degree, such as how beautifully someone sings or how lively the guests are. The primary scored evidence is choosing **for** for noun focus and **så** for degree focus. Correct statement word order remains required but supports the main focus choice. Successful responses show transfer of the target contrast from the cabin to a different everyday setting.

{{exercise: birthday_party_reactions}}

## Retrieval and review {#retrieval-and-review role=recap objectives=obj-exclamatives-with-for-and-sa-01}

**Request: Two-ways-to-react**

Without returning to the earlier examples, the learner should write two exclamations about one new everyday scene. One must focus on a person or thing with **for**; the other must focus on the degree of a quality with **så**. Any full clause must keep the subject before the verb. Success demonstrates retrieval of the central contrast: noun-focused **for**, degree-focused **så**, and no question inversion.

{{exercise: two_ways_to_react}}
