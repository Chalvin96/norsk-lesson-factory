## Meeting outside the cinema {#meeting-outside-cinema role=orient objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-object-01}

Sara is waiting outside a cinema when her friend Jonas arrives. They are meeting two other people, Emil and Amina.

::: {.reading translation="Hi, Jonas! Can you see me?" dialogue_id="meeting-outside-cinema" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Hei, Jonas! Ser du **meg**?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, now I can see you. There are lots of people here." dialogue_id="meeting-outside-cinema" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, nå ser jeg **deg**. Det er mange mennesker her.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Emil is standing by the entrance. Do you know him?" dialogue_id="meeting-outside-cinema" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Emil står ved inngangen. Kjenner du **ham**?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I know him from work. But where is Amina?" dialogue_id="meeting-outside-cinema" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, jeg kjenner **ham** fra jobben. Men hvor er Amina?
:::

::: {.reading translation="She is buying tickets. Emil is waiting for her." dialogue_id="meeting-outside-cinema" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Hun kjøper billetter. Emil venter på **henne**.
:::

::: {.reading translation="There she is! She sees us—" dialogue_id="meeting-outside-cinema" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Der kommer hun! Hun ser **oss**—
:::

For this lesson, stop before the final pronoun in Jonas’s sentence. The useful forms in the scene are **meg**, **deg**, **ham**, and **henne**. They let the speakers refer to people without repeating their names.

Sara could say:

::: examples
- no: **Emil venter på Amina.**
- en: *Emil is waiting for Amina.*
:::

Instead, she says:

::: examples
- no: **Emil venter på henne.**
- en: *Emil is waiting for her.*
:::

The meaning is the same, but **henne** avoids repeating *Amina*.

**Activity request: anchor-reference-notice**

**Purpose:** Notice what each object pronoun refers to before learning the full contrast.
**Context:** Sara and Jonas outside the cinema.
**Learner action:** For each highlighted pronoun in the dialogue, identify the person it means in that moment.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner understands that the pronoun points to the person affected, seen, known, or awaited—not automatically to the person speaking.

{{exercise: anchor_reference_notice}}

## The person doing something and the person receiving it {#person-doing-receiving role=model objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-object-01}

You already know subject pronouns such as **jeg**, **du**, **han**, and **hun**. A subject tells us who performs the action:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg ser Sara.**
- en: *I see Sara.*
- no: **Han kjenner Amina.**
- en: *He knows Amina.*
:::

The person after the verb is affected by the action. We call this person the **object**, and a pronoun used there is an **object pronoun (objektspronomen)**.

Compare the subject and object forms:

- **Subject form:** **jeg**; **Object form:** **meg**; **Basic meaning:** *I → me*
- **Subject form:** **du**; **Object form:** **deg**; **Basic meaning:** *you → you*
- **Subject form:** **han**; **Object form:** **ham**; **Basic meaning:** *he → him*
- **Subject form:** **hun**; **Object form:** **henne**; **Basic meaning:** *she → her*

The compact rule is:

- Use **meg** for the speaker when that person is the object.
- Use **deg** for the person being spoken to when that person is the object.
- Use **ham** for a male person who is the object.
- Use **henne** for a female person who is the object.

Look first at who performs the action, then ask who receives or is affected by it.

## *Jeg* becomes *meg* {#jeg-becomes-meg role=model objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-object-01}

Use **jeg** when “I” perform the action:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg ser Jonas.**
- en: *I see Jonas.*
:::

Use **meg** when another person sees, calls, knows, or helps “me”:

::: examples
- no: **Jonas ser meg.**
- en: *Jonas sees me.*
- no: **Sara ringer meg.**
- en: *Sara calls me.*
- no: **Emil kjenner meg.**
- en: *Emil knows me.*
:::

Notice the contrast:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg ser Sara.**
- en: *I see Sara.*
- no: **Sara ser meg.**
- en: *Sara sees me.*
:::

The same two people are involved, but their roles change. In the first sentence, **jeg** performs the action. In the second, **meg** receives it.

A common mistake is to keep the subject form after the verb:

::: examples
- no: ✗ **Sara ser jeg.**
- en: Intended meaning: *Sara sees me.*
:::

In a simple sentence with this meaning, use:

::: examples
- no: **Sara ser meg.**
- en: *Sara sees me.*
:::

English makes a similar distinction between *I* and *me*. Norwegian uses **jeg** and **meg**.

**Activity request: speaker-object-form**

**Purpose:** Establish the contrast between **jeg** as subject and **meg** as object.
**Context:** Short statements about Sara, Jonas, and the learner as speaker.
**Learner action:** Complete sentences in which someone sees, calls, knows, or helps the speaker, supplying the appropriate pronoun.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner chooses **meg** because the speaker is the object, rather than copying the subject form **jeg**.

{{exercise: speaker_object_form}}

## *Du* becomes *deg* {#du-becomes-deg role=model objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-object-01}

Use **du** when the person being spoken to performs the action:

::: examples
- no: **Du kjenner Emil.**
- en: *You know Emil.*
:::

Use **deg** when the person being spoken to receives the action:

::: examples
- no: **Emil kjenner deg.**
- en: *Emil knows you.*
:::

Compare:

::: examples
- no: **Du ser meg.**
- en: *You see me.*
- no: **Jeg ser deg.**
- en: *I see you.*
:::

These two sentences are especially useful in conversation. The pronoun depends on each person’s role:

- In **Du ser meg**, the listener performs the action, and the speaker receives it.
- In **Jeg ser deg**, the speaker performs the action, and the listener receives it.

More examples:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg ringer deg i kveld.**
- en: *I’ll call you this evening.*
- no: **Amina hjelper deg.**
- en: *Amina is helping you.*
- no: **Sara møter deg ved inngangen.**
- en: *Sara is meeting you by the entrance.*
:::

Do not use **du** merely because the sentence refers to the listener:

::: examples
- no: ✗ **Jeg ringer du.**
- en: Intended meaning: *I’m calling you.*
:::

The correct object form is:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg ringer deg.**
- en: *I’m calling you.*
:::

**Activity request: speaker-listener-role-switch**

**Purpose:** Distinguish **meg** from **deg** by tracking speaker and listener roles.
**Context:** Two people trying to find each other in a busy public place and then arranging a phone call.
**Learner action:** Supply the object pronoun in short exchanges where the object alternates between the speaker and the listener.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner chooses according to who is affected by the action, even when the speaker and listener switch roles from one sentence to the next.

{{exercise: speaker_listener_role_switch}}

## *Han* becomes *ham* {#han-becomes-ham role=model objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-object-01}

Use **han** when a male person performs the action:

::: examples
- no: **Han venter ved inngangen.**
- en: *He is waiting by the entrance.*
:::

Use **ham** when the action is directed toward that male person:

::: examples
- no: **Sara ser ham.**
- en: *Sara sees him.*
:::

The name can be replaced once the context makes the person clear:

::: examples
- no: **Emil står ved inngangen. Sara ser Emil.**
- en: *Emil is standing by the entrance. Sara sees Emil.*
:::

With an object pronoun:

::: examples
- no: **Emil står ved inngangen. Sara ser ham.**
- en: *Emil is standing by the entrance. Sara sees him.*
:::

More examples:

::: examples
- no: **Jonas kjenner ham fra jobben.**
- en: *Jonas knows him from work.*
- no: **Amina ringer ham.**
- en: *Amina calls him.*
- no: **Sara møter ham utenfor kinoen.**
- en: *Sara meets him outside the cinema.*
:::

Compare the two roles:

::: examples
- no: **Han ringer Sara.**
- en: *He calls Sara.*
- no: **Sara ringer ham.**
- en: *Sara calls him.*
:::

A likely misconception is that **han** can be used wherever English has a reference to a male person. It cannot. Use **han** when he performs the action and **ham** when the action is directed toward him.

**Activity request: male-reference-object-form**

**Purpose:** Connect a previously mentioned male person with **ham**.
**Context:** Emil’s actions before the film: friends see him, call him, know him, and meet him.
**Learner action:** Replace repeated references to Emil with the correct object pronoun in simple sentences.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner selects **ham** for a male object and does not retain subject-form **han**.

{{exercise: male_reference_object_form}}

## *Hun* becomes *henne* {#hun-becomes-henne role=model objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-object-01}

Use **hun** when a female person performs the action:

::: examples
- no: **Hun kjøper billetter.**
- en: *She is buying tickets.*
:::

Use **henne** when the action is directed toward that female person:

::: examples
- no: **Emil venter på henne.**
- en: *Emil is waiting for her.*
:::

Again, compare a repeated name with a pronoun:

::: examples
- no: **Amina kjøper billetter. Emil venter på Amina.**
- en: *Amina is buying tickets. Emil is waiting for Amina.*
- no: **Amina kjøper billetter. Emil venter på henne.**
- en: *Amina is buying tickets. Emil is waiting for her.*
:::

More examples:

::: examples
- no: **Jonas ser henne ved billettautomaten.**
- en: *Jonas sees her by the ticket machine.*
- no: **Sara kjenner henne godt.**
- en: *Sara knows her well.*
- no: **Emil ringer henne.**
- en: *Emil calls her.*
:::

Compare:

::: examples
- no: **Hun hjelper Jonas.**
- en: *She helps Jonas.*
- no: **Jonas hjelper henne.**
- en: *Jonas helps her.*
:::

Do not use **hun** when the female person receives the action:

::: examples
- no: ✗ **Jonas ser hun.**
- en: Intended meaning: *Jonas sees her.*
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: **Jonas ser henne.**
- en: *Jonas sees her.*
:::

**Activity request: female-reference-object-form**

**Purpose:** Connect a previously mentioned female person with **henne**.
**Context:** Amina is buying tickets while her friends try to find and contact her.
**Learner action:** Replace repeated references to Amina with the appropriate object pronoun.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner selects **henne** for a female object and distinguishes it from subject-form **hun**.

{{exercise: female_reference_object_form}}

## Choosing among all four forms {#choosing-all-four-forms role=contrast objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-object-01}

The central question is not simply “Who is mentioned?” Ask:

**Who receives the action in this sentence?**

Then identify that person’s relationship to the conversation:

- Object is the speaker; choose **meg**.
- Object is the listener; choose **deg**.
- Object is a male person; choose **ham**.
- Object is a female person; choose **henne**.

Compare these four sentences:

::: examples
- no: **Jonas ser meg.**
- en: *Jonas sees me.*
- no: **Jonas ser deg.**
- en: *Jonas sees you.*
- no: **Jonas ser ham.**
- en: *Jonas sees him.*
- no: **Jonas ser henne.**
- en: *Jonas sees her.*
:::

The sentence pattern remains the same. Only the identity of the person Jonas sees changes.

Now compare a different verb:

::: examples
- no: **Sara ringer meg.**
- en: *Sara calls me.*
- no: **Sara ringer deg.**
- en: *Sara calls you.*
- no: **Sara ringer ham.**
- en: *Sara calls him.*
- no: **Sara ringer henne.**
- en: *Sara calls her.*
:::

The choice of pronoun does not depend on the verb being *ser* or *ringer*. It depends on who is the object.

**Activity request: four-way-object-selection**

**Purpose:** Choose accurately among all four target pronouns.
**Context:** Friends coordinate before the film using short messages about seeing, calling, knowing, meeting, and helping people.
**Learner action:** Read each sentence’s context, determine who receives the action, and supply one object pronoun.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner distinguishes speaker, listener, male third person, and female third person as objects. Only pronoun selection is scored; surrounding verbs provide context.

{{exercise: four_way_object_selection}}

## Avoid choosing from the subject alone {#avoid-choosing-from-subject-alone role=contrast objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-object-01}

The subject does not determine the object pronoun.

Consider:

::: examples
- no: **Sara ringer meg.**
- en: *Sara calls me.*
- no: **Sara ringer deg.**
- en: *Sara calls you.*
- no: **Sara ringer ham.**
- en: *Sara calls him.*
- no: **Sara ringer henne.**
- en: *Sara calls her.*
:::

The subject is **Sara** every time, but the object changes.

The reverse is also possible:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg ringer henne.**
- en: *I call her.*
- no: **Du ringer henne.**
- en: *You call her.*
- no: **Han ringer henne.**
- en: *He calls her.*
- no: **Hun ringer henne.**
- en: *She calls her.*
:::

Here, the subject changes while **henne** remains the object. Choose the object pronoun by looking at the person receiving the call, not the person making it.

**Activity request: object-not-subject-decision**

**Purpose:** Prevent selection based on the sentence’s subject.
**Context:** Several people make calls to different friends before the cinema visit.
**Learner action:** Supply object pronouns across sentences where the subject sometimes stays the same and sometimes changes.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner bases the answer on the object’s identity rather than matching the pronoun to the subject.

{{exercise: object_not_subject_decision}}

## Repairing common mistakes {#repairing-common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-object-01}

Read these intended meanings:

*You see me.*

The person performing the action is “you,” so Norwegian uses **du** as the subject. The speaker receives the action, so the object is **meg**:

::: examples
- no: **Du ser meg.**
- en: *You see me.*
:::

*I see you.*

The speaker performs the action, so the subject is **jeg**. The listener receives the action, so the object is **deg**:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg ser deg.**
- en: *I see you.*
:::

*She knows him.*

The female person performs the action, so the subject is **hun**. The male person is known, so the object is **ham**:

::: examples
- no: **Hun kjenner ham.**
- en: *She knows him.*
:::

*He knows her.*

The male person performs the action, so the subject is **han**. The female person is known, so the object is **henne**:

::: examples
- no: **Han kjenner henne.**
- en: *He knows her.*
:::

These pairs show why it is important not to choose a form from gender or person alone. You must also check the person’s role in the sentence.

**Activity request: subject-form-error-repair**

**Purpose:** Correct the common use of **jeg**, **du**, **han**, or **hun** where an object form is required.
**Context:** Short messages between the four cinema-goers contain one pronoun error each.
**Learner action:** Find the incorrect subject-form pronoun used as an object and replace it with the appropriate target form.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner recognizes an object position in a simple sentence and supplies **meg**, **deg**, **ham**, or **henne** according to the intended person.

{{exercise: subject_form_error_repair}}

## Back at the cinema {#back-at-the-cinema role=model objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-object-01}

Amina reaches the group with the tickets.

::: {.reading translation="Hi! Jonas, I didn’t see you at first." dialogue_id="back-at-the-cinema" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Hei! Jonas, jeg så ikke **deg** først.
:::

::: {.reading translation="There were many people in front of me." dialogue_id="back-at-the-cinema" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Det var mange mennesker foran **meg**.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Where is Emil? I saw him by the entrance." dialogue_id="back-at-the-cinema" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Hvor er Emil? Jeg så **ham** ved inngangen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="He is buying popcorn. I am waiting for him here." dialogue_id="back-at-the-cinema" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Han kjøper popcorn. Jeg venter på **ham** her.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Amina has the tickets. Ask her." dialogue_id="back-at-the-cinema" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Amina har billettene. Spør **henne**.
:::

The same forms now work with slightly different situations. Jonas can be the listener in **jeg så ikke deg**, the speaker referred to in **foran meg**, and Emil or Amina can be replaced by **ham** or **henne**.

**Activity request: cinema-scene-completion**

**Purpose:** Integrate all four forms within the original situation.
**Context:** A continuation of Sara, Jonas, Emil, and Amina’s meeting outside the cinema.
**Learner action:** Complete missing object pronouns by tracking who is speaking and which named person has already been mentioned.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner maintains reference across a short connected exchange, rather than choosing pronouns in isolated sentences.

{{exercise: cinema_scene_completion}}

## Transfer to a new situation {#transfer-new-situation role=recap objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-object-01}

The next day, Jonas and Sara are at a café. Their friends Emil and Amina may join them later.

::: {.reading translation="Do you see Emil outside?" dialogue_id="cafe-transfer" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ser du Emil ute?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I see him by the bus." dialogue_id="cafe-transfer" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Ja, jeg ser **ham** ved bussen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And Amina?" dialogue_id="cafe-transfer" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Og Amina?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, I don’t see her. Can you call her?" dialogue_id="cafe-transfer" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Nei, jeg ser ikke **henne**. Kan du ringe **henne**?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Wait for me here." dialogue_id="cafe-transfer" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. Vent på **meg** her.
:::

::: {.reading translation="All right. I’ll find you afterwards." dialogue_id="cafe-transfer" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Greit. Jeg finner **deg** etterpå.
:::

The setting has changed, but the decision remains the same: identify the person receiving the action and choose the corresponding object form.

**Activity request: cafe-transfer-selection**

**Purpose:** Transfer pronoun selection to a new everyday setting.
**Context:** Two friends at a café look for Emil and Amina and coordinate by phone.
**Learner action:** Complete a short connected exchange with the correct object pronouns, using speaker, listener, and named-person clues.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner independently chooses **meg**, **deg**, **ham**, and **henne** outside the original cinema dialogue.
