## Finding the Library {#finding-the-library role=orient objectives=obj-ask-for-and-give-directions-01}

## A street conversation {#street-conversation role=model objectives=obj-ask-for-and-give-directions-01}

Maya has recently moved to Bergen. She is meeting a friend at the public library, but her phone has run out of battery. Near the railway station, she asks a passer-by, Henrik, for help.

::: {.reading translation="Excuse me, how do I get to the library?" dialogue_id=street-conversation speaker_id=maya speaker_name="Maya" character_id="maya"}
Unnskyld, hvordan kommer jeg meg til biblioteket?
:::

::: {.reading translation="The library? It isn’t far from here." dialogue_id=street-conversation speaker_id=henrik speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Biblioteket? Det er ikke langt herfra.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Can you explain the way?" dialogue_id=street-conversation speaker_id=maya speaker_name="Maya" character_id="maya"}
Fint. Kan du forklare veien?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Go straight ahead to the traffic lights. Turn left, and continue past the hotel." dialogue_id=street-conversation speaker_id=henrik speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Ja. Gå rett fram til lyskrysset. Ta til venstre, og fortsett forbi hotellet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Left at the traffic lights?" dialogue_id=street-conversation speaker_id=maya speaker_name="Maya" character_id="maya"}
Til venstre ved lyskrysset?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, that’s right. After the hotel, cross the street. The library is on the right-hand side." dialogue_id=street-conversation speaker_id=henrik speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Ja, det stemmer. Etter hotellet krysser du gata. Biblioteket ligger på høyre side.
:::

::: {.reading translation="So I go straight ahead, turn left at the traffic lights, and go past the hotel. Then I cross the street." dialogue_id=street-conversation speaker_id=maya speaker_name="Maya" character_id="maya"}
Så jeg går rett fram, tar til venstre ved lyskrysset og går forbi hotellet. Deretter krysser jeg gata.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Exactly right. You’ll see the library immediately." dialogue_id=street-conversation speaker_id=henrik speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Helt riktig. Du ser biblioteket med en gang.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thank you very much for the help!" dialogue_id=street-conversation speaker_id=maya speaker_name="Maya" character_id="maya"}
Tusen takk for hjelpen!
:::

::: {.reading translation="You’re welcome!" dialogue_id=street-conversation speaker_id=henrik speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Bare hyggelig!
:::

Maya does three important things in this conversation. She asks for a route, checks one detail while Henrik is speaking, and finally repeats the whole route in a shorter form. Henrik gives the route as a series of small steps and confirms that Maya has understood it.

## Asking for the way {#asking-for-the-way role=model objectives=obj-ask-for-and-give-directions-01}

A useful general question is:

::: examples
- no: Hvordan kommer jeg meg til biblioteket?
- en: How do I get to the library?
:::

You can replace the destination without changing the rest of the question:

::: examples
- no: Hvordan kommer jeg meg til stasjonen?
- en: How do I get to the station?
- no: Hvordan kommer jeg meg til sentrum?
- en: How do I get to the town centre?
- no: Hvordan kommer jeg meg til bussterminalen?
- en: How do I get to the bus terminal?
:::

The phrase **kommer jeg meg til** means “do I get to” when you are asking how to reach a place. For a beginner, it is best learned as one useful expression.

When the place is nearby, you can also ask:

::: examples
- no: Hvor ligger apoteket?
- en: Where is the pharmacy?
- no: Vet du hvor rådhuset ligger?
- en: Do you know where the town hall is?
:::

These questions can produce a location rather than a complete route. If you need actual steps, **Hvordan kommer jeg meg til …?** or **Kan du forklare veien?** makes that need clearer.

**Unnskyld** is a natural way to begin when speaking to a stranger:

::: examples
- no: Unnskyld, hvordan kommer jeg meg til museet?
- en: Excuse me, how do I get to the museum?
- no: Unnskyld, kan du forklare veien til havna?
- en: Excuse me, can you explain the way to the harbour?
:::

Notice the two patterns:

::: examples
- no: Hvordan kommer jeg meg til biblioteket?
- en: How do I get to the library?
- no: Kan du forklare veien til biblioteket?
- en: Can you explain the way to the library?
:::

In both patterns, **til** introduces the destination.

**Checkpoint: Choose a suitable route question**

Work with short situations involving a visitor, a named destination, and a nearby person who can help. Form a complete question beginning with **Unnskyld** and use either **Hvordan kommer jeg meg til …?** or **Kan du forklare veien til …?**

This activity belongs here because the first step in a direction exchange is making the request clear. Use familiar destinations such as a station, hotel, museum, pharmacy, or bus stop. A successful response would politely identify the destination and make it clear that the speaker needs a route.

## Giving short direction steps {#giving-short-direction-steps role=model objectives=obj-ask-for-and-give-directions-01}

Henrik does not give one long, complicated explanation. He divides the route into steps:

::: examples
- no: Gå rett fram til lyskrysset.
- en: Go straight ahead to the traffic lights.
- no: Ta til venstre.
- en: Turn left.
- no: Fortsett forbi hotellet.
- en: Continue past the hotel.
:::

These directions use the command form, called the **imperative (imperativ)**. In route instructions, the imperative is direct but normal. It does not sound rude when someone has asked for help.

Useful route commands include:

::: examples
- no: Gå rett fram.
- en: Go straight ahead.
- no: Ta til høyre.
- en: Turn right.
- no: Ta til venstre.
- en: Turn left.
- no: Fortsett langs gata.
- en: Continue along the street.
- no: Gå forbi banken.
- en: Go past the bank.
- no: Kryss gata.
- en: Cross the street.
- no: Stopp ved bussholdeplassen.
- en: Stop at the bus stop.
:::

A command becomes more useful when it includes a landmark:

::: examples
- no: Gå rett fram til kirken.
- en: Go straight ahead to the church.
- no: Ta til høyre ved kiosken.
- en: Turn right at the kiosk.
- no: Kryss gata etter hotellet.
- en: Cross the street after the hotel.
:::

The small words connecting the action to a landmark are important:

- **til** indicates the point you move toward.
- **ved** means “at” or “by.”
- **forbi** means “past.”
- **etter** means “after.”
- **langs** means “along.”

Compare these aligned route steps:

::: examples
- no: Gå til lyskrysset.
- en: Go to the traffic lights.
- no: Ta til venstre ved lyskrysset.
- en: Turn left at the traffic lights.
- no: Gå forbi lyskrysset.
- en: Go past the traffic lights.
:::

Each sentence uses the same landmark, but the relationship to it changes.

**Checkpoint: Build one clear step**

Take a simple action and connect it to one landmark. For example, describe where someone should turn, how far someone should walk, or what someone should pass.

This controlled activity follows the command forms because it makes each instruction precise before several instructions are combined. Use a small street context containing a hotel, traffic lights, a bank, and a station. A successful response would contain one natural command and one landmark that clearly marks where the action happens.

## Putting the steps in sequence {#putting-steps-in-sequence role=model objectives=obj-ask-for-and-give-directions-01}

A route is easier to follow when the listener can hear where one step ends and the next begins. Common sequence words are:

::: examples
- no: først
- en: first
- no: så
- en: then
- no: deretter
- en: after that
- no: etterpå
- en: afterwards
:::

For example:

::: examples
- no: Først går du rett fram til rundkjøringen. Så tar du til høyre.
- en: First, you go straight ahead to the roundabout. Then you turn right.
- no: Gå forbi skolen. Deretter krysser du gata.
- en: Go past the school. After that, cross the street.
:::

Norwegian directions often move naturally between commands and present-tense descriptions:

::: examples
- no: Gå rett fram og ta til venstre.
- en: Go straight ahead and turn left.
- no: Etter banken tar du til venstre.
- en: After the bank, you turn left.
:::

Both forms work in a direction exchange. Commands are especially useful for short, direct steps. Present-tense forms are common when explaining how the route develops.

Keep each step short. Compare:

::: examples
- no: Gå rett fram til hotellet. Ta til venstre. Biblioteket ligger på høyre side.
- en: Go straight ahead to the hotel. Turn left. The library is on the right-hand side.
:::

This is easier to follow than a long sentence containing every detail at once.

Useful expressions for the final location include:

::: examples
- no: Det ligger på høyre side.
- en: It is on the right-hand side.
- no: Det ligger på venstre side.
- en: It is on the left-hand side.
- no: Det ligger ved siden av banken.
- en: It is next to the bank.
- no: Det ligger rett overfor hotellet.
- en: It is directly opposite the hotel.
:::

## Movement and final location {#movement-and-final-location role=contrast objectives=obj-ask-for-and-give-directions-01}

There is a useful contrast between movement and final location:

::: examples
- no: Ta til høyre.
- en: Turn right.
- no: Apoteket ligger på høyre side.
- en: The pharmacy is on the right-hand side.
:::

Use **til høyre** for the direction of a turn. Use **på høyre side** to say where a place is located. The same contrast applies to the left:

::: examples
- no: Ta til venstre.
- en: Turn left.
- no: Kafeen ligger på venstre side.
- en: The café is on the left-hand side.
:::

**Checkpoint: Expand a route**

Use a simple map description with a starting point, two landmarks, and a destination. Write or say three short direction steps joined by **først**, **så**, or **deretter** where useful.

This activity belongs here because the learner can now form individual steps and must connect them into a route that another person could follow. A successful response would present the actions in a logical order, use landmarks accurately, and finish by locating the destination.

{{exercise: expand_route}}

## Checking one detail {#checking-one-detail role=model objectives=obj-ask-for-and-give-directions-01}

Listeners do not need to wait until the end if one step is unclear. Maya checks the turn immediately:

::: examples
- no: Til venstre ved lyskrysset?
- en: Left at the traffic lights?
:::

This is a shortened confirmation question. It repeats only the important information. Other useful checks are:

::: examples
- no: Rett fram til kirken?
- en: Straight ahead to the church?
- no: Til høyre etter banken?
- en: Right after the bank?
- no: Forbi hotellet?
- en: Past the hotel?
:::

You can also use a full question:

::: examples
- no: Skal jeg ta til venstre ved lyskrysset?
- en: Should I turn left at the traffic lights?
- no: Skal jeg gå forbi hotellet?
- en: Should I go past the hotel?
- no: Er det før eller etter banken?
- en: Is it before or after the bank?
:::

These questions focus on route details. They do not begin a new topic; they help the speakers complete the same practical task.

The person giving directions can confirm or correct the detail:

::: examples
- no: Ja, det stemmer.
- en: Yes, that’s right.
- no: Ja, helt riktig.
- en: Yes, exactly right.
- no: Nei, ta til høyre, ikke til venstre.
- en: No, turn right, not left.
- no: Nesten. Gå forbi banken først.
- en: Almost. Go past the bank first.
:::

**Checkpoint: Confirm one uncertain detail**

Read or listen to a short two-step route, then repeat one key detail as a question. Focus on the turn, landmark, or order that could easily be misunderstood.

This checkpoint belongs here because successful direction exchanges depend on repairing small misunderstandings before the listener leaves. Use Henrik’s route to the library or a parallel route to a pharmacy. A successful response would isolate the uncertain detail and turn it into a natural confirmation question.

{{exercise: confirm_uncertain_detail}}

## Repeating the route back {#repeating-route-back role=model objectives=obj-ask-for-and-give-directions-01}

At the end of the opening dialogue, Maya summarizes:

::: examples
- no: Så jeg går rett fram, tar til venstre ved lyskrysset og går forbi hotellet. Deretter krysser jeg gata.
- en: So I go straight ahead, turn left at the traffic lights, and go past the hotel. Then I cross the street.
:::

Beginning with **Så …** shows that the listener is checking the full route:

::: examples
- no: Så jeg går til kirken og tar til høyre?
- en: So I go to the church and turn right?
- no: Så først går jeg forbi skolen, og deretter krysser jeg gata?
- en: So first I go past the school, and after that I cross the street?
:::

The route giver can then confirm:

::: examples
- no: Ja, det stemmer.
- en: Yes, that’s right.
- no: Helt riktig.
- en: Exactly right.
:::

Repeating the route is not unnecessary repetition. It gives both speakers a final chance to notice a wrong turn or missing landmark.

**Checkpoint: Complete the exchange**

Create a short, guided exchange in which one person asks how to reach a named place, the other gives two or three route steps, and the first person repeats the route back beginning with **Så …**. End with confirmation or one brief correction.

This activity brings together all parts of the approved task: asking, giving short steps, and confirming understanding. Keep the route concrete and limited to familiar street landmarks. A successful exchange would contain a clear request, an ordered route, a listener check, and an explicit confirmation.

{{exercise: complete_exchange}}

## Common problems {#common-problems role=contrast objectives=obj-ask-for-and-give-directions-01}

**Omitting the destination marker**

Avoid:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Hvordan kommer jeg meg biblioteket?
- en: ✗ How do I get to the library?
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: Hvordan kommer jeg meg til biblioteket?
- en: How do I get to the library?
:::

The destination normally follows **til**.

**Confusing a turn with a location**

For a turn:

::: examples
- no: Ta til venstre.
- en: Turn left.
:::

For the location of the destination:

::: examples
- no: Biblioteket ligger på venstre side.
- en: The library is on the left-hand side.
:::

**Giving landmarks without actions**

::: examples
- no: Hotellet, banken, lyskrysset
- en: The hotel, the bank, the traffic lights
:::

does not tell the listener what to do. Connect each useful landmark to an action:

::: examples
- no: Gå forbi hotellet, og ta til høyre ved banken.
- en: Go past the hotel, and turn right at the bank.
:::

**Giving too much information in one step**

Several short instructions are usually clearer:

::: examples
- no: Gå rett fram. Ta til venstre ved skolen. Apoteket ligger ved siden av banken.
- en: Go straight ahead. Turn left at the school. The pharmacy is next to the bank.
:::

**Confirming only with “yes”**

**Ja** can confirm, but **Ja, det stemmer** or **Helt riktig** makes the confirmation clearer after the listener has repeated a route.

## Back at the library {#back-at-the-library role=model objectives=obj-ask-for-and-give-directions-01}

Maya reaches the library and meets her friend, Elias.

::: {.reading translation="Did you find your way?" dialogue_id=back-at-library speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Fant du fram?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. I asked a man near the station." dialogue_id=back-at-library speaker_id=maya speaker_name="Maya" character_id="maya"}
Ja. Jeg spurte en mann ved stasjonen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Was the route easy?" dialogue_id=back-at-library speaker_id=elias speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Var veien enkel?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Quite easy. I went straight ahead to the traffic lights, turned left, and went past the hotel. The library was on the right-hand side after I crossed the street." dialogue_id=back-at-library speaker_id=maya speaker_name="Maya" character_id="maya"}
Ganske enkel. Jeg gikk rett fram til lyskrysset, tok til venstre og gikk forbi hotellet. Biblioteket lå på høyre side etter at jeg krysset gata.
:::

A little later, a visitor enters the library and asks Maya for help.

::: {.reading translation="Excuse me, how do I get to the bus terminal?" dialogue_id=visitor-directions speaker_id=visitor speaker_name=Besøkende}
Unnskyld, hvordan kommer jeg meg til bussterminalen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Leave the library and turn left. Cross the street and go past the hotel. Turn right at the traffic lights. The terminal is next to the station." dialogue_id=visitor-directions speaker_id=maya speaker_name="Maya" character_id="maya"}
Gå ut av biblioteket og ta til venstre. Kryss gata og gå forbi hotellet. Ta til høyre ved lyskrysset. Terminalen ligger ved siden av stasjonen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="So I turn left outside the library, cross the street, go past the hotel, and turn right at the traffic lights?" dialogue_id=visitor-directions speaker_id=visitor speaker_name=Besøkende}
Så jeg tar til venstre utenfor biblioteket, krysser gata, går forbi hotellet og tar til høyre ved lyskrysset?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, that’s right." dialogue_id=visitor-directions speaker_id=maya speaker_name="Maya" character_id="maya"}
Ja, det stemmer.
:::

Maya now uses the same forms in a new role. Earlier she needed a route; now she can give one and confirm that another person has understood it.

## Final retrieval {#final-retrieval role=recap objectives=obj-ask-for-and-give-directions-01}

Without looking back, produce a brief direction exchange. Ask politely how to reach a familiar place, give three short steps using a turn and a landmark, repeat the route beginning with **Så …**, and finish with **Det stemmer** or **Helt riktig**. Successful retrieval demonstrates the complete sequence: request, route, check, and confirmation.
