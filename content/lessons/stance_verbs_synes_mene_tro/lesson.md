## A decision at the neighbourhood café {#a-decision-at-the-neighbourhood-cafe role=orient objectives=obj-stance-verbs-synes-mene-tro-01}

Mina and Jonas help organize events at a small neighbourhood café in Trondheim. They are discussing whether the café should begin opening on Sundays. Mina has spoken to several customers, while Jonas has looked at staffing and costs.

::: {.reading translation="I think the café is pleasant on Sundays." dialogue_id="cafe-opening" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Jeg synes det er hyggelig på kafeen på søndager.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but do you think we should open every Sunday?" dialogue_id="cafe-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, men mener du at vi bør holde åpent hver søndag?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I think we should try one Sunday a month at first." dialogue_id="cafe-opening" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Jeg mener at vi bør prøve én søndag i måneden først.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Do you think many customers will come?" dialogue_id="cafe-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Tror du mange kunder kommer?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I think so. Several people have asked about Sunday opening." dialogue_id="cafe-opening" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja, det tror jeg. Flere har spurt om søndagsåpent.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’m not so sure. I think it will be quiet in winter." dialogue_id="cafe-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg er ikke så sikker. Jeg tror det blir rolig om vinteren.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Perhaps. But what do you think of the idea in general?" dialogue_id="cafe-opening" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Kanskje. Men hva synes du om ideen generelt?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I think the idea is good, but I think we must examine the costs first." dialogue_id="cafe-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg synes ideen er god, men jeg mener at vi må undersøke kostnadene først.
:::

All three highlighted verbs can correspond to English *think*. Norwegian separates three kinds of thinking more clearly:

- **synes** presents an evaluation or personal impression;
- **mene** presents an opinion, viewpoint, or position;
- **tro** presents a belief about what is true, likely, or possible.

Jonas can therefore say that the idea seems good to him, argue that costs must be examined, and believe that winter Sundays will be quiet. These are related thoughts, but they are not the same kind of thought.

## Synes: giving an evaluation {#synes-giving-an-evaluation role=model objectives=obj-stance-verbs-synes-mene-tro-01}

Use **synes** when you evaluate something or describe your personal impression of it. Common English translations include *think*, *find*, and sometimes *feel*.

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes kafeen er koselig.
- en: I think the café is cosy.
- no: Mina synes søndagsåpent er en god idé.
- en: Mina thinks Sunday opening is a good idea.
- no: Vi synes filmen var litt lang.
- en: We thought the film was a little long.
- no: Hva synes du om den nye menyen?
- en: What do you think of the new menu?
:::

The speaker is not mainly claiming that something is objectively true. The speaker is evaluating it: pleasant, expensive, interesting, difficult, useful, boring, or good.

Compare two reactions to a concert:

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes konserten var fantastisk.
- en: I thought the concert was fantastic.
:::

This is an evaluation of the concert.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror konserten var utsolgt.
- en: I think the concert was sold out.
:::

This is a belief about a fact. The speaker may not know whether every ticket was sold.

**Checkpoint activity:** Read several short reactions to places, meals, films, courses, and events. Identify which sentences express a personal evaluation. Then restate those evaluations with **synes**. This belongs here because it isolates the most typical use of **synes** before other meanings of English *think* are introduced. A successful response would use **synes** for judgments such as pleasant, disappointing, practical, or difficult without treating them as uncertain facts.

## Mene: stating a viewpoint or position {#mene-stating-a-viewpoint-or-position role=model objectives=obj-stance-verbs-synes-mene-tro-01}

Use **mene** when you state what your opinion or position is, especially when the point could be discussed, defended, or acted on.

::: examples
- no: Jeg mener at kafeen bør holde åpent på søndager.
- en: I think the café should open on Sundays.
- no: Jonas mener at de må undersøke kostnadene først.
- en: Jonas thinks they must examine the costs first.
- no: Læreren mener at kurset trenger mer muntlig trening.
- en: The teacher thinks the course needs more speaking practice.
- no: Hva mener du om forslaget?
- en: What is your opinion of the proposal?
:::

The opinions expressed with **mene** often concern what people should do, which solution is best, or how a situation ought to be understood.

::: examples
- no: Jeg mener at kommunen bør bygge flere sykkelveier.
- en: I think the municipality should build more cycle paths.
- no: Hun mener at møtet bør begynne tidligere.
- en: She thinks the meeting should begin earlier.
- no: Vi mener at denne løsningen er mest rettferdig.
- en: We think this solution is the fairest.
:::

With **synes**, the speaker gives a personal assessment. With **mene**, the speaker takes a position.

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes den nye planen er komplisert.
- en: I think the new plan is complicated.
- no: Jeg mener at vi bør endre den nye planen.
- en: I think we should change the new plan.
:::

The first sentence evaluates the plan. The second recommends a course of action.

The boundary is not always absolute. A speaker may use either **synes** or **mene** when giving a general opinion, but the emphasis changes:

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes forslaget er godt.
- en: I think the proposal is good.
:::

The speaker evaluates the proposal positively.

::: examples
- no: Jeg mener at forslaget er den beste løsningen.
- en: I think the proposal is the best solution.
:::

The speaker presents a considered position, perhaps as part of a discussion.

**Checkpoint activity:** Return to Mina and Jonas’s meeting. Separate their personal reactions from their positions about what the café should do. Reformulate each position with **mene**. This activity follows the explanation of **mene** because the café discussion naturally contains both evaluations and recommendations. A successful response would distinguish liking the idea from arguing that the café should adopt a particular plan.

## Tro: expressing belief or uncertainty {#tro-expressing-belief-or-uncertainty role=model objectives=obj-stance-verbs-synes-mene-tro-01}

Use **tro** when you believe that something is true but do not present it as certain knowledge. It is also common for predictions and guesses.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror mange kunder kommer på søndag.
- en: I think many customers will come on Sunday.
- no: Jonas tror det blir rolig om vinteren.
- en: Jonas thinks it will be quiet in winter.
- no: Jeg tror bussen er forsinket.
- en: I think the bus is delayed.
- no: Tror du hun er hjemme?
- en: Do you think she is at home?
- no: Vi tror møtet slutter klokka fire.
- en: We think the meeting ends at four.
:::

These sentences concern facts or future situations that the speaker does not know for certain.

Compare an evaluation with a prediction:

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes søndager er fine.
- en: I think Sundays are nice.
- no: Jeg tror søndagen blir fin.
- en: I think Sunday will be nice.
:::

The first sentence evaluates Sundays in general. The second predicts what a particular Sunday will be like.

Compare a position with a belief:

::: examples
- no: Jeg mener at vi bør ansette en ekstra person.
- en: I think we should hire an additional person.
- no: Jeg tror vi trenger en ekstra person.
- en: I think we need an additional person.
:::

The first sentence gives a recommendation. The second presents the speaker’s belief about the staffing situation.

A useful short response is:

::: examples
- no: Kommer det mange kunder? – Ja, det tror jeg.
- en: Will many customers come? – Yes, I think so.
- no: Blir det dyrt? – Nei, det tror jeg ikke.
- en: Will it be expensive? – No, I don’t think so.
:::

Here **det** refers back to the whole idea in the question.

**Checkpoint activity:** Consider short situations in which information is incomplete: a late bus, tomorrow’s weather, attendance at an event, or the reason a colleague is absent. Express a reasonable belief using **tro**. This belongs here because uncertainty is the key contrast between **tro** and the other two verbs. A successful response would use **tro** for guesses and predictions rather than for personal ratings or recommendations.

## One situation, three different perspectives {#one-situation-three-different-perspectives role=contrast objectives=obj-stance-verbs-synes-mene-tro-01}

The clearest way to understand the verbs is to apply all three to one topic.

Imagine a new library:

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes det nye biblioteket er lyst og rolig.
- en: I think the new library is bright and quiet.
:::

This is the speaker’s impression.

::: examples
- no: Jeg mener at biblioteket bør være åpent lenger.
- en: I think the library should stay open longer.
:::

This is the speaker’s position about opening hours.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror mange studenter kommer til å bruke biblioteket om kvelden.
- en: I think many students are going to use the library in the evening.
:::

This is a prediction.

Now imagine a language course:

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes kurset er nyttig.
- en: I think the course is useful.
- no: Jeg mener at vi bør ha mer tid til samtale.
- en: I think we should have more time for conversation.
- no: Jeg tror neste undervisningstime blir vanskelig.
- en: I think the next class will be difficult.
:::

The topic stays the same, but the purpose changes: evaluation, position, then belief.

**Contrast activity:** Work with one familiar situation, such as a course, workplace, local service, or planned journey. Produce three statements about it: a personal evaluation with **synes**, a position with **mene**, and an uncertain belief or prediction with **tro**. This checkpoint belongs here because it requires a deliberate three-way contrast while keeping the context stable. A successful response would show three different communicative purposes rather than three loosely interchangeable translations of English *think*.

{{exercise: three_way_stance_contrast}}

## Asking what someone thinks {#asking-what-someone-thinks role=contrast objectives=obj-stance-verbs-synes-mene-tro-01}

The verb in a question helps show what kind of answer is expected.

::: examples
- no: Hva synes du om kafeen?
- en: What do you think of the café?
:::

This usually asks for an evaluation: Is it pleasant, noisy, expensive, or attractive?

::: examples
- no: Hva mener du om søndagsåpent?
- en: What is your opinion of Sunday opening?
:::

This asks for a viewpoint or position in a discussion.

::: examples
- no: Tror du kafeen får mange kunder på søndag?
- en: Do you think the café will get many customers on Sunday?
:::

This asks whether the other person believes a situation is likely or true.

The answers should match the questions:

::: examples
- no: Hva synes du om lokalet? – Jeg synes det er koselig.
- en: What do you think of the premises? – I think they are cosy.
:::

::: examples
- no: Hva mener du om planen? – Jeg mener at vi bør prøve den i én måned.
- en: What is your opinion of the plan? – I think we should try it for one month.
:::

::: examples
- no: Tror du planen virker? – Ja, det tror jeg.
- en: Do you think the plan will work? – Yes, I think so.
:::

**Question-and-response activity:** Read short questions containing **synes**, **mene**, or **tro**, and give a response that matches the type of question. Use the café, a workplace meeting, and a community event as contexts. This belongs here because choosing the correct verb involves recognizing another speaker’s purpose as well as producing your own statement. A successful response would answer an evaluation with an evaluation, a viewpoint question with a position, and a belief question with a belief or prediction.

{{exercise: question_response_stance_match}}

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-stance-verbs-synes-mene-tro-01}

**Using tro for every English use of think**

English *think* often leads learners to choose **tro** too widely.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror filmen er kjedelig.
- en: I think the film is boring.
:::

This can be correct if the speaker has not seen the film and expects it to be boring.

If the speaker has watched the film and is evaluating it, use **synes**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes filmen er kjedelig.
- en: I think the film is boring.
:::

Context decides whether *boring* is an uncertain expectation or a personal evaluation.

**Using synes for a factual guess**

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes møtet begynner klokka ni.
- en: Incorrect for this meaning: I think the meeting starts at nine.
:::

This is normally unsuitable if the speaker is uncertain about the schedule. The intended meaning is a belief about a fact:

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror møtet begynner klokka ni.
- en: I think the meeting starts at nine.
:::

Use **synes** only if the sentence genuinely expresses an evaluation.

**Using tro for a recommendation**

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror vi bør vente.
- en: I think we should wait.
:::

This form may occur when the speaker presents the recommendation tentatively. However, when the purpose is to state a clear position in a discussion, **mene** is the more direct choice:

::: examples
- no: Jeg mener at vi bør vente.
- en: I think we should wait.
:::

The difference is one of stance. **Tro** makes the thought sound more like a belief or tentative judgment; **mene** presents it as the speaker’s opinion.

**Treating synes and mener as completely interchangeable**

These verbs can overlap, but they highlight different things.

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes løsningen er upraktisk.
- en: I think the solution is impractical.
:::

This evaluates the solution.

::: examples
- no: Jeg mener at vi trenger en annen løsning.
- en: I think we need another solution.
:::

This states a position about what should happen.

A useful test is to ask yourself:

- Am I reacting to or evaluating something? Use **synes**.
- Am I taking a position in a discussion? Use **mene**.
- Am I saying what I believe is true or likely? Use **tro**.

**Mistake-identification activity:** Examine a statement in which the verb does not match the surrounding context, and select that verb. The feedback shows the fitting replacement and explains whether the context calls for an evaluation, a position, or a belief. This belongs here because learners must use context rather than translate *think* mechanically.

## Back at the café {#back-at-the-cafe role=recap objectives=obj-stance-verbs-synes-mene-tro-01}

A week later, Mina and Jonas have checked the costs and spoken to the café staff.

::: {.reading translation="Now I’ve looked at the figures. I think we can afford to open one Sunday a month." dialogue_id="back-at-the-cafe" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nå har jeg sett på tallene. Jeg tror vi får råd til å holde åpent én søndag i måneden.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That was good news. What do the staff think?" dialogue_id="back-at-the-cafe" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Det var gode nyheter. Hva mener de ansatte?
:::

::: {.reading translation="They think we should close a little earlier on Sundays." dialogue_id="back-at-the-cafe" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
De mener at vi bør stenge litt tidligere på søndager.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That sounds sensible. I also think a shorter opening day is better." dialogue_id="back-at-the-cafe" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Det høres fornuftig ut. Jeg synes også at en kortere åpningsdag er bedre.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Do you think the customers will be satisfied?" dialogue_id="back-at-the-cafe" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Tror du kundene blir fornøyde?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I think so. And I think this is a good start." dialogue_id="back-at-the-cafe" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja, det tror jeg. Og jeg synes dette er en god start.
:::

The verbs continue to divide the work:

- Jonas **tror** the café can afford the plan because he has formed a belief from the figures.
- The employees **mener** that the café should close earlier because they are stating a position.
- Mina **synes** a shorter day is better because she is evaluating the arrangement.
- Mina **tror** the customers will be satisfied because she is predicting their reaction.

**Context-completion activity:** Retell the café’s decision using the three verbs. Include the speakers’ evaluation of the plan, the staff’s position, and the prediction about customers. This belongs near the end because it returns to the chapter anchor and combines all three meanings in a coherent account. A successful response would choose verbs according to purpose while keeping Mina, Jonas, and the café decision logically consistent.
