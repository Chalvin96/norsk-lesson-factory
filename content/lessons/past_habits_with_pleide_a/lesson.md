## Back in the old neighborhood {#back-in-the-old-neighborhood role=orient objectives=obj-past-habits-with-pleide-a-01}

Maja has recently returned to the town where she grew up. She meets her childhood friend Jonas near the river.

::: {.reading translation="When I lived here, I used to cycle along the river every morning." dialogue_id="maja-jonas-opening" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Da jeg bodde her, pleide jeg å sykle langs elva hver morgen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, and you used to wait for me by the old bridge." dialogue_id="maja-jonas-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, og du pleide å vente på meg ved den gamle brua.
:::

::: {.reading translation="We used to buy ice cream at the kiosk after school too." dialogue_id="maja-jonas-opening" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Vi pleide å kjøpe is i kiosken etter skolen også.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s right. The owner used to know all the children in the area." dialogue_id="maja-jonas-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Det stemmer. Eieren pleide å kjenne alle barna i området.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yesterday I cycled along the river again." dialogue_id="maja-jonas-opening" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
I går syklet jeg langs elva igjen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Did you stop at the kiosk?" dialogue_id="maja-jonas-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Stoppet du ved kiosken?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I bought an ice cream. But the old owner doesn’t work there now." dialogue_id="maja-jonas-opening" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Ja, jeg kjøpte en is. Men den gamle eieren jobber ikke der nå.
:::

Maja and Jonas talk about two kinds of past experience. First, they remember routines from their childhood: cycling by the river, waiting at the bridge, and buying ice cream after school. These things happened regularly over a period of time. Then Maja describes what she did yesterday: she cycled, stopped, and bought one ice cream on one finished occasion.

That difference is the focus of this chapter.

**Activity request: notice-routine-and-event**

**Purpose:** Notice the meaning contrast before working with the form.

**Context:** Use Maja and Jonas’s conversation.

**Learner action:** Separate the actions that were recurring childhood routines from the actions that happened during Maja’s single trip yesterday. Explain which time expressions help you decide.

**Success demonstrates:** Recognition of recurring past behavior versus one finished past event. This activity scores the meaning distinction, not verb formation.

{{exercise: notice_routine_and_event}}

## Saying what someone used to do {#saying-what-someone-used-to-do role=model objectives=obj-past-habits-with-pleide-a-01}

Use **pleide å** when you want to present an action as a recurring past behavior or old routine.

::: examples
- no: Maja pleide å sykle langs elva.
- en: Maja used to cycle along the river.
:::

The construction has three parts:

**subject + pleide å + infinitive**

In the example above:

- Maja is the subject.
- pleide places the routine in the past.
- å is the infinitive marker (infinitivsmerket).
- sykle is the infinitive (infinitiv), the basic verb form.

The same pattern works with different subjects:

::: examples
- no: Jeg pleide å gå til skolen.
- en: I used to walk to school.
- no: Han pleide å lage middag på søndager.
- en: He used to make dinner on Sundays.
- no: Vi pleide å besøke bestemor hver sommer.
- en: We used to visit Grandma every summer.
- no: Barna pleide å leke ute etter skolen.
- en: The children used to play outside after school.
:::

The form **pleide** does not change with the subject:

::: examples
- no: Jeg pleide å lese før jeg la meg.
- en: I used to read before going to bed.
- no: De pleide å lese før de la seg.
- en: They used to read before going to bed.
:::

A compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use pleide å + infinitive to describe something that happened regularly during a past period.
:::

Words and phrases such as these often support that meaning:

- hver dag — every day
- hver sommer — every summer
- ofte — often
- vanligvis — usually
- i helgene — at weekends
- etter skolen — after school
- da jeg var liten — when I was little

These expressions do not create the grammar by themselves, but they help establish a recurring situation.

::: examples
- no: Da jeg var liten, pleide jeg å besøke tante Liv hver sommer.
- en: When I was little, I used to visit Aunt Liv every summer.
- no: Før pleide Amir å ta bussen til jobb.
- en: Amir used to take the bus to work before.
- no: Vi pleide å spise sammen på fredager.
- en: We used to eat together on Fridays.
:::

**Activity request: build-the-pleide-a-pattern**

**Purpose:** Retrieve the complete construction rather than remembering only pleide.

**Context:** Short descriptions of former routines involving school, work, family visits, and weekends.

**Learner action:** Turn each recurring-past cue into a full Norwegian sentence using the supplied subject and verb. Keep the main verb in the infinitive after å.

**Success demonstrates:** Accurate production of **subject + pleide å + infinitive**. Meaning cues are provided as context; the scored evidence is construction of the target form.

{{exercise: build_the_pleide_a_pattern}}

## A routine, not one finished event {#a-routine-not-one-finished-event role=contrast objectives=obj-past-habits-with-pleide-a-01}

Compare these pairs carefully.

## A repeated journey and one completed journey {#a-repeated-journey-and-one-completed-journey role=contrast objectives=obj-past-habits-with-pleide-a-01}

::: examples
- no: Før pleide Nora å gå til jobb.
- en: Nora used to walk to work before.
- no: I går gikk Nora til jobb.
- en: Yesterday Nora walked to work.
:::

The first sentence describes Nora’s former routine. The second reports one completed journey yesterday.

## Regular visits and one visit {#regular-visits-and-one-visit role=contrast objectives=obj-past-habits-with-pleide-a-01}

::: examples
- no: Vi pleide å besøke bestemor hver søndag.
- en: We used to visit Grandma every Sunday.
- no: Vi besøkte bestemor på søndag.
- en: We visited Grandma on Sunday.
:::

The regular visits belong with **pleide å**. The single Sunday visit is expressed with the ordinary past tense (preteritum), which you already know.

## A former evening habit and one evening {#a-former-evening-habit-and-one-evening role=contrast objectives=obj-past-habits-with-pleide-a-01}

::: examples
- no: Erik pleide å lese før han la seg.
- en: Erik used to read before going to bed.
- no: Erik leste ferdig boka i går kveld.
- en: Erik finished the book last night.
:::

The first sentence presents reading as Erik’s routine. The second tells us about one finished event.

The important question is not simply, Did this happen in the past? Both forms refer to the past. Ask instead:

::: {.rule}
Am I presenting this as a regular behavior over a past period, or as one finished event?
:::

Use **pleide å** for the regular behavior. Do not use it merely because a sentence contains a past-time expression.

::: examples
- no: I fjor pleide vi å dra på hytta hver helg.
- en: Last year we used to go to the cabin every weekend.
- no: I fjor dro vi på hytta én gang.
- en: Last year we went to the cabin once.
:::

Both sentences contain **i fjor**, but only the first describes a habit.

**Activity request: choose-past-habit-or-finished-event**

**Purpose:** Practise the central meaning decision.

**Context:** Brief everyday situations about commuting, meals, exercise, shopping, and family visits. Some describe a former routine; others describe one completed occasion.

**Learner action:** Decide whether each situation calls for **pleide å + infinitive** or an ordinary past-tense verb, then complete the sentence accordingly.

**Success demonstrates:** Selection of **pleide å** only when the context presents recurring past behavior. Correct formation of unrelated past-tense verbs is supporting context, not the primary scored goal.

{{exercise: choose_past_habit_or_finished_event}}

## More than once does not always mean a habit {#more-than-once-does-not-always-mean-a-habit role=contrast objectives=obj-past-habits-with-pleide-a-01}

A habit is a repeated behavior connected to a period or routine. An action can happen several times during one finished occasion without becoming a habit.

::: examples
- no: Da jeg studerte, pleide jeg å ringe mamma hver søndag.
- en: When I was a student, I used to call Mum every Sunday.
- no: I går ringte jeg mamma tre ganger.
- en: Yesterday I called Mum three times.
:::

Three calls are more than one call, but they all belong to one finished day. The second sentence does not describe an established routine.

Compare another pair:

::: examples
- no: Sara pleide å kjøpe kaffe på vei til jobb.
- en: Sara used to buy coffee on her way to work.
- no: I morges kjøpte Sara to kopper kaffe.
- en: This morning Sara bought two cups of coffee.
:::

Two cups do not make the event a past habit. The time frame is one particular morning.

**Activity request: test-the-habit-boundary**

**Purpose:** Prevent the misconception that any repeated action requires pleide å.

**Context:** Situations contrasting established routines with several actions inside one completed day or visit.

**Learner action:** Identify whether the repetition extends across a past period or belongs to one finished occasion. Write the appropriate sentence.

**Success demonstrates:** Understanding that **pleide å** marks recurring past behavior, not merely a numerical repetition.

{{exercise: test_the_habit_boundary}}

## Keep the next verb in the infinitive {#keep-the-next-verb-in-the-infinitive role=model objectives=obj-past-habits-with-pleide-a-01}

After **pleide å**, use the infinitive. Do not put the second verb in the past tense.

::: examples
- no: Hun pleide å gå hjem etter skolen.
- en: She used to walk home after school.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: Hun pleide å gikk hjem etter skolen.
- en: 'Incorrect: She used to walked home after school.'
:::

The past meaning is already carried by **pleide**. The verb after **å** remains in its infinitive form.

::: examples
- no: Vi pleide å spise middag klokka fem.
- en: We used to eat dinner at five o’clock.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: Vi pleide spiste middag klokka fem.
- en: 'Incorrect: We used ate dinner at five o’clock.'
:::

Do not omit **å** either:

::: examples
- no: Jonas pleide å vente ved brua.
- en: Jonas used to wait by the bridge.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: Jonas pleide vente ved brua.
- en: 'Incorrect: Jonas used to wait by the bridge.'
:::

For this lesson, remember the construction as one unit:

**pleide å + infinitive**

**Activity request: repair-the-target-construction**

**Purpose:** Strengthen the internal form of the construction after its meaning is clear.

**Context:** Sentences about believable former routines. Each sentence contains either a missing å or an incorrectly inflected verb after å.

**Learner action:** Rewrite each sentence with the complete target construction while preserving its original meaning.

**Success demonstrates:** Detection and correction of form errors inside **pleide å + infinitive**. The activity scores construction accuracy, not recognition of the habit/event contrast.

{{exercise: repair_the_target_construction}}

## What pleide å often suggests {#what-pleide-a-often-suggests role=model objectives=obj-past-habits-with-pleide-a-01}

A sentence with **pleide å** often suggests that the old routine has changed or ended. Context usually makes this clearer.

::: examples
- no: Før pleide jeg å ta toget til jobb, men nå jobber jeg hjemme.
- en: I used to take the train to work, but now I work from home.
- no: Lea pleide å spille håndball, men nå spiller hun fotball.
- en: Lea used to play handball, but now she plays football.
- no: Vi pleide å møtes hver fredag. Nå bor vi i forskjellige byer.
- en: We used to meet every Friday. Now we live in different cities.
:::

The essential meaning remains recurring past behavior. A contrast with the present can make the old-routine meaning especially natural, but you do not need to add a present-day contrast every time.

::: examples
- no: Bestefar pleide å fortelle morsomme historier.
- en: Grandpa used to tell funny stories.
:::

This is complete even without a sentence about the present.

## Common mistakes to avoid {#common-mistakes-to-avoid role=contrast objectives=obj-past-habits-with-pleide-a-01}

**Using pleide å for one dated event**

Incorrect for the intended meaning:

::: examples
- no: I går pleide jeg å møte Jonas på kafeen.
- en: 'Not: Yesterday I used to meet Jonas at the café. (for the intended meaning)'
:::

If there was one meeting yesterday, use a finished past event:

::: examples
- no: I går møtte jeg Jonas på kafeen.
- en: Yesterday I met Jonas at the café.
:::

Use **pleide å** only if meeting Jonas was a recurring past practice:

::: examples
- no: Før pleide jeg å møte Jonas på kafeen hver torsdag.
- en: I used to meet Jonas at the café every Thursday.
:::

**Putting two past forms together**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Vi pleide å kjøpte is etter skolen.
- en: 'Incorrect: We used to bought ice cream after school.'
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Vi pleide å kjøpe is etter skolen.
- en: We used to buy ice cream after school.
:::

**Choosing by time word alone**

A phrase such as **i fjor** can introduce either a routine or a finished event.

::: examples
- no: I fjor pleide jeg å svømme hver morgen.
- en: Last year I used to swim every morning.
- no: I fjor svømte jeg over fjorden én gang.
- en: Last year I swam across the fjord once.
:::

Read the whole situation. Look for recurring behavior, not merely a reference to the past.
