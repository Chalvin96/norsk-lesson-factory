## A meeting with incomplete information {#a-meeting-with-incomplete-information role=orient objectives=obj-hedging-stance-markers-01}

Maja and Henrik work at a small cultural centre. They are preparing an outdoor concert, but several details are still uncertain. Notice how they avoid presenting every statement as a definite fact.

::: {.reading translation='Apparently, around two hundred people are coming on Saturday.' dialogue_id='meeting' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Det kommer visstnok rundt to hundre mennesker på lørdag.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, but there may be fewer if it rains.' dialogue_id='meeting' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Ja, men det blir kanskje færre hvis det regner.
:::

::: {.reading translation='It probably will. The weather forecast does not look very promising.' dialogue_id='meeting' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Det gjør det nok. Værmeldingen ser ikke særlig lovende ut.
:::

::: {.reading translation='We should probably order an extra tent, then, shouldn’t we?' dialogue_id='meeting' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Vi bør vel bestille et ekstra telt, da?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, that is probably safest. But the tent apparently costs five thousand kroner.' dialogue_id='meeting' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jo, det er vel tryggest. Men teltet koster visst fem tusen kroner.
:::

::: {.reading translation='That much? I think it sounds expensive.' dialogue_id='meeting' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Så mye? Jeg synes det høres dyrt ut.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Agreed. After all, we only need a fairly small tent, something like one for twenty people.' dialogue_id='meeting' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Enig. Vi trenger jo bare et ganske lite telt, sånn til tjue personer.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Then perhaps we can borrow one from the sports club. They apparently have several.' dialogue_id='meeting' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Da kan vi kanskje låne ett av idrettslaget. De har visst flere.
:::

Maja and Henrik use several small words and expressions to show how strongly they stand behind what they say. They distinguish reported information from personal opinion, make careful inferences, appeal to shared knowledge, and give approximate numbers. These expressions are called **stance markers** because they show the speaker’s stance toward a statement.

Without them, the conversation would sound much more certain:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer to hundre mennesker.
- en: Two hundred people are coming.
:::

With a source-based hedge, Maja makes it clear that the number comes from information she has received:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer visstnok rundt to hundre mennesker.
- en: Apparently, around two hundred people are coming.
:::

The second version does not merely provide less information. It tells Henrik how to interpret the claim: the number is reported, and it may not be fully reliable.

## Expressing uncertainty with kanskje {#expressing-uncertainty-with-kanskje role=model objectives=obj-hedging-stance-markers-01}

The most common general marker of uncertainty is **kanskje**, meaning perhaps, maybe, or possibly.

::: examples
- no: Det blir kanskje færre hvis det regner.
- en: There may be fewer if it rains.
- no: Hun kommer kanskje senere.
- en: Perhaps she is coming later.
- no: Vi kan kanskje flytte konserten inn.
- en: We could perhaps move the concert indoors.
:::

Kanskje does not explain why the speaker is uncertain. The uncertainty might come from missing information, an undecided plan, or simple caution.

Its position can change the sentence structure. When kanskje appears inside the sentence, normal main-clause word order is common:

::: examples
- no: Vi kan kanskje låne et telt.
- en: Perhaps we can borrow a tent.
:::

When kanskje begins a main clause, two patterns are accepted:

::: examples
- no: Kanskje kan vi låne et telt.
- en: Perhaps we can borrow a tent.
- no: Kanskje vi kan låne et telt.
- en: Perhaps we can borrow a tent.
:::

The first pattern uses verb–subject order after fronted kanskje. The second uses sentence-initial kanskje followed by subject–verb order. Both are accepted Bokmål constructions, and the choice can reflect style or emphasis.

**Checkpoint: identifying open uncertainty**

Return to the concert preparations. Read several short updates about weather, attendance, transport, and equipment. For each update, rewrite one definite claim so that it becomes an open possibility with kanskje.

This activity belongs here because kanskje is the broadest hedge in the chapter. Starting with it lets you practise reducing certainty before distinguishing more specific kinds of stance. A successful response would use natural main-clause word order and would make the information sound possible rather than established.

## Making an inference with nok {#making-an-inference-with-nok role=model objectives=obj-hedging-stance-markers-01}

In Maja’s reply, **nok** does not mean enough:

::: examples
- no: Det gjør det nok.
- en: It probably will.
:::

Here, nok marks an inference or expectation. The speaker has some reason for believing the statement, but does not claim complete certainty. Maja has looked at the weather forecast and concludes that rain is probable.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Det regner kanskje på lørdag.
- en: Perhaps it will rain on Saturday.
- no: Det regner nok på lørdag.
- en: It will probably rain on Saturday.
:::

Kanskje presents rain as one possibility. Nok suggests that the speaker considers it the likely conclusion.

More examples:

::: examples
- no: Bussen er nok forsinket på grunn av snøen.
- en: The bus is probably delayed because of the snow.
- no: Sara har nok glemt møtet.
- en: Sara has probably forgotten the meeting.
- no: De finner nok en løsning.
- en: They will probably find a solution.
:::

Nok often appears after the finite verb in a main clause:

::: examples
- no: Han kommer nok snart.
- en: He will probably come soon.
- no: Det blir nok dyrere enn vi tror.
- en: It will probably be more expensive than we think.
:::

Be careful not to confuse stance-marking nok with quantity-marking nok:

::: examples
- no: Vi har nok stoler.
- en: We have enough chairs.
- no: Vi får nok flere stoler i morgen.
- en: We will probably get more chairs tomorrow.
:::

In the first sentence, nok modifies the quantity of chairs. In the second, it marks the speaker’s expectation about what will happen.

**Checkpoint: possibility or inference**

Use short situations with visible evidence: dark clouds, an unanswered message, a delayed train, or lights in an office late at night. Form one cautious conclusion for each situation. Choose perhaps-level uncertainty or a probable inference, and make the wording show the difference.

This activity follows kanskje because the learner must now decide not only whether a claim is uncertain, but also whether there is evidence supporting a likely conclusion. A successful response would use kanskje for an open possibility and nok for a reasoned expectation.

## Appealing to shared assumptions with jo {#appealing-to-shared-assumptions-with-jo role=model objectives=obj-hedging-stance-markers-01}

The marker **jo** often signals that the information should already be known, obvious, or accepted by both speakers.

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger jo bare et ganske lite telt.
- en: After all, we only need a fairly small tent.
:::

Maja uses jo to remind Henrik of a fact that supports her argument. Depending on context, English may use after all, as you know, of course, or no separate word at all.

::: examples
- no: Du kjenner jo Ingrid.
- en: You know Ingrid, after all.
- no: Det er jo søndag, så butikkene stenger tidlig.
- en: It is Sunday, of course, so the shops close early.
- no: Vi kan jo spørre naboen.
- en: We could always ask the neighbour.
:::

The last example presents a suggestion as readily available or already implicit in the situation. Jo can make conversation sound connected and cooperative, but it may also sound impatient if the supposed shared knowledge is not actually shared.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Møtet begynner klokka ni.
- en: The meeting starts at nine.
- no: Møtet begynner jo klokka ni.
- en: The meeting starts at nine, as you know.
:::

The second sentence may be a friendly reminder. With sharp intonation, it could also suggest: You should already know this.

A common mistake is to treat jo as a direct equivalent of English yes. Norwegian does use jo as a reply to a negative question or statement, but that is a different function:

::: {.reading translation='Aren’t you coming?' dialogue_id='negative-question' speaker_id='lars' speaker_name="Lars" character_id="lars"}
Kommer du ikke?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, I am coming now.' dialogue_id='negative-question' speaker_id='nora' speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Jo, jeg kommer nå.
:::

In this chapter, focus on jo inside a sentence as a marker of shared or obvious information.

**Checkpoint: building on common ground**

Imagine that Henrik worries about borrowing a tent from the sports club. Write brief replies that remind him of facts both colleagues already know: they cooperate with the club, the club is nearby, and the concert benefits the local community.

This activity belongs here because jo is useful only when the context supports shared knowledge. A successful response would not merely insert jo mechanically; it would connect an argument or suggestion to information that both speakers can reasonably be expected to accept.

## Seeking agreement or softening a claim with vel {#seeking-agreement-or-softening-a-claim-with-vel role=contrast objectives=obj-hedging-stance-markers-01}

**Vel** often presents a statement as tentative while inviting the listener to agree. Its exact English translation depends heavily on context.

::: examples
- no: Vi bør vel bestille et ekstra telt?
- en: We should probably order an extra tent, shouldn’t we?
- no: Det er vel tryggest.
- en: That is probably safest.
- no: Hun kommer vel klokka seks.
- en: She is coming at six, I suppose.
- no: Du har vel med deg billettene?
- en: You have brought the tickets, haven’t you?
:::

Vel can combine uncertainty with an appeal for confirmation. The speaker leans toward a conclusion but leaves space for the listener to correct it.

Compare these aligned forms:

::: examples
- no: Vi bør bestille et telt.
- en: We should order a tent.
- no: Vi bør nok bestille et telt.
- en: We should probably order a tent.
- no: Vi bør vel bestille et telt?
- en: We should probably order a tent, shouldn’t we?
:::

The plain sentence gives direct advice. Nok marks a likely conclusion. Vel makes the conclusion tentative and often looks toward the listener for agreement.

Compare jo and vel:

::: examples
- no: Du kjenner jo lederen.
- en: You know the manager, after all.
- no: Du kjenner vel lederen?
- en: You know the manager, don’t you?
:::

Jo presents the information as shared. Vel checks or assumes it without claiming full certainty.

Because vel can imply that a particular answer is expected, it is not completely neutral. If you genuinely have no idea whether someone knows the manager, a direct question may be better:

::: examples
- no: Kjenner du lederen?
- en: Do you know the manager?
:::

**Checkpoint: shared fact or expected confirmation**

Use paired mini-situations involving Maja and Henrik. In one version, both clearly know a fact; in the other, one speaker merely expects the fact to be true. Write a suitable sentence for each version using jo or vel.

This checkpoint belongs immediately after both markers have been introduced because their meanings overlap around speaker–listener knowledge. A successful response would use jo for treated-as-shared information and vel for a tentative assumption or request for confirmation.

## Reporting uncertain information with visst and visstnok {#reporting-uncertain-information-with-visst-and-visstnok role=model objectives=obj-hedging-stance-markers-01}

Maja says that the tent costs five thousand kroner, but she does not present herself as the source:

::: examples
- no: Teltet koster visst fem tusen kroner.
- en: The tent apparently costs five thousand kroner.
:::

Henrik later says:

::: examples
- no: De har visst flere.
- en: They apparently have several.
:::

**Visst** can indicate that the speaker has heard or learned something indirectly. It often corresponds to apparently or I understand that. The speaker passes on the information without fully guaranteeing it.

::: examples
- no: Kari har visst fått ny jobb.
- en: Apparently, Kari has got a new job.
- no: Veien er visst stengt.
- en: The road is apparently closed.
- no: De skal visst flytte til Bergen.
- en: They are apparently going to move to Bergen.
:::

**Visstnok** also marks reported information and often makes the distance from the claim especially clear. It can suggest reportedly, supposedly, or according to what I have heard.

::: examples
- no: Det kommer visstnok rundt to hundre mennesker.
- en: Apparently, around two hundred people are coming.
- no: Restauranten er visstnok en av de beste i byen.
- en: The restaurant is reportedly one of the best in the city.
- no: Denne bygningen er visstnok over tre hundre år gammel.
- en: This building is supposedly more than three hundred years old.
:::

In many contexts, visst and visstnok are close. Visst is very common in conversational Bokmål, while visstnok can place slightly more emphasis on the reported or unverified nature of the information.

Do not confuse stance-marking visst with **visst** meaning certainly in more formal or fixed uses, or with **viss** meaning certain or particular:

::: examples
- no: Det er visst en feil i programmet.
- en: Apparently, there is an error in the software.
- no: Under visse forhold fungerer programmet bedre.
- en: Under certain conditions, the software works better.
:::

The first sentence reports uncertain information. In the second, visse is an inflected form of viss and identifies particular conditions.

There is also an important difference between visst and nok:

::: examples
- no: Veien er visst stengt.
- en: Apparently, the road is closed.
- no: Veien er nok stengt.
- en: The road is probably closed.
:::

With visst, the speaker has apparently received the information from somewhere. With nok, the speaker reaches a likely conclusion, perhaps after seeing a barrier or a line of stationary cars.

**Checkpoint: report or inference**

Work with short pieces of context. Some should provide an indirect source, such as a colleague’s message, a local rumour, or an online notice. Others should provide observable evidence, such as barriers across a road or a dark, empty building. State the likely situation using a suitable stance marker.

This activity belongs here because source-based uncertainty and inference can produce similar English translations but express different reasoning in Norwegian. A successful response would use visst or visstnok when passing on information and nok when drawing a conclusion from evidence.

{{exercise: report_or_inference_checkpoint}}

## Marking personal opinion with synes, mener, and etter min mening {#marking-personal-opinion-with-synes-mener-and-etter-min-mening role=model objectives=obj-hedging-stance-markers-01}

Henrik reacts to the price with a personal evaluation:

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes det høres dyrt ut.
- en: I think it sounds expensive.
:::

**Synes** is common when expressing an opinion, impression, preference, or evaluation.

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes planen virker fornuftig.
- en: I think the plan seems sensible.
- no: Vi synes konserten var vellykket.
- en: We think the concert was successful.
- no: Synes du dette er en god løsning?
- en: Do you think this is a good solution?
:::

By framing the evaluation as personal, synes leaves room for another person to disagree. It is therefore a useful stance marker in discussion.

**Mener** can also mean think, but it commonly presents a more considered belief, position, or claim.

::: examples
- no: Jeg mener at kommunen bør støtte arrangementet.
- en: I believe that the municipality should support the event.
- no: Henrik mener at teltet er for dyrt.
- en: Henrik believes that the tent is too expensive.
:::

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes lokalet er hyggelig.
- en: I think the venue is pleasant.
- no: Jeg mener at lokalet er det beste alternativet.
- en: I believe that the venue is the best option.
:::

The first is a personal impression. The second sounds more like a position that could be supported with reasons.

**Etter min mening** means in my opinion. It explicitly labels the statement as the speaker’s view and is useful when contrasting positions.

::: examples
- no: Etter min mening bør vi flytte konserten inn.
- en: In my opinion, we should move the concert indoors.
- no: Dette er, etter min mening, den sikreste løsningen.
- en: This is, in my opinion, the safest solution.
:::

Do not pile up opinion markers without a reason:

Less natural:

::: examples
- no: Etter min mening synes jeg at planen er god.
- en: In my opinion, I think that the plan is good.
:::

Usually choose one clear frame:

::: examples
- no: Etter min mening er planen god.
- en: In my opinion, the plan is good.
- no: Jeg synes planen er god.
- en: I think the plan is good.
:::

A stance marker does not automatically make a forceful statement polite. Tone and wording still matter. Compare:

::: examples
- no: Dette er en dårlig idé.
- en: This is a bad idea.
- no: Jeg synes dette er en dårlig idé.
- en: I think this is a bad idea.
- no: Jeg synes kanskje vi bør vurdere en annen løsning.
- en: I think perhaps we should consider another solution.
:::

The final version combines personal opinion and uncertainty to create more room for discussion. It is softer, but its communicative purpose remains clear.

**Checkpoint: presenting a view without turning it into fact**

Continue the planning meeting. Give Maja and Henrik different reactions to the price, location, expected attendance, and weather plan. Express each reaction as a personal evaluation or considered position.

This activity belongs here because discussion requires speakers to separate their own judgments from externally verifiable information. A successful response would use synes naturally for impressions or evaluations and mener or etter min mening for a more explicit position.

## Giving approximate amounts {#giving-approximate-amounts role=model objectives=obj-hedging-stance-markers-01}

Several expressions make quantities less exact. In the opening dialogue, Maja says:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer visstnok rundt to hundre mennesker.
- en: Apparently, around two hundred people are coming.
:::

**Rundt** means around or approximately before numbers and quantities:

::: examples
- no: Det tar rundt en halvtime.
- en: It takes around half an hour.
- no: Vi trenger rundt femti stoler.
- en: We need around fifty chairs.
- no: Billetten koster rundt fire hundre kroner.
- en: The ticket costs around four hundred kroner.
:::

**Omtrent** also means approximately and is neutral in register:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer omtrent to hundre mennesker.
- en: Approximately two hundred people are coming.
- no: Turen er omtrent ti kilometer lang.
- en: The walk is approximately ten kilometres long.
:::

**Cirka**, often written **ca.** in practical texts, is another common equivalent:

::: examples
- no: Møtet varer cirka én time.
- en: The meeting lasts about one hour.
:::

In informal conversation, **sånn** can introduce a rough example, size, number, or category:

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger et telt sånn til tjue personer.
- en: We need a tent for something like twenty people.
- no: Det tar sånn fem–ti minutter.
- en: It takes something like five to ten minutes.
- no: Hun er sånn rundt tretti.
- en: She is, like, around thirty.
:::

Sånn is conversational and deliberately loose. It is useful when precision is unnecessary, but it can sound vague in formal reports or technical instructions. In those settings, omtrent, cirka, or a precise figure is usually more suitable.

Avoid unnecessary stacking in neutral prose:

Overloaded:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer kanskje sånn cirka rundt to hundre mennesker.
- en: 'Not: Perhaps something like around two hundred people are coming. (in this situation)'
:::

Clearer alternatives:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer kanskje rundt to hundre mennesker.
- en: Perhaps around two hundred people are coming.
- no: Det kommer visstnok cirka to hundre mennesker.
- en: Apparently, about two hundred people are coming.
:::

Multiple markers can be natural when they do different jobs. In the second sentence, visstnok marks uncertainty about the source, while cirka marks approximation of the number. The sentence communicates both: the report is not fully confirmed, and the reported figure is not exact.

**Checkpoint: exact, approximate, or informally rough**

Use details from an event plan: audience size, ticket price, walking distance, setup time, and tent capacity. Restate each figure with a degree of precision suited to the situation. Include both neutral planning language and informal conversation between Maja and Henrik.

This activity belongs here because approximation is another way of limiting commitment. A successful response would distinguish neutral rundt, omtrent, or cirka from informal sånn and would avoid combining several approximation markers without purpose.

## Combining stance markers naturally {#combining-stance-markers-naturally role=contrast objectives=obj-hedging-stance-markers-01}

Norwegian speakers often use more than one marker because each contributes different information. The key is to understand the job of each one.

::: examples
- no: Det kommer kanskje rundt hundre personer.
- en: Perhaps around one hundred people are coming.
:::

Kanskje marks uncertainty about whether that expectation is correct. Rundt marks the number as approximate.

::: examples
- no: De har visst et telt til sånn tretti personer.
- en: Apparently, they have a tent for something like thirty people.
:::

Visst marks indirect information. Sånn makes the capacity informally approximate.

::: examples
- no: Vi bør vel kanskje ringe dem først.
- en: Perhaps we should probably call them first, shouldn’t we?
:::

Both vel and kanskje soften the proposal. This combination is possible in conversation, but too many hedges can make a speaker sound hesitant. Often one is enough:

::: examples
- no: Vi bør vel ringe dem først.
- en: We should probably call them first, shouldn’t we?
- no: Kanskje vi bør ringe dem først.
- en: Perhaps we should call them first.
:::

Notice that the second example uses the accepted sentence-initial kanskje followed by subject–verb order: kanskje vi bør. It follows the same pattern as Kanskje vi kan låne et telt above.

Markers can also reveal different relationships to the same claim:

::: examples
- no: Konserten blir kanskje avlyst.
- en: Perhaps the concert will be cancelled.
- no: Konserten blir nok avlyst.
- en: The concert will probably be cancelled.
- no: Konserten blir visst avlyst.
- en: Apparently, the concert is going to be cancelled.
- no: Konserten blir vel avlyst?
- en: The concert is going to be cancelled, I suppose?
- no: Konserten blir jo avlyst.
- en: The concert is being cancelled, as we both know.
:::

These are not interchangeable decorations. Each version positions the speaker differently toward the same event.

**Checkpoint: changing only the stance**

Take one basic statement about the concert, such as the expected attendance, the need for a tent, or the chance of cancellation. Recast it several times so that it becomes an open possibility, an inference, reported information, a shared assumption, and a tentative request for agreement.

This checkpoint belongs here because it isolates stance from factual content. Keeping the event constant makes the pragmatic contrast easier to hear. A successful response would preserve the basic situation while changing the speaker’s relationship to the claim through the marker chosen.

## Back at the cultural centre {#back-at-the-cultural-centre role=model objectives=obj-hedging-stance-markers-01}

Later that afternoon, Henrik calls the sports club. He then returns to Maja with an update.

::: {.reading translation='Apparently, the sports club has a tent we can borrow.' dialogue_id='back-at-centre' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Idrettslaget har visst et telt vi kan låne.
:::

::: {.reading translation='That’s great! It is large enough, I suppose?' dialogue_id='back-at-centre' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Så bra! Det er vel stort nok?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, it is for something like thirty people. It will probably be enough.' dialogue_id='back-at-centre' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Ja, det er sånn til tretti personer. Det holder nok.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Perhaps we should collect it on Friday, if that suits them.' dialogue_id='back-at-centre' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Vi bør kanskje hente det på fredag, hvis det passer for dem.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Agreed. After all, we have to put it up before the sound check.' dialogue_id='back-at-centre' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Enig. Vi må jo sette det opp før lydprøven.
:::

::: {.reading translation='I think this turned into a much better solution.' dialogue_id='back-at-centre' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jeg synes dette ble en mye bedre løsning.
:::

The second dialogue reuses the same forms, but the situation has moved forward. Henrik now has indirect information from the club, so visst is appropriate. Maja expects the tent to be large enough and invites confirmation with vel. Henrik gives an informal capacity with sånn and makes an inference with nok. Maja offers a softened proposal with kanskje, and Henrik supports it with shared scheduling knowledge marked by jo. Finally, Maja clearly labels her positive evaluation with synes.

**Integrated practice: reconstructing the planning conversation**

Create a short written exchange between Maja and Henrik after a new problem arises: the delivery van may be unavailable, another volunteer has heard that the sound equipment is delayed, and the walk from the car park takes about ten minutes. Keep the exchange focused on how certain each person is and where the information comes from.

This activity belongs near the end because it brings all target functions back into one believable context without changing the communicative objective. A successful exchange would distinguish possibility, inference, shared assumptions, reported information, personal opinion, and approximation. It would also avoid using every marker in every turn; each marker should have a clear reason to be present.

## Common problems to watch for {#common-problems-to-watch-for role=contrast objectives=obj-hedging-stance-markers-01}

Do not use perhaps-level uncertainty when you mean reported information. Before correcting the example, recall that kanskje presents an open possibility, while visst indicates information heard or learned indirectly.

Correction task: A colleague has sent a message saying that Kari will come later, but a speaker says:

::: examples
- no: Kari kommer kanskje senere.
- en: Perhaps Kari is coming later.
:::

Identify the contextual cue that makes the marker mismatched, replace the marker, and state how the repair changes the source or certainty meaning.

{{exercise: common_problems_marker_repair}}

Repaired utterance:

::: examples
- no: Kari kommer visst senere.
- en: Apparently, Kari is coming later.
:::

With kanskje, her arrival is presented as possible. With visst, the speaker passes on information heard or learned from another source.

Do not use jo unless the information can reasonably be treated as shared or obvious:

::: examples
- no: Du bor jo i Tromsø.
- en: You live in Tromsø, as we both know.
:::

If you are not sure where the person lives, use a question or vel:

::: examples
- no: Du bor vel i Tromsø?
- en: You live in Tromsø, don’t you?
:::

Do not confuse a likely inference with certainty:

::: examples
- no: Han er nok hjemme.
- en: He is probably at home.
:::

Nok leaves room for the conclusion to be wrong.

Do not make informal approximation unnecessarily formal, or formal reporting unnecessarily vague:

::: examples
- no: Budsjettet er på omtrent 80 000 kroner.
- en: The budget is approximately 80,000 kroner.
- no: Det blir sånn åtti tusen, tror jeg.
- en: It will be something like eighty thousand, I think.
:::

This suits a neutral planning discussion.

This suits informal conversation but is less appropriate as an official budget figure.

Do not assume that more stance marking always means better Norwegian. A sentence crowded with markers can obscure the message. Choose the marker that shows the intended relationship to the claim. Some markers reduce certainty or precision by indicating open possibility, inference, an indirect source, personal judgment, or approximation. Jo instead treats information as shared, obvious, or already accepted and can make the assertion more insistent.

Include one uncertain possibility, one inference based on evidence, one piece of indirectly reported information, one reminder of shared knowledge, one tentative assumption seeking agreement, one personal opinion, and one approximate amount. Keep the update natural and connected rather than presenting separate example sentences. A successful response would make the source and strength of each claim clear through the stance markers.
