The problem changes several times during this short scene. First, darkness produces a consequence: Mina cannot see the keypad digits. Then Mina asks whether the light reaches the necessary level for seeing the digits and entering the code they were given. After Jonas retrieves the key, Mina describes her cold as beyond an acceptable limit, while the heavy door is presented as leading to another consequence. Finally, she says that Jonas has enough strength to succeed.

Norwegian uses three closely related patterns for these meaning choices:

- **så … at** presents a degree and its consequence;
- **for … til å** presents a degree that prevents or excludes an action;
- **… nok til å** presents a degree that meets the threshold for an action.

The three patterns often describe similar situations, but they do not organize the message in the same way.

## A consequence: så … at {#sec-consequence-saa-at role=model objectives=obj-degree-result-and-threshold-constructions-01}

Use **så** before an adjective or adverb and follow it with an **at-clause** when you want to present a consequence or result. The result clause can describe an event, response, modal possibility, or hypothetical result.

::: examples
- no: Det er så mørkt at Mina ikke kan se tallene.
- en: It is so dark that Mina cannot see the numbers.
- no: Døra er så tung at Jonas må bruke begge hendene.
- en: The door is so heavy that Jonas has to use both hands.
- no: Jonas snakker så lavt at Mina ikke hører ham.
- en: Jonas speaks so quietly that Mina cannot hear him.
:::

The basic pattern is:

**så + adjective/adverb + at + complete clause**

The part after **at** contains its own subject and a finite verb: **Mina kan**, **Jonas må**, **Mina hører**. It tells us the result of the high or low degree described before it.

Compare two possible consequences of the same condition:

::: examples
- no: Det er så kaldt at vannet fryser.
- en: It is so cold that the water freezes.
- no: Det er så kaldt at vi går inn.
- en: It is so cold that we go inside.
:::

The first sentence describes a physical result. The second describes a human response. The grammar does not require the consequence to be automatic. It only presents the consequence as connected to the degree.

The result can be positive or negative:

::: examples
- no: Filmen var så morsom at alle lo.
- en: The film was so funny that everyone laughed.
- no: Forklaringen var så uklar at ingen forsto oppgaven.
- en: The explanation was so unclear that nobody understood the task.
- no: Hun sang så fint at publikum ble helt stille.
- en: She sang so beautifully that the audience became completely quiet.
:::

**Compact rule**

::: {.rule}
Choose **så … at** when your main point is: **This degree is presented as leading to this consequence or result.**
:::

A common mistake is to treat **så** as if it already meant English *too*. It does not. **Så kald** means *so cold*, not automatically *too cold*. The consequence must show what the degree leads to.

::: examples
- no: Det er så kaldt at jeg tar på meg en jakke.
- en: It is so cold that I put on a jacket.
:::

This does not say that the cold prevents an action. It says that the cold causes a response.

**Context:** Mina and Jonas are still outside the cabin, dealing with darkness, cold, the lockbox, and the heavy door.

**Learner action:** Examine short descriptions from the scene and identify which result is presented as following from the first condition. Then restate each connection with **så … at**.

**Success would demonstrate:** The learner recognizes that the scored feature is a stated or presented consequence expressed in a complete **at-clause**. The result may be factual, modal, or hypothetical; choice of positive or negative consequence is supporting context, not a separate scored goal.

## A limit that blocks something: for … til å {#sec-limit-for-til-aa role=model objectives=obj-degree-result-and-threshold-constructions-01}

Use **for** before an adjective or adverb when the degree goes beyond a useful, safe, possible, or acceptable limit. The action after **til å** is in the **infinitive (infinitiv)**.

::: examples
- no: Mina er for kald til å vente ute.
- en: Mina is too cold to wait outside.
- no: Det er for mørkt til å lese koden uten lys.
- en: It is too dark to read the code without a light.
- no: Jonas snakker for lavt til å bli hørt gjennom døra.
- en: Jonas speaks too quietly to be heard through the door.
:::

The basic pattern is:

**for + adjective/adverb + til å + infinitive**

The degree prevents the action, makes it unsuitable, or places it outside a practical limit.

::: examples
- no: Suppa er for varm til å spise nå.
- en: The soup is too hot to eat now.
- no: Bussen kjører for fort til å stoppe her.
- en: The bus is going too fast to stop here.
- no: Jeg er for trøtt til å kjøre hjem.
- en: I am too tired to drive home.
:::

In the last sentence, tiredness blocks a reasonable or safe action. The speaker is not merely reporting that something happened because of tiredness. The speaker is judging the action against a limit.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er så trøtt at jeg legger meg.
- en: I am so tired that I go to bed.
- no: Jeg er for trøtt til å kjøre.
- en: I am too tired to drive.
:::

The first sentence presents a consequence: I go to bed. The second says that driving is not possible or advisable at this degree of tiredness.

The action after **til å** is usually understood in relation to the person or situation already mentioned:

::: examples
- no: Hun er for nervøs til å holde talen.
- en: She is too nervous to give the speech.
- no: Vi kom for sent til å ta det siste toget.
- en: We arrived too late to take the last train.
- no: Rommet er for lite til å bruke som kontor.
- en: The room is too small to use as an office.
:::

**The important meaning of for**

In this pattern, **for** means *too*, not *very*.

::: examples
- no: Kaffen er for sterk til å drikke uten melk.
- en: The coffee is too strong to drink without milk.
:::

This sentence does not simply emphasize that the coffee is strong. It says that its strength crosses a limit for drinking it without milk.

A frequent English-influenced mistake is to add **at** directly after this use of **for**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er for sliten til å lage middag.
- en: I am too tired to make dinner.
:::

The target pattern uses **til å + infinitive**, not **at + complete clause**. If you want a complete consequence clause, change the structure and usually the meaning:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er så sliten at jeg ikke lager middag.
- en: I am so tired that I am not making dinner.
:::

The **så … at** sentence reports the consequence. The **for … til å** sentence evaluates dinner-making as beyond the speaker’s present capacity.

**Activity request: distinguish-result-from-excess**

**Purpose:** Separate a stated or presented consequence from a degree that blocks an intended action.

**Context:** After entering the cabin, Mina and Jonas encounter an overheated stove, a heavy suitcase, weak phone batteries, and a late hour.

**Learner action:** For each situation, decide whether the message should present a result or say that an action is prevented. Express presented consequences, including modal or hypothetical ones, with **så … at** and blocked actions with **for … til å**.

## Reaching the necessary level: nok til å {#sec-nok-til-aa role=model objectives=obj-degree-result-and-threshold-constructions-01}

Use **nok** when a person, object, or situation reaches the necessary threshold. With an adjective or adverb, **nok** normally comes after that adjective or adverb.

::: examples
- no: Lyset er sterkt nok til å se tallene og taste inn koden.
- en: The light is strong enough to see the digits and enter the code.
- no: Jonas er sterk nok til å åpne døra.
- en: Jonas is strong enough to open the door.
- no: Mina snakker tydelig nok til å bli forstått.
- en: Mina speaks clearly enough to be understood.
:::

The basic pattern is:

**adjective/adverb + nok + til å + infinitive**

This word order deserves attention:

::: examples
- no: Batteriet er fullt nok til å vare hele kvelden.
- en: The battery is full enough to last all evening.
- no: Hun løper fort nok til å rekke bussen.
- en: She runs fast enough to catch the bus.
- no: Bordet er stort nok til åtte personer.
- en: The table is big enough for eight people.
:::

The last example has a noun phrase after **til**, not an infinitive. Our focus here is the action pattern **nok til å**, as in **stort nok til å bruke**. The central meaning remains the same: the required threshold is reached.

Compare excess and sufficiency:

::: examples
- no: Sekken er for tung til å bære alene.
- en: The backpack is too heavy to carry alone.
- no: Jeg er sterk nok til å bære sekken alene.
- en: I am strong enough to carry the backpack alone.
:::

The first sentence says that the backpack crosses a negative limit. The second says that the person reaches a positive threshold.

Now compare two descriptions of the same practical problem:

::: examples
- no: Hylla er for høy til å nå fra gulvet.
- en: The shelf is too high to reach from the floor.
- no: Stigen er høy nok til å nå hylla.
- en: The ladder is high enough to reach the shelf.
:::

The focus changes. In the first sentence, the shelf’s height blocks the action. In the second, the ladder’s height enables it.

**Negative sufficiency: ikke … nok til å**

Adding **ikke** means that the required threshold is not reached.

::: examples
- no: Lyset er ikke sterkt nok til å lese den lille teksten.
- en: The light is not strong enough to read the small text.
- no: Jeg snakker ikke godt nok norsk til å forstå alt.
- en: I do not speak Norwegian well enough to understand everything.
- no: Rommet er ikke varmt nok til å sove i ennå.
- en: The room is not warm enough to sleep in yet.
:::

This often describes nearly the same practical outcome as **for … til å**, but from a different direction:

::: examples
- no: Vannet er for kaldt til å bade i.
- en: The water is too cold to swim in.
- no: Vannet er ikke varmt nok til å bade i.
- en: The water is not warm enough to swim in.
:::

Both sentences say that swimming is not suitable. The first places the water beyond the cold limit. The second says it has not reached the required warmth.

Another aligned contrast:

::: examples
- no: Musikken er for lav til å høre fra kjøkkenet.
- en: The music is too quiet to hear from the kitchen.
- no: Musikken er ikke høy nok til å høre fra kjøkkenet.
- en: The music is not loud enough to hear from the kitchen.
:::

The practical conclusion is similar, but the chosen scale differs: excessive quietness versus insufficient loudness.

**Compact rule**

::: {.rule}
Choose **… nok til å** when your main point is: **The degree reaches the threshold needed for this action.** Choose **ikke … nok til å** when it fails to reach that threshold.
:::

A common word-order mistake is to place **nok** before the adjective by copying English. Norwegian normally says:

::: examples
- no: Hun er gammel nok til å reise alene.
- en: She is old enough to travel alone.
:::

Not **nok gammel** in this target construction.

**Activity request: place-nok-at-threshold**

**Purpose:** Build the Norwegian order **adjective/adverb + nok + til å** while keeping attention on sufficiency.

**Context:** Mina and Jonas check whether the cabin has enough light, heat, space, battery power, and mobile reception for their evening plans.

**Learner action:** Turn short threshold judgments into complete Norwegian sentences. Place **nok** after the relevant adjective or adverb and connect the enabled action with **til å**.

**Success would demonstrate:** The learner constructs the sufficiency pattern with correct placement of **nok**. Whether the situation concerns light, warmth, space, or another quality is contextual rather than separately scored.

{{exercise: place_nok_at_threshold}}

**Activity request: contrast-excess-and-insufficiency**

**Purpose:** Show two perspectives on a failed threshold without confusing their form.

**Context:** Everyday tasks at the cabin fail because objects or conditions are beyond one limit or below another: water temperature, shelf height, sound level, and available light.

## One situation, three different messages {#sec-one-situation-three-messages role=contrast objectives=obj-degree-result-and-threshold-constructions-01}

Consider a crowded living room during a birthday party.

::: examples
- no: Det er så fullt at noen må stå i gangen.
- en: It is so crowded that some people have to stand in the hallway.
:::

This sentence reports a consequence.

::: examples
- no: Det er for fullt til å sette inn flere stoler.
- en: It is too crowded to put in more chairs.
:::

This sentence says that the degree of crowding blocks an action.

::: examples
- no: Kjøkkenet er stort nok til å bruke som ekstra rom.
- en: The kitchen is big enough to use as an extra room.
:::

This sentence says that the kitchen meets the necessary threshold for another action.

The pattern is not selected only by the adjective. **Fullt**, **stort**, **kaldt**, or **sterkt** can appear in different constructions. Select the pattern according to what you want to say about the degree.

**Consequence**

::: examples
- no: Musikken er så høy at naboene kan høre den.
- en: The music is so loud that the neighbors can hear it.
:::

**Excess**

::: examples
- no: Musikken er for høy til å snakke normalt.
- en: The music is too loud to talk normally.
:::

**Sufficiency**

::: examples
- no: Musikken er høy nok til å høre ute på terrassen.
- en: The music is loud enough to hear out on the terrace.
:::

In all three examples, the music is loud. What changes is the relationship between loudness and the following event or action.

The new dialogue repeats the three meaning choices without repeating the original problem. Warmth is presented as producing a visible result, excessive heat blocks eating, and sufficient warmth and light enable practical actions. In **Jeg er så sulten at jeg kunne spist alt sammen**, **kunne spist** presents a modal, hyperbolic result rather than an actual eating event.

Notice that the same general scale can support all three patterns:

::: examples
- no: Ovnen er så varm at rommet blir varmt fort.
- en: The heater is so warm that the room becomes warm quickly.
- no: Ovnen er for varm til å ta på.
- en: The heater is too hot to touch.
- no: Ovnen er varm nok til å varme opp hele rommet.
- en: The heater is warm enough to heat the entire room.
:::

Do not begin by asking which construction “belongs” to **varm**. Ask what relationship the speaker wants:

- What consequence or result is presented, whether factual, modal, or hypothetical?
- What action is blocked because a limit is crossed?
- What action becomes possible because a threshold is reached?

## Common mistakes in connected sentences {#sec-common-mistakes-connected-sentences role=contrast objectives=obj-degree-result-and-threshold-constructions-01}

**Using så when the intended meaning is too**

::: examples
- no: Kofferten er for tung til å løfte.
- en: The suitcase is too heavy to lift.
:::

If you use **så**, you need to state a consequence:

::: examples
- no: Kofferten er så tung at jeg ikke klarer å løfte den.
- en: The suitcase is so heavy that I cannot lift it.
:::

These sentences are close in practical meaning, but their structures are different. The first evaluates lifting against a limit. The second presents a result.

**Following så with an infinitive**

After **så … at**, use a complete clause:

::: examples
- no: Hun snakker så fort at jeg ikke forstår henne.
- en: She speaks so fast that I do not understand her.
:::

After **for … til å** or **nok til å**, use an infinitive:

::: examples
- no: Hun snakker for fort til å bli forstått.
- en: She speaks too fast to be understood.
- no: Hun snakker tydelig nok til å bli forstått.
- en: She speaks clearly enough to be understood.
:::

**Putting nok before the adjective**

::: examples
- no: Bilen er stor nok til å ta med all bagasjen.
- en: The car is big enough to take all the luggage.
:::

In this pattern, **nok** follows **stor**. The same order appears with adverbs:

::: examples
- no: Han kjører sakte nok til å se skiltene.
- en: He drives slowly enough to see the signs.
:::

**Treating for … til å as a stated-result construction**

::: examples
- no: De kom for sent til å kjøpe billetter.
- en: They arrived too late to buy tickets.
:::

This says that their late arrival prevented the purchase. To present a consequence or result, use **så … at**:

::: examples
- no: De kom så sent at billettkontoret var stengt.
- en: They arrived so late that the ticket office was closed.
:::

**Activity request: select-pattern-from-intended-message**

**Purpose:** Retrieve all three patterns by meaning rather than by a familiar adjective.

**Context:** Short everyday situations involve missing transport, preparing food, hearing announcements, carrying luggage, and finding a place to work.

**Learner action:** Read the intended message in each situation—stated or presented consequence, blocked action, or reached threshold—and produce one sentence with the matching construction. A presented consequence may be factual, modal, or hypothetical.

**Success would demonstrate:** The learner maps a stated or presented consequence to **så … at**, excess to **for … til å**, and sufficiency to **… nok til å** without relying on a particular vocabulary item.

**Purpose:** Correct confusion between a complete **at-clause** and **til å + infinitive**.

**Context:** A traveler has written short messages about a delayed train, a heavy bag, a quiet announcement, and enough time to buy food.

**Success would demonstrate:** The learner selects **at + complete clause** for consequence and **til å + infinitive** for excess or sufficiency. This activity scores complement-pattern alignment, not the learner’s ability to invent a new ending.

## Short retrieval and review {#sec-short-retrieval-and-review role=recap objectives=obj-degree-result-and-threshold-constructions-01}

**Activity request: retrieve-three-degree-patterns**

Without looking back, recall how Norwegian expresses:

1. a degree followed by a stated or presented consequence;
2. a degree beyond the limit for an action;
3. a degree that reaches the threshold for an action.

The stated or presented consequence may be factual, modal, or hypothetical. Produce one brief Norwegian sentence for each meaning in a new everyday setting, such as cooking, studying, traveling, or working at home. A successful response uses **så … at**, **for … til å**, and **… nok til å** according to the intended relationship, with a complete clause after **at** and an infinitive after **til å**.
