## A volunteer day that nearly went wrong {#volunteer-day role=orient objectives=obj-discourse-connectors-01,obj-discourse-connectors-02}

Samir and Ingrid have organized a Saturday workday in their neighborhood garden. On Monday, they meet to prepare a short report for the local council.

::: {.reading translation="First, only six volunteers arrived." dialogue_id="garden-report" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Først kom det bare seks frivillige.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, and it was raining heavily. Nevertheless, we started as planned." dialogue_id="garden-report" speaker_id="samir" speaker_name="Samir"}
Ja, og det regnet kraftig. Likevel begynte vi som planlagt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then the school class arrived. In addition, the teacher had brought tools." dialogue_id="garden-report" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Deretter kom skoleklassen. I tillegg hadde læreren med seg verktøy.
:::

::: {.reading translation="So we had enough people, but we still lacked paint." dialogue_id="garden-report" speaker_id="samir" speaker_name="Samir"}
Vi hadde altså nok folk, men vi manglet fortsatt maling.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Therefore, I called the hardware store. They could not deliver, but they gave us a discount." dialogue_id="garden-report" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Derfor ringte jeg byggevarebutikken. De kunne ikke levere, men de ga oss rabatt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Although the store was far away, Nora collected the paint." dialogue_id="garden-report" speaker_id="samir" speaker_name="Samir"}
Selv om butikken lå langt unna, hentet Nora malingen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then we painted the benches. Moreover, we repaired the fence." dialogue_id="garden-report" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Så malte vi benkene. Dessuten reparerte vi gjerdet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Finally, we cleaned the whole area. All in all, the day was very successful." dialogue_id="garden-report" speaker_id="samir" speaker_name="Samir"}
Til slutt ryddet vi hele området. Alt i alt ble dagen svært vellykket.
:::

The events are simple, but Ingrid and Samir do more than list them. They show sequence, add information, acknowledge problems, explain results, and draw a conclusion. The expressions that perform these jobs are **discourse connectors**. They help listeners understand how one idea relates to another.

This chapter focuses on using such connectors while maintaining Norwegian verb-second word order, usually called the **V2 rule (V2-regelen)**.

## Connectors and the V2 rule {#connectors-v2-rule role=model objectives=obj-discourse-connectors-01}

In a Norwegian main clause, the finite verb normally occupies the second position. When an adverbial connector taught here, such as *først*, *deretter*, *derfor*, or *likevel*, fills the first position, the subject normally comes after the finite verb. The conjunction *men* does not itself trigger inversion: subject–verb order follows when the subject is the coordinated clause’s first constituent, while a taught fronted adverbial after *men* still requires verb–subject order. The main-clause pattern does not apply inside subordinate clauses introduced by *fordi* or *selv om*. When an entire *selv om* clause comes first, V2 applies in the following main clause.

::: examples
- no: Først kom det bare seks frivillige.
- en: First, only six volunteers arrived.
:::

The connector *først* takes the first position. The finite verb *kom* follows it, and the subject comes afterward.

The pattern can be read as follows:

- First position: Først
- Finite verb: kom
- Subject: det
- Rest: bare seks frivillige.
- First position: Deretter
- Finite verb: kom
- Subject: skoleklassen
- Rest: til hagen.
- First position: Derfor
- Finite verb: ringte
- Subject: jeg
- Rest: byggevarebutikken.
- First position: Likevel
- Finite verb: begynte
- Subject: vi
- Rest: som planlagt.
- First position: Til slutt
- Finite verb: ryddet
- Subject: vi
- Rest: hele området.

A common error is to keep ordinary subject–verb order after a fronted connector.

Incorrect: *Derfor jeg ringte byggevarebutikken.*

::: examples
- no: ✗ Derfor jeg ringte byggevarebutikken.
- en: Therefore, I called the hardware store.
:::

Correct: Derfor ringte jeg byggevarebutikken.

::: examples
- no: Derfor ringte jeg byggevarebutikken.
- en: Therefore, I called the hardware store.
:::

Incorrect: *Likevel vi begynte som planlagt.*

::: examples
- no: ✗ Likevel vi begynte som planlagt.
- en: Nevertheless, we started as planned.
:::

Correct: Likevel begynte vi som planlagt.

::: examples
- no: Likevel begynte vi som planlagt.
- en: Nevertheless, we started as planned.
:::

For the fronted adverbial connectors taught here, think of the connector and the subject as competing for the main clause’s opening position. If the adverbial connector comes first, the finite verb follows it, then the subject. This shortcut still applies when a taught adverbial is fronted after *men*, but *men* itself does not occupy that opening position or trigger inversion. Do not apply the shortcut inside a clause introduced by *fordi* or *selv om*.

**Checkpoint: notice the sentence structure**

Return to Ingrid and Samir’s report. In sentences beginning with a fronted adverbial connector, mark the connector, the following finite verb, and the subject. Treat *Selv om butikken lå langt unna, hentet Nora malingen* separately: mark subordinate-clause subject *butikken* before finite verb *lå*, then mark main-clause finite verb *hentet* before subject *Nora*. Do not treat *men* as a fronted adverbial connector. This activity belongs here because recognizing these distinct patterns should come before producing them. A successful response would distinguish V2 in connector-initial main clauses from subject–verb order inside the *selv om* clause and inversion in the following main clause.

## Showing sequence {#showing-sequence role=model objectives=obj-discourse-connectors-01}

Sequence connectors guide the listener through events or stages.

**Først: first**

Use *først* for the opening event or step.

::: examples
- no: Først undersøkte vi gjerdet.
- en: First, we inspected the fence.
- no: Først må vi finne ut hvor mye arbeidet koster.
- en: First, we must find out how much the work costs.
:::

Because *først* comes first, the finite verbs *undersøkte* and *må* appear before the subjects.

**Deretter: then, after that**

*Deretter* clearly presents a later step.

::: examples
- no: Deretter reparerte elevene porten.
- en: After that, the pupils repaired the gate.
- no: Deretter skal kommunen kontrollere arbeidet.
- en: The council will then inspect the work.
:::

**Så: then**

*Så* is very common in speech and informal writing.

::: examples
- no: Så malte vi benkene.
- en: Then we painted the benches.
- no: Så tok alle en kort pause.
- en: Then everyone took a short break.
:::

When *så* means *then* and begins the clause, it is followed by V2 inversion: *Så tok alle …*, not *Så alle tok …*.

**Til slutt: finally, in the end**

Use *til slutt* for the last event or final stage.

::: examples
- no: Til slutt ryddet vi bort alt avfallet.
- en: Finally, we cleared away all the rubbish.
- no: Til slutt kunne barna bruke hagen igjen.
- en: In the end, the children could use the garden again.
:::

Sequence connectors can turn a loose list into an organized account:

::: examples
- no: Vi undersøkte gjerdet. Vi kjøpte materialer. Vi reparerte porten. Vi ryddet området.
- en: We inspected the fence. We bought materials. We repaired the gate. We cleaned the area.
:::

::: examples
- no: Først undersøkte vi gjerdet. Deretter kjøpte vi materialer. Så reparerte vi porten. Til slutt ryddet vi området.
- en: First, we inspected the fence. After that, we bought materials. Then we repaired the gate. Finally, we cleaned the area.
:::

The second version makes the progression much easier to follow.

**Checkpoint: build a clear timeline**

Retell the garden workday as a four-stage sequence. Begin each stage with an appropriate sequence connector and maintain V2 word order. This activity uses the chapter’s original situation so that attention can remain on organization and word order rather than new content. A successful response would present events in a logical order and place the finite verb directly after each fronted connector.

## Adding another point {#adding-another-point role=model objectives=obj-discourse-connectors-01}

Sometimes the next idea is not a later event. It is extra information supporting the same point. Ingrid says that the school class arrived and then adds that the teacher brought tools.

**I tillegg: in addition**

::: examples
- no: I tillegg hadde læreren med seg verktøy.
- en: In addition, the teacher had brought tools.
- no: I tillegg fikk vi gratis planter fra naboene.
- en: In addition, we received free plants from the neighbors.
- no: I tillegg trenger vi to nye søppelkasser.
- en: In addition, we need two new rubbish bins.
:::

The whole phrase *i tillegg* fills the first position. The verb still follows immediately:

::: examples
- no: I tillegg trenger vi mer maling.
- en: In addition, we need more paint.
:::

Not: *I tillegg vi trenger mer maling.*

**Dessuten: moreover, besides**

*Dessuten* also adds information. It often introduces another useful reason, advantage, or fact.

::: examples
- no: Dessuten reparerte vi gjerdet.
- en: Moreover, we repaired the fence.
- no: Hagen er tryggere nå. Dessuten ser den mye bedre ut.
- en: The garden is safer now. Moreover, it looks much better.
- no: Vi bør kjøpe den malingen. Den varer lenge, og dessuten er den miljøvennlig.
- en: We should buy that paint. It lasts a long time, and besides, it is environmentally friendly.
:::

Both *i tillegg* and *dessuten* signal addition. *Dessuten* often sounds like a speaker is strengthening an argument with one more point.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Skolen sendte tjue elever. I tillegg hadde læreren med seg verktøy.
- en: The school sent twenty pupils. In addition, the teacher brought tools.
:::

::: examples
- no: Kommunen bør støtte prosjektet. Det skaper et tryggere område. Dessuten lærer barna å dyrke mat.
- en: The council should support the project. It creates a safer area. Moreover, the children learn to grow food.
:::

**Checkpoint: strengthen the report**

You have already practiced *deretter* for a later event and *i tillegg* and *dessuten* for extra information. First add two relevant facts to Ingrid and Samir’s council report, one introduced by *i tillegg* and one by *dessuten*. Then respond to Samir’s turn, *Skoleklassen kom.* If Ingrid intends to present the teacher’s tools as extra supporting information rather than as a later event, choose *i tillegg* or *deretter* and produce her follow-up. State how choosing the alternative would change the listener’s interpretation from addition to sequence or from sequence to addition. This follows sequence practice because the learner now needs to distinguish extra information from a later event. A successful response would add coherent supporting details, select the connector matching Ingrid’s intended relationship, explain the alternative interpretation, and use verb–subject order after each fronted adverbial connector.

## Expressing contrast and concession {#expressing-contrast-concession role=contrast objectives=obj-discourse-connectors-02}

A contrast places different or opposing facts next to each other. A concession acknowledges a difficulty but shows that it did not prevent the main event.

In the dialogue, rain was a genuine problem:

::: examples
- no: Det regnet kraftig. Likevel begynte vi som planlagt.
- en: It was raining heavily. Nevertheless, we started as planned.
:::

The second sentence presents an unexpected result. Rain might normally stop outdoor work, but it did not.

**Men: but**

*Men* is a coordinating conjunction. It connects two main clauses, but it does not itself cause inversion.

::: examples
- no: Det regnet, men vi begynte som planlagt.
- en: It was raining, but we started as planned.
- no: Butikken kunne ikke levere, men den ga oss rabatt.
- en: The shop could not deliver, but it gave us a discount.
:::

After *men*, ordinary main-clause subject–verb order is common:

::: examples
- no: men vi begynte
- en: but we started
:::

Not: *men begynte vi* when no other element has been fronted.

**Likevel: nevertheless, even so, still**

*Likevel* highlights the unexpected nature of the following information. When it starts the sentence, V2 applies.

::: examples
- no: Det regnet. Likevel fortsatte alle å arbeide.
- en: It was raining. Nevertheless, everyone continued working.
- no: Vi hadde lite tid. Likevel rakk vi å male alle benkene.
- en: We had little time. Even so, we managed to paint all the benches.
:::

*Likevel* can also appear later in the clause:

::: examples
- no: Alle fortsatte likevel å arbeide.
- en: Everyone nevertheless continued working.
:::

Compare the word order:

::: examples
- no: Likevel fortsatte alle å arbeide.
- en: Nevertheless, everyone continued working.
- no: Alle fortsatte likevel å arbeide.
- en: Everyone continued working nevertheless.
:::

Both are correct. Only the first begins with the connector and therefore has verb–subject order.

**Derimot: by contrast, on the other hand**

Use *derimot* when two people, things, or situations differ.

::: examples
- no: De voksne var slitne. Barna hadde derimot masse energi.
- en: The adults were tired. The children, by contrast, had plenty of energy.
- no: Den gamle porten var ødelagt. Gjerdet var derimot i god stand.
- en: The old gate was broken. The fence, on the other hand, was in good condition.
:::

It can also be placed first:

::: examples
- no: Derimot var gjerdet i god stand.
- en: By contrast, the fence was in good condition.
:::

When fronted, *derimot* is followed by the finite verb *var*, then the subject *gjerdet*.

**Selv om: although, even though**

*Selv om* introduces a **subordinate clause (leddsetning)**. It presents a difficulty or contrasting fact that does not change the result.

::: examples
- no: Selv om butikken lå langt unna, hentet Nora malingen.
- en: Although the shop was far away, Nora collected the paint.
:::

The opening subordinate clause fills the first position of the following main clause. Therefore, the main-clause verb comes before its subject.

The pattern can be read as follows:

- Opening subordinate clause: Selv om butikken lå langt unna,
- Main-clause verb: hentet
- Subject: Nora
- Rest: malingen.
- Opening subordinate clause: Selv om det regnet,
- Main-clause verb: fortsatte
- Subject: vi
- Rest: arbeidet.
- Opening subordinate clause: Selv om alle var slitne,
- Main-clause verb: ryddet
- Subject: de
- Rest: hele området.

A frequent error is:

Incorrect: *Selv om det regnet, vi fortsatte arbeidet.*

::: examples
- no: ✗ Selv om det regnet, vi fortsatte arbeidet.
- en: Although it was raining, we continued the work.
:::

Correct: Selv om det regnet, fortsatte vi arbeidet.

::: examples
- no: Selv om det regnet, fortsatte vi arbeidet.
- en: Although it was raining, we continued the work.
:::

The entire *selv om* clause comes first. The following main clause must therefore begin with its finite verb.

The subordinate clause may also come second:

::: examples
- no: Vi fortsatte arbeidet selv om det regnet.
- en: We continued the work although it was raining.
:::

Here the main clause begins normally with the subject *vi*, so no inversion is needed.

**Aligned contrast**

These forms can describe the same situation, but they organize it differently.

::: examples
- no: Det regnet, men vi fortsatte arbeidet.
- en: It was raining, but we continued the work.
:::

::: examples
- no: Det regnet. Likevel fortsatte vi arbeidet.
- en: It was raining. Nevertheless, we continued the work.
:::

::: examples
- no: Selv om det regnet, fortsatte vi arbeidet.
- en: Although it was raining, we continued the work.
:::

With *men* in this example, the second clause has subject–verb order because the subject *vi* is its first constituent; *men* itself does not trigger inversion. If a taught adverbial is fronted after *men*, the verb still comes before the subject. With fronted *likevel*, the verb comes before the subject. When a *selv om* clause comes first, the following main clause also has verb–subject order.

**Checkpoint: present an obstacle and response**

Connect each problem to the action that followed, using one *likevel* construction and one sentence beginning with a *selv om* clause. Then add one direct contrast between two people, things, or situations from the workday using *men* or *derimot*. This activity belongs here because it distinguishes direct contrast from two ways of expressing concession while keeping focus on V2. A successful response would make each relationship clear, use *men* or *derimot* for direct contrast, and use inversion in the correct main clauses with *likevel* and an opening *selv om* clause.

## Linking cause and result {#linking-cause-result role=contrast objectives=obj-discourse-connectors-02}

A speaker can state a cause with *fordi* and present a result with *derfor*. They point in different directions.

**Fordi: because**

*Fordi* introduces the reason.

::: examples
- no: Jeg ringte butikken fordi vi manglet maling.
- en: I called the shop because we lacked paint.
- no: Vi tok en pause fordi alle var slitne.
- en: We took a break because everyone was tired.
:::

In these examples, the main information comes first, followed by its reason. *Fordi* introduces the subordinate-clause pattern taught here. In *fordi vi manglet maling* and *fordi alle var slitne*, the subordinate-clause subject comes before the finite verb. In the negative model below, *fordi butikken ikke kunne levere*, the order is subject *butikken*, negation *ikke*, then finite verb *kunne*.

**Derfor: therefore, that is why**

*Derfor* introduces the result.

::: examples
- no: Vi manglet maling. Derfor ringte jeg butikken.
- en: We lacked paint. Therefore, I called the hardware store.
- no: Alle var slitne. Derfor tok vi en pause.
- en: Everyone was tired. Therefore, we took a break.
:::

When *derfor* begins the result clause, the finite verb comes before the subject:

::: examples
- no: Derfor tok vi en pause.
- en: Therefore, we took a break.
:::

Not: *Derfor vi tok en pause.*

Compare the direction of the link:

::: examples
- no: Nora hentet malingen fordi butikken ikke kunne levere.
- en: Nora collected the paint because the shop could not deliver.
:::

::: examples
- no: Butikken kunne ikke levere. Derfor hentet Nora malingen.
- en: The shop could not deliver. Therefore, Nora collected the paint.
:::

The factual relationship remains similar, but the structure changes. *Fordi* introduces the cause; *derfor* introduces the result.

**Combining cause and concession**

A connected report often needs more than one relationship:

::: examples
- no: Det regnet kraftig. Derfor arbeidet vi under tak. Likevel ble vi ferdige med benkene.
- en: It was raining heavily. Therefore, we worked under cover. Nevertheless, we finished the benches.
:::

The first connector presents a consequence of the rain. The second presents a result that might seem surprising despite the rain.

Avoid using connectors mechanically. Each one should express a genuine relationship:

::: examples
- no: Det regnet. Derfor tok vi på oss regnjakker.
- en: It was raining. Therefore, we put on rain jackets.
:::

::: examples
- no: Det regnet. Likevel arbeidet vi ute.
- en: It was raining. Nevertheless, we worked outside.
:::

*Derfor* works in the first pair because wearing rain jackets is a consequence of the rain. *Likevel* works in the second because continuing outdoors contrasts with what the rain might lead us to expect.

**Checkpoint: distinguish cause from unexpected contrast**

Describe one sensible consequence of bad weather. First state the bad weather, then introduce its consequence with *derfor*. Next express the same cause-and-result relationship in one sentence using *fordi*. This checkpoint prevents the learner from treating cause and result as interchangeable directions. A successful response would use *derfor* to introduce the result with correct V2 order and *fordi* to introduce the cause with the subordinate-clause word order modeled above.

## Drawing a conclusion {#drawing-a-conclusion role=model objectives=obj-discourse-connectors-01}

A report often ends by interpreting the information rather than adding another event.

**Altså: so, therefore, in other words**

*Altså* can signal a conclusion based on what has already been said.

::: examples
- no: Det kom over tretti frivillige. Vi hadde altså nok folk.
- en: More than thirty volunteers came. So we had enough people.
- no: Alt arbeidet er ferdig. Vi kan altså åpne hagen igjen.
- en: All the work is finished. We can therefore reopen the garden.
:::

Here *altså* appears inside the clause. The subject and finite verb keep their ordinary order:

::: examples
- no: Vi hadde altså nok folk.
- en: So we had enough people.
:::

When *altså* is fronted, V2 applies:

::: examples
- no: Altså hadde vi nok folk.
- en: So we had enough people.
:::

In ordinary reports, the mid-clause position is often natural because it allows the conclusion to sound less abrupt.

**Alt i alt: all in all**

*Alt i alt* summarizes an overall judgment.

::: examples
- no: Alt i alt ble dagen svært vellykket.
- en: All in all, the day was very successful.
- no: Alt i alt fungerte planen godt.
- en: All in all, the plan worked well.
- no: Alt i alt fikk nabolaget mye igjen for innsatsen.
- en: All in all, the neighborhood gained a great deal from the effort.
:::

Because *alt i alt* occupies the first position, the finite verb follows it.

**Til slutt versus alt i alt**

These expressions both occur near the end, but they have different functions.

::: examples
- no: Til slutt ryddet vi hele området.
- en: Finally, we cleaned the whole area.
- no: Alt i alt var prosjektet en suksess.
- en: All in all, the project was a success.
:::

*Til slutt* introduces the final event in a sequence. *Alt i alt* gives an overall conclusion or evaluation.

**Checkpoint: end the council report**

Write a final event from the workday using *til slutt*. Then add an overall evaluation using *alt i alt* or a conclusion containing *altså*. This activity belongs near the end because it asks the learner to distinguish chronological closure from logical conclusion. A successful response would use each connector for its intended purpose and maintain correct word order.

## Common mistakes to avoid {#common-mistakes-to-avoid role=contrast objectives=obj-discourse-connectors-02}

**Keeping the subject before the verb after a fronted adverbial connector**

Learner repair: The intended relationship is sequence—this happened after earlier work. Before comparing with the correction, explain why *deretter* matches that relationship, identify the word-order error, and rewrite the sentence.

Incorrect: *Deretter elevene reparerte porten.*

::: examples
- no: ✗ Deretter elevene reparerte porten.
- en: After that, the pupils repaired the gate.
:::

Correct: Deretter reparerte elevene porten.

::: examples
- no: Deretter reparerte elevene porten.
- en: After that, the pupils repaired the gate.
:::

{{exercise: repair_fronted_connector}}

**Using inversion directly after *men***

Incorrect in the intended neutral structure: *Det regnet, men fortsatte vi arbeidet.*

Correct: Det regnet, men vi fortsatte arbeidet.

::: examples
- no: Det regnet, men vi fortsatte arbeidet.
- en: It was raining, but we continued the work.
:::

**Forgetting inversion after an opening *selv om* clause**

Incorrect: *Selv om vi var slitne, vi ryddet området.*

Correct: Selv om vi var slitne, ryddet vi området.

::: examples
- no: Selv om vi var slitne, ryddet vi området.
- en: Although we were tired, we cleaned the area.
:::

**Confusing a final event with a conclusion**

::: examples
- no: Til slutt låste Ingrid porten.
- en: Finally, Ingrid locked the gate.
- no: Alt i alt hadde dagen gått bedre enn forventet.
- en: All in all, the day had gone better than expected.
:::

The first sentence tells what happened last. The second evaluates the whole day.

**Choosing a connector that does not match the relationship**

::: examples
- no: Vi manglet maling. Derfor dro Nora til butikken.
- en: We lacked paint. Therefore, Nora went to the shop.
- no: Butikken lå langt unna. Likevel dro Nora dit.
- en: The shop was far away. Nevertheless, Nora went there.
:::

The lack of paint explains the trip, so *derfor* fits. The long distance could have discouraged the trip, so *likevel* expresses concession.

## Extended practice: prepare a coherent project update {#extended-practice role=recap objectives=obj-discourse-connectors-01,obj-discourse-connectors-02}

Begin with the initial situation, organize the work in sequence, add at least one supporting fact, mention an obstacle and what happened despite it, explain one cause-and-result relationship, and end with both a final event and an overall conclusion.

This larger activity comes after focused practice because it requires choosing connectors by meaning while also controlling word order. Keep the garden project as the context, but do not copy the dialogue line by line. A successful response would read as a coherent report, use connectors for distinct functions, and consistently place the finite verb before the subject after the fronted adverbial connectors taught here and in the main clause following an opening subordinate clause. It would retain subject-before-verb order inside the modeled *fordi* and *selv om* clauses. After *men*, it would use subject–verb order when the subject is the coordinated clause’s first constituent and verb–subject order when a taught adverbial is fronted.
