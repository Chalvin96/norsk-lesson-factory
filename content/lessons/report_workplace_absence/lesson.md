## Telling work you cannot attend {#sec-telling-work-you-cannot-attend role=orient objectives=obj-report-workplace-absence-01}

**Before the workday**

It is 7:15 on Tuesday morning. Sara is supposed to start work at eight and lead a customer meeting at ten. She has become ill during the night, so she calls her team leader, Henrik.

::: {.reading translation="Hi, Henrik. It’s Sara. Unfortunately, I’m ill and can’t come to work today." dialogue_id=absence-morning-1 speaker_id=sara speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Hei, Henrik. Det er Sara. Jeg er dessverre syk og kan ikke komme på jobb i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’m sorry to hear that. Do you know whether you’ll be away all day?" dialogue_id=absence-morning-1 speaker_id=henrik speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Det var leit å høre. Vet du om du blir borte hele dagen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I’ll stay home all day. I’ll let you know by three o’clock whether I can come to work tomorrow." dialogue_id=absence-morning-1 speaker_id=sara speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Ja, jeg blir hjemme hele dagen. Jeg gir deg beskjed innen klokka tre om jeg kan komme på jobb i morgen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="All right. What do we do about the customer meeting at ten?" dialogue_id=absence-morning-1 speaker_id=henrik speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Greit. Hva gjør vi med kundemøtet klokka ti?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’ve sent the notes to Nora, so she can take the meeting." dialogue_id=absence-morning-1 speaker_id=sara speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Jeg har sendt notatene til Nora, så hun kan ta møtet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Thanks for letting me know. Get well soon!" dialogue_id=absence-morning-1 speaker_id=henrik speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Flott. Takk for at du sier fra. God bedring!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thanks. I’ll contact you this afternoon." dialogue_id=absence-morning-1 speaker_id=sara speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Takk. Jeg kontakter deg i ettermiddag.
:::

Sara’s call is short, but Henrik receives all the information he needs. She says that she cannot attend work, gives the relevant timing, and explains what will happen next. She also deals with one urgent task by handing it over to Nora.

## The essential absence message {#sec-essential-absence-message role=model objectives=obj-report-workplace-absence-01}

A useful workplace absence message has three parts:

1. Say that you cannot attend work.
2. Say when you will be absent.
3. State the next contact or handover.

Sara begins with a clear statement:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er syk og kan ikke komme på jobb i dag.
- en: I’m ill and can’t come to work today.
:::

The central expression is:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan ikke komme på jobb.
- en: I can’t come to work.
:::

In Norwegian, på jobb means at work or to work in expressions such as være på jobb and komme på jobb.

::: examples
- no: Jeg er på jobb nå.
- en: I’m at work now.
- no: Jeg kan ikke komme på jobb i morgen.
- en: I can’t come to work tomorrow.
:::

You can give a brief reason if it is relevant, but you do not need to give personal details.

::: examples
- no: Jeg er dessverre syk og kan ikke komme på jobb.
- en: Unfortunately, I’m ill and can’t come to work.
- no: Jeg må være hjemme og kan ikke komme på jobb i dag.
- en: I have to stay home and can’t come to work today.
- no: Jeg kan dessverre ikke møte på jobb i dag.
- en: Unfortunately, I can’t report for work today.
:::

Kan ikke is direct and practical. Dessverre softens the message and is common in professional communication.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kommer ikke på jobb i dag.
- en: I’m not coming to work today.
- no: Jeg kan ikke komme på jobb i dag.
- en: I can’t come to work today.
:::

The first sentence reports what will happen. The second also communicates that attending is not possible. In an absence message, kan ikke komme is often clearer.

**Checkpoint: Identify the central message**

Read Sara’s first turn again and locate the words that state the absence and the words that state the timing. This belongs here because the employer must understand those two facts immediately. Use the context of Sara’s Tuesday morning call. A successful response would correctly distinguish cannot come to work from today, without depending on the later details about the meeting.

## Giving useful timing {#sec-giving-useful-timing role=model objectives=obj-report-workplace-absence-01}

A message such as Jeg kan ikke komme på jobb is incomplete if the listener does not know when the absence applies. Add the most precise timing you currently know.

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan ikke komme på jobb i dag.
- en: I can’t come to work today.
- no: Jeg kan ikke komme på jobb i morgen.
- en: I can’t come to work tomorrow.
- no: Jeg blir borte hele dagen.
- en: I’ll be away all day.
- no: Jeg blir hjemme i dag og i morgen.
- en: I’ll stay home today and tomorrow.
- no: Jeg kan ikke komme før etter lunsj.
- en: I can’t come in until after lunch.
:::

The Norwegian structure før etter lunsj is possible in conversation, but the clearer workplace message is:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan komme på jobb etter lunsj, men ikke før.
- en: I can come to work after lunch, but not before.
:::

If you are uncertain about the following day, state only what you know and promise an update.

::: examples
- no: Jeg blir hjemme i dag. Jeg vet ikke ennå om jeg kan komme i morgen.
- en: I’ll stay home today. I don’t know yet whether I can come tomorrow.
- no: Jeg gir deg beskjed senere i dag.
- en: I’ll let you know later today.
:::

This is better than making a promise you may not be able to keep.

**Common mistake: vague timing**

A message can be grammatically correct but still unhelpful:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan ikke komme på jobb.
- en: I can’t come to work.
:::

The employer may have to ask whether you mean this morning, the whole day, or several days. Add timing:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan ikke komme på jobb i dag. Jeg blir borte hele dagen.
- en: I can’t come to work today. I’ll be away all day.
:::

**Checkpoint: Make the timing precise**

Imagine that Sara knows she will be absent for all of Tuesday but does not yet know about Wednesday. Draft the timing part of her message without adding new personal details. This activity belongs here because it separates confirmed information from uncertain information. A successful response would state Tuesday’s full-day absence clearly and leave Wednesday open for a later update.

## Saying what happens next {#sec-saying-what-happens-next role=model objectives=obj-report-workplace-absence-01}

After reporting the absence, tell the other person when they will hear from you again. Useful expressions include:

::: examples
- no: Jeg gir deg beskjed i ettermiddag.
- en: I’ll let you know this afternoon.
- no: Jeg kontakter deg senere i dag.
- en: I’ll contact you later today.
- no: Jeg sender en oppdatering innen klokka tre.
- en: I’ll send an update by three o’clock.
- no: Jeg ringer deg i morgen tidlig.
- en: I’ll call you tomorrow morning.
:::

The expression gi beskjed means to let someone know or give someone a message. You normally include the person as an object when necessary:

::: examples
- no: Jeg gir Henrik beskjed i ettermiddag.
- en: I’ll let Henrik know this afternoon.
:::

When the listener is already clear, you can use deg:

::: examples
- no: Jeg gir deg beskjed innen klokka tre.
- en: I’ll let you know by three o’clock.
:::

Notice the difference between a general time and a deadline:

::: examples
- no: Jeg sender en oppdatering klokka tre.
- en: I’ll send an update at three o’clock.
- no: Jeg sender en oppdatering innen klokka tre.
- en: I’ll send an update by three o’clock.
:::

Klokka tre names the expected time. Innen klokka tre means no later than three.

**Checkpoint: Add the next contact**

Return to Sara’s situation. Her team leader needs to know whether she can work on Wednesday. Add one sentence promising an update by a specific time on Tuesday. This belongs after the timing statement because it tells Henrik how the remaining uncertainty will be resolved. A successful response would name both the contact action and its deadline.

## Arranging a handover {#sec-arranging-a-handover role=model objectives=obj-report-workplace-absence-01}

Sometimes another person must handle an urgent task while you are away. A short handover statement prevents confusion.

::: examples
- no: Nora kan ta kundemøtet.
- en: Nora can take the customer meeting.
- no: Jeg har sendt notatene til Nora.
- en: I’ve sent the notes to Nora.
- no: Per følger opp kunden i dag.
- en: Per will follow up with the customer today.
- no: Dokumentene ligger i prosjektmappen.
- en: The documents are in the project folder.
:::

A strong handover often names the task and the person:

::: examples
- no: Nora kan ta møtet klokka ti.
- en: Nora can take the meeting at ten.
:::

It can also say where the necessary information is:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har sendt henne notatene.
- en: I’ve sent her the notes.
- no: Planen ligger i fellesmappen.
- en: The plan is in the shared folder.
:::

Keep the handover limited to information the employer needs immediately. Sara does not explain the whole customer case during her absence call. She states who can take the meeting and confirms that the notes have been sent.

**Common mistake: mentioning a task without resolving it**

This statement identifies a problem but not a handover:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har et kundemøte klokka ti.
- en: I have a customer meeting at ten.
:::

A more useful message states what will happen:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har et kundemøte klokka ti, men Nora kan ta det.
- en: I have a customer meeting at ten, but Nora can take it.
:::

The pronoun det refers to møtet.

**Checkpoint: Complete the handover**

Imagine that Sara cannot lead the ten o’clock meeting. Write a short handover statement saying that Nora can take it and that Nora has received the notes. This activity belongs here because Sara’s absence affects a specific scheduled task. A successful response would identify the replacement and confirm that the necessary material has been transferred.

## Putting the whole message together {#sec-putting-the-whole-message-together role=model objectives=obj-report-workplace-absence-01}

A complete message does not need to be long:

::: examples
- no: Hei, Henrik. Jeg er dessverre syk og kan ikke komme på jobb i dag. Jeg blir hjemme hele dagen. Nora kan ta kundemøtet, og jeg har sendt henne notatene. Jeg gir deg beskjed innen klokka tre om jeg kan komme på jobb i morgen.
- en: Hi, Henrik. Unfortunately, I’m ill and can’t come to work today. I’ll stay home all day. Nora can take the customer meeting, and I’ve sent her the notes. I’ll let you know by three o’clock whether I can come to work tomorrow.
:::

The order helps the reader:

- The absence comes first.
- The timing comes immediately afterward.
- The handover deals with urgent work.
- The next contact closes the message.

Avoid hiding the main point behind a long introduction. Henrik should not have to read several sentences before discovering that Sara will be absent.

## Common mistakes in a complete message {#sec-common-mistakes-in-a-complete-message role=contrast objectives=obj-report-workplace-absence-01}

Do not confuse i dag and på dagen:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan ikke komme på jobb i dag.
- en: I can’t come to work today.
:::

I dag gives the correct timing. På dagen usually means during the daytime and does not identify the date by itself.

Do not omit på in komme på jobb:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan ikke komme jobb i dag.
- en: I can’t come work today.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan ikke komme på jobb i dag.
- en: I can’t come to work today.
:::

Do not promise a future contact without saying when if the timing matters:

::: examples
- no: Jeg sier fra senere.
- en: I’ll let you know later.
:::

Clearer:

::: examples
- no: Jeg gir deg beskjed innen klokka tre.
- en: I’ll let you know by three o’clock.
:::

## Practice: Build a message in stages {#sec-practice-build-a-message-in-stages role=model objectives=obj-report-workplace-absence-01}

Imagine that Emil works in a warehouse. On Thursday morning, he cannot attend his shift. He knows he will be absent for the whole day. A colleague, Lise, can receive an expected delivery, and Emil will contact his manager by four about Friday.

First, write only Emil’s central absence statement and Thursday timing. This controlled step belongs here because it tests whether the main message remains clear before other information is added. A successful response would state that Emil cannot come to work on Thursday and will be away all day.

Next, add the handover concerning Lise and the delivery. This uses the same workplace situation while adding one practical consequence of the absence. A successful response would make clear who will receive the delivery.

Finally, add Emil’s next contact. State that he will update his manager by four about Friday. This final step completes the approved communicative task. A successful response would combine absence, relevant timing, and next action in a concise professional message.

## Later that afternoon {#sec-later-that-afternoon role=recap objectives=obj-report-workplace-absence-01}

At 2:40, Sara sends Henrik an update.

::: {.reading translation="Hi again. I’m still ill, so I can’t come to work tomorrow either. I’ll contact you tomorrow afternoon about Thursday." dialogue_id=absence-afternoon-1 speaker_id=sara speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Hei igjen. Jeg er fortsatt syk, så jeg kan ikke komme på jobb i morgen heller. Jeg kontakter deg i morgen ettermiddag om torsdag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thanks for the update. Nora took the customer meeting, and it went well. Get well soon." dialogue_id=absence-afternoon-1 speaker_id=henrik speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Takk for oppdateringen. Nora tok kundemøtet, og det gikk bra. God bedring videre.
:::

Sara follows the plan from the morning call: she contacts Henrik before three, gives the new timing, and states when the next update will come. Her second message is brief because the background and handover are already known.

## Final retrieval and review {#sec-final-retrieval-and-review role=recap objectives=obj-report-workplace-absence-01}

Without looking back at Sara’s messages, write a short workplace absence message for a new situation. State that you cannot attend work, give clear timing, and finish with either a specific next contact or a practical handover. A successful response will let the employer understand what is happening, when it applies, and what will happen next.
