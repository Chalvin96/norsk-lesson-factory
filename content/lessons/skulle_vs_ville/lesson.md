## A Saturday plan {#a-saturday-plan role=orient objectives=obj-skulle-vs-ville-01}

Nora, Emil, and Sara are organizing a small neighbourhood market. They agreed on several jobs earlier in the week, but Saturday morning is not going exactly as planned.

::: {.reading translation="You said that you were going to meet Sara at the community centre at nine." dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Du sa at du skulle møte Sara ved samfunnshuset klokka ni.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, we were supposed to meet at nine, but she wrote that she would be late." dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ja, vi skulle møtes klokka ni, men hun skrev at hun skulle bli forsinket.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Did she say that she was still willing to help?" dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Sa hun at hun fortsatt ville hjelpe til?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, she wrote that she was still willing to help and would gladly make coffee. She also asked what we would do if it started to rain." dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ja, hun skrev at hun fortsatt ville hjelpe og gjerne lage kaffe. Hun spurte også hva vi ville gjøre hvis det begynte å regne.
:::

::: {.reading translation="If it started to rain, we would move the tables inside." dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Hvis det begynte å regne, ville vi flytte bordene inn.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good idea. I was actually supposed to collect the tables now, but my car won’t start." dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
God idé. Jeg skulle egentlig hente bordene nå, men bilen min starter ikke.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Were you also going to collect the signs?" dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Jeg kan kjøre. Skulle du også hente skiltene?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. If the signs were lighter, I would take the bus." dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ja. Hvis skiltene var lettere, ville jeg ta bussen.
:::

The two forms appear in similar-looking sentences, but they do different jobs. Emil uses *skulle* for arrangements made earlier and for reports that preserve a recoverable earlier *skal*-type plan, schedule, expectation, promise, or commitment. He uses *ville* for willingness and for an imagined result.

That meaning choice is the focus of this chapter.

## Two related modal forms {#two-related-modal-forms role=model objectives=obj-skulle-vs-ville-01}

*Skulle* and *ville* are modal verbs (*modalverb*). In the patterns taught here, they take a bare infinitive (*infinitiv*) without *å*. An adverb such as *gjerne* may intervene between the modal and the infinitive.

::: examples
- no: Vi skulle møtes.
- en: We were going to meet.
- no: Hun ville hjelpe.
- en: She wanted to help.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: Vi skulle å møtes.
- en: 'Incorrect: We were going to meet.'
- no: Hun ville å hjelpe.
- en: 'Incorrect: She wanted to help.'
:::

Both forms can correspond to English *would*, so translating one English word at a time is unreliable. Ask instead what the sentence means:

- Does the report preserve a recoverable earlier *skal*-type plan, schedule, expectation, promise, or commitment? Use *skulle*. A future-from-past viewpoint alone does not decide the modal.
- In a past narrative or report, was someone willing or ready to act? Use *ville*.
- Is the result imagined or dependent on a hypothetical condition? Use *ville*.

## *Skulle* for an earlier plan {#skulle-for-an-earlier-plan role=model objectives=obj-skulle-vs-ville-01}

Use *skulle* in the reports taught here when context preserves a recoverable earlier *skal*-type plan, arrangement, schedule, expectation, promise, or commitment.

::: examples
- no: Jeg skulle ringe legen etter lunsj.
- en: I was going to call the doctor after lunch.
- no: Vi skulle spise sammen på fredag.
- en: We were supposed to eat together on Friday.
- no: Bussen skulle komme klokka halv ti.
- en: The bus was due to arrive at half past nine.
- no: Emil skulle hente bordene.
- en: Emil was going to collect the tables.
:::

The plan may or may not have happened. *Skulle* tells us about the plan, not necessarily its final result.

::: examples
- no: Jeg skulle ringe legen, men jeg glemte det.
- en: I was going to call the doctor, but I forgot.
- no: Vi skulle spise sammen, men restauranten var stengt.
- en: We were supposed to eat together, but the restaurant was closed.
- no: Bussen skulle komme halv ti, men den kom ikke før ti.
- en: The bus was due at half past nine, but it did not arrive until ten.
:::

A useful compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Report preserving a recoverable earlier *skal*-type plan or expected arrangement: *skulle* + infinitive. Future-from-past viewpoint alone does not select *skulle*.
:::

**Plan versus past willingness**

Compare these sentences carefully. In the second situation, Anna needed someone to visit and help her:

::: examples
- no: Jeg skulle besøke Anna.
- en: I was going to visit Anna.
- no: Jeg ville besøke Anna.
- en: I was willing to visit Anna.
:::

The first sentence presents the visit as an earlier plan. In the stated help context, the second presents the speaker as willing to make the visit.

Another contrast. In the second situation, the group asked whether anyone was willing to join the trip:

::: examples
- no: Per skulle bli med på turen.
- en: Per was going to come on the trip.
- no: Per ville bli med på turen.
- en: Per was willing to come on the trip.
:::

Plans and willingness can overlap. Context and the modal show whether the speaker emphasizes an existing arrangement or someone’s readiness to act.

**Practice request: notice-earlier-plans**

**Purpose:** Notice how *skulle* presents an action as previously arranged or expected.

**Context:** Revisit Nora and Emil’s market preparations and examine short statements about jobs agreed earlier in the week.

**Learner action:** Identify the actions presented as existing plans and explain what earlier arrangement each use of *skulle* points back to.

**Success demonstrates:** Recognition that *skulle* marks an earlier plan or expectation, whether or not the action is completed.

{{exercise: notice-earlier-plans}}

**Practice request: complete-planned-actions**

**Purpose:** Retrieve *skulle* in controlled everyday contexts.

**Context:** Use brief situations involving appointments, travel times, household jobs, and meetings that were arranged earlier.

**Learner action:** Complete each statement with the appropriate form and infinitive so that it expresses a prior plan rather than a personal wish.

**Success demonstrates:** Accurate production of *skulle* + infinitive for planned or expected actions.

{{exercise: complete-planned-actions}}

## *Skulle* for future viewed from the past {#skulle-for-future-viewed-from-the-past role=model objectives=obj-skulle-vs-ville-01}

In the reports taught here, *skulle* can express future seen from an earlier time, often called future in the past (*fortidens framtid*), when the report preserves a recoverable earlier *skal*-type plan, schedule, expectation, promise, or commitment.

At the moment Sara wrote her message, being late was still in the future:

::: examples
- no: Sara skrev at hun skulle bli forsinket.
- en: Sara wrote that she would be late.
:::

Other examples:

::: examples
- no: Læreren sa at prøven skulle begynne klokka ti.
- en: The teacher said that the test would begin at ten.
- no: Jonas lovet at han skulle sende dokumentet samme kveld.
- en: Jonas promised that he would send the document that evening.
- no: De trodde at toget skulle gå fra spor fire.
- en: They thought that the train would leave from platform four.
- no: Mina fortalte at hun skulle flytte til Tromsø i august.
- en: Mina said that she was going to move to Tromsø in August.
:::

The event is future relative to the earlier saying, promising, or thinking. This chapter uses *skulle* when the report preserves an earlier *skal* meaning: a plan, schedule, expectation, promise, or commitment. Norwegian *ville* can also have future-in-the-past readings, so past viewpoint alone does not always determine the modal.

Compare two reports with explicit source contexts. First, Maja had announced: *Jeg skal bli med*:

::: examples
- no: Maja sa at hun skulle bli med.
- en: Maja said that she would come along.
:::

Later, when the group asked who was willing to join, Maja volunteered:

::: examples
- no: Maja sa at hun ville bli med.
- en: Maja said that she was willing to come along.
:::

The first preserves an earlier plan expressed with *skal*. The second uses the stated request-and-volunteer context to emphasize past willingness. Without such context, an isolated clause with *ville* can also receive a future-in-the-past reading.

**Common misconception: English *would* does not determine one Norwegian modal**

Suppose he first said *Jeg skal komme senere*, presenting his arrival as a plan or commitment. A later report preserves that meaning with *skulle*:

::: examples
- no: Han sa at han skulle komme senere.
- en: He said that he would come later.
:::

If someone had asked who was willing to come later and he volunteered, the context supports *ville*:

::: examples
- no: Han sa at han ville komme senere.
- en: He said that he was willing to come later.
:::

*Ville* can also express future in the past in other contexts. Choose from the source meaning and surrounding context, not from the English word alone.

**Practice request: report-future-from-past**

**Purpose:** Use *skulle* when a past report preserves a recoverable earlier *skal*-type plan, schedule, expectation, promise, or commitment.

**Context:** Work with short messages, schedules, promises, announcements, and plans that explicitly use or clearly imply earlier *skal* and are reported later by another person.

**Learner action:** Rephrase each earlier plan, schedule, promise, expectation, or commitment as a past report using *skulle* + infinitive.

**Success demonstrates:** Correct use of *skulle* to preserve a recoverable earlier *skal* meaning. If supplied context does not distinguish that meaning from a possible future-in-the-past reading with *ville*, either natural answer must be accepted.

{{exercise: report-future-from-past}}

## *Ville* for past willingness {#ville-for-past-willingness role=model objectives=obj-skulle-vs-ville-01}

*Ville* is the past form of *vil*. In a past narrative or report, it can show that someone was willing or ready to act. A request, need, or voluntary offer in the context establishes that meaning.

When the market group needed someone to prepare drinks:

::: examples
- no: Sara ville lage kaffe.
- en: Sara was willing to make coffee.
- no: Ingen andre hadde tid, men Amir ville hjelpe.
- en: Nobody else had time, but Amir was willing to help.
:::

When the family needed a driver:

::: examples
- no: Bestefar ville gjerne kjøre oss til stasjonen.
- en: Grandfather was happy to drive us to the station.
:::

*Gjerne* can make an expression sound positive, but it does not by itself distinguish willingness from desire. Outside this chapter’s scored distinction, context can give *ville* a wanted or would-like meaning:

::: examples
- no: Barna ville se filmen en gang til.
- en: The children wanted to watch the film again.
- no: Jeg ville gjerne snakke med deg.
- en: I wanted to speak with you.
- no: Hun sa at hun gjerne ville delta.
- en: She said that she would like to participate.
:::

The negative form can express refusal or lack of willingness:

::: examples
- no: Katten ville ikke gå ut.
- en: The cat would not go outside.
- no: Han ville ikke svare på spørsmålet.
- en: He would not answer the question.
:::

Depending on context, *ville ikke* may mean did not want to or refused to.

**Arrangement versus past willingness**

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Nora skulle kjøre Emil.
- en: Nora was going to drive Emil.
:::

When Emil needed a driver, Nora said that she was ready to help:

::: examples
- no: Nora ville kjøre Emil.
- en: Nora was willing to drive Emil.
:::

The first is about the arrangement. In the stated past context, the second is about Nora’s willingness.

Likewise:

::: examples
- no: De skulle hjelpe oss på lørdag.
- en: They were going to help us on Saturday.
- no: De ville hjelpe oss på lørdag.
- en: They were willing to help us on Saturday.
:::

Do not use *ville* merely because a person had an intention. If the main point is an agreed schedule or arrangement, *skulle* is clearer.

**Practice request: recognize-willingness**

**Purpose:** Distinguish voluntary willingness from an existing arrangement.

**Context:** Read short past-tense exchanges in which neighbours, colleagues, or family members responded earlier to requests or needs for help.

**Learner action:** Determine which statements emphasize that a person was willing or ready to act, then supply *ville* with the relevant infinitive.

**Success demonstrates:** Correct interpretation and production of *ville* for willingness in an explicit past narrative or report.

{{exercise: recognize-willingness}}

**Practice request: contrast-plan-and-willingness**

**Purpose:** Make the meaning change between *skulle* and *ville* explicit.

**Context:** Use paired past situations with the same person and action, such as attending a meeting, visiting a friend, or helping at an event. One situation gives an earlier arrangement; the other gives an earlier request, need, or voluntary offer.

**Learner action:** Write one sentence presenting the action as an earlier plan and another presenting the person as willing or ready to act in response to the stated past context. Briefly state the meaning difference.

**Success demonstrates:** Deliberate choice between *skulle* for an arrangement and *ville* for contextually established past willingness, without relying on English word matching.

{{exercise: contrast-plan-and-willingness}}

## *Ville* for hypothetical results {#ville-for-hypothetical-results role=model objectives=obj-skulle-vs-ville-01}

Use *ville* for what would happen in an imagined situation. A common pattern combines a hypothetical condition (*hypotetisk vilkår*) with a result:

::: {.rule}
*Hvis* + past tense (*preteritum*), *ville* + infinitive.
:::

::: examples
- no: Hvis det regnet, ville vi flytte bordene inn.
- en: If it rained, we would move the tables inside.
- no: Hvis jeg hadde mer tid, ville jeg lære samisk.
- en: If I had more time, I would learn Sami.
- no: Hvis leiligheten var billigere, ville vi leie den.
- en: If the flat were cheaper, we would rent it.
- no: Hvis hun bodde nærmere, ville vi besøke henne oftere.
- en: If she lived closer, we would visit her more often.
:::

The past-tense form in the condition does not necessarily describe past time. It helps present the situation as imagined, uncertain, or contrary to present reality. *Ville* introduces the imagined result.

The condition can also come second:

::: examples
- no: Jeg ville gå mer på ski hvis jeg bodde nærmere fjellet.
- en: I would ski more if I lived closer to the mountains.
- no: Vi ville kjøpe huset hvis det hadde et større kjøkken.
- en: We would buy the house if it had a larger kitchen.
:::

Sometimes the condition is understood from context rather than stated:

::: examples
- no: Med bedre vær ville flere mennesker komme.
- en: With better weather, more people would come.
- no: Uten bilen ville turen ta mye lengre tid.
- en: Without the car, the journey would take much longer.
:::

Questions follow the same meaning pattern:

::: examples
- no: Hva ville du gjøre hvis du fant en lommebok på gata?
- en: What would you do if you found a wallet in the street?
- no: Hvor ville dere bo hvis dere kunne velge fritt?
- en: Where would you live if you could choose freely?
:::

**Reported future versus hypothetical result**

English uses *would* in both examples, but Norwegian makes the meaning distinction visible:

::: examples
- no: Hun sa at hun skulle kjøpe huset.
- en: She said that she would buy the house.
- no: Hun ville kjøpe huset hvis det var billigere.
- en: She would buy the house if it were cheaper.
:::

In the first sentence, the report preserves her earlier stated plan to buy the house. In the second, buying it depends on an imagined condition.

Another pair:

::: examples
- no: De trodde at mange mennesker skulle komme.
- en: They thought that many people would come.
- no: Med bedre vær ville mange mennesker komme.
- en: With better weather, many people would come.
:::

::: {.rule}
Report preserving a recoverable earlier *skal*-type plan, schedule, expectation, promise, or commitment: *skulle*. Imagined result: *ville*.
:::

Past viewpoint by itself is not a complete selection rule because *ville* can also occur with future-in-the-past meaning.

**Practice request: build-hypothetical-results**

**Purpose:** Produce *ville* as the result of an imagined condition.

**Context:** Use everyday hypothetical situations involving housing, transport, work schedules, weather, and free time.

**Learner action:** Complete or write result clauses with *ville* + infinitive so that each result depends on the stated condition.

**Success demonstrates:** Accurate use of *ville* for hypothetical meaning. Condition-clause forms provide context but are not the primary scored dimension.

{{exercise: build-hypothetical-results}}

**Practice request: distinguish-reported-and-hypothetical-would**

**Purpose:** Prevent automatic translation of every English *would* with the same Norwegian modal.

**Context:** Compare short reports of explicit earlier plans, schedules, expectations, promises, or commitments with imagined situations involving similar actions.

**Learner action:** Choose and produce *skulle* when the report preserves a recoverable earlier *skal* meaning and *ville* when the action is a hypothetical result.

**Success demonstrates:** Meaning-based selection between the two modals in constrained contexts. If a reported context leaves both *skulle* and future-in-the-past *ville* natural, either answer must be accepted.

{{exercise: distinguish-reported-and-hypothetical-would}}

## Back at the market {#back-at-the-market role=recap objectives=obj-skulle-vs-ville-01}

Sara arrives earlier than expected.

::: {.reading translation="We thought that you would come later." dialogue_id="market-follow-up" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Vi trodde at du skulle komme senere.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I thought so too, but my neighbour was willing to drive me." dialogue_id="market-follow-up" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Det trodde jeg også, men naboen min ville kjøre meg.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great! You said earlier that you were willing to make coffee. Do you still want to?" dialogue_id="market-follow-up" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Flott! Du sa tidligere at du ville lage kaffe. Har du fortsatt lyst til det?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. If you found a large thermos, I would make coffee for everyone." dialogue_id="market-follow-up" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Ja. Hvis dere fant en stor termos, ville jeg lage kaffe til alle.
:::

::: {.reading translation="The thermos was supposed to be in the kitchen, but I cannot find it." dialogue_id="market-follow-up" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Termosen skulle stå på kjøkkenet, men jeg finner den ikke.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Jakob said that he would bring it. I’ll call him." dialogue_id="market-follow-up" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Jakob sa at han skulle ta den med. Jeg ringer ham.
:::

Each modal now has a clear job:

- Sara *skulle komme senere*: an expected event.
- The neighbour *ville kjøre*: willingness.
- Sara said earlier that she *ville lage kaffe*: reported past willingness; the conditional sentence gives a hypothetical result.
- The thermos *skulle stå på kjøkkenet*: an expectation.
- Jakob said he *skulle ta den med*: a report preserving his earlier *skal*-type plan or commitment.

**Practice request: interpret-market-follow-up**

**Purpose:** Consolidate meaning in the original scene before transferring it elsewhere.

**Context:** Use the continuation of Nora, Emil, and Sara’s market preparations.

**Learner action:** Explain what each occurrence of *skulle* or *ville* contributes: earlier arrangement, a report preserving a recoverable earlier *skal*-type meaning, willingness, or hypothetical result.

**Success demonstrates:** Accurate interpretation of both forms in connected discourse rather than isolated sentences.

## Common mistakes to avoid {#common-mistakes-to-avoid role=contrast objectives=obj-skulle-vs-ville-01}

**Mistake 1: Using *ville* for every earlier plan**

If the appointment was arranged, use *skulle*:

::: examples
- no: Vi skulle møtes klokka seks.
- en: We were supposed to meet at six.
- no: Vi ville møtes klokka seks.
- en: We wanted to meet at six.
:::

The second sentence is possible, but it describes desire rather than the arrangement itself.

**Mistake 2: Letting English *would* determine the Norwegian modal**

Suppose she had promised *Jeg skal ringe*. A later report preserves that commitment:

::: examples
- no: Hun sa at hun skulle ringe.
- en: She said that she would call.
:::

Here, *skulle* reports the earlier *skal* commitment. Past viewpoint alone would not prove that every possible Norwegian sentence must use *skulle*, because *ville* can also have future-in-the-past readings.

::: examples
- no: Hun ville ringe hvis hun hadde nummeret.
- en: She would call if she had the number.
:::

Here, the call is explicitly hypothetical, so use *ville*.

**Mistake 3: Adding *å* after the modal**

Use a bare infinitive without *å*:

::: examples
- no: De skulle reise.
- en: They were going to leave.
- no: De ville reise.
- en: They wanted to leave.
:::

Do not place *å* before the infinitive. An adverb such as *gjerne* may intervene between the modal and the infinitive.

**Mistake 4: Assuming *skulle* proves that an action happened**

::: examples
- no: Jeg skulle kjøpe melk, men butikken var stengt.
- en: I was going to buy milk, but the shop was closed.
:::

The plan existed, but the purchase did not happen.

## Cumulative transfer: a delayed cabin trip {#cumulative-transfer-a-delayed-cabin-trip role=recap objectives=obj-skulle-vs-ville-01}

**Practice request: transfer-cabin-plans**

**Purpose:** Apply the distinction in a new connected situation.

**Context:** A written past-tense account of the cabin trip includes earlier travel plans, schedules, promises or commitments with recoverable *skal* meaning, responses to earlier requests for help, and imagined consequences of bad weather.
