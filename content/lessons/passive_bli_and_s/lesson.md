## A library gets ready to reopen {#library-reopening role=orient objectives=obj-passive-bli-and-s-01,obj-passive-bli-and-s-02}

The local library has been closed for repairs. On Monday morning, librarian Ingrid shows her colleague Marius what still needs to happen before the doors open.

::: {.reading translation="The library reopens on Wednesday." dialogue_id="library-reopening" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Biblioteket åpnes igjen på onsdag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Are all the rooms ready?" dialogue_id="library-reopening" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Er alle rommene klare?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Almost. The reading room is being cleaned today, and the meeting room will be painted tomorrow." dialogue_id="library-reopening" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Nesten. Lesesalen blir vasket i dag, og møterommet blir malt i morgen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="What about the new signs?" dialogue_id="library-reopening" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Hva med de nye skiltene?
:::

::: {.reading translation="They will be put up after lunch." dialogue_id="library-reopening" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
De blir hengt opp etter lunsj.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And how do people return books while the main door is closed?" dialogue_id="library-reopening" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Og hvordan leverer folk bøker mens hoveddøra er stengt?
:::

::: {.reading translation="The books are returned through the hatch next to the entrance." dialogue_id="library-reopening" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Bøkene leveres i luka ved siden av inngangen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Should we write who empties the hatch?" dialogue_id="library-reopening" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Skal vi skrive hvem som tømmer luka?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, that is not important to the public. The hatch is emptied every evening." dialogue_id="library-reopening" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Nei, det er ikke viktig for publikum. Luka tømmes hver kveld.
:::

::: {.reading translation="All right. Then I’ll also write that library cards are ordered online." dialogue_id="library-reopening" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Greit. Da skriver jeg også at lånekort bestilles på nettet.
:::

The dialogue describes actions without always naming the person who performs them. Readers need to know what happens to the library, rooms, signs, books, and hatch. They do not usually need to know which employee does each job.

Norwegian commonly uses two passive patterns here:

::: examples
- no: Lesesalen blir vasket i dag.
- en: The reading room is being cleaned today.
- no: Luka tømmes hver kveld.
- en: The hatch is emptied every evening.
:::

Both sentences use the passive voice (passiv). In the passive patterns taught here, attention commonly falls on the affected thing or the event rather than the person performing the action.

## The main meaning choice {#main-meaning-choice role=model objectives=obj-passive-bli-and-s-01}

The two passive patterns often describe similar situations, but they do not always present them in the same way.

A passive with **bli** commonly presents an action as an event: something happens to a person or thing. It is especially natural when discussing a particular job, change, or occurrence.

::: examples
- no: Lesesalen blir vasket i dag.
- en: The reading room is being cleaned today.
- no: Skiltene blir hengt opp etter lunsj.
- en: The signs will be put up after lunch.
:::

An **s-passive** is often natural for routines, general procedures, notices, schedules, and concise instructions.

::: examples
- no: Luka tømmes hver kveld.
- en: The hatch is emptied every evening.
- no: Lånekort bestilles på nettet.
- en: Library cards are ordered online.
:::

A useful working rule is:

- Use **bli + past participle** when presenting a particular action or event.
- Use an **s-passive** when presenting a routine, procedure, instruction, or compact institutional statement.

This is a strong practical tendency, not an absolute division. In some contexts, both forms are possible. The speaker’s choice affects what is foregrounded.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Døra åpnes hver morgen klokka ti.
- en: The door is opened every morning at ten.
- no: Døra blir åpnet i morgen klokka ti.
- en: The door will be opened tomorrow at ten.
:::

The affected noun and action are the same. **Hver morgen** presents a regular procedure, while **i morgen** presents one particular opening event.

## Forming the s-passive {#forming-s-passive role=model objectives=obj-passive-bli-and-s-01}

For many verbs whose infinitive ends in **-e**, replace that final **-e** with **-es**:

::: examples
- no: å vaske → vaskes
- en: to wash → is washed / are washed
- no: å åpne → åpnes
- en: to open → is opened / are opened
- no: å levere → leveres
- en: to deliver or return → is delivered or returned
:::

You can also recognize the form by starting with the ordinary present tense, removing final **-r**, and adding **-s**:

::: examples
- no: åpner → åpnes
- en: opens → is opened
- no: bestiller → bestilles
- en: orders → is ordered
- no: tømmer → tømmes
- en: empties → is emptied
:::

The s-passive does not change according to whether the affected noun is singular or plural:

::: examples
- no: Boka leveres i skranken.
- en: The book is returned at the desk.
- no: Bøkene leveres i skranken.
- en: The books are returned at the desk.
:::

This pattern is common in public information because it is short and impersonal.

::: examples
- no: Dørene åpnes klokka ti.
- en: The doors are opened at ten.
- no: Mat og drikke betales i kassen.
- en: Food and drink are paid for at the till.
- no: Skjemaet sendes elektronisk.
- en: The form is submitted electronically.
- no: Mobiltelefoner slås av før møtet.
- en: Mobile phones are switched off before the meeting.
:::

The last sentence is formally a statement, but in context it functions as an instruction.

**Common mistake: keeping the present-tense r**

Do not add **-s** directly to a present-tense form ending in **-r**.

::: examples
- no: ✗ Dørene åpners klokka ti.
- en: Intended meaning: The doors are opened at ten.
:::

The correct form is:

::: examples
- no: Dørene åpnes klokka ti.
- en: The doors are opened at ten.
:::

Think **å åpne → åpnes**, or **åpner → remove r → åpnes**.

## Forming the passive with bli {#forming-passive-with-bli role=model objectives=obj-passive-bli-and-s-01}

The bli-passive uses a form of **bli** followed by a past participle (perfektum partisipp):

**subject + blir/ble + past participle**

Use **blir** for a present or anticipated event:

::: examples
- no: Gulvet blir vasket nå.
- en: The floor is being washed now.
- no: Pakken blir sendt i ettermiddag.
- en: The package will be sent this afternoon.
- no: Vinduene blir skiftet neste uke.
- en: The windows will be replaced next week.
:::

Use **ble** when the event happened in the past:

::: examples
- no: Gulvet ble vasket i går.
- en: The floor was washed yesterday.
- no: Pakken ble sendt på fredag.
- en: The package was sent on Friday.
- no: Vinduene ble skiftet i fjor.
- en: The windows were replaced last year.
:::

Frequent participles include:

::: examples
- no: vasket
- en: washed
- no: åpnet
- en: opened
- no: sendt
- en: sent
- no: skrevet
- en: written
- no: gjort
- en: done
- no: hengt opp
- en: put up
:::

The form of **bli** carries the tense. The participle stays in its passive construction form:

::: examples
- no: Rommet blir malt.
- en: The room is being painted.
- no: Rommene blir malt.
- en: The rooms are being painted.
- no: Rommet ble malt.
- en: The room was painted.
:::

**Common mistake: using two passive markers**

Do not combine **bli** with an s-passive verb.

::: examples
- no: ✗ Rommet blir males.
- en: Intended meaning: The room is being painted.
:::

Use a past participle after **bli**:

::: examples
- no: Rommet blir malt.
- en: The room is being painted.
:::

Alternatively, use the s-passive without **bli** when the context calls for it:

::: examples
- no: Rommet males hvert tredje år.
- en: The room is painted every three years.
:::

## Routine or particular event? {#routine-or-particular-event role=contrast objectives=obj-passive-bli-and-s-01}

Now compare the two patterns more closely.

**Routine**

::: examples
- no: Avisene legges ut hver morgen.
- en: The newspapers are put out every morning.
:::

The time expression **hver morgen** signals a repeated routine. The s-passive is concise and natural.

**Particular event**

::: examples
- no: De nye avisstativene blir satt ut i morgen.
- en: The new newspaper racks will be put out tomorrow.
:::

This is one planned event involving particular racks. The bli-passive foregrounds what will happen to them.

**General instruction**

::: examples
- no: Kvitteringen kontrolleres ved utgangen.
- en: The receipt is checked at the exit.
:::

This tells customers what procedure to follow. It does not matter which employee checks the receipt.

**Event report**

::: examples
- no: Kvitteringen ble kontrollert ved utgangen.
- en: The receipt was checked at the exit.
:::

This reports one completed event.

**Regular handling**

::: examples
- no: E-post besvares innen to arbeidsdager.
- en: Email is answered within two working days.
:::

**One message**

::: examples
- no: Meldingen din blir besvart i morgen.
- en: Your message will be answered tomorrow.
:::

The difference is not simply plural versus singular. A singular noun can occur in a routine:

::: examples
- no: Luka tømmes hver kveld.
- en: The hatch is emptied every evening.
:::

A plural noun can occur in a particular event:

::: examples
- no: De nye skiltene blir hengt opp i dag.
- en: The new signs will be put up today.
:::

Look instead at meaning and context. Routines and instructions often favor the s-passive, while particular occurrences often favor the bli-passive. However, an s-passive can also be natural for a particular scheduled occurrence in a schedule, notice, or compact institutional statement, as in **Biblioteket åpnes igjen på onsdag**.

## Leaving out an irrelevant agent {#leaving-out-irrelevant-agent role=model objectives=obj-passive-bli-and-s-02}

An active sentence names the person or group performing the action:

::: examples
- no: Renholderne vasker lesesalen i dag.
- en: The cleaners are washing the reading room today.
:::

A passive sentence can omit that information:

::: examples
- no: Lesesalen blir vasket i dag.
- en: The reading room is being cleaned today.
:::

The passive version is useful because the update concerns the room and the reopening plan. The identity of the cleaners adds little.

Similarly:

::: examples
- no: De ansatte tømmer luka hver kveld.
- en: The employees empty the hatch every evening.
- no: Luka tømmes hver kveld.
- en: The hatch is emptied every evening.
:::

The second sentence is more suitable for a public notice. Visitors need the routine, not the staff assignment.

Do not remove a person merely because a passive is grammatically possible. Remove the agent when that person is unknown, obvious, or irrelevant to the message.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Kommunen stenger veien på mandag.
- en: The municipality is closing the road on Monday.
- no: Veien blir stengt på mandag.
- en: The road will be closed on Monday.
:::

The active sentence may be useful when responsibility matters. The passive sentence is natural when road users mainly need to know what will happen.

**Success demonstrates:** Appropriate omission of an irrelevant agent. This activity scores information focus; passive-pattern choice supports the rewrite but is not the primary evidence goal.

## A changed situation: opening a café {#cafe-opening-transfer role=recap objectives=obj-passive-bli-and-s-01,obj-passive-bli-and-s-02}

The same grammar is useful when a new café is preparing to open.

A general customer procedure may use the s-passive:

::: examples
- no: Mat bestilles ved disken.
- en: Food is ordered at the counter.
- no: Kaffe serveres fra klokka sju.
- en: Coffee is served from seven o’clock.
- no: Bord ryddes etter bruk.
- en: Tables are cleared after use.
:::

A particular preparation event may use **bli**:

::: examples
- no: Det nye skiltet blir hengt opp i kveld.
- en: The new sign will be put up this evening.
- no: Kaffemaskinen blir installert i morgen.
- en: The coffee machine will be installed tomorrow.
- no: Kjøkkenet ble kontrollert på fredag.
- en: The kitchen was inspected on Friday.
:::

Notice the transfer from the library context. The vocabulary has changed, but the meaning choice has not:

- routine, procedure, or instruction → often s-passive
- particular present, future, or past event → often bli-passive
- irrelevant agent → normally omitted

**Cumulative transfer request**

**Request: create-cafe-opening-update**

**Purpose:** Transfer the target grammar to a new everyday setting without copying the library dialogue.

**Context:** A café manager needs one short public notice and one conversational internal opening update. The public notice contains recurring routines and direct customer procedures. The internal update reports particular jobs happening now or already completed, rather than presenting a schedule or institutional notice.

**Learner action:** Form natural passive sentences. Use s-passives for the recurring public procedures and bli-passives for the conversational event updates. Omit workers when their identities do not matter.

**Success demonstrates:** Independent choice and formation of both passive patterns in contexts that strongly favor the practised prototypes. Another idiomatic passive is accepted if a sentence can naturally be read in a different register or framing.
