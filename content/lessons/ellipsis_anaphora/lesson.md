## A planning meeting at the community centre {#planning-meeting role=orient objectives=obj-ellipsis-anaphora-01}

Mina and Eirik are preparing a neighbourhood dinner at their local community centre. Most practical decisions have already been discussed, but several jobs remain.

::: {.reading translation="Have you ordered the food?" dialogue_id="planning_meeting" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Har du bestilt maten?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, but I’ll do it this afternoon." dialogue_id="planning_meeting" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Nei, men jeg gjør det i ettermiddag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. I can collect the key if you can’t." dialogue_id="planning_meeting" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Flott. Jeg kan hente nøkkelen hvis du ikke kan.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thanks. We also need to set up the tables. Can we do it the same way as last time?" dialogue_id="planning_meeting" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Takk. Vi må også sette opp bordene. Kan vi gjøre det slik som sist?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, that worked well. Long tables along the walls and small tables in the middle." dialogue_id="planning_meeting" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja, det fungerte godt. Lange bord langs veggene og små bord i midten.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. I always get a little stressed before events like this." dialogue_id="planning_meeting" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Bra. Jeg blir alltid litt stresset før slike arrangementer.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Same here. But it usually goes well." dialogue_id="planning_meeting" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Samme her. Men det pleier å gå bra.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Shall we ask Nora to welcome the guests?" dialogue_id="planning_meeting" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Skal vi be Nora om å ta imot gjestene?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Gladly. She knows almost everyone." dialogue_id="planning_meeting" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Gjerne. Hun kjenner nesten alle.
:::

This dialogue contains several ways of avoiding unnecessary repetition. When Eirik says jeg gjør det, he does not repeat bestiller maten. When Mina says hvis du ikke kan, she leaves out hente nøkkelen. Expressions such as det, gjøre det, gjerne, samme her and slik help speakers connect each new sentence to something already understood.

The goal is not to make every sentence as short as possible. The goal is to say enough for the meaning to remain clear, without repeating information that both speakers already have.

## Leaving understood words out {#omission role=model objectives=obj-ellipsis-anaphora-01}

Omission means leaving out words whose meaning can be recovered from the context. In Norwegian, this is especially common when a verb phrase has just been mentioned.

Mina says:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan hente nøkkelen hvis du ikke kan.
- en: I can collect the key if you can’t.
:::

The full meaning is:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan hente nøkkelen hvis du ikke kan hente nøkkelen.
- en: I can collect the key if you can’t collect the key.
:::

The repeated words hente nøkkelen are unnecessary. The modal verb kan is enough to carry the contrast between Mina’s ability and Eirik’s ability.

Compare these aligned examples:

::: examples
- no: Nora kan ta imot gjestene, men Lars kan ikke.
- en: Nora can welcome the guests, but Lars can’t.
- no: Jeg skal rydde kjøkkenet hvis ingen andre skal.
- en: I’ll clean the kitchen if nobody else will.
- no: Hun ville bli lenger, men vi ville ikke.
- en: She wanted to stay longer, but we didn’t.
- no: De har allerede betalt, men det har ikke vi.
- en: They have already paid, but we haven’t.
:::

The omitted material must be easy to identify. If two different actions are being discussed, omission may produce uncertainty.

::: examples
- no: Eirik kan hente nøkkelen, og Mina kan bestille maten.
- en: Eirik can collect the key, and Mina can order the food.
:::

A continuation such as men Nora kan ikke is unclear here. Can Nora not collect the key, order the food, or do either task? Repeating the relevant action is better:

::: examples
- no: Eirik kan hente nøkkelen, men Nora kan ikke hente den.
- en: Eirik can collect the key, but Nora can’t collect it.
:::

**Checkpoint: recovering omitted meaning**

Take short exchanges from the planning meeting and identify the words that could remain unspoken. Then produce a natural shortened response rather than repeating the complete verb phrase. This activity belongs here because omission is safe only when the listener can recover one clear meaning from the immediate context. A successful response would retain the necessary modal or auxiliary verb and omit only information already shared.

## Using det to point back {#det-reference role=model objectives=obj-ellipsis-anaphora-01}

The pronoun det often points back to a fact, statement, situation or previously mentioned matter. Its meaning comes from the surrounding conversation.

In the dialogue, Mina says:

::: examples
- no: Ja, det fungerte godt.
- en: Yes, that worked well.
:::

Here det points back to the previous table arrangement. Mina does not need to describe the whole arrangement again.

Later she says:

::: examples
- no: Men det pleier å gå bra.
- en: But it usually goes well.
:::

Here det refers more broadly to the event and the preparations.

More examples:

::: examples
- no: Møtet er flyttet til fredag. Visste du det?
- en: The meeting has been moved to Friday. Did you know that?
- no: Alle gjestene kom i tide, og det overrasket meg.
- en: All the guests arrived on time, and that surprised me.
- no: Vi mangler seks stoler. Jeg skal snakke med vaktmesteren om det.
- en: We are six chairs short. I’ll speak to the caretaker about it.
- no: Nora tror at det blir fullt. Jeg tror også det.
- en: Nora thinks it will be full. I think so too.
:::

In these examples, det does not simply replace one nearby noun. It packages an idea so the speaker can react to it, know it, discuss it or believe it.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg fant nøkkelen. Den lå på kontoret.
- en: I found the key. It was in the office.
- no: Jeg fant nøkkelen. Det var en lettelse.
- en: I found the key. That was a relief.
:::

In the first pair, den refers directly to the masculine noun nøkkelen. In the second, det points back to the whole fact that the key was found. This chapter focuses on the second kind of reference: using det to point back to shared content rather than repeating it.

**Checkpoint: packaging a fact with det**

Read a short statement about the dinner preparations, then add a reaction using det. The reaction might express knowledge, surprise, concern or relief. This activity follows the first explanation because the learner must now move beyond omitting a repeated action and learn to treat a whole fact as shared information. A successful response would make det refer clearly to the preceding statement without restating that statement.

## Using gjøre det for a repeated action {#gjore-det role=contrast objectives=obj-ellipsis-anaphora-01}

Gjør det combines gjøre, meaning do, with det. Together, gjøre det can stand in for an action that has already been named.

::: examples
- no: Har du bestilt maten?
- en: Have you ordered the food?
- no: Nei, men jeg gjør det i ettermiddag.
- en: No, but I’ll do it this afternoon.
:::

The action is bestille maten. Eirik refers back to that action with gjør det.

The form of gjøre changes with time:

::: examples
- no: Kan du sende deltakerlisten? Jeg gjør det nå.
- en: Can you send the participant list? I’ll do it now.
- no: Hvem flyttet bordene? Nora gjorde det.
- en: Who moved the tables? Nora did it.
- no: Har noen ringt vaktmesteren? Jeg har gjort det.
- en: Has anyone called the caretaker? I have done it.
- no: Du må låse døra etterpå. Jeg skal gjøre det.
- en: You have to lock the door afterwards. I’ll do it.
:::

Using gjøre det often makes the repeated action more explicit than simple omission.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Kan du hente nøkkelen? Ja, det kan jeg.
- en: Can you collect the key? Yes, I can.
- no: Kan du hente nøkkelen? Ja, jeg skal gjøre det etter jobb.
- en: Can you collect the key? Yes, I’ll do it after work.
:::

The first response confirms ability. The second presents the action as a task and adds when it will happen.

Det and gjøre det are related, but they are not interchangeable in every sentence. Det alone can point back to a fact:

::: examples
- no: Arrangementet er avlyst. Jeg vet det.
- en: The event has been cancelled. I know that.
:::

Gjør det points back to an action:

::: examples
- no: Kan du avlyse arrangementet? Jeg gjør det nå.
- en: Can you cancel the event? I’ll do it now.
:::

A useful question is: Am I referring to information or carrying out an action? Information often allows det. A repeated action often allows gjøre det.

**Checkpoint: choosing between det and gjøre det**

Continue brief planning exchanges by referring either to a fact or to an action. Use det when someone responds to information, and an appropriately inflected form of gjøre det when someone undertakes or reports an action. This contrast belongs here because both forms point backward, but they perform different jobs in conversation. A successful response would preserve that distinction and use present, past or perfect tense naturally where the context requires it.

## Using gjerne as an accepting response {#gjerne role=contrast objectives=obj-ellipsis-anaphora-01}

Gjerne means gladly or willingly. It is often used as a compact positive response to an invitation, offer or suggestion. The rest of the answer is understood.

In the opening dialogue, Mina responds to a suggestion:

::: examples
- no: Skal vi be Nora om å ta imot gjestene?
- en: Shall we ask Nora to welcome the guests?
- no: Gjerne. Hun kjenner nesten alle.
- en: Gladly. She knows almost everyone.
:::

Depending on the context, the fuller meaning might be ja, la oss gjerne gjøre det or ja, det vil jeg gjerne. The short response sounds natural because the proposed action has just been stated.

::: examples
- no: Vil du ha litt kaffe? Gjerne.
- en: Would you like some coffee? Yes, please.
- no: Kan du hjelpe meg med stolene? Ja, gjerne.
- en: Can you help me with the chairs? Yes, gladly.
- no: Skal vi gå gjennom planen én gang til? Gjerne.
- en: Shall we go through the plan once more? Gladly.
:::

Gjerne expresses willing acceptance, not merely agreement with a fact.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Det blir mange gjester i kveld. Ja, det tror jeg også.
- en: There will be many guests tonight. Yes, I think so too.
- no: Skal vi invitere noen flere? Ja, gjerne.
- en: Shall we invite a few more people? Yes, gladly.
:::

The first exchange concerns a belief. The second contains a suggestion that the speaker willingly accepts.

**Checkpoint: accepting without repetition**

Respond briefly to offers, invitations and suggestions connected with the community dinner. Avoid repeating the full proposed action. This activity belongs after gjøre det because the learner now practises a more socially expressive way of leaving the action unstated. A successful response would use gjerne only where willing acceptance makes sense, not as a general substitute for yes.

{{exercise: accept_with_gjerne}}

## Using samme her to share a reaction {#samme-her role=contrast objectives=obj-ellipsis-anaphora-01}

Samme her means same here. It lets a speaker say that the preceding description, feeling, preference or experience also applies to them.

In the dialogue:

::: examples
- no: Jeg blir alltid litt stresset før slike arrangementer.
- en: I always get a little stressed before events like this.
- no: Samme her.
- en: Same here.
:::

The second speaker avoids repeating:

::: examples
- no: Jeg blir også alltid litt stresset før slike arrangementer.
- en: I also always get a little stressed before events like this.
:::

More examples:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er ganske sliten etter all planleggingen.
- en: I’m quite tired after all the planning.
- no: Samme her.
- en: Same here.
- no: Jeg foretrekker små arrangementer.
- en: I prefer small events.
- no: Samme her.
- en: Same here.
- no: Jeg har aldri brukt dette kjøkkenet før.
- en: I’ve never used this kitchen before.
- no: Samme her.
- en: Same here.
:::

Samme her normally shares the first speaker’s position. It does not accept a request.

::: examples
- no: Jeg er sulten. Samme her.
- en: I’m hungry. Same here.
- no: Kan du skjære brødet? Ja, gjerne.
- en: Can you slice the bread? Yes, gladly.
:::

Using samme her in the second exchange would not clearly answer whether the speaker will perform the task.

**Checkpoint: sharing rather than accepting**

Read remarks from volunteers about their feelings, preferences and experiences. Respond where the same statement applies to you, without reproducing the full sentence. Then distinguish these remarks from requests that require an accepting response. This activity belongs here because samme her and gjerne are both compact social responses, but they connect to different kinds of preceding turns. A successful response would show whether the speaker is sharing someone’s situation or willingly accepting a proposed action.

{{exercise: share_reaction_with_samme_her}}

## Using slik to point to a way of doing something {#slik role=contrast objectives=obj-ellipsis-anaphora-01}

Slik means in this way, in that way or like this, depending on the context. It points back to a method, arrangement or manner rather than repeating its details.

Eirik asks:

::: examples
- no: Kan vi gjøre det slik som sist?
- en: Can we do it the same way as last time?
:::

The details of the previous arrangement are already known to Mina. Slik points to that way of arranging the room.

::: examples
- no: Sett de store bordene langs veggen. Gjør det slik.
- en: Put the large tables along the wall. Do it like this.
- no: Først teller vi gjestene, og deretter bestiller vi maten. Slik unngår vi å bestille for mye.
- en: First we count the guests, and then we order the food. In this way, we avoid ordering too much.
- no: Nora skrev navnene på små kort og la dem ved tallerkenene. Vi kan organisere plassene slik igjen.
- en: Nora wrote the names on small cards and placed them by the plates. We can organize the seating that way again.
:::

Notice the different reference in this pair:

::: examples
- no: Vi satte bordene langs veggene. Det fungerte godt.
- en: We put the tables along the walls. That worked well.
- no: Vi satte bordene langs veggene. Vi bør gjøre det slik igjen.
- en: We put the tables along the walls. We should do it that way again.
:::

Det in the first example points back to the arrangement as a fact or result. Slik in the second points specifically to the manner in which the task was done.

Slik can also introduce a method before the details are given:

::: examples
- no: Vi gjør det slik: Du tar imot gjestene, og jeg viser dem hvor de skal sitte.
- en: We’ll do it like this: You welcome the guests, and I’ll show them where to sit.
:::

Here slik points forward to an explanation. Its function is still to represent a way of doing something.

**Checkpoint: referring to a method**

Describe a simple method for arranging the room or welcoming guests. Then refer to that method later with slik rather than repeating all its steps. This activity belongs here because the learner has already practised pointing back to facts and actions; the new focus is specifically manner. A successful response would make slik refer to a recognizable procedure or arrangement.

## Choosing the right compact form {#compact-forms role=contrast objectives=obj-ellipsis-anaphora-01}

All the target forms reduce repetition, but they do not carry identical meanings.

**First speaker’s contribution**

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan hente nøkkelen.
- en: I can collect the key.
:::

**Natural continuation**

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan også.
- en: I can too.
:::

**What the continuation does:** Omits a clearly understood action.

**First speaker’s contribution**

::: examples
- no: Møtet begynner klokka seks.
- en: The meeting starts at six.
:::

**Natural continuation**

::: examples
- no: Jeg vet det.
- en: I know that.
:::

**What the continuation does:** Refers to information.

**First speaker’s contribution**

::: examples
- no: Noen må ringe Nora.
- en: Someone must call Nora.
:::

**Natural continuation**

::: examples
- no: Jeg gjør det.
- en: I’ll do it.
:::

**What the continuation does:** Takes responsibility for an action.

**First speaker’s contribution**

::: examples
- no: Vil du bli med?
- en: Would you like to come?
:::

**Natural continuation**

::: examples
- no: Gjerne.
- en: Gladly.
:::

**What the continuation does:** Accepts willingly.

**First speaker’s contribution**

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker best små grupper.
- en: I prefer small groups.
:::

**Natural continuation**

::: examples
- no: Samme her.
- en: Same here.
:::

**What the continuation does:** Shares the same view.

**First speaker’s contribution**

::: examples
- no: Legg serviettene under tallerkenene.
- en: Put the napkins under the plates.
:::

**Natural continuation**

::: examples
- no: Skal jeg gjøre det slik?
- en: Shall I do it like this?
:::

**What the continuation does:** Refers to manner.

More than one short form may sometimes be grammatically possible, but the choice changes what the speaker highlights.

::: examples
- no: Kan du sende invitasjonen?
- en: Can you send the invitation?
- no: Ja, det kan jeg.
- en: Yes, I can.
:::

This confirms ability.

::: examples
- no: Ja, jeg gjør det i kveld.
- en: Yes, I’ll do it tonight.
:::

This commits to the action.

::: examples
- no: Ja, gjerne.
- en: Yes, gladly.
:::

This accepts the request willingly.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-ellipsis-anaphora-01}

**Omitting too much**

Omission fails when the listener cannot identify the missing action.

Unclear:

::: examples
- no: Mina skal hente maten, og Eirik skal låse døra, men Nora skal ikke.
- en: Mina will collect the food, and Eirik will lock the door, but Nora won’t.
:::

Clearer:

::: examples
- no: Mina skal hente maten, men Nora skal ikke hente den.
- en: Mina will collect the food, but Nora won’t collect it.
:::

**Repeating gjøre unnecessarily**

Avoid combining gjøre det with a repeated full action.

Awkward:

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal gjøre det bestille maten i kveld.
- en: Incorrect: I’m going to do it order the food tonight.
:::

Natural alternatives:

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal bestille maten i kveld.
- en: I’m going to order the food tonight.
- no: Jeg skal gjøre det i kveld.
- en: I’m going to do it tonight.
:::

**Using gjerne for shared feelings**

Gjerne accepts something willingly; it does not mean that the same description applies to you.

::: examples
- no: Jeg er nervøs. Samme her.
- en: I’m nervous. Same here.
- no: Vil du hjelpe til? Gjerne.
- en: Would you like to help? Gladly.
:::

**Using samme her for a request**

Samme her shares a statement. It does not normally promise action.

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker denne planen. Samme her.
- en: I like this plan. Same here.
- no: Kan du følge denne planen? Ja, det kan jeg.
- en: Can you follow this plan? Yes, I can.
:::

**Using det when manner is central**

Det can refer broadly to an event or idea. Use slik when the important meaning is how something is done.

::: examples
- no: Vi organiserte bordene i små grupper. Det var praktisk.
- en: We organized the tables in small groups. That was practical.
- no: Vi organiserte bordene i små grupper. La oss gjøre det slik igjen.
- en: We organized the tables in small groups. Let’s do it that way again.
:::

## Back at the community centre {#back-at-community-centre role=model objectives=obj-ellipsis-anaphora-01}

On the afternoon of the dinner, Mina and Eirik meet again.

::: {.reading translation="Did you manage to order the food?" dialogue_id="afternoon_centre" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Fikk du bestilt maten?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I did it yesterday. They’re delivering at five." dialogue_id="afternoon_centre" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Ja, jeg gjorde det i går. De leverer klokka fem.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. Nora has offered to receive the delivery if we can’t." dialogue_id="afternoon_centre" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Bra. Nora har tilbudt seg å ta imot leveransen hvis vi ikke kan.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That was kind of her. Do you have a plan for the tables?" dialogue_id="afternoon_centre" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Det var fint av henne. Har du en plan for bordene?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. We’ll do it like this: The long tables go by the walls, and the small ones go by the windows." dialogue_id="afternoon_centre" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja. Vi gjør det slik: De lange bordene står ved veggene, og de små står ved vinduene.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Shall I start with the small ones?" dialogue_id="afternoon_centre" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Flott. Skal jeg begynne med de små?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, please. I’ll take the long ones." dialogue_id="afternoon_centre" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Gjerne. Jeg tar de lange.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I hope we finish everything before the guests arrive." dialogue_id="afternoon_centre" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Jeg håper vi rekker alt før gjestene kommer.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Same here. But if we don’t, Nora can help us." dialogue_id="afternoon_centre" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Samme her. Men hvis vi ikke gjør det, kan Nora hjelpe oss.
:::

The conversation remains clear even though several phrases are not repeated. Gjorde det refers to ordering the food. Hvis vi ikke kan leaves out ta imot leveransen. Det var fint refers to Nora’s offer to receive the delivery. Slik introduces the table arrangement. Gjerne accepts an offer, and samme her shares Eirik’s hope.

**Integrated practice: making a conversation cohesive**

Rewrite a short planning conversation in which every speaker repeats complete actions, facts and reactions. Replace only the unnecessary repetition with omission, det, gjøre det, gjerne, samme her or slik. Keep enough detail for each reference to remain unambiguous. This final practice belongs here because it requires choosing among all the target forms inside one believable exchange rather than treating them as isolated grammar items. A successful conversation would sound connected and economical while remaining immediately understandable.
