## A cabin that might have been {#a-cabin-that-might-have-been role=orient objectives=obj-irrealis-constructions-01}

Maja and Eirik are considering a weekend in the mountains. Some possibilities are still open, while others belong to a missed opportunity.

::: {.reading translation="Have you seen the cabin that Nora sent us?" dialogue_id="cabin-that-might-have-been" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Har du sett hytta som Nora sendte oss?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but I’m working on Friday, and my car is at the garage." dialogue_id="cabin-that-might-have-been" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Ja, men jeg jobber på fredag, og bilen min er på verksted.
:::

::: {.reading translation="If I had Friday off, I would book it immediately." dialogue_id="cabin-that-might-have-been" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Hvis jeg hadde fri på fredag, ville jeg bestille den med én gang.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And if we had a car, we could drive up before it got dark." dialogue_id="cabin-that-might-have-been" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Og hvis vi hadde bil, kunne vi kjøre opp før det ble mørkt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="We waited too long last time. If we had booked earlier, we would have got the cheap cabin." dialogue_id="cabin-that-might-have-been" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Vi ventet for lenge sist. Hvis vi hadde bestilt tidligere, ville vi ha fått den billige hytta.
:::

::: {.reading translation="If my car hadn’t been at the garage, we could have travelled then too." dialogue_id="cabin-that-might-have-been" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Hvis bilen min ikke hadde vært på verksted, kunne vi ha reist da også.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Perhaps we can plan better this time. If my boss gave me time off, would you come along?" dialogue_id="cabin-that-might-have-been" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Denne gangen kan vi kanskje planlegge bedre. Hvis sjefen ga meg fri, ville du bli med?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I would." dialogue_id="cabin-that-might-have-been" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Ja, det ville jeg.
:::

Maja and Eirik use two closely related patterns. They imagine a different present or future: Maja does not currently have Friday off, but she imagines what she would do if she did. They also look back at a different past: they did not book early enough, but they imagine the result of an earlier booking.

The central choice is therefore about time. Are you imagining a different situation now or later, or are you imagining a different past?

## Creating distance from reality {#creating-distance-from-reality role=model objectives=obj-irrealis-constructions-01}

An ordinary condition can present something as a real possibility.

::: examples
- no: Hvis jeg får fri, bestiller jeg hytta.
- en: If I get time off, I’ll book the cabin.
:::

The speaker considers getting time off a genuine possibility. To make the situation more hypothetical, Norwegian commonly moves the verb into the past tense (preteritum), even when the imagined situation concerns the present or future.

::: examples
- no: Hvis jeg fikk fri, ville jeg bestille hytta.
- en: If I got time off, I would book the cabin.
:::

Here fikk does not mean that the speaker received time off in the past. Its past form creates distance from present reality. The speaker is imagining what would happen under a condition that is uncertain, unlikely, or currently untrue.

The result normally uses ville followed by an infinitive:

::: examples
- no: ville bestille
- en: would book
:::

The basic pattern is:

**hvis + past tense, ville + infinitive**

::: examples
- no: Hvis jeg bodde nærmere, ville jeg besøke deg oftere.
- en: If I lived closer, I would visit you more often.
- no: Hvis leiligheten var større, ville vi invitere flere gjester.
- en: If the apartment were bigger, we would invite more guests.
- no: Hvis hun hadde mer tid, ville hun lære norsk.
- en: If she had more time, she would learn Norwegian.
:::

In the last example, hadde is the past tense of ha. It refers to an imagined present situation: she does not have enough time now.

## Real possibility and hypothetical situation {#real-possibility-and-hypothetical-situation role=contrast objectives=obj-irrealis-constructions-01}

Compare the meanings carefully:

::: examples
- no: Hvis toget går klokka seks, tar vi det.
- en: If the train leaves at six, we’ll take it.
- no: Hvis toget gikk klokka seks, ville vi ta det.
- en: If the train left at six, we would take it.
:::

The first sentence treats the departure time as an open factual question. The second imagines a schedule that may not be real.

Another contrast:

::: examples
- no: Hvis jeg finner en billig billett, reiser jeg på fredag.
- en: If I find a cheap ticket, I’ll travel on Friday.
- no: Hvis jeg fant en billig billett, ville jeg reise på fredag.
- en: If I found a cheap ticket, I would travel on Friday.
:::

The past tense in the second Norwegian sentence does not automatically locate the search in the past. Together with ville, it marks the situation as hypothetical.

## What would happen? {#what-would-happen role=model objectives=obj-irrealis-constructions-01}

Ville describes the imagined result. Other modal verbs can appear when their meanings are needed. In the cabin dialogue, Eirik says:

::: examples
- no: Hvis vi hadde bil, kunne vi kjøre opp.
- en: If we had a car, we could drive up.
:::

Kunne expresses an imagined possibility or ability. Compare it with ville:

::: examples
- no: Hvis vi hadde bil, ville vi kjøre opp.
- en: If we had a car, we would drive up.
- no: Hvis vi hadde bil, kunne vi kjøre opp.
- en: If we had a car, we could drive up.
:::

The first sentence tells us what they would do. The second tells us what having a car would make possible. Both conditions use the same hypothetical past form.

In these hvis-subordinate clauses, ikke normally comes before the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: Hvis jeg ikke jobbet på fredag, ville jeg bli med.
- en: If I weren’t working on Friday, I would come along.
- no: Hvis hytta ikke var så dyr, ville vi bestille den.
- en: If the cabin weren’t so expensive, we would book it.
:::

As a brief word-order reminder, when the hvis-clause comes first, the following main clause keeps normal Norwegian inversion: the verb comes before the subject.

::: examples
- no: Hvis jeg fikk fri, ville jeg bestille hytta.
- en: If I got time off, I would book the cabin.
:::

The clauses can also change places:

::: examples
- no: Jeg ville bestille hytta hvis jeg fikk fri.
- en: I would book the cabin if I got time off.
:::

The meaning remains hypothetical. Only the information flow changes. This prerequisite word order supports the examples but is not a separate goal here.

## Imagining a different past {#imagining-a-different-past role=model objectives=obj-irrealis-constructions-01}

Maja and Eirik also discuss their previous failed trip:

::: examples
- no: Hvis vi hadde bestilt tidligere, ville vi ha fått den billige hytta.
- en: If we had booked earlier, we would have got the cheap cabin.
:::

This is not an uncertain future possibility. They already know what happened: they booked too late and did not get the cheap cabin.

For a condition contrary to a known past, Norwegian uses the past perfect (pluskvamperfektum): hadde + past participle.

::: examples
- no: hadde bestilt
- en: had booked
:::

The clear full form practiced here for the imagined past result is ville ha + past participle.

::: examples
- no: ville ha fått
- en: would have got
:::

The full pattern practiced here is:

**hvis + hadde + past participle, ville ha + past participle**

::: examples
- no: Hvis jeg hadde stått opp tidligere, ville jeg ha rukket bussen.
- en: If I had got up earlier, I would have caught the bus.
:::

The actual facts are the opposite: I did not get up early enough, and I did not catch the bus.

::: examples
- no: Hvis de hadde sjekket adressen, ville de ha funnet hotellet.
- en: If they had checked the address, they would have found the hotel.
:::

They did not check the address, and they did not find the hotel.

::: examples
- no: Hvis været hadde vært bedre, ville vi ha gått hele veien.
- en: If the weather had been better, we would have walked the whole way.
:::

The weather was not better, and they did not walk the whole way.

Kunne can again express an unrealized possibility:

::: examples
- no: Hvis du hadde ringt meg, kunne jeg ha hjulpet deg.
- en: If you had called me, I could have helped you.
- no: Hvis vi hadde hatt mer tid, kunne vi ha besøkt museet.
- en: If we had had more time, we could have visited the museum.
:::

The sequence hadde hatt may look unusual, but each word has a different job. The first hadde forms the past perfect; hatt is the past participle of ha.

## Present hypothesis or past counterfactual? {#present-hypothesis-or-past-counterfactual role=contrast objectives=obj-irrealis-constructions-01}

Compare the two time frames:

::: examples
- no: Hvis jeg hadde mer tid, ville jeg lese boka.
- en: If I had more time, I would read the book.
- no: Hvis jeg hadde hatt mer tid, ville jeg ha lest boka.
- en: If I had had more time, I would have read the book.
:::

The first sentence concerns the speaker’s current situation. Hadde is a simple past form used to create hypothetical distance.

The second concerns a completed period in the past. Hadde hatt is past perfect, and ville ha lest marks the unreal past result.

Another aligned contrast:

::: examples
- no: Hvis bilen virket, ville vi kjøre til hytta.
- en: If the car worked, we would drive to the cabin.
- no: Hvis bilen hadde virket, ville vi ha kjørt til hytta.
- en: If the car had worked, we would have driven to the cabin.
:::

In the first sentence, the trip remains imagined in the present or future. In the second, the opportunity to make the trip has already passed.

## Checkpoint request: build-past-counterfactual-condition {#checkpoint-build-past-counterfactual-condition role=contrast objectives=obj-irrealis-constructions-01}

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-irrealis-constructions-01}

**Do not copy English would into the if-clause**

English learners sometimes place ville in both clauses.

Incorrect for the intended ordinary hypothetical meaning:

::: examples
- no: Hvis jeg ville få fri, ville jeg bestille hytta.
- en: 'Not: If I would get time off, I would book the cabin. (for the intended ordinary hypothetical meaning)'
:::

Use the past tense in the condition:

::: examples
- no: Hvis jeg fikk fri, ville jeg bestille hytta.
- en: If I got time off, I would book the cabin.
:::

Norwegian can use ville in a hvis-clause when ville has its own meaning of willingness or intention, but that is not the basic pattern taught here. For an ordinary hypothetical condition, use fikk, hadde, var, bodde, or another appropriate past-tense verb.

**Prerequisite reminders in the target examples**

In the full unreal-past form practiced here, ha is the infinitive after a modal verb such as ville or kunne:

::: examples
- no: Hvis vi hadde bestilt tidligere, ville vi ha fått hytta.
- en: If we had booked earlier, we would have got the cabin.
:::

Normal inversion after an initial hvis-clause also remains visible in the examples:

::: examples
- no: Hvis jeg hadde mer penger, ville jeg reist oftere.
- en: If I had more money, I would travel more often.
:::

A fully explicit form with an infinitive is also natural:

::: examples
- no: Hvis jeg hadde mer penger, ville jeg reise oftere.
- en: If I had more money, I would travel more often.
:::

For this lesson, ville + infinitive provides the clearest core pattern. These prerequisite mechanics support the target examples but are not separate scored goals.

**Keep the intended time frame clear**

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Hvis hun hadde øvd mer, ville hun ha spilt bedre.
- en: If she had practised more, she would have played better.
:::

Both the practice and the performance belong to an unreal past. The full form with ha makes that relationship especially clear and is the form practiced here. Bokmål also accepts a result without overt ha:

::: examples
- no: Hvis hun hadde øvd mer, ville hun spilt bedre.
- en: If she had practised more, she would have played better.
:::

Omitting ha alone is therefore not an error.

## Checkpoint request: repair-irrealis-construction-errors {#checkpoint-repair-irrealis-construction-errors role=contrast objectives=obj-irrealis-constructions-01}

The learner should correct sentences containing one targeted irrealis error: ville used as English-style would in an ordinary hypothetical hvis-clause, a simple-past condition used for a known completed past, or a result placed in the wrong intended time frame. Each item should isolate one error so the activity scores the contrast between the taught hypothetical and counterfactual constructions. Finite modal complementation, general inversion, and omission of overt ha are not scored as errors here. Success demonstrates that the learner can select the construction matching the intended hypothetical time reference without relying on English word-for-word structure.

## Back to Maja and Eirik {#back-to-maja-and-eirik role=model objectives=obj-irrealis-constructions-01}

Later that afternoon, Maja receives a message from her manager.

::: {.reading translation="I can get Friday off, but only if I work longer on Thursday." dialogue_id="cabin-return-scene" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jeg kan få fri på fredag, men bare hvis jeg jobber lenger på torsdag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then we might have a real possibility. If the car were ready, we would be all set." dialogue_id="cabin-return-scene" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Da har vi kanskje en reell mulighet. Hvis bilen var ferdig, ville vi være klare.
:::

::: {.reading translation="The garage says it won’t be ready. If we had booked the train yesterday, the tickets would have been cheaper." dialogue_id="cabin-return-scene" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Verkstedet sier at den ikke blir ferdig. Hvis vi hadde bestilt toget i går, ville billettene ha vært billigere.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s true. But if we found cheap tickets now, I would still come along." dialogue_id="cabin-return-scene" speaker_id="eirik" speaker_name="Eirik" character_id="eirik"}
Det er sant. Men hvis vi fant billige billetter nå, ville jeg fortsatt bli med.
:::

The situation now contains three levels of reality. Maja’s agreement with her manager is a genuine condition. A finished car is hypothetical because the garage has said it will not be ready. Booking the train yesterday is impossible to change, so Maja uses the unreal-past pattern.
