## A weekend plan {#a-weekend-plan role=orient objectives=obj-bli-med-or-vaere-med-01}

Sara is arranging a weekend at a cabin with some friends. She calls Jonas while she is checking who is coming.

::: {.reading translation="We’re going to the cabin on Friday. Would you like to come along?" dialogue_id="weekend-plan" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Vi drar til hytta på fredag. Vil du bli med?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I’d love to. I’ll come!" dialogue_id="weekend-plan" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, gjerne. Jeg blir med!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Lea is coming too." dialogue_id="weekend-plan" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Flott. Lea blir også med.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Is Omar taking part this time?" dialogue_id="weekend-plan" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Er Omar med denne gangen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, he’s included in the trip, but he won’t arrive until Saturday." dialogue_id="weekend-plan" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Ja, han er med på turen, men han kommer først på lørdag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Who is on the cooking team?" dialogue_id="weekend-plan" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hvem er med på matlaget?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Lea and I are on it. Would you like to join?" dialogue_id="weekend-plan" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Lea og jeg er med. Har du lyst til å bli med?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I can do that. Then there are three of us on the team." dialogue_id="weekend-plan" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, det kan jeg. Da er vi tre på laget.
:::

Two closely related expressions appear several times:

- **bli med** focuses on entering, joining, accepting, or coming along.
- **være med** focuses on participation or inclusion as an existing situation.

The difference is often one of viewpoint. Are we looking at the moment someone joins, or at the situation after that person is included?

## The central contrast {#central-contrast role=model objectives=obj-bli-med-or-vaere-med-01}

The verb **bli** often presents a change. In the expression **bli med**, someone goes from not participating to participating.

::: examples
- no: Vil du bli med på tur?
- en: Would you like to come along on the trip?
:::

The speaker is offering the listener a chance to join.

::: examples
- no: Ja, jeg blir med.
- en: Yes, I’ll come along.
:::

The listener accepts and enters the plan.

The verb **være** presents a situation or state. In **være med**, the person already participates, belongs to the group, or is included in the activity.

::: examples
- no: Ingrid er med på turen.
- en: Ingrid is taking part in the trip.
:::

Here the question of joining has already been settled. Ingrid is one of the participants.

Compare the two stages:

::: examples
- no: Først spør Sara om Jonas vil bli med.
- en: First Sara asks whether Jonas wants to come along.
- no: Etterpå er Jonas med på turen.
- en: Afterwards Jonas is included in the trip.
:::

This before-and-after picture is the main tool for choosing between the expressions:

**not yet participating → bli med → participating → være med**

**First checkpoint: finding the change**

Return to the opening dialogue and locate each use of **bli med** and **være med**. For every example, decide whether the speaker is focusing on an invitation or decision to join, or on someone’s existing participation.

This activity belongs here because the distinction is easier to see inside a developing plan than in isolated sentences. A successful response would identify Sara’s invitation to Jonas as entry into the trip, but her statement about Omar as information about who is already included.

## Invitations and decisions with bli med {#invitations-decisions-bli-med role=model objectives=obj-bli-med-or-vaere-med-01}

**Bli med** is especially common when someone invites another person to join an activity or accompany a group.

::: examples
- no: Vil du bli med på kafé?
- en: Would you like to come along to a café?
- no: Kan Emma bli med på festen?
- en: Can Emma come to the party?
- no: Bli med oss!
- en: Come with us!
:::

It also appears when someone accepts, refuses, or reports a decision.

::: examples
- no: Jeg blir med på konserten.
- en: I’m coming to the concert.
- no: Dessverre kan jeg ikke bli med.
- en: Unfortunately, I can’t come along.
- no: Adrian bestemte seg for å bli med.
- en: Adrian decided to join.
:::

The most useful present-tense form is **blir med**.

::: examples
- no: Blir du med i kveld?
- en: Are you coming along tonight?
:::

Although the Norwegian form is present tense, the question often concerns an upcoming activity. Its main force is not simply future time. It asks whether the listener will join the plan.

The past form is **ble med**.

::: examples
- no: Nora ble med på turen i siste liten.
- en: Nora joined the trip at the last minute.
:::

This describes the point at which Nora entered the group or accepted the plan.

::: examples
- no: Vi spurte Henrik, og han ble med.
- en: We asked Henrik, and he joined us.
:::

**Controlled practice: choosing the joining viewpoint**

Complete a short set of contextual sentences about invitations, late decisions, and people entering groups. Use the appropriate form of **bli med**, adjusting it to the time of the sentence.

The contexts should make the moment of entry clear: a friend receives an invitation, a colleague changes her mind, or a child asks permission to join a game. This practice belongs here because it keeps attention on one strong signal—someone is moving into participation. A successful response would use **blir med** for a present or upcoming decision and **ble med** when the joining happened in the past.

## Participation and inclusion with være med {#participation-inclusion-vaere-med role=model objectives=obj-bli-med-or-vaere-med-01}

Use **være med** when the important information is that someone participates or belongs to the relevant group.

::: examples
- no: Er du med på kurset?
- en: Are you taking part in the course?
- no: Ja, jeg er med.
- en: Yes, I am.
- no: Femten personer er med i prosjektet.
- en: Fifteen people are involved in the project.
- no: Barna var med på hele arrangementet.
- en: The children took part in the entire event.
:::

Here the useful forms are **er med** for a present situation and **var med** for a past situation.

::: examples
- no: Sander er med på laget nå.
- en: Sander is on the team now.
- no: Sander var med på laget i fjor.
- en: Sander was on the team last year.
:::

The focus is not the moment when Sander joined. It is his membership during a particular period.

**Være med** can also emphasize inclusion or access.

::: examples
- no: Alle kan være med.
- en: Everyone can take part.
- no: Er alle med på bildet?
- en: Is everyone included in the picture?
- no: Navnet mitt er ikke med på lista.
- en: My name isn’t included on the list.
:::

These examples share the same basic viewpoint: the question is whether a person or item is included, not how it enters.

**Second checkpoint: checking participation**

Read brief descriptions of a class, a sports team, a volunteer project, and a guest list. Complete statements about who currently participates, who participated earlier, and who is included.

This activity follows the work on **bli med** so that the learner can now hold the joining moment still and describe the resulting situation. A successful response would choose **er med** or **var med** when the context reports participation or inclusion without highlighting entry.

## The same event from two viewpoints {#same-event-two-viewpoints role=contrast objectives=obj-bli-med-or-vaere-med-01}

Both expressions can refer to the same person and activity. The difference lies in what the speaker wants to highlight.

**An invitation followed by participation**

::: examples
- no: Mari spurte om Leo ville bli med på møtet.
- en: Mari asked whether Leo wanted to join the meeting.
- no: Nå er Leo med på møtet.
- en: Leo is now taking part in the meeting.
:::

The first sentence presents an invitation. The second reports Leo’s participation.

**A late decision followed by inclusion**

::: examples
- no: Først sa Amina nei, men senere ble hun med på prosjektet.
- en: At first Amina said no, but later she joined the project.
- no: Nå er hun med i prosjektgruppa.
- en: Now she is part of the project group.
:::

The first sentence highlights the change. The second presents the result.

**A question before and after a decision**

::: examples
- no: Blir Daniel med på middagen?
- en: Is Daniel joining us for dinner?
- no: Ja, Daniel er med. Vi har bestilt bord til ham.
- en: Yes, Daniel is included. We have reserved a place for him.
:::

The first speaker does not yet know Daniel’s decision. The answer treats his participation as settled.

**Contrast practice: before and after**

Follow one person through a change of plan. Choose the pair that describes first entering an activity and then participating in it. The context must tell you which viewpoint belongs at each stage.

## A useful overlap in invitations {#useful-overlap-invitations role=contrast objectives=obj-bli-med-or-vaere-med-01}

The distinction is strong, but it is not a rigid rule that only **bli med** can appear in an invitation. Norwegian speakers also ask:

::: examples
- no: Vil du være med på kino?
- en: Would you like to go to the cinema with us?
:::

This is natural. It asks whether the listener wants to participate in the proposed activity.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Vil du bli med på kino?
- en: Would you like to come along to the cinema?
- no: Vil du være med på kino?
- en: Would you like to take part in the cinema outing?
:::

In everyday English, both translations may simply be Would you like to come to the cinema? The Norwegian expressions give slightly different perspectives. **Bli med** highlights accepting and joining. **Være med** treats participation in the activity as the central question.

The same overlap appears with future plans:

::: examples
- no: Blir du med på møtet i morgen?
- en: Are you joining the meeting tomorrow?
- no: Skal du være med på møtet i morgen?
- en: Are you going to take part in the meeting tomorrow?
:::

The first question focuses more directly on whether the person will join. The second asks about the person’s planned participation. Both can be correct, depending on the speaker’s viewpoint.

A short answer can also use either expression:

::: examples
- no: Ja, jeg blir med.
- en: Yes, I’ll join you.
- no: Ja, jeg er med.
- en: Yes, I’m in.
:::

**Jeg blir med** explicitly accepts the invitation. **Jeg er med** presents the decision as settled: the speaker now counts as a participant.

**Viewpoint practice**

Read short exchanges in which one person proposes an activity and another person responds. Decide whether each missing expression should emphasize accepting the proposal or confirming participation as settled. Then complete the exchange naturally.

This checkpoint matters because real conversation allows more than one viewpoint. A successful response would not rely only on whether the event is in the future. It would consider whether the speaker is highlighting entry into the plan or inclusion in it.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-bli-med-or-vaere-med-01}

**Mixing the two verb forms**

Do not combine **er** and **bli med**.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Er du bli med på turen?
- en: 'Incorrect: Are you join the trip?'
:::

Choose one complete expression.

::: examples
- no: Blir du med på turen?
- en: Are you coming along on the trip?
- no: Er du med på turen?
- en: Are you taking part in the trip?
:::

The first asks about joining. The second asks about participation or inclusion.

**Using bli med for a stable situation without a change**

If the context simply describes who belongs to a group now, **være med** is normally the clearer choice.

Less suitable for a stable description:

::: examples
- no: Mina blir med i gruppa nå.
- en: Mina joins the group now.
:::

This can be correct if Mina is entering the group at this moment. If the intended meaning is that she already belongs to it, use:

::: examples
- no: Mina er med i gruppa nå.
- en: Mina is in the group now.
:::

Context determines whether **nå** means a current change or a current state.

**Using være med when the joining decision is the point**

If a story emphasizes someone changing their mind and entering an activity, **bli med** expresses that change clearly.

::: examples
- no: Først ville Theo bli hjemme, men så ble han med.
- en: At first Theo wanted to stay home, but then he joined us.
:::

Using **var med** would only report that he participated. It would weaken the contrast between his first decision and his later one.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Theo ble med etter middag.
- en: Theo joined us after dinner.
- no: Theo var med etter middag.
- en: Theo was with us after dinner.
:::

The first marks his entry after dinner. The second describes his participation during the period after dinner.

## Back at the cabin trip {#back-at-cabin-trip role=contrast objectives=obj-bli-med-or-vaere-med-01}

On Friday afternoon, the friends meet beside Sara’s car.

::: {.reading translation="Lea, are you coming in my car?" dialogue_id="cabin-trip" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Lea, blir du med i bilen min?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Jonas is coming with us too, right?" dialogue_id="cabin-trip" speaker_id="lea" speaker_name="Lea" character_id="lea"}
Ja. Jonas er også med, ikke sant?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, he’s included. He’s buying food before we leave." dialogue_id="cabin-trip" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Ja, han er med. Han kjøper mat før vi drar.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And Omar?" dialogue_id="cabin-trip" speaker_id="lea" speaker_name="Lea" character_id="lea"}
Og Omar?
:::

::: {.reading translation="He’s taking part in the trip, but he won’t join us until tomorrow." dialogue_id="cabin-trip" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Han er med på turen, men han blir med først i morgen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’ve bought the food now. Who will come and help carry it?" dialogue_id="cabin-trip" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nå har jeg kjøpt maten. Hvem blir med og bærer?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’ll come!" dialogue_id="cabin-trip" speaker_id="lea" speaker_name="Lea" character_id="lea"}
Jeg blir med!
:::

Omar can already be **med på turen** because he is an included participant in the weekend plan. At the same time, he does not **bli med** until Saturday because that is when he physically joins the others. The two expressions describe different aspects of the same situation.

**Context-building practice**

Reconstruct the cabin plan in a short written sequence. State who accepts the invitation, who is already included in the trip, who joins later, and who takes part in the cooking team. Reuse both present and past forms where the timeline requires them.

This activity returns to the chapter’s original people and tests whether the learner can follow participation across several stages. A successful response would maintain the distinction consistently: decisions and arrivals use **bli med**, while established participation and team membership use **være med**.

Without looking back, complete two short pairs from new contexts:

1. Describe someone accepting an invitation to a study group, then describe that person as a member of the group.
2. Describe someone who participated in a concert last year, then describe a friend deciding to join this year.

A successful review response will show the core sequence: use **bli med** for entering or accepting participation, and **være med** for existing participation or inclusion.
