## At the second-hand shop {#at-the-second-hand-shop role=orient}

Maja and Erik are furnishing a small apartment. They have found several possible tables, chairs, lamps, and cupboards, but they cannot take everything.

::: {.reading translation="Which table should we buy?" dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Hvilket bord skal vi kjøpe?
:::

::: {.reading translation="The small one fits best in the kitchen." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="erik" speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Det lille passer best på kjøkkenet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="What about the chairs?" dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Hva med stolene?
:::

::: {.reading translation="We’ll take the blue ones, not the black ones." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="erik" speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Vi tar de blå, ikke de svarte.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Do we also need a cupboard?" dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Trenger vi også et skap?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but not the tall one. I like the low one." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="erik" speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Ja, men ikke det høye. Jeg liker det lave.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And the lamps? The old one is nice." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Og lampene? Den gamle er fin.
:::

::: {.reading translation="It is nice, but we need a new one." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop" speaker_id="erik" speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Den er fin, men vi trenger en ny.
:::

Maja and Erik repeatedly leave out nouns that are already clear from the conversation. Instead of saying det lille bordet, Erik says det lille. Instead of repeating de blå stolene, he says de blå.

Norwegian can therefore use an adjective as a complete noun phrase. The adjective describes an understood person or thing. Context identifies the omitted noun, while determiners and adjective agreement jointly express relevant features such as number, definiteness, and, in singular forms, gender where the forms distinguish them.

The usual Norwegian term is a nominalized adjective (substantivert adjektiv).

This is similar to English expressions such as the blue one and the old ones. Norwegian does not normally add a word corresponding to one or ones. The adjective takes the agreement form required by the understood noun, although some forms are identical and do not overtly reveal every feature.

## Understanding what has been left out {#understanding-what-has-been-left-out role=model objectives=obj-adjectives-as-nouns-01}

Look at Erik’s first answer:

::: examples
- no: Det lille passer best på kjøkkenet.
- en: The small one fits best in the kitchen.
:::

The conversation has already established that they are discussing et bord, a table. Bord is neuter and singular. The full phrase would be:

::: examples
- no: det lille bordet
- en: the small table
:::

When bordet is omitted, its grammatical features still control the phrase:

::: examples
- no: det lille
- en: the small one
:::

The same principle applies to the chairs:

::: examples
- no: de blå stolene
- en: the blue chairs
:::

::: examples
- no: de blå
- en: the blue ones
:::

Stolene is definite plural, so the shortened phrase uses de and the plural or definite adjective form blå.

The listener must be able to identify the missing noun from the situation. An adjective cannot normally stand alone if nobody knows what it refers to.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker den røde.
- en: I like the red one.
:::

This works if the speakers are already looking at dresses, bicycles, cups, or another known group of things.

Without that context, den røde may leave the listener wondering what the speaker means.

**Identify-the-understood-noun**

This noticing activity belongs here because the learner must first connect each adjective phrase to something already present in the context. Use Maja and Erik’s shop conversation. For each shortened phrase, the learner should state which previously mentioned object is understood. Success demonstrates that the learner recognizes why the noun can be omitted; adjective endings are not yet the primary scored goal.

{{exercise: identify_understood_noun}}

## Definite forms: the red one and the red ones {#definite-forms-the-red-one-and-the-red-ones role=model objectives=obj-adjectives-as-nouns-01}

A definite phrase points to a particular person or thing: the small one, the old one, the blue ones. Norwegian normally uses den, det, or de before a definite adjective used without a noun.

Common-gender singular:

::: examples
- no: den røde
- en: the red one
:::

Neuter singular:

::: examples
- no: det røde
- en: the red one
:::

Plural:

::: examples
- no: de røde
- en: the red ones
:::

The adjective usually has the definite ending -e in all three patterns.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar den røde.
- en: I’ll take the red one.
:::

The understood noun might be en stol, en kopp, or another common-gender noun.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar det røde.
- en: I’ll take the red one.
:::

Here the understood noun is neuter, perhaps et skjerf or et glass.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar de røde.
- en: I’ll take the red ones.
:::

Here more than one item is understood.

The compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
For a definite adjective phrase without a noun, choose den, det, or de from the gender and number of the understood noun, and normally use the adjective’s -e form.
:::

This means that neuter singular has det, but the adjective does not keep its indefinite neuter -t ending.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: et rødt skjerf
- en: a red scarf
:::

::: examples
- no: det røde skjerfet
- en: the red scarf
:::

::: examples
- no: det røde
- en: the red one
:::

A common mistake is:

::: examples
- no: det rødt
- en: the red one
:::

This is incorrect. The -t form belongs to an indefinite neuter phrase such as et rødt. A definite adjective after det normally has -e:

::: examples
- no: det røde
- en: the red one
:::

**Distinguish-definite-gender-and-number**

This controlled recognition activity uses objects in the second-hand shop. The learner should identify whether each definite adjective phrase refers to one common-gender object, one neuter object, or several objects. Success demonstrates correct interpretation of den, det, and de. Adjective meaning serves only as context.

{{exercise: distinguish_definite_gender_and_number}}

**Build-definite-shortened-phrases**

This production activity follows recognition because the learner is now ready to form the pattern. Give a definite noun phrase from the shop, establish its object clearly, and ask the learner to omit the noun while preserving its gender, number, and adjective form. Success demonstrates production of den/det/de plus the correct definite adjective form.

{{exercise: build_definite_shortened_phrases}}

## Indefinite singular: a new one {#indefinite-singular-a-new-one role=contrast objectives=obj-adjectives-as-nouns-01}

Maja says that the old lamp is attractive, but Erik answers:

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger en ny.
- en: We need a new one.
:::

The understood noun is en lampe, a lamp. Because it is common gender and indefinite singular, the phrase uses en and the corresponding adjective form:

::: examples
- no: en ny lampe
- en: a new lamp
:::

::: examples
- no: en ny
- en: a new one
:::

With a neuter noun, use et and the neuter adjective form:

::: examples
- no: et nytt bord
- en: a new table
:::

::: examples
- no: et nytt
- en: a new one
:::

Compare the forms directly:

Understood noun: en stol

Indefinite phrase:

::: examples
- no: en stol
- en: a chair
:::

::: examples
- no: en rød
- en: a red one
:::

Definite phrase:

::: examples
- no: den røde
- en: the red one
:::

Understood noun: et skjerf

Indefinite phrase:

::: examples
- no: et skjerf
- en: a scarf
:::

::: examples
- no: et rødt
- en: a red one
:::

Definite phrase:

::: examples
- no: det røde
- en: the red one
:::

Understood noun: flere stoler

Indefinite phrase:

::: examples
- no: flere stoler
- en: several chairs
:::

::: examples
- no: røde
- en: red ones
:::

Definite phrase:

::: examples
- no: de røde
- en: the red ones
:::

Definiteness changes the introductory word and, where the adjective’s paradigm distinguishes the forms, its singular ending.

::: examples
- no: Jeg trenger en billig.
- en: I need a cheap one.
:::

::: examples
- no: Jeg velger den billige.
- en: I’m choosing the cheap one.
:::

::: examples
- no: Hun vil kjøpe et stort.
- en: She wants to buy a large one.
:::

::: examples
- no: Hun vil kjøpe det store.
- en: She wants to buy the large one.
:::

The first sentence in each pair introduces an unspecified item. The second points to a particular item that the speakers can identify.

Another common mistake is to use the English word one as if Norwegian required an extra noun:

::: examples
- no: Jeg trenger en ny en.
- en: I need a new one.
:::

This form can occur in informal speech in some contexts, but it is not the basic pattern taught here. The standard, economical phrase is:

::: examples
- no: Jeg trenger en ny.
- en: I need a new one.
:::

**Contrast-indefinite-and-definite-singular**

This activity uses Maja and Erik before and after they have chosen particular items. The learner should complete short exchanges with an adjective phrase that either introduces an unspecified object or identifies a particular known object. Success demonstrates the definiteness contrast between en/et plus the indefinite adjective and den/det plus the definite adjective. Number remains singular throughout.

{{exercise: contrast_indefinite_and_definite_singular}}

**Produce-neuter-singular-forms**

This focused activity is needed because neuter creates the most likely ending error. Use familiar neuter objects from the shop, first as unspecified choices and then as identified choices. The learner should replace each full noun phrase with a shortened adjective phrase. Success demonstrates the contrast between et plus the -t form and det plus the -e form.

{{exercise: produce_neuter_singular_forms}}

## Plural forms: new ones and the new ones {#plural-forms-new-ones-and-the-new-ones role=contrast objectives=obj-adjectives-as-nouns-01}

When several items are meant, the adjective takes its plural form. An indefinite plural adjective phrase often has no article:

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger nye.
- en: We need new ones.
:::

::: examples
- no: De selger billige.
- en: They sell cheap ones.
:::

::: examples
- no: Maja fant to fine.
- en: Maja found two nice ones.
:::

When the plural group is definite, use de:

::: examples
- no: Vi kjøper de nye.
- en: We are buying the new ones.
:::

::: examples
- no: De billige står ved døra.
- en: The cheap ones are by the door.
:::

::: examples
- no: Maja valgte de fine.
- en: Maja chose the nice ones.
:::

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Erik ser etter gamle lamper.
- en: Erik is looking for old lamps.
:::

::: examples
- no: Erik ser etter gamle.
- en: Erik is looking for old ones.
:::

::: examples
- no: Erik ser etter de gamle lampene.
- en: Erik is looking for the old lamps.
:::

::: examples
- no: Erik ser etter de gamle.
- en: Erik is looking for the old ones.
:::

In the indefinite plural, gamle appears without de. In the definite plural, de gamle refers to a particular known group.

Do not automatically use de merely because the meaning is plural. The choice depends on definiteness:

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger svarte.
- en: We need black ones.
:::

The black items have not yet been identified.

::: examples
- no: Vi tar de svarte.
- en: We’ll take the black ones.
:::

The speakers are choosing a particular visible group.

**Separate-plural-number-from-definiteness**

This sorting activity belongs here because learners may wrongly treat de as a general plural marker. Use shop statements about searching for items and selecting visible items. The learner should decide whether each context requires an indefinite plural adjective or a definite plural phrase with de. Success demonstrates the definiteness decision within plural noun phrases; adjective spelling is supplied as context rather than scored.

{{exercise: separate_plural_number_from_definiteness}}

**Form-plural-adjective-phrases**

This controlled production activity asks the learner to shorten plural noun phrases in brief shop exchanges. Some refer to an unspecified group, while others refer to a known group. Success demonstrates correct use of the plural adjective form and correct presence or absence of de.

{{exercise: form_plural_adjective_phrases}}

## A familiar irregular adjective: liten {#a-familiar-irregular-adjective-liten role=model objectives=obj-adjectives-as-nouns-01}

The dialogue uses:

::: examples
- no: det lille
- en: the small one
:::

The adjective liten follows forms that learners may already know from adjective agreement:

Common-gender singular:

::: examples
- no: en liten
- en: a small one
:::

::: examples
- no: den lille
- en: the small one
:::

Neuter singular:

::: examples
- no: et lite
- en: a small one
:::

::: examples
- no: det lille
- en: the small one
:::

Plural:

::: examples
- no: små
- en: small ones
:::

::: examples
- no: de små
- en: the small ones
:::

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil ha en liten.
- en: I want a small one.
:::

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil ha et lite.
- en: I want a small one.
:::

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil ha den lille.
- en: I want the small one.
:::

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil ha det lille.
- en: I want the small one.
:::

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger små.
- en: We need small ones.
:::

::: examples
- no: Vi tar de små.
- en: We’ll take the small ones.
:::

The adjective is still agreeing with an understood noun. Omitting the noun does not make liten regular.

## Returning to Maja and Erik {#returning-to-maja-and-erik role=contrast objectives=obj-adjectives-as-nouns-01}

After walking around the shop, Maja and Erik compare their final choices.

::: {.reading translation="We can’t buy both tables. Are we taking the large one or the small one?" dialogue_id="second-hand-shop-returning" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Vi kan ikke kjøpe begge bordene. Tar vi det store eller det lille?
:::

::: {.reading translation="We’ll take the small one. But we need large chairs." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop-returning" speaker_id="erik" speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Vi tar det lille. Men vi trenger store stoler.
:::

::: {.reading translation="The large ones by the window are nice." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop-returning" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
De store ved vinduet er fine.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but they are expensive. Are there other chairs?" dialogue_id="second-hand-shop-returning" speaker_id="erik" speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Ja, men de er dyre. Finnes det andre stoler?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I saw some black ones by the door." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop-returning" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Ja, jeg så noen svarte ved døra.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I don’t like the black ones. Let’s take the blue ones." dialogue_id="second-hand-shop-returning" speaker_id="erik" speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Jeg liker ikke de svarte. La oss ta de blå.
:::

Notice how the same adjective can appear in different phrases as the situation changes:

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger store.
- en: We need large ones.
:::

The items are plural but not yet identified.

::: examples
- no: De store ved vinduet er fine.
- en: The large ones by the window are nice.
:::

The speakers now mean a particular group.

Likewise:

::: examples
- no: Jeg så noen svarte.
- en: I saw some black ones.
:::

This introduces an indefinite group.

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker ikke de svarte.
- en: I don’t like the black ones.
:::

This refers back to that known group.

The essential decision is not whether the noun is spoken aloud. The essential decision is what the understood noun phrase means:

1. Is it singular or plural?
2. If singular, is the understood noun common gender or neuter?
3. Is the person or thing indefinite or definite?
4. Which adjective form agrees with those features?

**Track-reference-across-the-dialogue**

This contextual activity uses the second conversation. The learner should follow how an indefinite adjective phrase introduces a group and how a later definite phrase refers back to it. Success demonstrates control of definiteness across connected turns rather than in isolated sentences. Number and adjective agreement remain necessary context, but reference tracking is the primary scored goal.

{{exercise: track_reference_across_the_dialogue}}

## Transfer to a new situation {#transfer-to-a-new-situation role=recap objectives=obj-adjectives-as-nouns-01}

At a bakery, Nora and Hassan are choosing cakes for a meeting. Several cakes are already on the counter, so both speakers can see which ones they mean. Some cake names use common-gender nouns, while others use neuter nouns.

The learner should first describe an unspecified choice, such as wanting a small one or needing two large ones. Next, the learner should identify a particular visible choice, such as choosing the small one or rejecting the expensive ones. The nouns should then be omitted once the context makes them clear.

**Transfer-adjective-noun-phrases-to-the-bakery**

This cumulative activity changes the setting while preserving the target grammar. The learner should produce short Norwegian responses using adjectives as complete noun phrases for indefinite and definite choices, singular common-gender and neuter items, and plural items. Success demonstrates coordinated control of definiteness, number, and neuter forms without relying on wording from the furniture-shop dialogue.

{{exercise: transfer_adjective_noun_phrases_to_the_bakery}}
