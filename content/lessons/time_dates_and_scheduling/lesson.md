## A course begins {#a-course-begins role=orient objectives=obj-time-dates-and-scheduling-01}

Nora and Amir have registered for an evening Norwegian course. Nora has the confirmation message open on her phone, but she wants to make sure that she has understood the date and time correctly.

**Nora:**

::: {.reading translation="When does the Norwegian course begin?" dialogue_id="course-begins" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Når begynner norskkurset?
:::

**Amir:**

::: {.reading translation="Tuesday, 14 May." dialogue_id="course-begins" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Tirsdag 14. mai.
:::

**Nora:**

::: {.reading translation="Is that the fourteenth?" dialogue_id="course-begins" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Er det den fjortende?
:::

**Amir:**

::: {.reading translation="Yes. It begins at half past five." dialogue_id="course-begins" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja. Det begynner klokka halv seks.
:::

**Nora:**

::: {.reading translation="Half past five? In other words, 17:30?" dialogue_id="course-begins" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Halv seks? Altså 17.30?
:::

**Amir:**

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Shall we meet a quarter of an hour earlier?" dialogue_id="course-begins" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja. Skal vi møtes et kvarter før?
:::

**Nora:**

::: {.reading translation="Gladly. Does quarter past five work?" dialogue_id="course-begins" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Gjerne. Passer kvart over fem?
:::

**Amir:**

::: {.reading translation="Yes, we’ll meet by the entrance at quarter past five." dialogue_id="course-begins" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, vi møtes ved inngangen klokka kvart over fem.
:::

**Nora:**

::: {.reading translation="Great. Then I’ll see you on Tuesday, the fourteenth of May." dialogue_id="course-begins" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Flott. Da ses vi tirsdag den fjortende mai.
:::

The important moment in this conversation is Nora’s reaction to halv seks. Norwegian tells half hours differently from English. Norwegian halv seks means halfway toward six, so it is 5:30, not 6:30.

The conversation also shows two ways to express the date. A written notice may show 14. mai, while the date is read aloud as den fjortende mai or simply fjortende mai.

Finally, Nora and Amir use the time and date to make a simple plan. They confirm when the course begins and choose an earlier meeting time.

## Asking and telling the time {#asking-and-telling-the-time role=model objectives=obj-time-dates-and-scheduling-01}

To ask what time it is, Norwegian commonly uses:

::: examples
- no: Hva er klokka?
- en: What time is it?
:::

Klokka is the definite form of klokke, meaning clock. Klokken is also correct, but klokka is very common in everyday Bokmål.

To give an exact hour, use klokka er followed by the number.

::: examples
- no: Klokka er to.
- en: It is two o’clock.
- no: Klokka er fem.
- en: It is five o’clock.
- no: Klokka er ni.
- en: It is nine o’clock.
- no: Klokka er åtte presis.
- en: It is eight o’clock exactly.
:::

You can add presis when you mean exactly on the hour.

When the sentence already contains another verb, klokka introduces the time.

::: examples
- no: Kurset begynner klokka seks.
- en: The course begins at six o’clock.
- no: Bussen går klokka åtte.
- en: The bus leaves at eight o’clock.
- no: Vi møtes klokka fire.
- en: We’ll meet at four o’clock.
:::

English normally uses at before a clock time. Norwegian usually does not use a separate preposition here: klokka itself introduces the time.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Vi møtes klokka fem.
- en: We’ll meet at five.
- no: Vi møtes på klokka fem.
- en: We’ll meet at five.
:::

Not:

The Norwegian sentence does not need på before klokka.

## Minutes after and before the hour {#minutes-after-and-before-the-hour role=contrast objectives=obj-time-dates-and-scheduling-01}

Use over for minutes after an hour.

::: examples
- no: Klokka er fem over tre.
- en: It is five past three.
- no: Klokka er ti over åtte.
- en: It is ten past eight.
- no: Klokka er kvart over fem.
- en: It is quarter past five.
:::

Kvart means a quarter. The expression kvart over fem therefore means a quarter after five: 5:15.

Use på for minutes before the next hour.

::: examples
- no: Klokka er fem på fire.
- en: It is five to four.
- no: Klokka er ti på ni.
- en: It is ten to nine.
- no: Klokka er kvart på seks.
- en: It is quarter to six.
:::

A useful way to understand på is to look forward to the coming hour:

::: examples
- no: kvart på seks
- en: a quarter before six
:::

The hour named after på is the hour that is approaching.

**An aligned contrast**

::: examples
- no: 5.05 — fem over fem
- en: 5.05 — five past five
- no: 5.15 — kvart over fem
- en: 5.15 — quarter past five
- no: 5.45 — kvart på seks
- en: 5.45 — quarter to six
- no: 5.55 — fem på seks
- en: 5.55 — five to six
:::

Notice that the named hour changes after the half-hour. At 5:45, Norwegian looks toward six, so the expression is kvart på seks.

## The half hour: looking toward the next hour {#the-half-hour-looking-toward-the-next-hour role=model objectives=obj-time-dates-and-scheduling-01}

Norwegian half-hour expressions look forward to the next hour.

::: examples
- no: Klokka er halv seks.
- en: It is half past five.
:::

Literally, halv seks is half toward six. The time is therefore 5:30.

Compare the pattern:

::: examples
- no: 1.30 — halv to
- en: 1.30 — half past one
- no: 4.30 — halv fem
- en: 4.30 — half past four
- no: 5.30 — halv seks
- en: 5.30 — half past five
- no: 8.30 — halv ni
- en: 8.30 — half past eight
:::

This is one of the most important differences between Norwegian and English time-telling.

A common mistake is to translate each word directly and interpret halv seks as half past six. That would give the wrong time by one hour. Instead, ask: Which hour are we halfway toward?

::: examples
- no: Filmen begynner halv ni.
- en: The film begins at half past eight.
- no: Toget går halv to.
- en: The train leaves at half past one.
- no: Vi spiser middag halv sju.
- en: We eat dinner at half past six.
:::

## Times close to the half hour {#times-close-to-the-half-hour role=model objectives=obj-time-dates-and-scheduling-01}

Norwegian can also count minutes before or after the half-hour.

::: examples
- no: Klokka er fem på halv seks.
- en: It is twenty-five past five.
:::

This is five minutes before halv seks, so it is 5:25.

::: examples
- no: Klokka er fem over halv seks.
- en: It is twenty-five to six.
:::

This is five minutes after halv seks, so it is 5:35.

::: examples
- no: Klokka er ti på halv seks.
- en: It is twenty past five.
- no: Klokka er ti over halv seks.
- en: It is twenty to six.
:::

The logic remains consistent:

- Before the half-hour, count toward halv plus the next hour.
- After the half-hour, count from halv plus the next hour.

::: examples
- no: 5.20 — ti på halv seks
- en: 5.20 — twenty past five
- no: 5.25 — fem på halv seks
- en: 5.25 — twenty-five past five
- no: 5.30 — halv seks
- en: 5.30 — half past five
- no: 5.35 — fem over halv seks
- en: 5.35 — twenty-five to six
- no: 5.40 — ti over halv seks
- en: 5.40 — twenty to six
:::

These forms are common in everyday conversation. At first, it can help to locate halv seks at 5:30 and then move backward or forward by the stated number of minutes.

## Saying digital times {#saying-digital-times role=model objectives=obj-time-dates-and-scheduling-01}

Norwegian schedules often use the 24-hour clock in writing.

::: examples
- no: Kurset begynner 17.30.
- en: The course begins at 17:30.
- no: Toget går 08.15.
- en: The train leaves at 08:15.
- no: Butikken stenger 21.00.
- en: The shop closes at 21:00.
:::

In speech, you may read a formal timetable time with the 24-hour numbers.

::: examples
- no: Toget går klokka sytten tretti.
- en: The train leaves at seventeen thirty.
- no: Møtet begynner klokka fjorten femten.
- en: The meeting begins at fourteen fifteen.
:::

In ordinary conversation, speakers often use the twelve-hour expressions when the context makes morning or evening clear.

::: examples
- no: Toget går kvart over åtte.
- en: The train leaves at quarter past eight.
- no: Møtet begynner kvart over to.
- en: The meeting begins at quarter past two.
:::

Nora uses both systems to check the same time:

::: examples
- no: Halv seks? Altså 17.30?
- en: Half past five? In other words, 17:30?
:::

This is a useful checking strategy when a spoken time might be misunderstood.

## Reading dates {#reading-dates role=model objectives=obj-time-dates-and-scheduling-01}

Norwegian normally writes the day before the month.

::: examples
- no: 14. mai
- en: 14 May
- no: 3. juni
- en: 3 June
- no: 21. oktober
- en: 21 October
:::

Month names are normally written with a lowercase letter:

::: examples
- no: januar
- en: January
- no: februar
- en: February
- no: mars
- en: March
- no: april
- en: April
- no: mai
- en: May
- no: juni
- en: June
- no: juli
- en: July
- no: august
- en: August
- no: september
- en: September
- no: oktober
- en: October
- no: november
- en: November
- no: desember
- en: December
:::

The full stop after the day number is important. It shows that the number is an ordinal number: first, second, third, and so on.

A written date such as 14. mai is therefore read with an ordinal number:

::: examples
- no: fjortende mai
- en: the fourteenth of May
:::

You may also hear den before the date.

::: examples
- no: den fjortende mai
- en: the fourteenth of May
:::

Both forms can occur in complete sentences:

::: examples
- no: Kurset begynner fjortende mai.
- en: The course begins on the fourteenth of May.
- no: Kurset begynner den fjortende mai.
- en: The course begins on the fourteenth of May.
:::

Useful ordinal forms for dates include:

::: examples
- no: 1. — første
- en: 1. — first
- no: 2. — andre
- en: 2. — second
- no: 3. — tredje
- en: 3. — third
- no: 4. — fjerde
- en: 4. — fourth
- no: 5. — femte
- en: 5. — fifth
- no: 6. — sjette
- en: 6. — sixth
- no: 7. — sjuende
- en: 7. — seventh
- no: 8. — åttende
- en: 8. — eighth
- no: 9. — niende
- en: 9. — ninth
- no: 10. — tiende
- en: 10. — tenth
- no: 11. — ellevte
- en: 11. — eleventh
- no: 12. — tolvte
- en: 12. — twelfth
- no: 13. — trettende
- en: 13. — thirteenth
- no: 14. — fjortende
- en: 14. — fourteenth
- no: 15. — femtende
- en: 15. — fifteenth
- no: 16. — sekstende
- en: 16. — sixteenth
- no: 17. — syttende
- en: 17. — seventeenth
- no: 18. — attende
- en: 18. — eighteenth
- no: 19. — nittende
- en: 19. — nineteenth
- no: 20. — tjuende
- en: 20. — twentieth
- no: 21. — tjueførste
- en: 21. — twenty-first
- no: 22. — tjueandre
- en: 22. — twenty-second
- no: 30. — trettiende
- en: 30. — thirtieth
- no: 31. — trettiførste
- en: 31. — thirty-first
:::

The written number and the spoken form do different jobs. You write 14. but say fjortende. Do not normally read it as the cardinal number fjorten when it represents a date.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har fjorten bøker.
- en: I have fourteen books.
- no: Kurset begynner 14. mai.
- en: The course begins on 14 May.
:::

The first sentence uses the cardinal number fjorten because it counts books. The date is read fjortende because it identifies the fourteenth day of the month.

## Asking about a date {#asking-about-a-date role=model objectives=obj-time-dates-and-scheduling-01}

To ask for a calendar date, use:

::: examples
- no: Hvilken dato er det?
- en: What is the date?
- no: Det er 14. mai.
- en: It is 14 May.
:::

To ask when an event happens, use når.

::: examples
- no: Når begynner kurset?
- en: When does the course begin?
- no: Det begynner 14. mai.
- en: It begins on 14 May.
:::

A weekday can appear before the date.

::: examples
- no: Det er tirsdag 14. mai.
- en: It is Tuesday, 14 May.
- no: Kurset begynner tirsdag 14. mai.
- en: The course begins on Tuesday, 14 May.
:::

When a weekday is used by itself to identify a day, på is common.

::: examples
- no: Vi møtes på tirsdag.
- en: We’ll meet on Tuesday.
:::

With a full written schedule, the date may follow directly.

::: examples
- no: Vi møtes tirsdag 14. mai.
- en: We’ll meet on Tuesday, 14 May.
:::

## Making a simple plan {#making-a-simple-plan role=model objectives=obj-time-dates-and-scheduling-01}

Clock times and dates become useful when you arrange to do something.

A common suggestion is:

::: examples
- no: Skal vi møtes?
- en: Shall we meet?
:::

Add a day, date, or time to make the plan more specific.

::: examples
- no: Skal vi møtes på fredag?
- en: Shall we meet on Friday?
- no: Skal vi møtes 12. juni?
- en: Shall we meet on 12 June?
- no: Skal vi møtes klokka tre?
- en: Shall we meet at three?
- no: Skal vi møtes fredag 12. juni klokka tre?
- en: Shall we meet on Friday, 12 June, at three?
:::

To ask whether a proposed time is suitable, use passer.

::: examples
- no: Passer klokka tre?
- en: Does three o’clock work?
- no: Passer fredag?
- en: Does Friday work?
- no: Passer fredag 12. juni?
- en: Does Friday, 12 June work?
:::

Useful short responses include:

::: examples
- no: Ja, det passer.
- en: Yes, that works.
- no: Nei, det passer ikke.
- en: No, that doesn’t work.
:::

::: examples
- no: Passer halv fire?
- en: Does half past three work?
:::

After agreement, confirm the plan clearly.

::: examples
- no: Da møtes vi klokka halv fire.
- en: Then we’ll meet at half past three.
- no: Vi møtes fredag 12. juni klokka halv fire.
- en: We’ll meet on Friday, 12 June, at half past three.
:::

The confirmation matters because it puts the day, date, and time together in one clear plan.

## Common mistakes to avoid {#common-mistakes-to-avoid role=contrast objectives=obj-time-dates-and-scheduling-01}

**Attaching halv to the current hour**

Incorrect interpretation:

::: examples
- no: halv seks
- en: 'Not: half past six (for this Norwegian phrase)'
:::

Correct interpretation:

::: examples
- no: halv seks
- en: half past five
:::

Norwegian names the next hour in a half-hour expression.

**Looking backward after på**

Incorrect interpretation:

::: examples
- no: kvart på seks
- en: 'Not: quarter past six (for this Norwegian phrase)'
:::

Correct interpretation:

::: examples
- no: kvart på seks
- en: quarter to six
:::

På points toward the approaching hour.

**Reading a date as a counting number**

Less natural for the date:

::: examples
- no: fjorten mai
- en: fourteen May
:::

Standard date reading:

::: examples
- no: fjortende mai
- en: the fourteenth of May
:::

The written date 14. mai represents an ordinal day.

**Adding på before klokka**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Vi møtes på klokka fem.
- en: 'Incorrect: We’ll meet at five.'
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Vi møtes klokka fem.
- en: We’ll meet at five.
:::

Use på with a weekday such as på fredag, but use klokka directly before a clock time.
