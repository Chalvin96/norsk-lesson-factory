## A key for Nora {#a-key-for-nora role=orient objectives=obj-two-objects-or-prepositional-recipient-01}

Jonas is leaving a shared office when he realizes that Nora needs the key to the storeroom. He asks Mari for help.

::: {.reading translation="Can you give Nora the key?" dialogue_id=a-key-for-nora speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Kan du gi Nora nøkkelen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Shall I give it to her today?" dialogue_id=a-key-for-nora speaker_id=mari speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Ja. Skal jeg gi den til henne i dag?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Please do. And can you send me her address?" dialogue_id=a-key-for-nora speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Gjerne. Og kan du sende meg adressen hennes?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’ll send the address to you now." dialogue_id=a-key-for-nora speaker_id=mari speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Jeg sender adressen til deg nå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thanks! Then you’ll find her office." dialogue_id=a-key-for-nora speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Takk! Da finner du kontoret hennes.
:::

Two things move from one person to another in this scene: a key and an address. Norwegian can present each transfer in two ways:

::: examples
- no: Gi Nora nøkkelen.
- en: Give Nora the key.
- no: Gi nøkkelen til Nora.
- en: Give the key to Nora.
:::

The basic event is the same. Nora receives the key. What changes is how the recipient and the thing are arranged.

## Two participants after the verb {#two-participants-after-the-verb role=model objectives=obj-two-objects-or-prepositional-recipient-01}

With verbs such as gi, to give, and sende, to send, we often mention:

- the **recipient**: the person who receives something
- the **theme**: the thing that is given or sent

In the opening dialogue, Nora is the recipient and nøkkelen is the theme:

::: examples
- no: Jonas gir Nora nøkkelen.
- en: Jonas gives Nora the key.
:::

Norwegian licenses two useful frames for this meaning.

**Frame 1: recipient before theme**

The recipient comes directly after the verb. The theme follows it.

**verb + recipient + theme**

::: examples
- no: Jonas gir Nora nøkkelen.
- en: Jonas gives Nora the key.
- no: Mari sender Jonas adressen.
- en: Mari sends Jonas the address.
- no: Jeg gir henne boka.
- en: I give her the book.
- no: Vi sender kunden en e-post.
- en: We send the customer an email.
:::

This frame is especially common when the recipient is short and already clear in the conversation, often a name or an object pronoun such as meg, deg, ham, henne, oss or dem.

Compare the parts directly:

- **Verb:** gir; **Recipient:** Nora; **Theme:** nøkkelen
- **Verb:** sender; **Recipient:** Jonas; **Theme:** adressen
- **Verb:** gir; **Recipient:** henne; **Theme:** boka
- **Verb:** sender; **Recipient:** kunden; **Theme:** en e-post

The central ordering rule is simple:

::: {.rule}
Without til, put the recipient before the theme.
:::

**Checkpoint request: notice-recipient-before-theme**

The learner should identify the recipient and the theme in short sentences based on the office scene. This belongs here because the learner must first notice which participant receives and which thing moves before choosing an order. The activity should use names and familiar object pronouns from the dialogue. A successful response demonstrates recognition that the first element after gi or sende is the recipient and that the following element is the theme.

{{exercise: notice_recipient_before_theme}}

## Frame 2: theme before til + recipient {#frame-2-theme-before-til-recipient role=model objectives=obj-two-objects-or-prepositional-recipient-01}

The second frame places the theme directly after the verb. The recipient follows til, to.

**verb + theme + til + recipient**

::: examples
- no: Jonas gir nøkkelen til Nora.
- en: Jonas gives the key to Nora.
- no: Mari sender adressen til Jonas.
- en: Mari sends the address to Jonas.
- no: Jeg gir boka til henne.
- en: I give the book to her.
- no: Vi sender en e-post til kunden.
- en: We send an email to the customer.
:::

Here the order is reversed because til marks the recipient.

Compare the parts directly:

- **Verb:** gir; **Theme:** nøkkelen; **til + recipient:** til Nora
- **Verb:** sender; **Theme:** adressen; **til + recipient:** til Jonas
- **Verb:** gir; **Theme:** boka; **til + recipient:** til henne
- **Verb:** sender; **Theme:** en e-post; **til + recipient:** til kunden

The corresponding rule is:

::: {.rule}
With til, put the theme before til + recipient.
:::

The two frames can therefore be aligned as follows:

::: examples
- no: Jeg sender deg bildet.
- en: I send you the picture.
- no: Jeg sender bildet til deg.
- en: I send the picture to you.
- no: Hun gir barnet maten.
- en: She gives the child the food.
- no: Hun gir maten til barnet.
- en: She gives the food to the child.
- no: Vi sender læreren dokumentet.
- en: We send the teacher the document.
- no: Vi sender dokumentet til læreren.
- en: We send the document to the teacher.
:::

The recipient and theme keep their roles even though their positions change.

**Checkpoint request: match-roles-across-frames**

The learner should match sentences that describe the same transfer using the two different frames. This checkpoint follows the presentation of both patterns so that attention stays on stable meaning despite changed word order. The context should include giving the office key and sending the address. A successful response demonstrates that the learner can track the same recipient and theme across both frames.

{{exercise: match_roles_across_frames}}

## Choosing the frame {#choosing-the-frame role=contrast objectives=obj-two-objects-or-prepositional-recipient-01}

Both frames are normal Bokmål. The first frame is often compact and natural when the recipient is short:

::: examples
- no: Send meg nummeret.
- en: Send me the number.
- no: Gi Nora nøkkelen.
- en: Give Nora the key.
:::

The til frame is also common. It can make the recipient especially clear, and it works naturally when the speaker presents the thing first:

::: examples
- no: Send nummeret til meg.
- en: Send the number to me.
- no: Gi nøkkelen til Nora.
- en: Give the key to Nora.
:::

At this level, the important choice is structural rather than stylistic:

- If there is no til, use recipient before theme.
- If the recipient is introduced by til, use theme before til + recipient.

Do not mix the orders.

**Common mistake: keeping the first-frame order with til**

A learner may add til but leave the recipient first:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Gi til Nora nøkkelen.
- en: Give to Nora the key.
:::

This is not the target pattern. Once til introduces the recipient, put the theme first:

::: examples
- no: Gi nøkkelen til Nora.
- en: Give the key to Nora.
:::

**Common mistake: putting the theme first without til**

Another possible error is:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Send adressen meg.
- en: Send the address me.
:::

For the two-object frame, the recipient must precede the theme:

::: examples
- no: Send meg adressen.
- en: Send me the address.
:::

Alternatively, use til:

::: examples
- no: Send adressen til meg.
- en: Send the address to me.
:::

The compact contrast is:

::: examples
- no: Send meg adressen.
- en: Send me the address.
- no: Send adressen til meg.
- en: Send the address to me.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Send adressen meg.
- en: Send the address me.
:::

**Checkpoint request: choose-licensed-recipient-frame**

The learner should choose a complete sentence that follows one of the two licensed frames. The items should vary between names and object pronouns while keeping gi and sende central. This belongs here because the learner has now seen both rules and the two likely ordering errors. A successful response demonstrates selection of either verb + recipient + theme or verb + theme + til + recipient without mixing them. Only frame choice and ordering should be scored.

{{exercise: choose_licensed_recipient_frame}}

## Changing from one frame to the other {#changing-from-one-frame-to-the-other role=model objectives=obj-two-objects-or-prepositional-recipient-01}

To change frames, move both participants as a unit of meaning. Do not simply insert or remove til.

Start with:

::: examples
- no: Mari sender Jonas adressen.
- en: Mari sends Jonas the address.
:::

Jonas is the recipient. Adressen is the theme. In the til frame, the theme moves before the marked recipient:

::: examples
- no: Mari sender adressen til Jonas.
- en: Mari sends the address to Jonas.
:::

The same process works with pronouns:

::: examples
- no: Jonas gir henne nøkkelen.
- en: Jonas gives her the key.
- no: Jonas gir nøkkelen til henne.
- en: Jonas gives the key to her.
:::

And with indefinite themes:

::: examples
- no: Vi sender kunden en bekreftelse.
- en: We send the customer a confirmation.
- no: Vi sender en bekreftelse til kunden.
- en: We send a confirmation to the customer.
:::

Notice that the identity of the recipient does not change. In both versions, kunden receives en bekreftelse.

**Practice request: transform-to-til-recipient**

The learner should rewrite two-object sentences as til-recipient sentences. The activity should explicitly preserve the same recipient, theme and event. It belongs here because transformation forces the learner to coordinate the preposition with the required change in order. A successful response demonstrates production of verb + theme + til + recipient. The scored goal is accurate construction of the til frame.

{{exercise: transform_to_til_recipient}}

**Practice request: transform-to-two-object-frame**

The learner should rewrite til-recipient sentences without til, placing the recipient before the theme. Use both named recipients and short object pronouns. This separate activity is needed because removing til requires a different retrieval step from adding it. A successful response demonstrates production of verb + recipient + theme while preserving who receives what.

{{exercise: transform_to_two_object_frame}}

## Returning to the office {#returning-to-the-office role=model objectives=obj-two-objects-or-prepositional-recipient-01}

Later that afternoon, Mari finds Nora’s office.

::: {.reading translation="Hi, Nora. Jonas asked me to give you this key." dialogue_id=returning-to-the-office speaker_id=mari speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Hei, Nora. Jonas ba meg gi deg denne nøkkelen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thanks very much. Can you send Jonas a message too?" dialogue_id=returning-to-the-office speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Tusen takk. Kan du sende Jonas en melding også?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I’ll send a message to him right away." dialogue_id=returning-to-the-office speaker_id=mari speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Ja, jeg sender en melding til ham med en gang.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Give him this folder the next time you see him." dialogue_id=returning-to-the-office speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Flott. Gi ham denne mappen neste gang du ser ham.
:::

The scene now uses both frames:

::: examples
- no: gi deg denne nøkkelen
- en: give you this key
- no: sende Jonas en melding
- en: send Jonas a message
- no: sende en melding til ham
- en: send a message to him
- no: gi ham denne mappen
- en: give him this folder
:::

Recipient: deg
Theme: denne nøkkelen

Recipient: Jonas
Theme: en melding

Theme: en melding
Recipient: ham

Recipient: ham
Theme: denne mappen

The object pronouns change with the people in the conversation, but the two frame patterns remain stable.

**Practice request: complete-office-transfers**

The learner should complete short exchanges continuing the office situation. Each response should express a specified transfer, such as sending a person a message or giving a person a document, using the frame signaled by the surrounding sentence. This belongs after the return dialogue because the learner can now use the grammar inside connected communication rather than isolated examples. A successful response demonstrates correct recipient–theme ordering in context. Frame construction is scored; vocabulary in the scene only supplies context.

{{exercise: complete_office_transfers}}

## Using the same patterns elsewhere {#using-the-same-patterns-elsewhere role=contrast objectives=obj-two-objects-or-prepositional-recipient-01}

The patterns are not limited to offices.

**At home**

::: examples
- no: Kan du gi barnet vannet?
- en: Can you give the child the water?
- no: Kan du gi vannet til barnet?
- en: Can you give the water to the child?
:::

**At a hotel**

::: examples
- no: Resepsjonisten sender gjesten en bekreftelse.
- en: The receptionist sends the guest a confirmation.
- no: Resepsjonisten sender en bekreftelse til gjesten.
- en: The receptionist sends a confirmation to the guest.
:::

**Between friends**

::: examples
- no: Jeg sender deg bildene i kveld.
- en: I’ll send you the pictures tonight.
- no: Jeg sender bildene til deg i kveld.
- en: I’ll send the pictures to you tonight.
:::

**In a shop**

::: examples
- no: Ekspeditøren gir kunden kvitteringen.
- en: The shop assistant gives the customer the receipt.
- no: Ekspeditøren gir kvitteringen til kunden.
- en: The shop assistant gives the receipt to the customer.
:::

Across all these situations, ask two questions:

1. Who receives something?
2. What is given or sent?

Then choose the frame:

**recipient first, no til**

::: examples
- no: Hun gir kunden kvitteringen.
- en: She gives the customer the receipt.
:::

**theme first, recipient with til**

::: examples
- no: Hun gir kvitteringen til kunden.
- en: She gives the receipt to the customer.
:::

**Practice request: build-frame-from-transfer-context**

The learner should produce a complete sentence from a short everyday situation that identifies a giver or sender, a recipient and a theme. Some prompts should require the two-object frame; others should require the til frame. This activity belongs here because it moves from transformation to independent construction while keeping the intended frame explicit. A successful response demonstrates correct ordering of recipient and theme in the required frame.

{{exercise: build_frame_from_transfer_context}}

## Cumulative transfer: a forgotten backpack {#cumulative-transfer-a-forgotten-backpack role=recap objectives=obj-two-objects-or-prepositional-recipient-01}

Imagine that Emil has left his backpack at a café. Lea finds it and speaks to an employee. Later she contacts Emil.

The learner should work with transfers involving the backpack, a message, a phone number and a receipt. The situation changes, but the grammatical decision remains the same: either place the recipient before the theme without til, or place the theme before til + recipient.

**Practice request: transfer-recipient-patterns-to-cafe-scene**

The learner should write context-appropriate sentences for the café situation, alternating between the two licensed frames as directed. Each sentence should make it clear who receives what. This cumulative activity belongs at the end because it requires the learner to retrieve the patterns without copying the office dialogue. A successful response demonstrates accurate frame choice and recipient–theme order in a new everyday context.

{{exercise: transfer_recipient_patterns_to_cafe_scene}}

## Short review {#short-review role=recap objectives=obj-two-objects-or-prepositional-recipient-01}

**Retrieval request: recall-two-recipient-frames**

Without looking back, state the two Norwegian ordering patterns for gi and sende. Then express one new transfer twice: first with the recipient before the theme, and then with the theme before til + recipient. Success means that both sentences preserve the same recipient and theme while using the two licensed orders.

{{exercise: recall_two_recipient_frames}}
