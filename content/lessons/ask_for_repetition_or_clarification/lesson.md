## A message that does not arrive clearly {#message-does-not-arrive-clearly role=orient objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

Mina has recently started helping at a small community café in Bergen. During her first morning shift, her colleague Jonas explains where an order should go. The room is noisy, and Mina misses part of the message.

::: {.reading translation="Jonas: Mina, can you put the coffee on the table by the window?" dialogue_id=opening-cafe speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Mina, kan du sette kaffen på bordet ved vinduet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Mina: Sorry, can you repeat that?" dialogue_id=opening-cafe speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Unnskyld, kan du gjenta?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Jonas: Can you put the coffee on the table by the window?" dialogue_id=opening-cafe speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Kan du sette kaffen på bordet ved vinduet?
:::

Mina hears the sentence this time, but she is not sure which table Jonas means.

::: {.reading translation="Mina: Do you mean the small table?" dialogue_id=opening-cafe speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Mener du det lille bordet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Jonas: Yes, the small table by the window." dialogue_id=opening-cafe speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, det lille bordet ved vinduet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Mina: Oh yes, the small table. All right!" dialogue_id=opening-cafe speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Å ja, det lille bordet. Greit!
:::

Mina then takes the coffee to the correct table.

This short exchange contains a complete communication repair. Mina first notices the problem: she did not hear the message. She asks Jonas to repeat it. After hearing it again, she identifies a second, more specific problem: she does not know which table he means. She checks the detail, confirms the corrected message, and continues with the task.

That sequence is the focus of this chapter:

1. Identify what went wrong.
2. Choose a suitable repair move.
3. Confirm the corrected message.
4. Continue the conversation or task.

You do not need to understand every Norwegian word immediately. You need a way to keep the conversation moving when understanding breaks down.

## First repair move: ask for repetition {#first-repair-move-ask-for-repetition role=model objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

When you do not hear a message, ask the speaker to say it again.

::: examples
- no: Unnskyld, kan du gjenta?
- en: Sorry, can you repeat that?
:::

This is polite, clear, and useful in many situations. **Unnskyld** can attract attention or soften the request. The central request is:

::: examples
- no: Kan du gjenta?
- en: Can you repeat that?
:::

You can also say:

::: examples
- no: Kan du si det en gang til?
- en: Can you say that one more time?
:::

Both requests concern the whole message. They are useful when noise, speed, distance, or unfamiliar pronunciation prevents you from hearing enough.

At the café, Mina could use either form:

::: examples
- no: Unnskyld, kan du gjenta?
- en: Sorry, can you repeat that?
- no: Unnskyld, kan du si det en gang til?
- en: Sorry, can you say that one more time?
:::

The speaker will normally understand that repetition is needed. A brief **Hva?**—*What?*—may sometimes be heard in casual conversation, but it can sound abrupt. At A1 level, **Unnskyld, kan du gjenta?** is a safer general-purpose choice.

## Checkpoint: recognizing a hearing problem {#recognizing-hearing-problem role=contrast objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

Read or listen to a short café exchange in which Jonas gives Mina an instruction, but surrounding noise hides part of it. Decide whether Mina should ask for repetition or ask about one particular word. Then formulate a polite repair request.

This activity belongs here because the first decision is not which phrase you remember; it is what kind of communication problem has occurred. The café context continues the opening situation. A successful response would show that you recognize a missing whole message and use a request such as **Kan du gjenta?**—*Can you repeat that?*—rather than pretending to understand.

## When repetition is not enough: ask for slower speech {#when-repetition-is-not-enough-ask-for-slower-speech role=model objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

Sometimes you can hear the speaker, but the message arrives too quickly. Repeating it at the same speed may not solve the problem. Ask the person to speak more slowly.

::: examples
- no: Kan du snakke litt saktere?
- en: Can you speak a little more slowly?
- no: Litt saktere, takk.
- en: A little more slowly, please.
:::

The word **litt**—*a little*—makes the request sound natural and moderate. **Takk**—*thank you/please*—adds politeness.

Imagine that Jonas gives Mina several details quickly:

::: {.reading translation="Jonas: The customer wants two coffees, one glass of water, and a bun." dialogue_id=fast-order speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Kunden vil ha to kaffe, ett glass vann og en bolle.
:::

Mina hears the words, but not clearly enough to follow the full order.

::: {.reading translation="Mina: Can you speak a little more slowly?" dialogue_id=fast-order speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Kan du snakke litt saktere?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Jonas: Yes. Two coffees, one glass of water, and a bun." dialogue_id=fast-order speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. To kaffe, ett glass vann og en bolle.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Mina: Two coffees, one glass of water, and a bun. All right." dialogue_id=fast-order speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
To kaffe, ett glass vann og en bolle. Greit.
:::

Here Mina does more than say that she has understood. She repeats the important information. This gives Jonas an opportunity to correct her if necessary.

Compare the two repair moves:

::: examples
- no: Kan du gjenta?
- en: Can you repeat that?
:::

Use this when you did not hear the message or missed a large part of it.

::: examples
- no: Kan du snakke litt saktere?
- en: Can you speak a little more slowly?
:::

Use this when speed is the main problem.

In real conversation, both problems can occur together. You can combine the requests:

::: examples
- no: Kan du gjenta litt saktere?
- en: Can you repeat that a little more slowly?
:::

This compact request tells the speaker exactly what would help.

## Checkpoint: choosing help that matches the problem {#choosing-help-that-matches-the-problem role=contrast objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

Work with three brief situations: a noisy room, a speaker who talks too quickly, and a message that disappears because you were looking away. For each situation, state the communication problem and make one suitable request in Norwegian.

This activity belongs here because effective repair depends on matching the request to the obstacle. Use everyday contexts such as the café counter, a bus stop, or a short telephone call. A successful response would demonstrate that you can distinguish a need for repetition from a need for slower speech and choose a clear, polite request.

## Ask about the unclear part {#ask-about-the-unclear-part role=model objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

You may hear most of a sentence but fail to understand one word or expression. In that case, asking for the entire message again may be unnecessary. Point to the unclear part.

::: examples
- no: Hva betyr «hent»?
- en: What does “hent” mean?
- no: Hva betyr det?
- en: What does that mean?
- no: Hva mener du?
- en: What do you mean?
:::

These questions solve slightly different problems.

Use **Hva betyr …?**—*What does … mean?*—when a word or expression is unfamiliar.

::: examples
- no: Hva betyr «ledig»?
- en: What does “ledig” mean?
- no: «Ledig» betyr at ingen bruker bordet.
- en: “Ledig” means that nobody is using the table.
:::

Use **Hva mener du?**—*What do you mean?*—when you heard the words but do not understand the speaker’s intended message.

::: {.reading translation="Jonas: You can take it afterwards." dialogue_id=meaning-clarification speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Du kan ta den etterpå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Mina: Sorry, what do you mean?" dialogue_id=meaning-clarification speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Unnskyld, hva mener du?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Jonas: You can take a break after the customers leave." dialogue_id=meaning-clarification speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Du kan ta pause etter at kundene går.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Mina: Oh, a break afterwards. Now I understand." dialogue_id=meaning-clarification speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Å, pause etterpå. Nå forstår jeg.
:::

Mina does not merely request help. After Jonas explains, she states the corrected interpretation: **pause etterpå**—*a break afterwards*. She then confirms that communication can continue: **Nå forstår jeg**—*Now I understand.*

A useful short pattern is:

::: examples
- no: Hva betyr X?
- en: What does X mean?
- no: Det betyr Y.
- en: It means Y.
- no: Å, Y. Nå forstår jeg.
- en: Oh, Y. Now I understand.
:::

The final line matters. It closes the repair sequence and tells the other person that no further explanation is needed.

## Checkpoint: isolating an unfamiliar word {#isolating-an-unfamiliar-word role=contrast objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

Return to Mina’s shift. Jonas uses one unfamiliar word in an otherwise understandable instruction. Ask what that word means. After receiving a simple explanation, confirm the meaning in your own short response and indicate that you now understand.

This activity belongs here because learners often understand enough to identify one specific gap. Asking about that gap is more efficient than restarting the whole conversation. A successful response would contain a focused question such as **Hva betyr …?**—*What does … mean?*—followed by a confirmation such as **Å, nå forstår jeg**—*Oh, now I understand.*

## Check a detail with *Mener du …?* {#check-a-detail-with-mener-du role=model objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

Sometimes every word is familiar, but one detail remains uncertain. Mina heard **bordet ved vinduet**—*the table by the window*—but there was more than one possible table. She checked her interpretation:

::: examples
- no: Mener du det lille bordet?
- en: Do you mean the small table?
:::

**Mener du …?**—*Do you mean …?*—allows you to offer your best interpretation and ask the speaker to confirm or correct it.

::: examples
- no: Mener du i dag?
- en: Do you mean today?
- no: Mener du klokka to?
- en: Do you mean at two o’clock?
- no: Mener du bussen til sentrum?
- en: Do you mean the bus to the city centre?
:::

This move is especially useful for places, times, people, numbers, and objects.

A repair can be very short:

::: {.reading translation="Aksel: We’ll meet outside." dialogue_id=place-confirmation speaker_id=aksel speaker_name=Aksel}
Vi møtes utenfor.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Nora: Do you mean outside the library?" dialogue_id=place-confirmation speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Mener du utenfor biblioteket?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Aksel: Yes, outside the library." dialogue_id=place-confirmation speaker_id=aksel speaker_name=Aksel}
Ja, utenfor biblioteket.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Nora: Good, see you there." dialogue_id=place-confirmation speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Fint, vi ses der.
:::

Nora identifies the uncertain detail, proposes an interpretation, receives confirmation, and continues by accepting the arrangement.

If the interpretation is wrong, the other person corrects it:

::: {.reading translation="Aksel: We’ll meet outside." dialogue_id=place-correction speaker_id=aksel speaker_name=Aksel}
Vi møtes utenfor.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Nora: Do you mean outside the library?" dialogue_id=place-correction speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Mener du utenfor biblioteket?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Aksel: No, outside the cinema." dialogue_id=place-correction speaker_id=aksel speaker_name=Aksel}
Nei, utenfor kinoen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Nora: Oh, outside the cinema. See you there." dialogue_id=place-correction speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Å, utenfor kinoen. Vi ses der.
:::

Notice how Nora repeats the corrected place. A simple **Ja**—*Yes*—would not show whether she had registered the correction. **Å, utenfor kinoen**—*Oh, outside the cinema*—makes the repaired message explicit.

## Checkpoint: confirming a place, time, or object {#confirming-place-time-or-object role=contrast objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

Use short situations in which most of the message is clear but one detail could have two interpretations. Ask **Mener du …?**—*Do you mean …?*—with your best interpretation. Then respond to either confirmation or correction by repeating the final detail and continuing the exchange.

This activity belongs here because partial understanding calls for a focused check, not a general request for repetition. Use familiar details from the café and other everyday settings: which table, which drink, which stop, or what time. A successful response would demonstrate that you can test an interpretation, accept a correction, and continue with the corrected information.

## Confirm before continuing {#confirm-before-continuing role=model objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

After the speaker repeats or explains, do not leave the exchange unfinished. Confirm what you now understand.

Several short confirmation expressions are useful:

::: examples
- no: Å ja, nå forstår jeg.
- en: Oh yes, now I understand.
- no: Greit.
- en: All right.
- no: Ok, klokka tre.
- en: OK, at three o’clock.
- no: Så du mener fredag?
- en: So you mean Friday?
- no: Riktig?
- en: Correct?
:::

Repeating the crucial information is often stronger than saying only **Jeg forstår**—*I understand*.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Greit.
- en: All right.
:::

This signals acceptance, but it does not reveal what you understood.

::: examples
- no: Greit, to kaffe og ett glass vann.
- en: All right, two coffees and one glass of water.
:::

This confirms the important details and lets the speaker detect an error.

A useful full sequence is:

::: examples
- no: Unnskyld, kan du gjenta?
- en: Sorry, can you repeat that?
- no: To kaffe og ett glass vann.
- en: Two coffees and one glass of water.
- no: Å ja, to kaffe og ett glass vann. Greit.
- en: Oh yes, two coffees and one glass of water. All right.
:::

Another sequence begins with a specific check:

::: examples
- no: Mener du bordet ved døra?
- en: Do you mean the table by the door?
- no: Nei, bordet ved vinduet.
- en: No, the table by the window.
- no: Å, bordet ved vinduet. Jeg tar kaffen dit.
- en: Oh, the table by the window. I’ll take the coffee there.
:::

The final sentence continues the task. Communication repair is not complete until both people can move on.

## Checkpoint: completing the repair cycle {#completing-repair-cycle role=contrast objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

Take a short interrupted exchange and supply Mina’s contributions at three stages: request help, confirm the corrected message, and continue with the café task. The initial problem might be a missed order, an unfamiliar word, or uncertainty about a table.

This activity belongs here because knowing one repair phrase is not the full outcome. The learner must carry the exchange from breakdown to successful continuation. A successful response would match the repair move to the problem, repeat or acknowledge the corrected information, and add a natural next step such as **Greit, jeg gjør det nå**—*All right, I’ll do it now.*

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=recap objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

## Pretending to understand {#pretending-to-understand role=contrast objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

A learner may say **Ja**—*Yes*—even when the message is unclear. This keeps the conversation moving for a moment, but often creates a larger problem later.

If you do not hear an order, say:

::: examples
- no: Unnskyld, kan du gjenta?
- en: Sorry, can you repeat that?
:::

Do not confirm information that you have not understood.

## Using a general question for a specific problem {#using-general-question-for-specific-problem role=contrast objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

If only one word is unfamiliar, **Kan du gjenta?**—*Can you repeat that?*—may produce exactly the same unfamiliar word. Ask directly:

::: examples
- no: Hva betyr «kvittering»?
- en: What does “receipt” mean?
:::

If only the location is uncertain, check it:

::: examples
- no: Mener du bordet ved døra?
- en: Do you mean the table by the door?
:::

A specific repair request makes a useful answer more likely.

## Asking for slower speech when meaning is the problem {#asking-for-slower-speech-when-meaning-is-the-problem role=contrast objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

**Kan du snakke litt saktere?**—*Can you speak a little more slowly?*—helps when speed prevents understanding. It does not explain an unknown word. If the word itself is the obstacle, use **Hva betyr …?**—*What does … mean?*

## Failing to register a correction {#failing-to-register-a-correction role=contrast objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

Consider this exchange:

::: {.reading translation="Jonas: No, I mean table five." dialogue_id=correction-registration speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nei, jeg mener bord fem.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Mina: All right." dialogue_id=correction-registration speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Greit.
:::

Mina may have understood, but Jonas cannot be certain. A clearer response is:

::: examples
- no: Å, bord fem. Greit.
- en: Oh, table five. All right.
:::

Repeating the corrected detail shows that the repair worked.

## Continuing without closing the repair {#continuing-without-closing-repair role=contrast objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

After an explanation, add a brief confirmation and next step:

::: examples
- no: Å ja, nå forstår jeg. Jeg tar den med en gang.
- en: Oh yes, now I understand. I’ll take it right away.
:::

This sounds more complete than asking a question and then giving no response.

## Back at the café {#back-at-the-cafe role=model objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

Later in the morning, Jonas gives Mina another message.

::: {.reading translation="Jonas: Mina, the customer by the door wants to pay by card, and afterwards you can clear table six." dialogue_id=complete-cafe-repair speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Mina, kunden ved døra vil betale med kort, og etterpå kan du rydde bord seks.
:::

Mina hears most of the message, but Jonas speaks too quickly.

::: {.reading translation="Mina: Sorry, can you repeat that a little more slowly?" dialogue_id=complete-cafe-repair speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Unnskyld, kan du gjenta litt saktere?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Jonas: The customer by the door wants to pay by card. Afterwards you can clear table six." dialogue_id=complete-cafe-repair speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Kunden ved døra vil betale med kort. Etterpå kan du rydde bord seks.
:::

Mina is unsure about the table number.

::: {.reading translation="Mina: Do you mean table sixteen?" dialogue_id=complete-cafe-repair speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Mener du bord seksten?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Jonas: No, table six." dialogue_id=complete-cafe-repair speaker_id=jonas speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nei, bord seks.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Mina: Oh, table six. First I’ll help the customer, and then I’ll clear the table." dialogue_id=complete-cafe-repair speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Å, bord seks. Først hjelper jeg kunden, og så rydder jeg bordet.
:::

This time Mina handles the complete repair independently. She identifies speed as the first problem and requests slower repetition. She then identifies one uncertain detail and checks it. Jonas corrects the number. Mina repeats the corrected number and states what she will do next.

## Integrated practice: repairing a complete exchange {#integrated-practice-repairing-complete-exchange role=contrast objectives=obj-ask-for-repetition-or-clarification-01}

Create a guided exchange based on Mina’s workday. Begin with a message that contains one realistic communication problem: Mina misses the whole message, the speaker is too fast, one word is unfamiliar, or one detail is uncertain. Have Mina choose a matching repair move. After the message is repeated, explained, or corrected, have her confirm the important information and continue the task.

This activity belongs near the end because it combines all stages without changing the lesson’s communicative goal. The context remains concrete and familiar. A successful response would make the problem visible through the chosen request, show that Mina has understood the corrected message, and end with a relevant continuation rather than stopping after the repair phrase.
