## At the swimming pool {#at-the-swimming-pool role=orient objectives=obj-possessive-pronouns-01}

Mina and Jonas have finished swimming. Their clothes, towels, and bags are mixed together on a bench. Mina’s daughter, Lea, is waiting nearby.

::: {.reading translation="Where is my towel?" dialogue_id="pool-anchor" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Hvor er håndkleet mitt?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Is this your towel?" dialogue_id="pool-anchor" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Er dette håndkleet ditt?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, mine is blue. That is your towel." dialogue_id="pool-anchor" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Nei, mitt er blått. Det der er håndkleet ditt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="You’re right. And here is your bag." dialogue_id="pool-anchor" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Du har rett. Og her er veska di.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thanks. But where are my shoes?" dialogue_id="pool-anchor" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Takk. Men hvor er skoene mine?
:::

::: {.reading translation="They’re under the bench. Is this my jacket?" dialogue_id="pool-anchor" speaker_id="lea" speaker_name="Lea" character_id="lea"}
De står under benken. Er dette jakka mi?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. And here are our clothes. Now we have everything." dialogue_id="pool-anchor" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja. Og her er klærne våre. Nå har vi alt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Wait a moment! Where is our access card?" dialogue_id="pool-anchor" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Vent litt! Hvor er adgangskortet vårt?
:::

::: {.reading translation="It’s in your pocket, Jonas." dialogue_id="pool-anchor" speaker_id="lea" speaker_name="Lea" character_id="lea"}
Det ligger i lomma di, Jonas.
:::

The situation is simple: several people need to say who things belong to. Norwegian words corresponding to my, your, and our are **possessive pronouns**. The Norwegian term is **eiendomspronomen**.

The dialogue contains several forms:

::: examples
- no: håndkleet mitt
- en: my towel
- no: håndkleet ditt
- en: your towel
- no: veska di
- en: your bag
- no: skoene mine
- en: my shoes
- no: jakka mi
- en: my jacket
- no: klærne våre
- en: our clothes
- no: adgangskortet vårt
- en: our access card
:::

The possessive changes to match the noun. To choose the correct form, ask what gender and number the possessed noun has. The owner does not determine the ending.

## One owner, different nouns {#one-owner-different-nouns role=model objectives=obj-possessive-pronouns-01}

Mina says **mitt**, **mi**, and **mine** because the things she owns have different grammatical forms.

**My: min, mi, mitt, mine**

Use **min** with a singular masculine noun:

::: examples
- no: bilen min
- en: my car
- no: sekken min
- en: my backpack
:::

Use **mi** with a singular feminine noun:

::: examples
- no: veska mi
- en: my bag
- no: jakka mi
- en: my jacket
:::

Use **mitt** with a singular neuter noun:

::: examples
- no: håndkleet mitt
- en: my towel
- no: kortet mitt
- en: my card
:::

Use **mine** with plural nouns of any gender:

::: examples
- no: skoene mine
- en: my shoes
- no: vennene mine
- en: my friends
:::

The compact pattern is:

- masculine singular: min
- feminine singular: mi
- neuter singular: mitt
- plural: mine

The important question is not whether the owner is a man or a woman. Mina can say all four forms:

::: examples
- no: Det er bilen min.
- en: It is my car.
- no: Det er veska mi.
- en: It is my bag.
- no: Det er håndkleet mitt.
- en: It is my towel.
- no: Det er skoene mine.
- en: They are my shoes.
:::

The noun controls the form. **Bil** is masculine, **veske** is feminine, **håndkle** is neuter, and **skoene** is plural.

**A note on feminine nouns**

In Bokmål, many feminine nouns can also be treated as masculine. You may therefore meet both of these:

::: examples
- no: veska mi
- en: my bag
- no: vesken min
- en: my bag
:::

Both are standard Bokmål. Keep the noun form and the possessive form consistent. If you use the feminine definite form **veska**, use **mi**. If you use the masculine-style definite form **vesken**, use **min**.

For this chapter, feminine examples mainly use forms such as **veska mi** and **jakka mi**.

## Your works in the same way {#your-works-in-the-same-way role=model objectives=obj-possessive-pronouns-01}

Jonas asks Mina:

::: examples
- no: Er dette håndkleet ditt?
- en: Is this your towel?
:::

Later, Lea says that the access card is in Jonas’s pocket:

::: examples
- no: Det ligger i lomma di.
- en: It is in your pocket.
:::

The basic forms for your when speaking to one person are **din**, **di**, **ditt**, and **dine**.

**Your: din, di, ditt, dine**

Use **din** with a singular masculine noun:

::: examples
- no: telefonen din
- en: your phone
- no: nøkkelen din
- en: your key
:::

Use **di** with a singular feminine noun:

::: examples
- no: jakka di
- en: your jacket
- no: boka di
- en: your book
:::

Use **ditt** with a singular neuter noun:

::: examples
- no: kortet ditt
- en: your card
- no: rommet ditt
- en: your room
:::

Use **dine** with plural nouns:

::: examples
- no: skoene dine
- en: your shoes
- no: nøklene dine
- en: your keys
:::

The pattern closely follows the forms for my:

- masculine singular — My: min; Your: din
- feminine singular — My: mi; Your: di
- neuter singular — My: mitt; Your: ditt
- plural — My: mine; Your: dine

Compare the forms in complete sentences:

::: examples
- no: Her er sekken min.
- en: Here is my backpack.
- no: Her er sekken din.
- en: Here is your backpack.
- no: Jeg finner ikke jakka mi.
- en: I can’t find my jacket.
- no: Jeg finner ikke jakka di.
- en: I can’t find your jacket.
- no: Kortet mitt ligger på bordet.
- en: My card is on the table.
- no: Kortet ditt ligger på bordet.
- en: Your card is on the table.
- no: Skoene mine er våte.
- en: My shoes are wet.
- no: Skoene dine er våte.
- en: Your shoes are wet.
:::

The noun stays in the same gender or number category. The first part of the possessive changes because the owner changes from my to your.

## Our: vår, vårt, våre {#our-forms-vaar-vart-vaare role=model objectives=obj-possessive-pronouns-01}

Near the end of the dialogue, Mina and Jonas are concerned with things they share:

::: examples
- no: Her er klærne våre.
- en: Here are our clothes.
- no: Hvor er adgangskortet vårt?
- en: Where is our access card?
:::

The forms for our are **vår**, **vårt**, and **våre**.

Use **vår** with a singular masculine or feminine noun:

::: examples
- no: bilen vår
- en: our car
- no: hytta vår
- en: our cabin
:::

Use **vårt** with a singular neuter noun:

::: examples
- no: huset vårt
- en: our house
- no: rommet vårt
- en: our room
:::

Use **våre** with plural nouns:

::: examples
- no: klærne våre
- en: our clothes
- no: vennene våre
- en: our friends
:::

The compact pattern is:

- masculine or feminine singular: vår
- neuter singular: vårt
- plural: våre

Compare all three owners:

::: examples
- no: Dette er rommet mitt.
- en: This is my room.
- no: Dette er rommet ditt.
- en: This is your room.
- no: Dette er rommet vårt.
- en: This is our room.
:::

Because **rom** is neuter, the forms are **mitt**, **ditt**, and **vårt**.

Now compare plural nouns:

::: examples
- no: Her er billettene mine.
- en: Here are my tickets.
- no: Her er billettene dine.
- en: Here are your tickets.
- no: Her er billettene våre.
- en: Here are our tickets.
:::

Because **billettene** is plural, the forms are **mine**, **dine**, and **våre**.

A common mistake is to build **our** exactly like **my** and **your** by expecting four distinct forms. Basic Bokmål normally uses **vår** for both masculine and feminine singular nouns:

::: examples
- no: naboen vår
- en: our neighbor
- no: døra vår
- en: our door
:::

There is no separate basic feminine form corresponding to **mi** or **di**.

## The usual noun pattern {#usual-noun-pattern role=model objectives=obj-possessive-pronouns-01}

In the swimming-pool dialogue, the possessive normally comes after the noun:

::: examples
- no: håndkleet mitt
- en: my towel
- no: veska di
- en: your bag
- no: klærne våre
- en: our clothes
:::

When the possessive comes after the noun, the noun normally has its definite form. This is the form often translated with the in other contexts.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: et håndkle
- en: a towel
- no: håndkleet
- en: the towel
- no: håndkleet mitt
- en: my towel
:::

And:

::: examples
- no: en sekk
- en: a backpack
- no: sekken
- en: the backpack
- no: sekken din
- en: your backpack
:::

For a feminine noun:

::: examples
- no: ei jakke
- en: a jacket
- no: jakka
- en: the jacket
- no: jakka mi
- en: my jacket
:::

For a plural noun:

::: examples
- no: sko
- en: shoes
- no: skoene
- en: the shoes
- no: skoene våre
- en: our shoes
:::

A useful basic pattern is therefore:

**definite noun + matching possessive**

::: examples
- no: telefonen min
- en: my phone
- no: boka di
- en: your book
- no: huset vårt
- en: our house
- no: nøklene våre
- en: our keys
:::

English does not say the phone my or the house our. Norwegian uses this structure naturally, so avoid translating the word order piece by piece.

**Possessive before the noun**

A possessive can also come before the noun. In that pattern, the noun uses its indefinite form:

::: examples
- no: min telefon
- en: my phone
- no: di bok
- en: your book
- no: vårt hus
- en: our house
- no: våre nøkler
- en: our keys
:::

Compare the two orders:

::: examples
- no: telefonen min
- en: my phone
- no: min telefon
- en: my phone
- no: huset vårt
- en: our house
- no: vårt hus
- en: our house
:::

Both orders are grammatical. The after-noun pattern is very common in everyday Norwegian and is the main pattern in this chapter. The before-noun pattern can place more emphasis or contrast on the owner.

For example, if Mina and Jonas are distinguishing two towels, Mina could stress ownership:

::: examples
- no: Dette er mitt håndkle, ikke ditt håndkle.
- en: This is my towel, not your towel.
:::

In a neutral question, she is likely to say:

::: examples
- no: Hvor er håndkleet mitt?
- en: Where is my towel?
:::

The agreement form still depends on the noun in both positions. **Håndkle** is neuter, so the form is **mitt** whether it appears before or after the noun.

Do not mix the noun forms:

::: examples
- no: håndkleet mitt
- en: my towel
- no: mitt håndkle
- en: my towel
:::

The combination **mitt håndkleet** incorrectly combines the before-noun possessive with the definite noun. Likewise, **håndkle mitt** omits the definite ending expected in the usual after-noun pattern.

## Choosing the form step by step {#choosing-the-form-step-by-step role=model objectives=obj-possessive-pronouns-01}

When you need to say my, your, or our, make the decisions in this order.

First, identify the owner:

- the speaker: my
- the person being addressed: your
- the speaker together with another person: our

Next, identify the noun’s gender or number:

- masculine singular
- feminine singular
- neuter singular
- plural

Then choose the matching possessive form.

For example, suppose Jonas wants to say your key to Mina.

The owner is the person he is addressing, so he needs the **din** set. **Nøkkel** is masculine singular, so the phrase is:

::: examples
- no: nøkkelen din
- en: your key
:::

Suppose Mina wants to say our room.

The owners are Mina and another person, so she needs the **vår** set. **Rom** is neuter singular, so the phrase is:

::: examples
- no: rommet vårt
- en: our room
:::

Suppose Lea wants to say my shoes.

The owner is Lea herself, so she needs the **min** set. **Sko** is plural here, so the phrase is:

::: examples
- no: skoene mine
- en: my shoes
:::

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-possessive-pronouns-01}

**Choosing from the owner’s gender**

Incorrect reasoning: Mina is female, so all her possessions use **mi**.

Correct reasoning: The possessed noun determines the agreement form.

For this chapter’s target of my, Mina herself would say:

::: examples
- no: Jeg finner bilen min.
- en: I find my car.
- no: Jeg finner jakka mi.
- en: I find my jacket.
- no: Jeg finner kortet mitt.
- en: I find my card.
- no: Jeg finner skoene mine.
- en: I find my shoes.
:::

The different forms come from **bil**, **jakke**, **kort**, and plural **sko**, not from Mina’s gender.

**Using the neuter form with every singular noun**

The **-t** forms belong with singular neuter nouns:

::: examples
- no: håndkleet mitt
- en: my towel
- no: kortet ditt
- en: your card
- no: huset vårt
- en: our house
:::

They do not belong with every singular object. Use **min**, **mi**, **din**, **di**, or **vår** when the noun requires those forms:

::: examples
- no: sekken min
- en: my backpack
- no: døra di
- en: your door
- no: bilen vår
- en: our car
:::

**Forgetting the plural form**

Plural agreement is the same across noun genders:

::: examples
- no: bøkene mine
- en: my books
- no: kortene dine
- en: your cards
- no: sekkene våre
- en: our backpacks
:::

Do not choose a singular form merely because each individual object has a gender. Once the noun is plural, use **mine**, **dine**, or **våre**.

**Mixing possessive position and noun form**

Use:

::: examples
- no: veska mi
- en: my bag
- no: mi veske
- en: my bag
- no: kortet vårt
- en: our card
- no: vårt kort
- en: our card
:::

Keep each pattern intact.

The same agreement pattern continues even as the owner changes:

::: examples
- no: lua mi
- en: my hat
- no: lua di
- en: your hat
- no: håndklærne våre
- en: our towels
- no: kortet vårt
- en: our card
- no: bilen vår
- en: our car
:::

The scene is no longer a collection of isolated labels. Mina, Jonas, and Lea use possessives to solve a practical problem: identifying belongings and making sure everyone leaves with the right things.
