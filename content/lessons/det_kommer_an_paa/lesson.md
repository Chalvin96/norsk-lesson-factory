## A weekend plan {#weekend-plan role=orient objectives=obj-det-kommer-an-paa-01}

On Thursday afternoon, Nora and Amir are deciding whether to spend Saturday at Nora’s family cabin. The cabin is outside the city, and getting there involves both a train and a short walk.

::: {.reading translation="Are you coming to the cabin on Saturday?" dialogue_id=weekend-plan speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Skal du være med på hytta på lørdag?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Maybe. How long does the trip take?" dialogue_id=weekend-plan speaker_id=amir speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Kanskje. Hvor lang tid tar turen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="It depends on the train. If we catch the express train, it takes about an hour." dialogue_id=weekend-plan speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det kommer an på toget. Hvis vi rekker hurtigtoget, tar det omtrent en time.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And do we have to walk far from the station?" dialogue_id=weekend-plan speaker_id=amir speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Og må vi gå langt fra stasjonen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="It depends on where we get off. From the first stop it takes forty minutes, but from the second it takes only ten." dialogue_id=weekend-plan speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det kommer an på hvor vi går av. Fra den første holdeplassen tar det førti minutter, men fra den andre tar det bare ti.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Is it cold at the cabin?" dialogue_id=weekend-plan speaker_id=amir speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Er det kaldt på hytta?
:::

::: {.reading translation="It depends on the weather. When the sun is shining, it gets quite warm inside." dialogue_id=weekend-plan speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det kommer an på været. Når sola skinner, blir det ganske varmt inne.
:::

::: {.reading translation="What will we eat for dinner?" dialogue_id=weekend-plan speaker_id=amir speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Hva skal vi spise til middag?
:::

::: {.reading translation="It depends on who is coming. Sara doesn’t eat meat." dialogue_id=weekend-plan speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det kommer an på hvem som blir med. Sara spiser ikke kjøtt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then I’ll come if it doesn’t rain all day." dialogue_id=weekend-plan speaker_id=amir speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Da blir jeg med hvis det ikke regner hele dagen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. I’ll check the weather forecast tomorrow." dialogue_id=weekend-plan speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Flott. Jeg sjekker værmeldingen i morgen.
:::

Amir asks several questions that look as if they should have simple answers: How long does the trip take? Is the cabin cold? What will they eat for dinner? Nora cannot give one answer without knowing more. Each answer varies with a particular factor: the train, the weather, the place where they get off, or the people who come.

The useful expression is **det kommer an på**: *it depends on*.

## One answer, several possibilities {#one-answer-several-possibilities role=model objectives=obj-det-kommer-an-paa-01}

Use **det kommer an på** when the answer changes according to a named factor.

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på været.
- en: It depends on the weather.
:::

Good weather may produce one result; bad weather may produce another. **Været** is the factor that decides which answer is appropriate.

The expression is fixed. It is best to learn **kommer an på** as one unit rather than translating each word separately. In this expression, **det** does not refer to a specific object. The complete phrase says that the result varies according to a named factor.

Compare the following answers:

::: examples
- no: Er det dyrt å bo i Oslo?
- en: Is it expensive to live in Oslo?
- no: Ja, det er dyrt.
- en: Yes, it is expensive.
:::

This gives one direct answer.

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på hvor du bor.
- en: It depends on where you live.
:::

This says that the answer varies. One neighborhood may be expensive, while another may be less expensive. The speaker also names the factor: **hvor du bor**.

Here are more situations in which a direct yes-or-no answer is not enough:

::: examples
- no: Er restauranten god?
- en: Is the restaurant good?
- no: Det kommer an på hva du bestiller.
- en: It depends on what you order.
- no: Kan vi gå dit?
- en: Can we walk there?
- no: Det kommer an på avstanden.
- en: It depends on the distance.
- no: Blir møtet langt?
- en: Will the meeting be long?
- no: Det kommer an på hvor mange spørsmål folk har.
- en: It depends on how many questions people have.
:::

In every case, the expression signals variation and the rest of the sentence identifies what creates that variation.

## Checkpoint: identify the deciding factor {#identify-deciding-factor role=contrast objectives=obj-det-kommer-an-paa-01}

At this point, the learner should return to Nora and Amir’s conversation and identify the factor in each use of **det kommer an på**. The activity belongs here because understanding the relationship between an uncertain answer and its deciding factor must come before producing the expression independently.

The learner should explain, in simple terms, why the trip time depends on the train, why the cabin temperature depends on the weather, and why the meal depends on the people attending. A successful response would demonstrate that the learner is not treating **det kommer an på** as a vague way to avoid answering. It should show that each use points to a real factor that can change the answer.

## Naming the factor with a noun {#naming-factor-with-noun role=model objectives=obj-det-kommer-an-paa-01}

The shortest pattern is:

::: {.rule}
**Det kommer an på + noun.**
:::

Nora uses this pattern twice:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på toget.
- en: It depends on the train.
- no: Det kommer an på været.
- en: It depends on the weather.
:::

The noun can refer to a person, a thing, a condition, or a practical consideration.

::: examples
- no: Når kan vi dra? Det kommer an på jobben.
- en: When can we leave? It depends on work.
- no: Skal barna være med? Det kommer an på foreldrene.
- en: Are the children coming? It depends on the parents.
- no: Er kurset vanskelig? Det kommer an på nivået.
- en: Is the course difficult? It depends on the level.
- no: Kan vi spise ute? Det kommer an på temperaturen.
- en: Can we eat outside? It depends on the temperature.
:::

A bare noun may be enough when both speakers understand the situation. If two friends are discussing a picnic, **Det kommer an på været** is clear because they know how weather affects the plan.

You can make the connection more explicit by adding contrasting results:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på prisen. Hvis det er billig, kjøper jeg det.
- en: It depends on the price. If it is cheap, I’ll buy it.
- no: Det kommer an på trafikken. Uten kø tar turen tjue minutter.
- en: It depends on the traffic. Without a traffic jam, the trip takes twenty minutes.
- no: Det kommer an på formen. Hvis jeg føler meg bra, løper jeg hele veien.
- en: It depends on how I’m feeling. If I feel well, I’ll run the whole way.
:::

The second sentence is not part of the fixed expression, but it helps the listener understand how the factor changes the answer.

## Controlled practice: complete an answer with a named noun {#complete-answer-with-named-noun role=contrast objectives=obj-det-kommer-an-paa-01}

The learner should now respond to short practical questions by using **Det kommer an på** followed by a relevant noun already present in the context. Suitable contexts include choosing whether to travel, deciding whether to eat outside, estimating a journey, or judging whether something is expensive.

For example, the learner might hear a question about whether a train journey will be quick after being told that some trains stop at every station. The response should name **toget** as the deciding factor. In another prompt, a proposed outdoor activity may be affected by rain, so the response should name **været**.

This controlled activity belongs here because the sentence frame remains stable while the deciding noun changes. A successful response would contain the complete fixed expression, include **på**, and name a factor that genuinely explains why the answer can vary.

## Naming a fuller situation {#naming-fuller-situation role=model objectives=obj-det-kommer-an-paa-01}

Sometimes one noun is not precise enough. Norwegian can follow **det kommer an på** with a fuller question-like clause:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på hvor vi går av.
- en: It depends on where we get off.
- no: Det kommer an på hva du bestiller.
- en: It depends on what you order.
- no: Det kommer an på hvem som blir med.
- en: It depends on who is coming.
- no: Det kommer an på når du reiser.
- en: It depends on when you travel.
:::

These clauses name the factor more precisely. They can specify **where**, **what**, **who**, or **when** the result varies.

Compare a broad factor with a more precise one:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på reisen.
- en: It depends on the journey.
- no: Det kommer an på hvordan du reiser.
- en: It depends on how you travel.
:::

The first answer is possible, but broad. The second tells the listener exactly which aspect matters: the means or manner of travel.

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på maten.
- en: It depends on the food.
- no: Det kommer an på hva vi skal spise.
- en: It depends on what we are going to eat.
:::

Again, both can be natural. The fuller version identifies the unresolved question more clearly.

A clause beginning with **om** is useful when the deciding factor is whether something happens or is true:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på om det regner.
- en: It depends on whether it rains.
- no: Det kommer an på om butikken er åpen.
- en: It depends on whether the shop is open.
- no: Det kommer an på om vi får billetter.
- en: It depends on whether we get tickets.
:::

Notice the difference:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på været.
- en: It depends on the weather.
:::

This names a broad factor.

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på om det regner.
- en: It depends on whether it rains.
:::

This names one specific condition.

Both answers fit Nora and Amir’s weekend plan, but they focus on the uncertainty at different levels.

## Checkpoint: choose enough detail {#choose-enough-detail role=contrast objectives=obj-det-kommer-an-paa-01}

The learner should compare pairs of possible factors and decide which one gives the listener enough information in a particular context. For Nora and Amir, **været** may be sufficient when discussing the weekend generally, while **om det regner** is more precise when Amir’s decision specifically depends on rain.

This activity belongs here because learners often understand the fixed phrase but name a factor that is too broad to be useful. A successful response would select or produce a factor at the right level of detail and explain how that factor changes the answer.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-det-kommer-an-paa-01}

**Leaving out an**

The target expression is **kommer an på**, not simply **kommer på**.

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på prisen.
- en: It depends on the price.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Det kommer på prisen.
- en: ✗ It comes on the price.
:::

Without **an**, the sentence no longer has the intended fixed-expression meaning.

**Replacing på with another preposition**

Keep **på** in this expression.

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på været.
- en: It depends on the weather.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Det kommer an av været.
- en: ✗ It depends from the weather.
:::

It is safer to remember the entire sequence **kommer an på** as one phrase.

**Giving no meaningful factor**

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på.
- en: It depends.
:::

This can occur when the factor is obvious from the conversation, but it does not meet the lesson goal when the listener needs to know what the answer depends on. Prefer:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer an på hvor mye tid vi har.
- en: It depends on how much time we have.
:::

Naming the factor turns a vague answer into useful information.

**Using the phrase when the answer does not vary**

If you know that the shop is closed, say so directly:

::: examples
- no: Nei, butikken er stengt.
- en: No, the shop is closed.
:::

**Det kommer an på** is appropriate only when different conditions could lead to different answers.

## Guided practice: explain two possible outcomes {#explain-two-possible-outcomes role=contrast objectives=obj-det-kommer-an-paa-01}

The learner should now answer questions with two parts: first **Det kommer an på** plus a named factor, then a brief explanation of how two conditions produce different outcomes.

Contexts should remain practical and familiar. A journey might be short without traffic but long during rush hour. A restaurant might be affordable at lunchtime but expensive in the evening. A walk might be pleasant in dry weather but difficult in heavy rain.

This activity belongs here because it checks more than phrase recall. The learner must show why dependence exists. A successful response would name a relevant factor and connect at least two possible values of that factor to different results, without changing the fixed form **kommer an på**.

## Back to the cabin plan {#back-to-cabin-plan role=model objectives=obj-det-kommer-an-paa-01}

On Friday, Nora checks the forecast and calls Amir.

::: {.reading translation="Now I’ve checked the weather forecast. There will be a little rain in the morning, but sun after lunch." dialogue_id=friday-cabin-plan speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Nå har jeg sett på værmeldingen. Det blir litt regn om morgenen, men sol etter lunsj.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then we can go. When shall we meet?" dialogue_id=friday-cabin-plan speaker_id=amir speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Da kan vi dra. Når møtes vi?
:::

::: {.reading translation="It depends on which train we take. If we take the express train, we’ll meet at nine. It leaves at quarter past nine." dialogue_id=friday-cabin-plan speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det kommer an på hvilket tog vi tar. Hvis vi tar hurtigtoget, møtes vi klokka ni. Det går kvart over ni.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Let’s take that one. How much food should I buy?" dialogue_id=friday-cabin-plan speaker_id=amir speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
La oss ta det. Hvor mye mat skal jeg kjøpe?
:::

::: {.reading translation="It depends on how many people are coming. Sara will reply this evening." dialogue_id=friday-cabin-plan speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det kommer an på hvor mange som kommer. Sara svarer i kveld.
:::

::: {.reading translation="All right. And how long will we stay at the cabin?" dialogue_id=friday-cabin-plan speaker_id=amir speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Greit. Og hvor lenge blir vi på hytta?
:::

::: {.reading translation="It depends on the weather on Sunday. If it is nice, we’ll stay until the evening." dialogue_id=friday-cabin-plan speaker_id=nora speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det kommer an på været på søndag. Hvis det blir fint, blir vi til kvelden.
:::

The new conversation reuses the expression naturally, but the factors are more precise. Nora does not say only **toget**; she says **hvilket tog vi tar**. She does not say only **folkene**; she says **hvor mange som kommer**. The expression helps Amir understand what information they still need before they can settle each part of the plan.

## Contextual practice: continue the planning conversation {#continue-planning-conversation role=contrast objectives=obj-det-kommer-an-paa-01}

The learner should continue Nora and Amir’s exchange by responding to new questions about the cabin trip. Each answer should use **det kommer an på** and name a factor supplied by the situation, such as transport, number of guests, weather, cost, or available time. Some prompts should call for a noun; others should require a fuller clause such as **om …**, **hvor …**, or **hvem …**.

This activity belongs near the end because the learner must now choose both the relevant factor and an appropriate amount of detail while maintaining one coherent context. A successful response would sound like a useful contribution to the planning conversation rather than an isolated grammar example.

{{exercise: continue_planning_conversation}}

## Final retrieval {#final-retrieval role=recap objectives=obj-det-kommer-an-paa-01}

Without looking back, answer three new practical questions with **det kommer an på**. Name a different deciding factor each time, and make one answer precise with **om**, **hva**, **hvem**, **hvor**, **når**, or **hvordan**. Success means using the complete expression naturally when the answer genuinely varies with the factor you name.
