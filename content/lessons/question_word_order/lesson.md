## A plan for the evening {#sec-a-plan-for-the-evening role=orient objectives=obj-question-word-order-01}

Sara and Jonas are meeting outside a small cinema. Sara has already bought her ticket, but Jonas is uncertain about the plan.

::: {.reading translation="Hi, Jonas! Are you coming to the film tonight?" dialogue_id="cinema-evening" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Hei, Jonas! Kommer du på filmen i kveld?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but doesn’t it start at seven?" dialogue_id="cinema-evening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, men begynner den ikke klokka sju?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, it starts at seven. Have you bought a ticket?" dialogue_id="cinema-evening" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Jo, den begynner klokka sju. Har du kjøpt billett?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, not yet. Where do I buy a ticket?" dialogue_id="cinema-evening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nei, ikke ennå. Hvor kjøper jeg billett?
:::

::: {.reading translation="You can buy it inside." dialogue_id="cinema-evening" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Du kan kjøpe den inne.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Can I pay by card?" dialogue_id="cinema-evening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Kan jeg betale med kort?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, you can. Shall we go inside now?" dialogue_id="cinema-evening" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Ja, det kan du. Skal vi gå inn nå?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Wait a moment. Where is Emil? Isn’t he coming?" dialogue_id="cinema-evening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Vent litt. Hvor er Emil? Kommer han ikke?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, he is coming, but he is a little late." dialogue_id="cinema-evening" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Jo, han kommer, men han er litt sen.
:::

Sara and Jonas ask several kinds of questions, but they repeatedly use the same central pattern: the verb comes before the subject. Compare Sara’s statement with her question:

::: examples
- no: Du kommer på filmen i kveld.
- en: You are coming to the film tonight.
- no: Kommer du på filmen i kveld?
- en: Are you coming to the film tonight?
:::

In the statement, the subject du comes before the verb kommer. In the question, kommer moves in front of du.

This chapter will help you ask and recognize four closely connected kinds of question:

- simple yes/no questions
- negative questions
- questions beginning with a question word
- questions asking for permission

The main decision is whether the question begins directly with the verb or begins with a question word followed by the verb.

## Simple yes/no questions {#sec-simple-yes-no-questions role=model objectives=obj-question-word-order-01}

A yes/no question asks whether something is true. The expected response might be ja or nei, although the speaker can naturally give more information.

In a Norwegian main-clause statement, the subject commonly comes before the verb:

::: examples
- no: Du bor i Bergen.
- en: You live in Bergen.
:::

To turn this into a yes/no question, put the verb first:

::: examples
- no: Bor du i Bergen?
- en: Do you live in Bergen?
:::

The compact pattern is:

::: {.rule}
verb + subject + rest of the question
:::

Compare the word order:

::: examples
- no: Du jobber her.
- en: You work here.
- no: Jobber du her?
- en: Do you work here?
- no: Hun snakker norsk.
- en: She speaks Norwegian.
- no: Snakker hun norsk?
- en: Does she speak Norwegian?
- no: Dere kommer i morgen.
- en: You are coming tomorrow.
- no: Kommer dere i morgen?
- en: Are you coming tomorrow?
:::

English often adds do or does to make a present-tense question. Norwegian normally does not add a corresponding extra word.

::: examples
- no: Liker du kaffe?
- en: Do you like coffee?
- no: ✗ Gjør du liker kaffe?
- en: Do you like coffee?
:::

Norwegian simply puts liker before du.

The same verb-first order works with er:

::: examples
- no: Bussen er sen.
- en: The bus is late.
- no: Er bussen sen?
- en: Is the bus late?
:::

It also works when a sentence contains more than one verb. The first verb moves before the subject:

::: examples
- no: Du har kjøpt billett.
- en: You have bought a ticket.
- no: Har du kjøpt billett?
- en: Have you bought a ticket?
- no: Vi skal gå inn.
- en: We are going to go inside.
- no: Skal vi gå inn?
- en: Shall we go inside?
:::

**Common mistake: keeping statement order**

A question mark does not turn statement word order into normal question word order.

Less natural as a neutral question:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Du kommer i kveld?
- en: You’re coming tonight?
:::

This order can occur when someone is surprised or checking something they think is already true. For an ordinary yes/no question, use the verb first:

::: examples
- no: Kommer du i kveld?
- en: Are you coming tonight?
:::

**Purpose:** Notice the verb-before-subject pattern before producing it independently.

**Context:** Short statements and questions about Sara and Jonas’s cinema plans.

**Learner action:** Identify which lines are ordinary yes/no questions by attending to the position of the verb and subject.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates recognition that an ordinary yes/no question begins with the verb rather than the subject.

## Negative questions {#sec-negative-questions role=model objectives=obj-question-word-order-01}

A negative question contains ikke. It may ask whether something is not true, or it may show that the speaker expected something different.

Jonas thinks the film starts at seven, so he asks:

::: examples
- no: Begynner den ikke klokka sju?
- en: Doesn’t it start at seven?
:::

Sara expects Emil to come:

::: examples
- no: Kommer han ikke?
- en: Isn’t he coming?
:::

In a simple negative yes/no question, use this order:

::: {.rule}
verb + subject + ikke + rest
:::

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Han kommer ikke i kveld.
- en: He is not coming tonight.
- no: Kommer han ikke i kveld?
- en: Isn’t he coming tonight?
- no: Du har ikke billett.
- en: You do not have a ticket.
- no: Har du ikke billett?
- en: Don’t you have a ticket?
- no: Kafeen er ikke åpen.
- en: The café is not open.
- no: Er kafeen ikke åpen?
- en: Isn’t the café open?
:::

The first verb still begins the question. Ikke normally follows the subject in these simple questions.

::: examples
- no: Kommer du i kveld?
- en: Are you coming tonight?
- no: Kommer du ikke i kveld?
- en: Aren’t you coming tonight?
- no: Har hun en billett?
- en: Does she have a ticket?
- no: Har hun ikke en billett?
- en: Doesn’t she have a ticket?
- no: Er dere klare?
- en: Are you ready?
- no: Er dere ikke klare?
- en: Aren’t you ready?
:::

A negative question can be a genuine request for information:

::: examples
- no: Jobber du ikke på mandager?
- en: Don’t you work on Mondays?
:::

It can also check an expectation:

::: examples
- no: Skal vi ikke møte Emil her?
- en: Aren’t we going to meet Emil here?
:::

The grammar is the same in both cases. Context and tone tell the listener whether the speaker is neutral, surprised, or seeking confirmation.

**Answering a negative question with jo**

In the dialogue, Jonas asks whether Emil is not coming:

::: examples
- no: Kommer han ikke?
- en: Isn’t he coming?
:::

Sara rejects the negative idea with jo:

::: examples
- no: Jo, han kommer.
- en: Yes, he is coming.
:::

Jo is useful when the question or previous statement contains a negative idea and you want to contradict it.

::: examples
- no: Har du ikke billett?
- en: Don’t you have a ticket?
- no: Jo, jeg har billett.
- en: Yes, I do have a ticket.
:::

Use nei when the negative idea is correct:

::: examples
- no: Har du ikke billett?
- en: Don’t you have a ticket?
- no: Nei, jeg har ikke billett.
- en: No, I don’t have a ticket.
:::

The focus here remains question formation: recognize that negative questions still begin with the verb, while ikke follows the subject.

**Common mistake: putting ikke before the subject**

Avoid copying the position of English not too directly.

Not the target pattern:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Kommer ikke han i kveld?
- en: Isn’t he coming tonight?
:::

That order can place special contrast on han, as if asking whether someone else rather than he is coming. For a neutral negative question, use:

::: examples
- no: Kommer han ikke i kveld?
- en: Isn’t he coming tonight?
:::

**Request: place-ikke-in-negative-questions**

**Purpose:** Isolate the position of ikke in negative yes/no questions.

**Context:** Sara checks assumptions about Emil, the film time, and the tickets.

**Learner action:** Turn controlled positive questions into negative questions by adding ikke in the appropriate position.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates the order verb + subject + ikke.

{{exercise: place_ikke_in_negative_questions}}

**Request: recognize-negative-question-meaning**

**Purpose:** Connect negative question form with its basic communicative meaning.

**Context:** Brief exchanges in which a speaker checks an expectation about an everyday plan.

**Learner action:** Decide what information or expectation each negative question is checking, using the surrounding exchange.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates recognition of a negative question without requiring abstract clause classification.

## Questions with a question word {#sec-questions-with-a-question-word role=model objectives=obj-question-word-order-01}

Some questions request specific information rather than a yes or no. They begin with a question word such as:

::: examples
- no: hva
- en: what
- no: hvor
- en: where
- no: når
- en: when
- no: hvorfor
- en: why
- no: hvordan
- en: how
:::

After the question word, the verb normally comes before the subject:

::: {.rule}
question word + verb + subject + rest
:::

In the dialogue, Jonas asks:

::: examples
- no: Hvor kjøper jeg billett?
- en: Where do I buy a ticket?
:::

The order is hvor + kjøper + jeg.

Sara asks about Emil’s location:

::: examples
- no: Hvor er Emil?
- en: Where is Emil?
:::

Here er is the verb and Emil is the subject.

Compare statements and questions:

::: examples
- no: Du kjøper billett inne.
- en: You buy a ticket inside.
- no: Hvor kjøper du billett?
- en: Where do you buy a ticket?
- no: Filmen begynner klokka sju.
- en: The film starts at seven.
- no: Når begynner filmen?
- en: When does the film start?
- no: Dere går til kinoen med buss.
- en: You go to the cinema by bus.
- no: Hvordan kommer dere til kinoen?
- en: How do you get to the cinema?
- no: Jonas venter fordi Emil er sen.
- en: Jonas is waiting because Emil is late.
- no: Hvorfor venter Jonas?
- en: Why is Jonas waiting?
:::

Notice again that Norwegian does not normally add do or does:

::: examples
- no: Hvor bor du?
- en: Where do you live?
- no: Hva lager hun?
- en: What is she making?
- no: Når åpner kafeen?
- en: When does the café open?
:::

The question word occupies the first position. The verb follows immediately, before the subject.

::: examples
- no: Kjøper du billett inne?
- en: Do you buy a ticket inside?
- no: Hvor kjøper du billett?
- en: Where do you buy a ticket?
- no: Begynner filmen klokka sju?
- en: Does the film start at seven?
- no: Når begynner filmen?
- en: When does the film start?
- no: Kommer dere med buss?
- en: Are you coming by bus?
- no: Hvordan kommer dere hit?
- en: How are you getting here?
:::

The yes/no question begins with the verb because there is no question word. The information question begins with the question word, but the verb still comes before the subject.

**Negative question-word questions**

Question-word questions can also contain ikke. Keep the same basic order, with ikke after the subject:

::: {.rule}
question word + verb + subject + ikke + rest
:::

::: examples
- no: Hvorfor kommer Emil ikke?
- en: Why isn’t Emil coming?
- no: Hvorfor har du ikke kjøpt billett?
- en: Why haven’t you bought a ticket?
- no: Når jobber du ikke?
- en: When don’t you work?
:::

These questions combine patterns already introduced. The question word comes first, the verb comes before the subject, and ikke follows the subject.

**Common mistake: putting the subject before the verb**

English learners may be tempted to translate word by word and produce statement order after the question word.

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Hvor du bor?
- en: Where do you live?
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: Hvor bor du?
- en: Where do you live?
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Når filmen begynner?
- en: When does the film start?
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: Når begynner filmen?
- en: When does the film start?
:::

A useful check is to look at the first three parts:

::: examples
- no: Hvor – bor – du
- en: Where – live – you
- no: Når – begynner – filmen
- en: When – starts – the film
- no: Hvorfor – kommer – han
- en: Why – comes – he
:::

The English translations sound natural in English, but the Norwegian sequence makes the grammar visible.

**Learner action:** Determine whether each prompt requires a yes/no question or a question-word question, then supply the appropriate opening structure.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates accurate selection between verb + subject and question word + verb + subject.

## Asking for permission {#sec-asking-for-permission role=model objectives=obj-question-word-order-01}

Norwegian permission questions use the same verb-first pattern as other yes/no questions. Two common openings are kan jeg and får jeg.

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg betale med kort?
- en: Can I pay by card?
- no: Kan jeg sitte her?
- en: Can I sit here?
- no: Får jeg åpne vinduet?
- en: May I open the window?
- no: Får jeg gå inn nå?
- en: May I go inside now?
:::

Kan can refer to ability, but in everyday requests kan jeg often asks whether an action is allowed or acceptable.

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg bruke telefonen din?
- en: Can I use your phone?
:::

Får jeg more explicitly asks for permission:

::: examples
- no: Får jeg bruke telefonen din?
- en: May I use your phone?
:::

Both begin with a verb:

- kan + jeg
- får + jeg

The action follows:

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg kjøpe billett her?
- en: Can I buy a ticket here?
- no: Får vi vente inne?
- en: May we wait inside?
- no: Kan barna bli med?
- en: Can the children come along?
:::

Permission questions can also be negative:

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg ikke betale med kort?
- en: Can’t I pay by card?
- no: Får vi ikke gå inn ennå?
- en: Aren’t we allowed to go inside yet?
:::

Here the speaker may be surprised that permission appears to be refused. The order remains verb + subject + ikke.

**Permission and ordinary information**

Compare these two questions:

::: examples
- no: Betaler du med kort?
- en: Are you paying by card?
- no: Kan jeg betale med kort?
- en: Can I pay by card?
:::

The first asks about what another person does. The second asks whether the speaker may or can do something. Both put the verb before the subject, but the opening verb creates a different question meaning.

**Request: form-permission-questions**

**Purpose:** Use verb-first order to ask for permission in predictable everyday situations.

**Context:** Buying a cinema ticket, waiting inside, choosing a seat, and using a card.

**Learner action:** Turn short action cues into polite questions beginning with kan jeg, får jeg, kan vi, or får vi as specified by the cue.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates accurate formation and recognition of permission questions.

{{exercise: form_permission_questions}}

## One decision at a time {#sec-one-decision-at-a-time role=contrast objectives=obj-question-word-order-01}

When forming a question, use this short sequence.

First, decide whether you need a question word.

If the answer can be yes or no, begin with the verb:

::: examples
- no: Kommer Emil?
- en: Is Emil coming?
:::

If you need specific information, begin with a question word and then put the verb before the subject:

::: examples
- no: Når kommer Emil?
- en: When is Emil coming?
:::

Next, decide whether the question is negative. If it is, place ikke after the subject:

::: examples
- no: Kommer Emil ikke?
- en: Isn’t Emil coming?
- no: Hvorfor kommer Emil ikke?
- en: Why isn’t Emil coming?
:::

For a permission question, begin with the relevant verb:

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg vente her?
- en: Can I wait here?
- no: Får vi gå inn?
- en: May we go inside?
:::

The central pattern remains stable: the verb comes before the subject in all these questions.
