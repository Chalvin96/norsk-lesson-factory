## Getting ready for a party {#getting-ready-for-a-party role=orient objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

Maja, Jonas, and Nora are leaving for a birthday party. Their bus departs soon, but not everyone is ready.

::: {.reading translation="It’s half past five! We have to hurry." dialogue_id="party" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Klokka er halv seks! Vi må skynde oss.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’m almost ready. I’m just washing." dialogue_id="party" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg er nesten klar. Jeg vasker meg bare.
:::

::: {.reading translation="After that, you have to get dressed. The bus leaves in twenty minutes." dialogue_id="party" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Etterpå må du kle på deg. Bussen går om tjue minutter.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I know. Has Nora got ready?" dialogue_id="party" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg vet det. Har Nora gjort seg klar?
:::

::: {.reading translation="She’s getting dressed now. She’s looking forward to the party." dialogue_id="party" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Hun kler på seg nå. Hun gleder seg til festen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Now I’m ready! Are you going to sit down now?" dialogue_id="party" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Nå er jeg klar! Skal dere sette dere nå?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, we’re just sitting down for a moment." dialogue_id="party" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, vi setter oss bare et øyeblikk.
:::

::: {.reading translation="A moment? If we don’t hurry, we’ll miss the bus." dialogue_id="party" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Et øyeblikk? Hvis vi ikke skynder oss, mister vi bussen.
:::

Everyone in this scene is using one of the reflexive constructions taught here: Jonas washes himself, Nora dresses herself, and Jonas and Maja sit themselves down. These constructions express the relationship with reflexive pronouns. Some common verbs also require a reflexive pronoun for their intended meaning, as in å glede seg til noe — to look forward to something.

The practical goal is to choose a reflexive pronoun that matches the subject and to use it with common reflexive verbs.

## When the subject and object refer to the same person {#subject-and-object-same-person role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

In the washing, dressing, and sitting constructions taught here, a reflexive pronoun (refleksivt pronomen) is used when the object refers back to the subject.

::: examples
- no: Jeg vasker meg.
- en: I wash myself. / I wash.
:::

The subject jeg and the object meg refer to the same person.

Compare this with an ordinary object:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vasker bilen.
- en: I wash the car.
:::

Here, jeg and bilen do not refer to the same thing, so no reflexive pronoun is needed.

The same contrast appears with other subjects:

::: examples
- no: Du vasker deg.
- en: You wash yourself. / You wash.
- no: Du vasker hunden.
- en: You wash the dog.
- no: Barna vasker seg.
- en: The children wash themselves.
- no: Barna vasker hunden.
- en: The children wash the dog.
:::

English often leaves out myself, yourself, or themselves when the meaning is obvious. Norwegian commonly keeps the reflexive pronoun.

::: examples
- no: Jeg kler på meg.
- en: I get dressed.
:::

A more literal translation would be I dress myself, but get dressed is more natural English.

## The matching forms {#matching-forms role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

The reflexive pronoun must match the subject:

- jeg — meg
- du — deg
- han, hun, den, det — seg
- vi — oss
- dere — dere
- de — seg

Notice that only third-person subjects use seg.

::: examples
- no: Jeg skynder meg.
- en: I hurry.
- no: Du skynder deg.
- en: You hurry.
- no: Han skynder seg.
- en: He hurries.
- no: Vi skynder oss.
- en: We hurry.
- no: Dere skynder dere.
- en: You hurry.
- no: De skynder seg.
- en: They hurry.
:::

A compact rule is:

**Choose the reflexive pronoun from the subject: jeg–meg, du–deg, han/hun–seg, vi–oss, dere–dere, de–seg.**

Do not choose the pronoun according to the nearest noun or according to gender. It always points back to the subject.

::: examples
- no: Nora kler på seg.
- en: Nora gets dressed.
:::

Nora is feminine, but the reflexive form is still seg. Norwegian does not have separate reflexive forms corresponding to English himself and herself.

## Choosing the pronoun from the subject {#choosing-pronoun-from-subject role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

Return to the party scene:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vasker meg.
- en: I wash.
- no: Du kler på deg.
- en: You get dressed.
- no: Hun gjør seg klar.
- en: She gets ready.
- no: Vi skynder oss.
- en: We hurry.
- no: Dere setter dere.
- en: You sit down.
:::

Each reflexive pronoun follows the subject. If the subject changes, the pronoun changes too.

Singular situation.

::: examples
- no: Jeg gjør meg klar.
- en: I get ready.
:::

Plural situation.

::: examples
- no: Vi gjør oss klare.
- en: We get ready.
:::

Singular situation.

::: examples
- no: Du setter deg.
- en: You sit down.
:::

Plural situation.

::: examples
- no: Dere setter dere.
- en: You sit down.
:::

Singular situation.

::: examples
- no: Hun kler på seg.
- en: She gets dressed.
:::

Plural situation.

::: examples
- no: De kler på seg.
- en: They get dressed.
:::

The form seg can refer to one third-person subject or several:

::: examples
- no: Erik setter seg ved vinduet.
- en: Erik sits down by the window.
- no: Erik og Nora setter seg ved vinduet.
- en: Erik and Nora sit down by the window.
:::

The subject tells you whether seg means himself, herself, itself, or themselves.

## A common mistake: using seg with every subject {#common-mistake-using-seg-with-every-subject role=contrast objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

Because seg is common, learners sometimes use it after jeg, du, vi, or dere. That is incorrect.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg skynder seg.
- en: ✗ I hurry.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Jeg skynder meg.
- en: I hurry.
:::

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Vi setter seg.
- en: ✗ We sit down.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Vi setter oss.
- en: We sit down.
:::

Seg belongs with third-person subjects such as han, hun, Nora, barna, or de. It does not function as one universal word for self.

## Common reflexive verbs in everyday routines {#common-reflexive-verbs-everyday-routines role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

Some verbs are especially common with reflexive pronouns. Learn the infinitive with seg, but change the pronoun when the subject changes.

## å vaske seg — to wash oneself, to wash {#wash-oneself role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

::: examples
- no: Jeg vasker meg før frokost.
- en: I wash before breakfast.
- no: Barna vasker seg etter fotballtreningen.
- en: The children wash after football practice.
:::

The verb can also take an ordinary object:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vasker hendene.
- en: I wash my hands.
- no: Hun vasker barnet.
- en: She washes the child.
:::

Use the reflexive form when the object is the same person as the subject.

## å kle på seg — to get dressed {#get-dressed role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

::: examples
- no: Jonas kler på seg på badet.
- en: Jonas gets dressed in the bathroom.
- no: Vi kler på oss før vi går ut.
- en: We get dressed before we go out.
:::

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Nora kler på seg.
- en: Nora gets dressed.
- no: Nora kler på barnet.
- en: Nora dresses the child.
:::

In the first sentence, Nora dresses herself. In the second, she dresses someone else.

## å gjøre seg klar — to get ready {#get-ready role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

::: examples
- no: Jeg gjør meg klar til jobb.
- en: I get ready for work.
- no: De gjør seg klare til festen.
- en: They get ready for the party.
:::

The pronoun still follows the subject:

::: examples
- no: Du gjør deg klar.
- en: You get ready.
- no: Vi gjør oss klare.
- en: We get ready.
:::

## å sette seg — to sit down {#sit-down role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

::: examples
- no: Hun setter seg ved bordet.
- en: She sits down at the table.
- no: Vi setter oss på setene våre på bussen.
- en: We sit down in our seats on the bus.
:::

Å sette seg describes moving into a seated position. The reflexive pronoun is part of this common expression.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jonas setter seg på stolen.
- en: Jonas sits down on the chair.
- no: Jonas setter koppen på bordet.
- en: Jonas puts the cup on the table.
:::

The second sentence has an ordinary object, koppen. Jonas is not putting himself on the table.

## Verbs that require reflexive use for a particular meaning {#verbs-requiring-reflexive-use role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

With some verbs, the reflexive form gives a familiar meaning that should be learned as a whole expression.

## å skynde seg — to hurry {#hurry role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

::: examples
- no: Jeg skynder meg til bussen.
- en: I hurry to the bus.
- no: Skynder dere dere?
- en: Are you hurrying?
- no: De må skynde seg.
- en: They have to hurry.
:::

In the dialogue, Maja says:

::: examples
- no: Vi må skynde oss.
- en: We have to hurry.
:::

Do not treat seg as a fixed part that remains unchanged. The dictionary form is å skynde seg, but an actual sentence must match the pronoun to the subject.

::: examples
- no: Han skynder seg.
- en: He hurries.
- no: Jeg skynder meg.
- en: I hurry.
:::

## å glede seg til — to look forward to {#look-forward-to role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

::: examples
- no: Nora gleder seg til festen.
- en: Nora is looking forward to the party.
- no: Jeg gleder meg til helga.
- en: I’m looking forward to the weekend.
- no: Vi gleder oss til ferien.
- en: We’re looking forward to the holiday.
- no: Gleder du deg til konserten?
- en: Are you looking forward to the concert?
:::

The reflexive pronoun comes before til:

::: examples
- no: Hun gleder seg til besøket.
- en: She is looking forward to the visit.
:::

The complete useful pattern is:

**subject + gleder + matching reflexive pronoun + til + event or thing**

Do not omit the reflexive pronoun in this meaning.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg gleder til ferien.
- en: ✗ I’m looking forward to the holiday.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Jeg gleder meg til ferien.
- en: I’m looking forward to the holiday.
:::

Changing or removing the reflexive pronoun can change the meaning of a verb, not merely its grammar.

::: examples
- no: Filmen gleder barna.
- en: The film delights the children.
- no: Barna gleder seg til filmen.
- en: The children are looking forward to the film.
:::

The lesson’s practical point is not to analyze every possible meaning of glede. It is to recognize and use å glede seg til as a complete everyday expression.

## Reflexive or ordinary object? {#reflexive-or-ordinary-object role=contrast objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

A useful question for the washing, dressing, and sitting contrasts taught here is:

**Does the object refer to the same person or people as the subject?**

If yes, use the matching reflexive pronoun in these taught constructions.

::: examples
- no: Sara vasker seg.
- en: Sara washes herself.
:::

If no, use an ordinary object.

::: examples
- no: Sara vasker hunden.
- en: Sara washes the dog.
:::

Compare these pairs carefully:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kler på meg.
- en: I get dressed.
- no: Jeg kler på barnet.
- en: I dress the child.
:::

::: examples
- no: Han vasker seg.
- en: He washes.
- no: Han vasker bilen.
- en: He washes the car.
:::

::: examples
- no: De setter seg i sofaen.
- en: They sit down on the sofa.
- no: De setter maten på bordet.
- en: They put the food on the table.
:::

The verb alone does not always decide between the taught reflexive and ordinary-object contrasts. In these contrasts, meaning decides: ask who receives the action.

**When a name is the subject**

A person’s name works like han or hun, so use seg.

::: examples
- no: Maja skynder seg.
- en: Maja hurries.
- no: Jonas vasker seg.
- en: Jonas washes.
:::

Two or more named people also take seg because they correspond to de.

::: examples
- no: Maja og Jonas gjør seg klare.
- en: Maja and Jonas get ready.
- no: Nora og Maja gleder seg til festen.
- en: Nora and Maja are looking forward to the party.
:::

Do not replace seg with oss merely because the sentence describes several people. Use oss when the subject includes the speaker, as in vi or a coordinated subject such as Maja og jeg. Third-person subjects such as Nora og Maja use seg.

::: examples
- no: Vi gleder oss til festen.
- en: We are looking forward to the party.
- no: De gleder seg til festen.
- en: They are looking forward to the party.
:::

## Back to Maja, Jonas, and Nora {#back-to-maja-jonas-and-nora role=recap objectives=obj-reflexive-verbs-01}

The group finally reaches the bus stop.

::: {.reading translation="I’m looking forward to seeing everyone at the party." dialogue_id="bus-stop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Jeg gleder meg til å se alle på festen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Me too. But now I need to sit down for a moment." dialogue_id="bus-stop" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg også. Men nå må jeg sette meg litt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="You can sit down in a seat when we’re on the bus. Now we have to hurry!" dialogue_id="bus-stop" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Du kan sette deg på et sete når vi er på bussen. Nå må vi skynde oss!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Look, the bus is coming. We got ready just in time." dialogue_id="bus-stop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Se, bussen kommer. Vi gjorde oss klare akkurat i tide.
:::

The same forms now serve different purposes within one situation:

- Nora uses meg because the subject is jeg.
- Jonas also uses meg after jeg.
- Maja uses deg because she speaks to du.
- The group uses oss because the subject is vi.

The ending would not work naturally if every speaker used seg. Each reflexive pronoun must follow its own subject.
