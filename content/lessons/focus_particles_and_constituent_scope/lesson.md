## A cabin dinner {#cabin-dinner role=orient objectives=obj-focus-particles-and-constituent-scope-01}

Sofie is arranging dinner at a rented cabin. Jonas wants to know who is coming and what each person will bring.

::: {.reading translation='Who is coming at six?' dialogue_id='cabin-dinner' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hvem kommer klokka seks?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Only Amir is coming at six. The others are coming later.' dialogue_id='cabin-dinner' speaker_id='sofie' speaker_name='Sofie' voice_profile="feminine"}
Bare Amir kommer klokka seks. De andre kommer senere.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Is Liv coming later?' dialogue_id='cabin-dinner' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Kommer Liv senere?
:::

::: {.reading translation='No, Liv is coming at six too.' dialogue_id='cabin-dinner' speaker_id='sofie' speaker_name='Sofie' voice_profile="feminine"}
Nei, også Liv kommer klokka seks.
:::

::: {.reading translation='What are they bringing?' dialogue_id='cabin-dinner' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hva tar de med?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Amir is bringing only bread. Liv is bringing cheese as well.' dialogue_id='cabin-dinner' speaker_id='sofie' speaker_name='Sofie' voice_profile="feminine"}
Amir tar bare brød. Liv tar også ost.
:::

::: {.reading translation='But Liv doesn’t like strong cheese, as you know.' dialogue_id='cabin-dinner' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Men Liv liker jo ikke sterk ost.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Tonight she is even bringing blue cheese.' dialogue_id='cabin-dinner' speaker_id='sofie' speaker_name='Sofie' voice_profile="feminine"}
I kveld tar hun til og med blåmuggost.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Even Liv?' dialogue_id='cabin-dinner' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Selv Liv?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, even Liv wants to try it.' dialogue_id='cabin-dinner' speaker_id='sofie' speaker_name='Sofie' voice_profile="feminine"}
Ja, selv Liv vil prøve den.
:::

Four short expressions organize much of this conversation:

::: examples
- no: bare Amir
- en: only Amir
- no: også Liv
- en: Liv too
- no: også ost
- en: cheese as well
- no: til og med blåmuggost
- en: even blue cheese
- no: selv Liv
- en: even Liv
:::

The position of each small word tells us what Sofie is restricting, adding, or presenting as surprising.

**Activity request: notice-focus-in-cabin-dialogue**

**Purpose:** Notice which part of a message each particle points to before producing the forms.

**Context:** Sofie and Jonas’s cabin dinner.

**Learner action:** For each use of **bare, også, selv**, and **til og med**, state which person or thing is restricted, added, or presented as surprising. Then paraphrase the intended meaning in English.

**Success demonstrates:** Learner can identify particle scope from placement and context.

{{exercise: notice_focus_in_cabin_dialogue}}

## What does the particle point to? {#particle-scope role=model objectives=obj-focus-particles-and-constituent-scope-01}

Words such as **bare, også, selv**, and **til og med** are focus particles (*fokuspartikler*). A focus particle highlights one part of a sentence. Its **scope** is the word or group of words that it points to.

Compare these sentences:

::: examples
- no: Bare Lea møter oss på kafeen.
- en: Only Lea is meeting us at the café.
- no: Lea møter bare oss på kafeen.
- en: Lea is meeting only us at the café.
- no: Lea møter oss bare på kafeen.
- en: Lea meets us only at the café.
:::

The basic event remains similar, but the restriction moves:

- **bare Lea** restricts the person who meets us;
- **bare oss** restricts the people Lea meets;
- **bare på kafeen** restricts the place.

A useful first rule is:

::: {.rule}
Put the focus particle immediately before the word or phrase it is meant to highlight whenever that placement is possible.
:::

Norwegian main-clause word order still applies. If a focused phrase begins the sentence, the finite verb remains in second position:

::: examples
- no: Bare Lea kommer i dag.
- en: Only Lea is coming today.
:::

Here **bare Lea** forms the first part of the sentence, followed by **kommer**.

Not:

::: examples
- no: Bare Lea i dag kommer.
- en: 'Not: Only Lea is coming today. (in this situation)'
:::

The particle does not cancel ordinary main-clause word order.

## Restricting with *bare* {#restricting-with-bare role=model objectives=obj-focus-particles-and-constituent-scope-01}

**Bare** usually means *only* or *just*. It excludes alternatives.

Return to Sofie’s sentence:

::: examples
- no: Bare Amir kommer klokka seks.
- en: Only Amir is coming at six.
:::

The focus is **Amir**. Liv, Sofie, and the others are excluded from the group described as coming at six—until Sofie corrects or expands the information.

Now move **bare**:

::: examples
- no: Amir kommer bare klokka seks.
- en: Amir comes only at six.
:::

Here the restricted information is the time. In a suitable context, this could contrast six o’clock with other possible times.

More aligned contrasts:

::: examples
- no: Bare Nora arbeider på fredag.
- en: Only Nora is working on Friday.
- no: Nora arbeider bare på fredag.
- en: Nora works only on Friday.
- no: Bare vi kjenner den nye læreren.
- en: Only we know the new teacher.
- no: Vi kjenner bare den nye læreren.
- en: We know only the new teacher.
- no: Oskar kjøper bare kaffe.
- en: Oskar buys only coffee.
- no: Oskar kjøper kaffe bare på lørdager.
- en: Oskar buys coffee only on Saturdays.
:::

In the first sentence, other people are excluded. In the second, other days are excluded.

The first sentence restricts who knows the teacher. The second restricts which teacher or person we know.

The first sentence restricts what Oskar buys. The second restricts when he buys it.

## A common mistake: assuming *bare* belongs to the whole sentence {#bare-scope-mistake role=contrast objectives=obj-focus-particles-and-constituent-scope-01}

Learners sometimes place **bare** in one familiar position without checking the intended contrast.

Suppose someone writes:

::: examples
- no: Bare Liv kjøper dessert.
- en: Only Liv is buying dessert.
:::

This does not mean that dessert is Liv’s only purchase. It means that no one except Liv is buying dessert.

To restrict Liv’s purchase, say:

::: examples
- no: Liv kjøper bare dessert.
- en: Liv is buying only dessert.
:::

Before placing **bare**, ask: “Which alternatives am I excluding?” Put **bare** before the answer.

**Activity request: place-bare-by-restricted-constituent**

**Purpose:** Practise connecting **bare** with the exact person, object, time, or place being restricted.

**Context:** Preparations for the cabin dinner, including who comes, what guests bring, and when tasks happen.

**Learner action:** Rewrite short statements by placing **bare** so that each sentence expresses a supplied meaning, such as restricting the guest rather than the food, or the day rather than the worker.

**Success demonstrates:** Learner places **bare** before the intended constituent and preserves normal main-clause word order.

{{exercise: place_bare_by_restricted_constituent}}

**Activity request: interpret-bare-position-contrasts**

**Purpose:** Confirm that changed placement creates a changed meaning.

**Context:** Paired messages about the same everyday event.

**Learner action:** Explain the difference between each pair of sentences and identify the alternatives excluded in each version.

**Success demonstrates:** Learner interprets scope rather than translating **bare** mechanically as *only*.

## Adding information with *også* {#adding-with-ogsa role=model objectives=obj-focus-particles-and-constituent-scope-01}

**Også** usually means *also*, *too*, or *as well*. It adds one person, thing, place, or other constituent to an existing set.

In the dialogue, Jonas already knows that Amir is coming at six. Sofie adds Liv:

::: examples
- no: Også Liv kommer klokka seks.
- en: Liv is coming at six too.
:::

The focused phrase **også Liv** stands first. Because this is a main clause, the verb follows it:

**Også Liv kommer ...**

Not:

::: examples
- no: Også Liv hun kommer ...
- en: 'Incorrect: Liv is coming too ...'
:::

Later Sofie says:

::: examples
- no: Liv tar også ost.
- en: Liv is bringing cheese as well.
:::

Here **ost** is added to what Liv is bringing. Bread or another item is already understood, and cheese joins the list.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Også Daniel besøker Bergen.
- en: Daniel is visiting Bergen too.
- no: Daniel besøker også Bergen.
- en: Daniel is visiting Bergen as well.
- no: Også naboene får en invitasjon.
- en: The neighbors are getting an invitation too.
- no: Naboene får også en gave.
- en: The neighbors are also getting a gift.
:::

Daniel is added to the people visiting Bergen.

Bergen is added to the places Daniel visits, assuming the context has already mentioned another place.

The first sentence adds the neighbors to a group of recipients. The second adds a gift to what the neighbors receive.

In ordinary conversation, sentences with **også** can sometimes depend on context or spoken emphasis:

::: examples
- no: Mina kjøper også kaffe.
- en: Mina also buys coffee.
:::

This might be understood as adding coffee to Mina’s purchases, or as adding Mina’s coffee-buying to other known facts. When a contrast must be unmistakable in writing, place **også** directly before the added noun phrase:

::: examples
- no: Også Mina kjøper kaffe.
- en: Mina buys coffee too.
- no: Mina kjøper også kaffe.
- en: Mina buys coffee as well.
:::

The first explicitly adds Mina. The second most naturally adds coffee in a context about Mina’s purchases.

**Activity request: place-ogsa-by-added-constituent**

**Purpose:** Practise showing exactly what new element joins an established set.

**Context:** Sofie updates Jonas’s dinner plan after he has already received partial information.

**Learner action:** Add a specified person, item, time, or place to each earlier statement by placing **også** before that element. Keep the rest of the message unchanged as far as natural Norwegian permits.

**Success demonstrates:** Learner uses placement to distinguish an added participant from an added object, time, or place.

{{exercise: place_ogsa_by_added_constituent}}

## Marking a surprising inclusion with *selv* and *til og med* {#surprising-inclusion role=model objectives=obj-focus-particles-and-constituent-scope-01}

Both **selv** and **til og med** can correspond to English *even*. They show that someone or something is included despite being unexpected.

In the cabin dialogue, Liv normally dislikes strong cheese. This makes her choice surprising:

::: examples
- no: Selv Liv vil prøve blåmuggosten.
- en: Even Liv wants to try the blue cheese.
- no: Til og med Liv vil prøve blåmuggosten.
- en: Even Liv wants to try the blue cheese.
:::

The surprise concerns **Liv**. Other people may be willing to try it, but Liv is the least expected member of that group.

Sofie can also say:

**Til og med Liv vil prøve blåmuggosten.**

**Til og med** is especially clear and flexible before different kinds of phrases:

::: examples
- no: Liv tar til og med blåmuggost.
- en: Liv is even bringing blue cheese.
- no: Vi spiser ute til og med i november.
- en: We eat outside even in November.
- no: De inviterte til og med den nye sjefen.
- en: They even invited the new boss.
:::

In each sentence, the phrase after **til og med** is the surprising inclusion.

**Selv** is compact and common before a focused person or group:

::: examples
- no: Selv barna likte suppa.
- en: Even the children liked the soup.
- no: Selv læreren glemte datoen.
- en: Even the teacher forgot the date.
- no: Selv naboene hørte musikken.
- en: Even the neighbors heard the music.
:::

## A common mistake: confusing focus *selv* with “himself/herself” {#personal-selv-mistake role=contrast objectives=obj-focus-particles-and-constituent-scope-01}

**Selv** has another common use:

::: examples
- no: Liv lagde maten selv.
- en: Liv made the food herself.
- no: Selv Liv lagde maten.
- en: Even Liv made the food.
:::

Here **selv** does not mean *even*. It emphasizes that Liv did the work personally.

Compare:

**Selv Liv lagde maten.**

**Liv lagde maten selv.**

Position changes the message completely. Before **Liv**, the word marks Liv as a surprising participant. After **maten**, it emphasizes that Liv acted without someone else doing it for her.

For clear *even* meanings beyond a focused subject, **til og med** is often the safest choice:

::: examples
- no: Liv spiste til og med desserten.
- en: Liv even ate the dessert.
:::

The intended surprise is the dessert, not the person who performed the action.

**Activity request: place-surprising-inclusion-particle**

**Purpose:** Practise positioning a supplied **selv** or **til og med** before the surprising constituent.

**Context:** Unexpected events during the cabin evening: unusual guests participate, unlikely foods are eaten, and activities continue at surprising times or places.

**Learner action:** Complete or rewrite each statement so that the named person, object, time, or place is clearly presented as unexpected.

**Success demonstrates:** Learner places the supplied focus particle where its surprising scope is unambiguous.

{{exercise: place_surprising_inclusion_particle}}

**Activity request: distinguish-even-selv-from-personal-selv**

**Purpose:** Prevent confusion between focus **selv** meaning *even* and **selv** meaning *himself/herself/themselves*.

**Context:** Short reports about who prepared food and who unexpectedly participated.

**Learner action:** Interpret each sentence, then revise any sentence whose placement gives the personal-action meaning when an *even* meaning is intended.

**Success demonstrates:** Learner recognizes how the position of **selv** changes its function and can produce the intended focus meaning.

{{exercise: distinguish_even_selv_from_personal_selv}}

## Back at the cabin {#back-at-cabin role=contrast objectives=obj-focus-particles-and-constituent-scope-01}

Jonas writes a summary for the group:

::: {.reading translation='Only Liv is buying dessert, and Amir is buying bread as well.' dialogue_id='cabin-summary' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Bare Liv kjøper dessert, og Amir kjøper også brød.
:::

Sofie notices a problem.

::: {.reading translation='The first part is wrong. Amir is buying dessert too. Liv is buying only dessert.' dialogue_id='cabin-summary' speaker_id='sofie' speaker_name='Sofie' voice_profile="feminine"}
Den første delen er feil. Amir kjøper også dessert. Liv kjøper bare dessert.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Oh yes. I put “only” before the person, but I meant the thing.' dialogue_id='cabin-summary' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Å ja. Jeg plasserte *bare* foran personen, men jeg mente tingen.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Exactly. And write that even the neighbors are coming after dinner.' dialogue_id='cabin-summary' speaker_id='sofie' speaker_name='Sofie' voice_profile="feminine"}
Nettopp. Og skriv at til og med naboene kommer etter middagen.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Are the neighbors coming too? Then there will be a lot of us!' dialogue_id='cabin-summary' speaker_id='jonas' speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Kommer også naboene? Da blir vi mange!
:::

Jonas’s original sentence excluded everyone except Liv from buying dessert:

::: examples
- no: Bare Liv kjøper dessert.
- en: Only Liv is buying dessert.
:::

Sofie’s intended message restricts Liv’s purchases:

::: examples
- no: Liv kjøper bare dessert.
- en: Liv is buying only dessert.
:::

Her correction also adds Amir to the dessert buyers:

::: examples
- no: Amir kjøper også dessert.
- en: Amir is buying dessert too.
:::

Finally, **til og med naboene** presents the neighbors as unexpectedly included.

## Compact placement guide {#compact-placement-guide role=recap objectives=obj-focus-particles-and-constituent-scope-01}

Ask one question before choosing the position:

- Who or what is the only one? Put **bare** before that constituent.
- Who or what is being added? Put **også** before that constituent.
- Who or what is unexpectedly included? Put **selv** or **til og med** before that constituent.

Compare the full set:

::: examples
- no: Bare Mari kommer.
- en: Only Mari is coming.
- no: Også Mari kommer.
- en: Mari is coming too.
- no: Selv Mari kommer.
- en: Even Mari is coming.
- no: Til og med Mari kommer.
- en: Even Mari is coming.
- no: Mari kjøper bare kake.
- en: Mari is buying only cake.
- no: Mari kjøper også kake.
- en: Mari is buying cake as well.
- no: Mari kjøper til og med kake.
- en: Mari is even buying cake.
:::

The event remains similar. Particle and placement tell the listener how **Mari** or **kake** relates to the alternatives.

## Transfer: planning a workplace picnic {#transfer-workplace-picnic role=recap objectives=obj-focus-particles-and-constituent-scope-01}

Now move away from the cabin. Colleagues are planning a picnic. Some employees attend, several people bring food, and bad weather may change the location.

**Activity request: transfer-focus-to-workplace-picnic**

**Purpose:** Apply all four target forms in a changed everyday situation.

**Context:** Written updates about who attends a workplace picnic, what people bring, where the group meets, and which details are unexpected.

**Learner action:** Produce short Norwegian statements from supplied meanings. Use **bare** to restrict a specified constituent, **også** to add one, and a supplied **selv** or **til og med** to mark a surprising inclusion. Each statement should make the intended person, item, time, or place clear through placement.

**Success demonstrates:** Learner independently places focus particles so scope remains clear across new sentence content.

{{exercise: transfer_focus_to_workplace_picnic}}

**Activity request: compare-picnic-scope-shifts**

**Purpose:** Demonstrate control of meaning changes caused by moving one particle.

**Context:** Two reports about the same picnic event.

**Learner action:** Create paired sentences in which the event remains broadly constant but particle scope changes—for example, from restricting a participant to restricting an item, or from adding a person to adding a location. Briefly explain the contrast in English.

**Success demonstrates:** Learner can intentionally shift scope rather than placing particles by memorized sentence position.

{{exercise: compare_picnic_scope_shifts}}
