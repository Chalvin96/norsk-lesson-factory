## A missing key before dinner {#missing-key-before-dinner role=orient}

Sigrid and Amir are preparing a birthday dinner in Sigrid’s flat. Their friend Lea will arrive soon, but the key to the storage room has disappeared.

::: {.reading translation='It’s the key to the storage room I’m looking for.' dialogue_id='missing-key-before-dinner' speaker_id='sigrid' speaker_name="Sigrid" character_id="sigrid"}
Det er nøkkelen til boden jeg leter etter.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Wasn’t it Lea who used the key last?' dialogue_id='missing-key-before-dinner' speaker_id='amir' speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Var det ikke Lea som brukte den sist?
:::

::: {.reading translation='No, it was I who collected the chairs yesterday.' dialogue_id='missing-key-before-dinner' speaker_id='sigrid' speaker_name="Sigrid" character_id="sigrid"}
Nei, det var jeg som hentet stolene i går.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Where did you put the key?' dialogue_id='missing-key-before-dinner' speaker_id='amir' speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Hvor la du nøkkelen?
:::

::: {.reading translation='I think it was in the kitchen that I put it.' dialogue_id='missing-key-before-dinner' speaker_id='sigrid' speaker_name="Sigrid" character_id="sigrid"}
Jeg tror det var på kjøkkenet jeg la den.
:::

::: {.reading translation='It was yesterday that you collected the chairs, right?' dialogue_id='missing-key-before-dinner' speaker_id='amir' speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Det var i går du hentet stolene, ikke sant?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes. Wait a moment—it’s the blue jacket that’s hanging by the door. The key may be in the pocket.' dialogue_id='missing-key-before-dinner' speaker_id='sigrid' speaker_name="Sigrid" character_id="sigrid"}
Ja. Vent litt – det er den blå jakka som henger ved døra. Nøkkelen ligger kanskje i lomma.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Here it is!' dialogue_id='missing-key-before-dinner' speaker_id='amir' speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Her er den!
:::

Sigrid and Amir already know the basic facts: someone collected the chairs, the key was put somewhere, and Sigrid is looking for something. What matters in their conversation is which part of each fact needs special attention.

Compare these two sentences:

::: examples
- no: Lea brukte nøkkelen sist.
- en: Lea used the key last.
- no: Det var Lea som brukte nøkkelen sist.
- en: It was Lea who used the key last.
:::

The first sentence gives a neutral statement. The second highlights Lea. Amir is not merely reporting that Lea used the key; he is identifying Lea as the relevant person.

This way of organizing a sentence is called a cleft sentence. In Bokmål, a common cleft begins with det er or det var. It allows you to emphasize a person, thing, time, or place.

## The central meaning choice {#central-meaning-choice role=model objectives=obj-cleft-sentences-01}

A neutral sentence presents an event without marking one part as especially important:

::: examples
- no: Sigrid hentet stolene i går.
- en: Sigrid collected the chairs yesterday.
- no: Det var Sigrid som hentet stolene i går.
- en: It was Sigrid who collected the chairs yesterday.
- no: Det var stolene Sigrid hentet i går.
- en: It was the chairs that Sigrid collected yesterday.
- no: Det var i går Sigrid hentet stolene.
- en: It was yesterday that Sigrid collected the chairs.
:::

Each sentence describes the same basic event, but answers a different question.

Who collected the chairs? Sigrid.
What did Sigrid collect? The chairs.
When did she collect them? Yesterday.

The highlighted element comes directly after det er or det var.

A compact rule is:

**det er / det var + highlighted information + the rest of the message**

Use det er when the identification belongs to the present situation. Use det var when the identification belongs to a past situation.

::: examples
- no: Det er Amir som har nøkkelen nå.
- en: It is Amir who has the key now.
- no: Det var Amir som hadde nøkkelen i går.
- en: It was Amir who had the key yesterday.
:::

The important decision is not simply whether the whole sentence is true. Decide what the listener needs to notice most.

**Request: Find-the-highlighted-information**

This first activity should check recognition before production. Use sentences from Sigrid and Amir’s search for the key. The learner should identify whether each cleft highlights a person, a thing, a time, or a place, and briefly state what question the highlighted information answers. Success demonstrates that the learner understands the information focus created by the cleft. Sentence formation is present as context but is not the scored goal.

{{exercise: find_the_highlighted_information}}

## Highlighting who {#highlighting-who role=contrast objectives=obj-cleft-sentences-01}

When the highlighted person performs the action, Bokmål normally uses som after that person.

::: examples
- no: Det var jeg som hentet stolene.
- en: It was I who collected the chairs.
- no: Det er Lea som kommer først.
- en: It is Lea who is coming first.
- no: Det var naboen som fant telefonen.
- en: It was the neighbour who found the phone.
- no: Det er Amir som lager maten.
- en: It is Amir who is making the food.
:::

The corresponding neutral sentences are:

::: examples
- no: Jeg hentet stolene.
- en: I collected the chairs.
- no: Lea kommer først.
- en: Lea is coming first.
- no: Naboen fant telefonen.
- en: The neighbour found the phone.
- no: Amir lager maten.
- en: Amir is making the food.
:::

In the cleft versions, the person is separated from the rest of the message and placed after det er or det var.

This is especially useful when correcting an assumption or distinguishing between people:

::: examples
- no: Det var ikke Lea som hentet stolene. Det var Sigrid som gjorde det.
- en: It wasn’t Lea who collected the chairs. It was Sigrid who did it.
- no: Det er ikke jeg som har billettene. Det er Morten som har dem.
- en: It isn’t I who have the tickets. It’s Morten who has them.
:::

A common mistake is to leave out som when the highlighted person is the one performing the following action.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Det var Sigrid hentet stolene.
- en: It was Sigrid who collected the chairs.
:::

Natural Bokmål requires som here:

::: examples
- no: Det var Sigrid som hentet stolene.
- en: It was Sigrid who collected the chairs.
:::

Do not treat som as the main point of the lesson, however. Its job here is to connect the highlighted person to the action.

**Request: Identify-the-responsible-person**

Use short misunderstandings from preparations for the birthday dinner. In each situation, one person has been wrongly credited with an action. The learner should produce a cleft sentence that corrects the misunderstanding and highlights the actual person. Success demonstrates accurate use of det er or det var with a highlighted person and som. Choosing present or past forms supports the context, but person focus is the primary scored evidence.

{{exercise: identify_the_responsible_person}}

## Highlighting what {#highlighting-what role=model objectives=obj-cleft-sentences-01}

A cleft can also place special attention on a thing.

Return to Sigrid’s opening sentence:

::: examples
- no: Det er nøkkelen til boden jeg leter etter.
- en: It’s the key to the storage room I’m looking for.
- no: Jeg leter etter nøkkelen til boden.
- en: I’m looking for the key to the storage room.
:::

Sigrid could have said:

::: examples
- no: Jeg leter etter nøkkelen til boden.
- en: I’m looking for the key to the storage room.
:::

That neutral sentence is complete and natural. The cleft is useful because Sigrid wants to specify exactly what is missing. Perhaps Amir thought she was looking for her phone or the shopping list.

More examples:

::: examples
- no: Det er den store kjelen vi trenger.
- en: It is the large saucepan we need.
- no: Det var billettene jeg glemte hjemme.
- en: It was the tickets I forgot at home.
- no: Det er denne boka Nora anbefaler.
- en: It is this book Nora recommends.
- no: Det var den røde kofferten politiet fant.
- en: It was the red suitcase the police found.
:::

Notice that these sentences do not need som before the person carrying out the action:

::: examples
- no: Det var billettene jeg glemte hjemme.
- en: It was the tickets I forgot at home.
:::

Here, jeg is already the person performing glemte. The highlighted item, billettene, is what was forgotten.

Compare the patterns:

::: examples
- no: Det var jeg som glemte billettene.
- en: It was I who forgot the tickets.
- no: Det var billettene jeg glemte.
- en: It was the tickets I forgot.
:::

The first sentence highlights who forgot something. The second highlights what was forgotten. This contrast is important: moving a noun after det var changes the listener’s focus.

A likely misconception is that a cleft always highlights the first person mentioned in the event. It does not. The highlighted information is whatever comes immediately after det er or det var.

**Request: Shift-the-focus-from-person-to-thing**

Use pairs of short statements about the dinner preparations. Begin with a cleft that highlights the person, then ask the learner to express the same event while highlighting the relevant object instead. Success demonstrates that the learner can preserve the basic event while moving emphasis from who to what. Correct object-focused cleft formation is scored; vocabulary and tense are supporting context.

{{exercise: shift_focus_person_to_thing}}

## Highlighting when {#highlighting-when role=model objectives=obj-cleft-sentences-01}

Time expressions can occupy the highlighted position.

::: examples
- no: Det var i går Sigrid hentet stolene.
- en: It was yesterday that Sigrid collected the chairs.
- no: Det er på fredag vi får besøk.
- en: It is on Friday that we are having visitors.
- no: Det var klokka seks møtet begynte.
- en: It was at six o’clock that the meeting began.
- no: Det er om morgenen jeg arbeider best.
- en: It is in the morning that I work best.
- no: Det var da vi bodde i Bergen vi møttes.
- en: It was when we lived in Bergen that we met.
:::

The time expression may be a single word or a longer phrase.

At this level, concentrate on the basic pattern with familiar time expressions. The cleft tells the listener that the timing is the information being identified or corrected.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Vi får besøk på fredag.
- en: We are having visitors on Friday.
- no: Det er på fredag vi får besøk.
- en: It is on Friday that we are having visitors.
:::

The neutral version simply includes the time. The cleft version is natural when someone has asked which day, remembered the wrong day, or needs the date clarified.

::: examples
- no: Kommer de på torsdag?
- en: Are they coming on Thursday?
- no: Nei, det er på fredag de kommer.
- en: No, it is on Friday that they are coming.
:::

In writing, at can also appear after a highlighted time expression:

::: examples
- no: Det var i går at Sigrid hentet stolene.
- en: It was yesterday that Sigrid collected the chairs.
- no: Det var i går Sigrid hentet stolene.
- en: It was yesterday that Sigrid collected the chairs.
:::

The version without at is also natural.

For this lesson, both patterns may be recognized, but the main skill remains choosing and highlighting the time expression.

**Request: Correct-the-time**

Use brief exchanges in which one speaker remembers the wrong day, hour, or part of the day. The learner should reply with a cleft sentence that highlights the corrected time. Success demonstrates that the learner can use det er or det var to make time the central information. The activity scores time focus, not recall of the surrounding event.

{{exercise: correct_the_time}}

## Highlighting where {#highlighting-where role=model objectives=obj-cleft-sentences-01}

Place expressions work in the same highlighted position.

::: examples
- no: Det var på kjøkkenet Sigrid la nøkkelen.
- en: It was in the kitchen that Sigrid put the key.
- no: Det er ved døra jakka henger.
- en: It is by the door that the jacket is hanging.
- no: Det var i Tromsø de møttes.
- en: It was in Tromsø that they met.
- no: Det er på biblioteket jeg vanligvis studerer.
- en: It is at the library that I usually study.
:::

These clefts are useful when the location is uncertain, contrasted, or newly identified.

::: examples
- no: Ligger nøkkelen i gangen?
- en: Is the key in the hall?
- no: Nei, det er på kjøkkenet den ligger.
- en: No, it is in the kitchen that it is.
:::

Compare the neutral and focused versions:

::: examples
- no: Vi møttes på kafeen.
- en: We met at the café.
- no: Det var på kafeen vi møttes.
- en: It was at the café that we met.
:::

The first sentence reports where the meeting happened. The second presents the café as the answer to a location question or as a correction to another location.

As with time expressions, at may occur:

::: examples
- no: Det var på kafeen at vi møttes.
- en: It was at the café that we met.
- no: Det var på kafeen vi møttes.
- en: It was at the café that we met.
:::

The shorter form is also common.

Do not confuse the highlighted place with ordinary main-clause fronting:

::: examples
- no: På kafeen møtte vi Lea.
- en: At the café, we met Lea.
- no: Det var på kafeen vi møtte Lea.
- en: It was at the café that we met Lea.
:::

Both sentences begin by drawing attention to the place, but only the second uses the det var cleft pattern. This lesson focuses on producing that pattern.

**Request: Locate-the-missing-item**

Continue the search scene with several misplaced party items. Give enough context to establish the item and the actual location. The learner should answer a location question or correct a wrong guess by highlighting where the item is or was. Success demonstrates accurate place-focused clefts. Identifying the item is context only; place emphasis is the scored goal.

{{exercise: locate_the_missing_item}}

## Choosing det er or det var {#choosing-det-er-or-det-var role=contrast objectives=obj-cleft-sentences-01}

The opening phrase normally reflects whether the identification belongs to a present or past situation.

Present identification:

::: examples
- no: Det er Nora som står utenfor.
- en: It is Nora who is standing outside.
- no: Det er den grønne posen vi trenger.
- en: It is the green bag we need.
- no: Det er på kontoret han jobber nå.
- en: It is at the office that he works now.
:::

Past identification:

::: examples
- no: Det var Nora som ringte i går.
- en: It was Nora who called yesterday.
- no: Det var den grønne posen vi tok med.
- en: It was the green bag we brought.
- no: Det var på kontoret han møtte henne.
- en: It was at the office that he met her.
:::

A common error is to choose det er merely because the speaker is talking now:

::: examples
- no: Det er Nora som ringte i går.
- en: It is Nora who called yesterday.
:::

This can occur in some contexts, but a learner who is identifying a person within a completed past event will usually need:

::: examples
- no: Det var Nora som ringte i går.
- en: It was Nora who called yesterday.
:::

Use det er for a present identification and det var for a past identification unless the context gives a clear reason to do otherwise.

**Request: Place-the-focus-in-time**

Use matched present and past situations involving the same people and objects. The learner should complete or produce clefts with det er or det var according to the stated context. Success demonstrates control of the opening form’s time reference. Who, what, when, and where may all occur in the examples, but the sole scored dimension is the choice between det er and det var.

{{exercise: place_the_focus_in_time}}

## One event, four possible focuses {#one-event-four-possible-focuses role=model objectives=obj-cleft-sentences-01}

A useful way to master clefts is to begin with one neutral sentence and ask what needs correction.

Neutral event:

::: examples
- no: Amir fant nøkkelen på kjøkkenet i morges.
- en: Amir found the key in the kitchen this morning.
If the question is who:
- no: Det var Amir som fant nøkkelen på kjøkkenet i morges.
- en: It was Amir who found the key in the kitchen this morning.
If the question is what:
- no: Det var nøkkelen Amir fant på kjøkkenet i morges.
- en: It was the key Amir found in the kitchen this morning.
If the question is where:
- no: Det var på kjøkkenet Amir fant nøkkelen i morges.
- en: It was in the kitchen that Amir found the key this morning.
If the question is when:
- no: Det var i morges Amir fant nøkkelen på kjøkkenet.
- en: It was this morning that Amir found the key in the kitchen.
:::

Only one part is placed directly after det var. That position tells the listener what contrast matters.

The cleft does not have to express surprise. It can simply make an answer precise:

::: examples
- no: Hvem skal møte Lea på stasjonen?
- en: Who is going to meet Lea at the station?
- no: Det er Sigrid som skal møte henne.
- en: It is Sigrid who is going to meet her.
:::

It is also useful in explicit correction:

::: examples
- no: Skal dere møte henne på flyplassen?
- en: Are you going to meet her at the airport?
- no: Nei, det er på stasjonen vi skal møte henne.
- en: No, it is at the station that we are going to meet her.
:::

**Request: Match-the-cleft-to-the-question**

Use one familiar event at a time, followed by different information questions about that event. The learner should create a cleft whose highlighted element directly answers the question. Success demonstrates purposeful selection of who, what, when, or where as focus. Form accuracy supports the response, but the scored goal is whether the chosen focus matches the communicative need.

{{exercise: match_the_cleft_to_the_question}}

## Common mistakes to avoid {#common-mistakes-to-avoid role=contrast objectives=obj-cleft-sentences-01}

**Highlighting-too-much**

A cleft works best when one element is clearly identified.

Unclear:

::: examples
- no: Det var Sigrid i går på kjøkkenet som fant nøkkelen.
- en: It was Sigrid yesterday in the kitchen who found the key.
:::

Choose the information that needs emphasis:

::: examples
- no: Det var Sigrid som fant nøkkelen på kjøkkenet i går.
- en: It was Sigrid who found the key in the kitchen yesterday.
- no: Det var på kjøkkenet Sigrid fant nøkkelen i går.
- en: It was in the kitchen that Sigrid found the key yesterday.
:::

**Using-the-wrong-focus-for-the-question**

Question:

::: examples
- no: Hvor fant Amir nøkkelen?
- en: Where did Amir find the key?
:::

Mismatched response:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Det var Amir som fant nøkkelen.
- en: It was Amir who found the key.
:::

This sentence is grammatical, but it answers who, not where.

A suitable location-focused response is:

::: examples
- no: Det var på kjøkkenet Amir fant nøkkelen.
- en: It was in the kitchen that Amir found the key.
:::

**Forgetting-som-with-a-highlighted-subject**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Det var Lea brukte nøkkelen sist.
- en: It was Lea who used the key last.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Det var Lea som brukte nøkkelen sist.
- en: It was Lea who used the key last.
:::

**Copying-English-word-order-without-the-Norwegian-pattern**

Do not omit det:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Var Lea som brukte nøkkelen.
- en: It was Lea who used the key.
:::

Use the full opening:

::: examples
- no: Det var Lea som brukte nøkkelen.
- en: It was Lea who used the key.
:::

## Back at the flat {#back-at-the-flat role=recap}

A few minutes later, Lea arrives.

::: {.reading translation='Did you find the key?' dialogue_id='back-at-the-flat' speaker_id='lea' speaker_name="Lea" character_id="lea"}
Fant dere nøkkelen?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes. It was Sigrid who remembered where it might be.' dialogue_id='back-at-the-flat' speaker_id='amir' speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja. Det var Sigrid som kom på hvor den kunne være.
:::

::: {.reading translation='But it was Amir who found it in the jacket pocket.' dialogue_id='back-at-the-flat' speaker_id='sigrid' speaker_name="Sigrid" character_id="sigrid"}
Men det var Amir som fant den i jakkelomma.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Was it the blue jacket it was in?' dialogue_id='back-at-the-flat' speaker_id='lea' speaker_name="Lea" character_id="lea"}
Var det den blå jakka den lå i?
:::

::: {.reading translation='Yes, and it was by the door that the jacket was hanging.' dialogue_id='back-at-the-flat' speaker_id='amir' speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, og det var ved døra jakka hang.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Good. It’s the chairs we need now. The guests are coming soon.' dialogue_id='back-at-the-flat' speaker_id='lea' speaker_name="Lea" character_id="lea"}
Bra. Det er stolene vi trenger nå. Gjestene kommer snart.
:::

The second part of the scene reuses the same grammar for new purposes. Sigrid receives credit for remembering, Amir receives credit for finding, a jacket is identified, a location is confirmed, and the chairs become the important object. The cleft pattern stays stable while the highlighted information changes.

**Request: Reconstruct-the-key-search**

Give the learner a short sequence of facts from the complete scene without using clefts. Ask for several separate cleft sentences, each responding to a specified question about who, what, when, or where. Each item should score only the requested focus dimension. Success demonstrates that the learner can reorganize familiar information according to a listener’s immediate need rather than mechanically repeating one sentence pattern.

{{exercise: reconstruct_the_key_search}}

## Cumulative transfer: a delayed train {#cumulative-transfer-delayed-train role=recap}

Now move to a new everyday situation. Jonas and Mari are arranging to meet a visitor whose train is delayed. The important details include who will collect the visitor, which train is delayed, when it will arrive, and where everyone will meet.

The learner should first read a short neutral account of the plan. Then the learner should respond to a series of misunderstandings by producing cleft sentences. One response should highlight the responsible person, one the relevant train or item, one the corrected arrival time, and one the meeting place. Each response should have one clearly specified focus so that different evidence goals remain separate.

**Request: Correct-the-travel-plan-person**

Ask the learner to correct a mistaken assumption about who will collect the visitor. Success demonstrates a person-focused cleft with det er or det var and som.

{{exercise: correct_the_travel_plan_person}}

**Request: Correct-the-travel-plan-item**

Ask the learner to identify which train, ticket, or piece of luggage is relevant. Success demonstrates a thing-focused cleft that preserves the surrounding event.

{{exercise: correct_the_travel_plan_item}}

**Request: Correct-the-travel-plan-time**

Ask the learner to replace an incorrect arrival time with the actual time. Success demonstrates a time-focused cleft.

{{exercise: correct_the_travel_plan_time}}

**Request: Correct-the-travel-plan-place**

Ask the learner to replace an incorrect meeting place with the actual location. Success demonstrates a place-focused cleft.

{{exercise: correct_the_travel_plan_place}}
