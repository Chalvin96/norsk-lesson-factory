## A study evening with an empty cupboard {#study-evening-with-an-empty-cupboard role=orient objectives=obj-indefinite-pronouns-01}

Lina has invited several classmates to her flat for a study evening. Marius arrives early to help her prepare.

::: {.reading translation="Are any of the others coming soon?" dialogue_id="study-evening" speaker_id="lina" speaker_name="Lina" character_id="lina"}
Kommer noen av de andre snart?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, no one is coming before six o’clock." dialogue_id="study-evening" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Nei, ingen kommer før klokka seks.
:::

::: {.reading translation="All right. Do we have anything to serve?" dialogue_id="study-evening" speaker_id="lina" speaker_name="Lina" character_id="lina"}
Greit. Har vi noe å servere?
:::

::: {.reading translation="We have some apples and some biscuits." dialogue_id="study-evening" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Vi har noen epler og noen kjeks.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. Do we have anything to drink?" dialogue_id="study-evening" speaker_id="lina" speaker_name="Lina" character_id="lina"}
Fint. Har vi noe å drikke?
:::

::: {.reading translation="There is no coffee left, and we don’t have any tea." dialogue_id="study-evening" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Det er ingen kaffe igjen, og vi har ikke noe te.
:::

::: {.reading translation="So we have nothing to drink?" dialogue_id="study-evening" speaker_id="lina" speaker_name="Lina" character_id="lina"}
Så vi har ingenting å drikke?
:::

::: {.reading translation="We have water. But no one wants only water on a Friday evening!" dialogue_id="study-evening" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Vi har vann. Men ingen vil ha bare vann på en fredagskveld!
:::

Lina and Marius are talking about two kinds of unknown or absent things: people and things. Norwegian helps distinguish them with four common words:

- **noen** as a stand-alone person form or before plural countable nouns
- **noe** as a stand-alone thing form, with substances or amounts, or before an infinitive
- **ingen** as a stand-alone form meaning *no one*, or before a singular en- or ei-noun or plural noun to express absence
- **ingenting** for no thing

The same words can appear in statements and questions. Their English translation may change between *some*, *any*, *someone*, *anyone*, *something*, and *anything*. The Norwegian choice depends mainly on what you are referring to and whether you mean that something exists or that none exists.

## First distinction: noen or noe? {#first-distinction-noen-or-noe role=model objectives=obj-indefinite-pronouns-01}

Both **noen** and **noe** refer to an unspecified person or thing. They do not identify exactly who or what.

**Noen in the person and plural-countable patterns taught here**

Use **noen** as the person form taught here when you mean an unspecified person or unspecified people without directly modifying a noun.

::: examples
- no: Det står noen utenfor døra.
- en: Someone is standing outside the door.
- no: Er det noen hjemme?
- en: Is anyone at home?
- no: Noen av studentene kommer senere.
- en: Some of the students are coming later.
:::

Use **noen** before plural countable nouns when you mean *some* or *any*.

::: examples
- no: Vi har noen epler.
- en: We have some apples.
- no: Har dere noen rene kopper?
- en: Do you have any clean cups?
- no: Hun kjøper noen nye penner.
- en: She is buying some new pens.
:::

In the dialogue, **noen** appears with both people and plural objects:

::: examples
- no: Kommer noen snart?
- en: Is anyone coming soon?
- no: Vi har noen kjeks.
- en: We have some biscuits.
:::

The precise English word is not the main point. In both sentences, **noen** signals an unspecified person, group, or number of countable items.

**Noe in the thing, substance, amount, and infinitive patterns taught here**

Use stand-alone **noe** when you mean an unspecified thing.

::: examples
- no: Jeg hører noe.
- en: I hear something.
- no: Er det noe i posen?
- en: Is there anything in the bag?
- no: Hun vil fortelle deg noe.
- en: She wants to tell you something.
:::

Use **noe** with substances and amounts that are not being counted as separate items.

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger noe kaffe.
- en: We need some coffee.
- no: Kan jeg få noe vann?
- en: Can I have some water?
- no: De kjøper noe mat til møtet.
- en: They are buying some food for the meeting.
:::

**Noe** also appears before an infinitive when you mean something to do, eat, drink, read, or similar.

::: examples
- no: Har du noe å lese?
- en: Do you have anything to read?
- no: Vi trenger noe å spise.
- en: We need something to eat.
- no: Barna vil ha noe å gjøre.
- en: The children want something to do.
:::

This is why Lina asks:

::: examples
- no: Har vi noe å servere?
- en: Do we have anything to serve?
:::

She is not asking about a specific countable set. She is asking whether there is anything suitable to serve.

**A compact choice**

For the patterns taught here, think first about the kind of unknown referent:

- Choose **noen** as the person form or before plural countable nouns.
- Choose stand-alone **noe** for a thing, **noe** with a substance or amount, or **noe** before an infinitive.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg ser noen på kjøkkenet.
- en: I see someone in the kitchen.
- no: Jeg ser noe på kjøkkenet.
- en: I see something in the kitchen.
:::

The first sentence suggests a person. The second suggests a thing, although the speaker does not identify it.

Compare plural items with a substance:

::: examples
- no: Vi kjøper noen flasker.
- en: We are buying some bottles.
- no: Vi kjøper noe juice.
- en: We are buying some juice.
:::

The bottles can be counted individually, so Norwegian uses **noen**. The juice is treated as an amount, so Norwegian uses **noe**.

**Common mistake: choosing by the English word alone**

English *some* can correspond to either **noen** or **noe**.

Incorrect reasoning: “The English sentence contains *some*, so one Norwegian form always works.”

Instead, look at what follows:

::: examples
- no: noen venner
- en: some friends
- no: noe mat
- en: some food
- no: noen spørsmål
- en: some questions
- no: noe hjelp
- en: some help
:::

The countable plural nouns take **noen**. The substances or general amounts take **noe**.

## Questions: some becomes any {#questions-some-becomes-any role=contrast objectives=obj-indefinite-pronouns-01}

In a positive statement, **noen** and **noe** are often translated with *some* or *someone/something*.

::: examples
- no: Vi har noen kopper.
- en: We have some cups.
- no: Vi har noe kaffe.
- en: We have some coffee.
:::

In a question, the same Norwegian forms are often translated with *any*, *anyone*, or *anything*.

::: examples
- no: Har vi noen kopper?
- en: Do we have any cups?
- no: Har vi noe kaffe?
- en: Do we have any coffee?
- no: Kommer noen i kveld?
- en: Is anyone coming tonight?
- no: Skjer det noe i kveld?
- en: Is anything happening tonight?
:::

Do not replace **noen** or **noe** merely because English changes from *some* to *any*. In the patterns taught here, Norwegian keeps the demonstrated distinction between the person or plural-countable uses of **noen** and the thing, substance, or amount uses of **noe**.

## Second distinction: ingen or ingenting? {#second-distinction-ingen-or-ingenting role=model objectives=obj-indefinite-pronouns-01}

Now consider the negative side of the scene. Marius reports that no classmates will arrive before six and that there is no coffee or tea. Lina asks whether that means they have nothing to drink, and Marius corrects her by pointing out that they have water.

**Ingen: no one or absence with selected noun patterns**

When **ingen** stands alone and refers to people, it means *no one* or *nobody*.

::: examples
- no: Ingen kommer før klokka seks.
- en: No one is coming before six o’clock.
- no: Ingen vet hvor Sara er.
- en: No one knows where Sara is.
- no: Jeg ringte på, men ingen åpnet.
- en: I rang the bell, but no one opened.
:::

**Ingen** can also come before singular en- or ei-nouns and plural nouns. In these patterns, it means *no* or *not any*.

::: examples
- no: Vi har ingen gjester ennå.
- en: We have no guests yet.
- no: Det er ingen kaffe igjen.
- en: There is no coffee left.
- no: Hun har ingen ledig stol.
- en: She has no free chair.
- no: De har ingen rene glass.
- en: They have no clean glasses.
:::

Here, each noun is a singular en- or ei-noun or a plural noun naming what is absent. **Ingen** does not itself mean “nothing”; in these patterns, it makes the named group or amount negative.

**Ingenting: nothing**

Use **ingenting** when it stands for a thing and means *nothing*.

::: examples
- no: Det er ingenting i skapet.
- en: There is nothing in the cupboard.
- no: Jeg hører ingenting.
- en: I hear nothing.
- no: Han sier ingenting.
- en: He says nothing.
- no: Vi har ingenting å spise.
- en: We have nothing to eat.
:::

Like **noe**, **ingenting** can be followed by an infinitive:

::: examples
- no: Hun har ingenting å lese.
- en: She has nothing to read.
- no: Barna har ingenting å gjøre.
- en: The children have nothing to do.
- no: Jeg har ingenting å spørre om.
- en: I have nothing to ask about.
:::

**Compare people and things**

::: examples
- no: Ingen venter ute.
- en: No one is waiting outside.
- no: Ingenting ligger ute.
- en: Nothing is lying outside.
- no: Ingen svarte.
- en: No one answered.
- no: Ingenting skjedde.
- en: Nothing happened.
:::

Use stand-alone **ingen** when the absent referent is a person. Use stand-alone **ingenting** when it is a thing or event.

**Compare the demonstrated noun patterns and a stand-alone pronoun**

::: examples
- no: Vi har ingen kaffe.
- en: We have no coffee.
- no: Vi har ingenting.
- en: We have nothing.
- no: Hun har ingen spørsmål.
- en: She has no questions.
- no: Hun spør ingenting.
- en: She asks nothing.
:::

In the first sentence of each pair, a noun names what is absent. In the second, **ingenting** stands alone as the absent thing.

A useful compact rule is:

- Stand-alone **ingen** can mean *no one*; before a noun, **ingen** appears in the demonstrated pattern with a singular en- or ei-noun or plural noun.
- **ingenting** means *nothing* and stands in place of the thing.

## Two ways to express absence {#two-ways-to-express-absence role=contrast objectives=obj-indefinite-pronouns-01}

Norwegian often allows a negative idea to be expressed with **ingen** or **ingenting**, or with **ikke** plus **noen** or **noe**.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Ingen kommer tidlig.
- en: No one is coming early.
- no: Det kommer ikke noen tidlig.
- en: No one is coming early.
:::

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Vi har ingenting å drikke.
- en: We have nothing to drink.
- no: Vi har ikke noe å drikke.
- en: We do not have anything to drink.
:::

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Vi har ingen rene kopper.
- en: We have no clean cups.
- no: Vi har ikke noen rene kopper.
- en: We do not have any clean cups.
:::

The pairs have similar practical meanings. The important pattern is that **ingen** and **ingenting** already carry negative meaning. **Noen** and **noe** need **ikke** when they appear in these negative alternatives.

**Common mistake: adding ikke to an already negative word**

Avoid combining **ikke** with **ingen** or **ingenting** when you intend the simple meaning *no one* or *nothing*.

Instead of trying to build a negative sentence word by word from English, choose one clear pattern:

::: examples
- no: Ingen kommer.
- en: No one is coming.
:::

or:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer ikke noen.
- en: No one is coming.
:::

Likewise:

::: examples
- no: Jeg ser ingenting.
- en: I see nothing.
:::

or:

::: examples
- no: Jeg ser ikke noe.
- en: I do not see anything.
:::

At this level, treat these as two alternative patterns. Do not use both negative markers together for a simple negative meaning.

:::

::: {.reading translation="Great! We had nothing except tap water." dialogue_id="kitchen-exchange" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Flott! Vi hadde ingenting bortsett fra vann fra springen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Is no one else coming early?" dialogue_id="kitchen-exchange" speaker_id="sara" speaker_name="Sara" character_id="sara"}
Kommer ingen andre tidlig?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No. And we have no clean glasses." dialogue_id="kitchen-exchange" speaker_id="lina" speaker_name="Lina" character_id="lina"}
Nei. Og vi har ingen rene glass.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That doesn’t matter. We can wash some glasses." dialogue_id="kitchen-exchange" speaker_id="marius" speaker_name="Marius" character_id="marius"}
Det gjør ingenting. Vi kan vaske noen glass.
:::

This continuation reuses all four forms:

- **noen** refers to an unknown person, plural bottles, and some glasses.
- **noe** asks about an unspecified thing to drink.
- **ingen** refers to absent people and a named group of glasses.
- The first **ingenting** transparently means nothing; the second occurs in the fixed expression **Det gjør ingenting**.

Learn **Det gjør ingenting** as a whole expression meaning *It doesn’t matter*. It is not evidence for the transparent stand-alone rule in which **ingenting** means *nothing*.

## A four-way meaning map {#four-way-meaning-map role=model objectives=obj-indefinite-pronouns-01}

For the patterns taught here, the four choices can be organized around two questions.

First ask: Does the form indicate unspecified existence, or complete absence?

- Unspecified existence, whether asserted or asked about: **noen**, **noe**
- Complete absence, whether stated or asked about: **ingen**, **ingenting**

Then ask: What kind of referent is involved?

- Stand-alone person form: **noen** / **ingen**
- Before plural countable nouns: **noen**, or **ingen** to express absence
- Stand-alone unspecified thing, a substance or amount, or a form before an infinitive: **noe**
- No thing, with no following noun naming it: **ingenting**
- Before a singular en- or ei-noun or plural noun to express absence: **ingen**

Compare the set in the patterns taught here:

::: examples
- no: Noen venter i gangen.
- en: Someone is waiting in the hall.
- no: Ingen venter i gangen.
- en: No one is waiting in the hall.
- no: Det ligger noe i gangen.
- en: Something is lying in the hall.
- no: Det ligger ingenting i gangen.
- en: Nothing is lying in the hall.
:::

And with food:

::: examples
- no: Vi har noen bananer.
- en: We have some bananas.
- no: Vi har ingen bananer.
- en: We have no bananas.
- no: Vi har noe mat.
- en: We have some food.
- no: Vi har ingenting å spise.
- en: We have nothing to eat.
:::
