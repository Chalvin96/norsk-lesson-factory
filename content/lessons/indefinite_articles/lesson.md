## A room to rent {#a-room-to-rent role=orient objectives=obj-indefinite-articles-01}

Amina has recently moved to Bergen to study. She is looking at a room in a shared flat. Lars, who already lives there, shows her around.

::: {.reading translation="Hi! You must be Amina." dialogue_id="a-room-to-rent" speaker_id="lars" speaker_name="Lars" character_id="lars"}
Hei! Du må være Amina.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, that’s right. I’m looking for a room." dialogue_id="a-room-to-rent" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Ja, det stemmer. Jeg ser etter et rom.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Here is the room. It has a bed, a shelf, and a desk." dialogue_id="a-room-to-rent" speaker_id="lars" speaker_name="Lars" character_id="lars"}
Her er rommet. Det har en seng, ei hylle og et skrivebord.
:::

::: {.reading translation="It looks nice. Is there a kitchen too?" dialogue_id="a-room-to-rent" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Det ser fint ut. Er det et kjøkken også?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Would you like coffee or tea?" dialogue_id="a-room-to-rent" speaker_id="lars" speaker_name="Lars" character_id="lars"}
Ja. Vil du ha kaffe eller te?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Coffee, please. Are there other students living here?" dialogue_id="a-room-to-rent" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Kaffe, takk. Er det andre studenter som bor her?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, but Ingrid lives in the room next door. She is a teacher. I’m a student." dialogue_id="a-room-to-rent" speaker_id="lars" speaker_name="Lars" character_id="lars"}
Nei, men Ingrid bor i rommet ved siden av. Hun er lærer. Jeg er student.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then it suits me well. I’m a student too." dialogue_id="a-room-to-rent" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Da passer det bra. Jeg er student også.
:::

Amina needs to talk about individual things in the room: a bed, a shelf, a desk, and a kitchen. Norwegian uses three forms corresponding to English *a* or *an*: **en**, **ei**, and **et**.

However, Lars and Amina do not use an article when they say what they do:

::: examples
- no: **Hun er lærer.**
- en: *She is a teacher.*
- no: **Jeg er student.**
- en: *I am a student.*
:::

They also say **kaffe** and **te** without an article when they mean the drinks as substances rather than one particular cup. This chapter will help you make that choice: **en**, **ei**, **et**, or no article.

## One thing: en, ei, or et {#one-thing-en-ei-et role=model objectives=obj-indefinite-articles-01}

The words **en**, **ei**, and **et** are forms of the indefinite article (*ubestemt artikkel*). Use an indefinite article when you mean one person or one countable thing that has not already been identified.

The form depends on the noun’s grammatical gender, which you should learn together with the noun.

**Gender — Article — Norwegian — English**

**Masculine — Article: en**

::: examples
- no: **en seng**
- en: *a bed*
:::

**Feminine — Article: ei**

::: examples
- no: **ei hylle**
- en: *a shelf*
:::

**Neuter — Article: et**

::: examples
- no: **et skrivebord**
- en: *a desk*
:::

The same pattern appears with people and other everyday objects:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg ser en mann.**
- en: *I see a man.*
- no: **Hun kjøper ei bok.**
- en: *She is buying a book.*
- no: **Vi trenger et kart.**
- en: *We need a map.*
:::

The article belongs with the noun. It is useful to learn **en seng**, not simply **seng**, and **et rom**, not simply **rom**.

**The compact rule**

When you mean one countable person or thing, choose the article that matches the noun’s gender:

**en** with a masculine noun, **ei** with a feminine noun, and **et** with a neuter noun.

English spelling cannot tell you which Norwegian article to use. English *a room* is **et rom**, while *a bed* is **en seng**. The English article is the same, but the Norwegian articles differ because the nouns have different grammatical genders.

**Feminine nouns in Bokmål**

Many Bokmål speakers use **en** with some or all feminine nouns, so you may encounter both **ei bok** and **en bok**. Both are accepted Bokmål. This lesson uses the three-gender pattern, with **ei** for feminine nouns:

::: examples
- no: **ei bok**
- en: *a book*
- no: **ei lampe**
- en: *a lamp*
- no: **ei dør**
- en: *a door*
:::

Follow one consistent pattern during practice so that the noun’s gender remains clear.

**Request: Room-Article-Hunt**

**Purpose:** Notice that individual countable objects in Amina’s room have gender-matching articles.

**Context:** Revisit Lars’s description of the room and a short continuation containing familiar room objects.

**Learner action:** Identify each singular countable noun phrase and state whether it uses **en**, **ei**, or **et**. Use the noun’s known gender rather than its English translation.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Correctly distinguish the three article forms according to noun gender.

**Success meaning:** A successful response shows that the learner treats article and noun as a unit, such as **en seng**, **ei hylle**, and **et skrivebord**.

## Choosing the article in a sentence {#choosing-the-article-in-a-sentence role=model objectives=obj-indefinite-articles-01}

The article remains directly before the noun in a simple noun phrase:

::: examples
- no: **Amina trenger en stol.**
- en: *Amina needs a chair.*
- no: **Lars har ei lampe.**
- en: *Lars has a lamp.*
- no: **De deler et kjøkken.**
- en: *They share a kitchen.*
:::

A common mistake is to omit the article because the listener understands which kind of object is meant:

::: examples
- no: ✗ **Jeg trenger stol.**
- en: *I need chair.*
:::

For one ordinary countable object, this is incomplete. Say:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg trenger en stol.**
- en: *I need a chair.*
:::

Another mistake is to choose an article according to the person connected with the noun. Grammatical gender does not change according to a person’s sex:

::: examples
- no: **Ingrid snakker med en lærer.**
- en: *Ingrid is speaking with a teacher.*
:::

The noun **lærer** takes **en** when it means one teacher, whether that teacher is a man or a woman.

**Request: Furnishing-the-Room**

**Purpose:** Retrieve the correct article rather than merely recognize it in the dialogue.

**Context:** Amina makes a practical list of single items she still needs for her room and kitchen.

**Learner action:** Add the appropriate article before each familiar singular noun and then use the completed noun phrase in a short sentence about what Amina needs or has.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Produce **en**, **ei**, or **et** correctly from the noun’s gender.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates independent article selection in complete, natural noun phrases.

{{exercise: furnishing_the_room}}

## No article for a role after være {#no-article-for-a-role-after-vaere role=contrast objectives=obj-indefinite-articles-01}

Now compare two uses of **lærer**:

::: examples
- no: **Ingrid er lærer.**
- en: *Ingrid is a teacher.*
- no: **Amina snakker med en lærer.**
- en: *Amina is speaking with a teacher.*
:::

In the first sentence, **lærer** names Ingrid’s profession or role. After **å være** (*to be*), Norwegian normally uses no article before a simple profession or role.

::: examples
- no: **Jeg er student.**
- en: *I am a student.*
- no: **Han er lege.**
- en: *He is a doctor.*
- no: **Mona er kokk.**
- en: *Mona is a cook.*
:::

In the second sentence, **en lærer** means one individual teacher. It is an ordinary countable noun phrase, so it has an article.

::: examples
- no: **Vi trenger en lærer.**
- en: *We need a teacher.*
- no: **Hun møter en lege.**
- en: *She is meeting a doctor.*
- no: **De kjenner en kokk.**
- en: *They know a cook.*
:::

The important choice is not simply “profession words have no article.” The sentence’s meaning and pattern matter.

Role or identity after **være** — One countable person

::: examples
- no: **Hun er lærer.**
- en: *She is a teacher.*
- no: **Hun møter en lærer.**
- en: *She is meeting a teacher.*
- no: **Han er lege.**
- en: *He is a doctor.*
- no: **Han ringer en lege.**
- en: *He is calling a doctor.*
- no: **Jeg er student.**
- en: *I am a student.*
- no: **Jeg kjenner en student.**
- en: *I know a student.*
:::

A likely error is:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg er en student.**
- en: *I am a student.*
:::

This can occur when the speaker strongly emphasizes being one particular kind of student, but it is not the normal neutral way to state a simple role. For the basic meaning *I am a student*, use:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg er student.**
- en: *I am a student.*
:::

**Request: Role-or-Person**

**Purpose:** Distinguish a role after **være** from a reference to one individual person.

**Context:** Amina meets people in her new building and learns their occupations. Other sentences describe people she sees, calls, or needs.

**Learner action:** Complete each simple noun phrase with the correct article or leave it without an article, according to whether the noun states a role after **være** or refers to one person.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Choose no article for a simple role after **være**, while retaining the correct article for one countable person.

**Success meaning:** A successful response shows understanding of the contrast between **Hun er lærer** and **Hun møter en lærer**, not merely memorization of profession words.

{{exercise: role_or_person}}

## No article for a substance {#no-article-for-a-substance role=contrast objectives=obj-indefinite-articles-01}

At the flat, Lars asks:

::: examples
- no: **Vil du ha kaffe eller te?**
- en: *Would you like coffee or tea?*
:::

Here, **kaffe** and **te** refer to drinks as substances. Lars is not counting individual cups, so there is no article.

::: examples
- no: **Jeg drikker kaffe.**
- en: *I drink coffee.*
- no: **Hun kjøper te.**
- en: *She buys tea.*
- no: **Vi trenger melk.**
- en: *We need milk.*
- no: **Han spiser brød.**
- en: *He eats bread.*
:::

These nouns can sometimes be used with an article when the speaker means one countable serving or item. Compare:

::: examples
- no: **Amina kjøper kaffe.**
- en: *Amina is buying coffee.*
- no: **Amina kjøper en kaffe.**
- en: *Amina is buying a coffee.*
:::

The first sentence can mean that she buys coffee as a product, perhaps a bag of coffee for the flat. The second means one serving, usually one cup of coffee.

The same contrast appears in a café:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg vil gjerne ha te.**
- en: *I would like tea.*
- no: **Jeg vil gjerne ha en te.**
- en: *I would like a tea.*
:::

The version without an article focuses on the drink. The version with **en** treats the order as one serving.

Do not assume that every food or drink noun must always lack an article. Ask what the speaker means: a substance in general, or one countable serving or item?

**Request: Drink-or-Serving**

**Purpose:** Connect article choice to the intended meaning of a food or drink noun.

**Context:** Amina buys supplies for the shared kitchen and later orders something in a café.

**Learner action:** Decide whether each noun refers to a substance or one serving. Use no article for the substance meaning and the appropriate article when the context clearly presents one countable serving.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Choose between an article and no article according to substance-versus-serving meaning.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the learner can interpret and express the difference between **kaffe** and **en kaffe**.

## Bringing the choices together {#bringing-the-choices-together role=recap objectives=obj-indefinite-articles-01}

The basic decision can now be made in two stages.

First, ask whether the noun phrase refers to one countable person or thing. If it does, use **en**, **ei**, or **et** according to noun gender:

::: examples
- no: **en nabo**
- en: *a neighbor*
- no: **ei lampe**
- en: *a lamp*
- no: **et rom**
- en: *a room*
:::

If it does not present one countable person or thing, check whether it uses one of the no-article patterns studied here: a simple role after **være**, or a substance such as coffee, tea, milk, or bread.

::: examples
- no: **Naboen er lærer.**
- en: *The neighbor is a teacher.*
- no: **Amina drikker te.**
- en: *Amina drinks tea.*
:::

These are meaning choices, not arbitrary omissions.

Compare all four possibilities in Amina’s new home:

::: examples
- no: **Hun har en stol.**
- en: *She has a chair.*
- no: **Hun har ei bok.**
- en: *She has a book.*
- no: **Hun har et kart.**
- en: *She has a map.*
- no: **Hun drikker kaffe.**
- en: *She drinks coffee.*
:::

The final sentence lacks an article because **kaffe** is a substance here. It is not a fourth noun gender.

**Request: Repair-Amina’s-Message**

**Purpose:** Correct common article errors in connected everyday language.

**Context:** Amina writes a short message describing her room, what she drinks in the morning, and the occupations of people in the flat. The draft contains omitted articles before individual objects, incorrect gender forms, and unnecessary articles before roles or substances.

**Learner action:** Rewrite each affected noun phrase while keeping the intended meaning unchanged. Briefly connect each correction to noun gender, role, or substance meaning.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Correct inappropriate article choices across simple singular noun phrases.

**Success meaning:** A successful response shows that the learner can diagnose why an article is required, which article is required, or why no article is needed.

## Back at the flat {#back-at-the-flat role=recap objectives=obj-indefinite-articles-01}

Later that evening, Amina sends a message to a friend:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg har funnet et rom. Det har en seng, ei hylle og et skrivebord. Jeg deler kjøkken med Lars og Ingrid. Lars er student, og Ingrid er lærer. De drikker mye kaffe, så jeg tror jeg kommer til å trives her.**
- en: *I have found a room. It has a bed, a shelf, and a desk. I share a kitchen with Lars and Ingrid. Lars is a student, and Ingrid is a teacher. They drink a lot of coffee, so I think I’m going to enjoy living here.*
:::

The same article choices now help Amina describe the whole situation. The individual room and furniture items take articles. The occupations after **er** do not. Coffee as a substance does not.

**Request: New-Kitchen-Transfer**

**Purpose:** Transfer the article choice to a changed everyday situation.

**Context:** Amina and Ingrid visit a shop to buy one cup, one bowl, one glass, and coffee for their kitchen. During the trip, they meet a person whose profession is mentioned and later order one serving of coffee at a café.

**Learner action:** Complete a short account of the trip using **en**, **ei**, **et**, or no article. Use noun gender for individual objects, no article for a simple role after **være**, and the intended substance or serving meaning for coffee.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Select the appropriate article status in new simple singular noun phrases.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates flexible use of the chapter’s target forms outside the original room-viewing dialogue.

{{exercise: new_kitchen_transfer}}

**Request: Four-Way-Retrieval-Review**

Without looking back, produce one natural noun phrase or short sentence with **en**, one with **ei**, one with **et**, and one with no article. Make the no-article example express either a simple role after **være** or a substance.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Retrieve and apply all four approved choices from meaning and noun gender.

**Success meaning:** A successful response shows that you can independently choose **en**, **ei**, **et**, or no article in simple singular noun phrases.
