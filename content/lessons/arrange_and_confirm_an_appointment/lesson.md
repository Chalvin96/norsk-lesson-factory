## At the bicycle workshop {#bicycle-workshop role=model objectives=obj-arrange-and-confirm-an-appointment-01}

Mina uses her bicycle every day. Recently, the brakes have started making a strange noise, so she calls a local workshop.

::: {.reading translation="Bicycle workshop, Erik speaking." dialogue_id=workshop-call speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Sykkelverkstedet, det er Erik.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Hello. I’d like to make an appointment to have the brakes checked." dialogue_id=workshop-call speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Hei. Jeg vil gjerne bestille en time for å sjekke bremsene.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, of course. When works for you?" dialogue_id=workshop-call speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Ja, selvfølgelig. Når passer det for deg?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Do you have anything available on Thursday afternoon?" dialogue_id=workshop-call speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Har dere noe ledig torsdag ettermiddag?
:::

::: {.reading translation="We have an available appointment at half past two." dialogue_id=workshop-call speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Vi har en ledig time klokka halv tre.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Unfortunately, that doesn’t suit me. I’m at work until four. Can I come after four?" dialogue_id=workshop-call speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Dessverre passer ikke det. Jeg er på jobb til klokka fire. Kan jeg komme etter klokka fire?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Not on Thursday, but how about Friday at quarter past four?" dialogue_id=workshop-call speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Ikke på torsdag, men hva med fredag klokka kvart over fire?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, that suits me well." dialogue_id=workshop-call speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja, det passer fint.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Then I’ll book you in for Friday, 14 March, at 4:15 p.m. for a brake check. Is that correct?" dialogue_id=workshop-call speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Flott. Da setter jeg deg opp fredag 14. mars klokka 16.15 for en bremsesjekk. Stemmer det?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, that’s correct. Friday, 14 March, at quarter past four." dialogue_id=workshop-call speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja, det stemmer. Fredag 14. mars klokka kvart over fire.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. See you on Friday, then." dialogue_id=workshop-call speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Fint. Da sees vi på fredag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thank you. Goodbye!" dialogue_id=workshop-call speaker_id=mina speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Takk. Ha det!
:::

Mina does not simply ask for an appointment and accept the first suggestion. She asks what is available, rejects a time that conflicts with work, proposes a different part of the day, and accepts a new slot. Erik then checks the purpose, date, and time; Mina confirms his summary and explicitly repeats the date and time. This sequence is useful for appointments with workshops, doctors, dentists, hairdressers, public offices, and many other services.

## Asking for an appointment {#asking-for-appointment role=orient objectives=obj-arrange-and-confirm-an-appointment-01}

A common way to begin is:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil gjerne bestille en time.
- en: I’d like to make an appointment.
:::

Here, **en time** means an appointment or booked session. In other contexts, it can also mean an hour. The situation normally makes the meaning clear.

You can add the purpose with **for**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil gjerne bestille en time for en synstest.
- en: I’d like to make an appointment for an eye test.
- no: Jeg vil gjerne bestille en time for en hårklipp.
- en: I’d like to make an appointment for a haircut.
:::

You can also use **for å** followed by an action:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil gjerne bestille en time for å sjekke bremsene.
- en: I’d like to make an appointment to have the brakes checked.
- no: Jeg vil gjerne bestille en time for å snakke med legen.
- en: I’d like to make an appointment to speak with the doctor.
:::

In Mina’s call, the purpose is clear from the beginning: the bicycle brakes need checking. This allows Erik to confirm not only when she is coming, but also what the workshop should do.

## Asking about availability {#asking-about-availability role=model objectives=obj-arrange-and-confirm-an-appointment-01}

Once the purpose is clear, you need to find a possible time. Mina asks:

::: examples
- no: Har dere noe ledig torsdag ettermiddag?
- en: Do you have anything available on Thursday afternoon?
:::

**Ledig** means available or free. You can ask broadly:

::: examples
- no: Har dere noe ledig neste uke?
- en: Do you have anything available next week?
- no: Har du noe ledig på mandag?
- en: Do you have anything available on Monday?
:::

Use **dere** when asking about the business’s or staff’s availability. Use **du** when asking the individual interlocutor what works for them, including an employee who answers on behalf of a business.

You can also ask about a specific time:

::: examples
- no: Er klokka ti ledig?
- en: Is ten o’clock available?
- no: Har dere en ledig time klokka tre?
- en: Do you have an available appointment at three?
:::

Another useful question is:

::: examples
- no: Når har dere en ledig time?
- en: When do you have an available appointment?
:::

Compare the questions:

::: examples
- no: Har dere noe ledig på tirsdag?
- en: Do you have anything available on Tuesday?
:::

This asks whether Tuesday has any possible slots.

::: examples
- no: Når har dere noe ledig?
- en: When do you have anything available?
:::

This leaves the day and time open.

::: examples
- no: Passer tirsdag for deg?
- en: Does Tuesday suit you?
:::

This asks whether Tuesday works for the other person, not whether a business has an open slot.

Time expressions often move naturally to the end:

::: examples
- no: Har dere noe ledig fredag morgen?
- en: Do you have anything available on Friday morning?
- no: Har dere noe ledig etter lunsj?
- en: Do you have anything available after lunch?
- no: Har dere noe ledig mellom klokka to og fire?
- en: Do you have anything available between two and four?
:::

## Understanding an offered slot {#understanding-offered-slot role=model objectives=obj-arrange-and-confirm-an-appointment-01}

Erik replies:

::: examples
- no: Vi har en ledig time klokka halv tre.
- en: We have an available appointment at half past two.
:::

Norwegian clock expressions need careful attention. **Halv tre** means halfway to three, so it is 2:30, not 3:30.

::: examples
- no: halv ni
- en: half past eight
- no: kvart over fire
- en: quarter past four
- no: kvart på fem
- en: quarter to five
:::

Appointments may also be stated with the twenty-four-hour clock:

::: examples
- no: Timen er klokka 14.30.
- en: The appointment is at 2:30 p.m.
- no: Vi kan tilby en time klokka 16.15.
- en: We can offer an appointment at 4:15 p.m.
:::

In everyday conversation, Mina repeats **16.15** as **kvart over fire**. Both refer to the same time.

An offered slot often appears in one of these patterns:

::: examples
- no: Vi har en ledig time på onsdag klokka elleve.
- en: We have an available appointment on Wednesday at eleven.
- no: Du kan komme torsdag morgen.
- en: You can come on Thursday morning.
- no: Hva med fredag klokka kvart over fire?
- en: How about Friday at quarter past four?
- no: Passer mandag klokka ni?
- en: Does Monday at nine suit you?
:::

The last two forms invite you to accept, reject, or make another suggestion.

## Accepting a slot {#accepting-slot role=model objectives=obj-arrange-and-confirm-an-appointment-01}

Mina accepts the Friday appointment with:

::: examples
- no: Ja, det passer fint.
- en: Yes, that suits me well.
:::

Other natural confirmations include:

::: examples
- no: Ja, det passer bra.
- en: Yes, that works well.
- no: Det passer perfekt.
- en: That works perfectly.
- no: Fredag klokka fire passer for meg.
- en: Friday at four works for me.
:::

A short **Ja, det passer** is often enough when the proposed time is already clear.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Ja, fredag passer fint.
- en: Yes, Friday suits me well.
:::

This confirms the day, but not necessarily a particular time.

::: examples
- no: Ja, fredag klokka fire passer fint.
- en: Yes, Friday at four suits me well.
:::

This confirms both day and time and is safer when several slots have been discussed.

## Rejecting a slot politely {#rejecting-slot-politely role=contrast objectives=obj-arrange-and-confirm-an-appointment-01}

The first appointment does not work for Mina:

::: examples
- no: Dessverre passer ikke det.
- en: Unfortunately, that doesn’t suit me.
:::

This is direct but polite. You can add a short reason:

::: examples
- no: Dessverre passer ikke det. Jeg er på jobb da.
- en: Unfortunately, that doesn’t suit me. I’m at work then.
- no: Tirsdag passer ikke for meg. Jeg er bortreist.
- en: Tuesday doesn’t work for me. I’m away.
- no: Jeg kan dessverre ikke komme klokka ti.
- en: Unfortunately, I can’t come at ten.
:::

Notice where **ikke** appears:

::: examples
- no: Det passer ikke.
- en: That doesn’t suit me.
- no: Jeg kan ikke komme.
- en: I can’t come.
:::

A common mistake is to reject the time without showing what should happen next:

::: examples
- no: Nei, det passer ikke.
- en: No, that doesn’t suit me.
:::

This is understandable, but the conversation may stop unless the other person makes another offer. Mina keeps the arrangement moving by adding a limit:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er på jobb til klokka fire. Kan jeg komme etter klokka fire?
- en: I’m at work until four. Can I come after four?
:::

## Making a counterproposal {#making-counterproposal role=model objectives=obj-arrange-and-confirm-an-appointment-01}

A counterproposal is a new suggestion after the first slot does not work. Mina asks:

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg komme etter klokka fire?
- en: Can I come after four?
:::

You can suggest a specific alternative:

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg komme klokka halv fem i stedet?
- en: Can I come at half past four instead?
- no: Passer fredag klokka ti?
- en: Does Friday at ten suit you?
- no: Hva med torsdag ettermiddag?
- en: How about Thursday afternoon?
- no: Har dere noe ledig litt senere?
- en: Do you have anything available a little later?
:::

You can also state when you are free:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan på mandag etter klokka to.
- en: I’m available on Monday after two.
- no: Jeg kan ikke om morgenen, men jeg kan om ettermiddagen.
- en: I can’t in the morning, but I can in the afternoon.
- no: Onsdag er vanskelig, men torsdag passer bra.
- en: Wednesday is difficult, but Thursday works well.
:::

A useful appointment exchange often follows this pattern:

::: examples
- no: Passer tirsdag klokka ett?
- en: Does Tuesday at one suit you?
- no: Dessverre passer ikke det. Hva med onsdag morgen?
- en: Unfortunately, that doesn’t suit me. How about Wednesday morning?
:::

The rejection and new suggestion belong together. The first sentence closes one possibility; the second opens another.

## Aligned contrast {#aligned-contrast role=contrast objectives=obj-arrange-and-confirm-an-appointment-01}

::: examples
- no: Det passer ikke.
- en: That doesn’t work.
:::

This rejects the suggestion but gives no alternative.

::: examples
- no: Det passer ikke, men jeg kan etter klokka tre.
- en: That doesn’t work, but I’m available after three.
:::

This rejects the suggestion and gives a useful time range.

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg komme senere?
- en: Can I come later?
:::

This asks for a change but remains broad.

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg komme klokka halv fem i stedet?
- en: Can I come at half past four instead?
:::

This gives a precise counterproposal.

## Confirming purpose, date, and time {#confirming-purpose-date-time role=model objectives=obj-arrange-and-confirm-an-appointment-01}

After Mina accepts the Friday slot, Erik confirms all essential details:

::: examples
- no: Da setter jeg deg opp fredag 14. mars klokka 16.15 for en bremsesjekk. Stemmer det?
- en: Then I’ll book you in for Friday, 14 March, at 4:15 p.m. for a brake check. Is that correct?
:::

**Å sette noen opp** means to enter someone in a schedule or book them in.

::: examples
- no: Jeg setter deg opp på mandag klokka ni.
- en: I’ll book you in for Monday at nine.
:::

For learners, the most important part is recognizing and checking the complete appointment:

- purpose
- date
- time

Mina confirms Erik’s summary and explicitly repeats the date and time:

::: examples
- no: Ja, det stemmer. Fredag 14. mars klokka kvart over fire.
- en: Yes, that’s correct. Friday, 14 March, at quarter past four.
:::

Useful checking questions include:

::: examples
- no: Stemmer det?
- en: Is that correct?
- no: Var det fredag klokka fire?
- en: Was it Friday at four?
- no: Bare for å bekrefte: Timen er torsdag 8. mai klokka 10.30?
- en: Just to confirm: The appointment is Thursday, 8 May, at 10:30?
- no: Og timen gjelder en synstest, ikke sant?
- en: And the appointment is for an eye test, right?
:::

If a detail is wrong, correct that detail clearly:

::: examples
- no: Nei, det er fredag, ikke torsdag.
- en: No, it’s Friday, not Thursday.
- no: Timen er klokka halv to, ikke halv tre.
- en: The appointment is at half past one, not half past two.
- no: Jeg bestilte time for en hårklipp, ikke for farging.
- en: I booked an appointment for a haircut, not for coloring.
:::

Repeating the complete appointment is especially useful when several dates or times have appeared in the conversation.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-arrange-and-confirm-an-appointment-01}

**Confusing an appointment with availability**

::: examples
- no: Jeg har en time fredag.
- en: I have an appointment on Friday.
:::

This reports an existing appointment.

::: examples
- no: Har dere en time fredag?
- en: Do you have an appointment on Friday?
:::

This may be understood, but it can sound as though you are asking whether the business itself has an appointment. For availability, this is clearer:

::: examples
- no: Har dere en ledig time fredag?
- en: Do you have an available appointment on Friday?
:::

**Confusing Norwegian half-hours**

**Klokka halv tre** means 2:30.
**Klokka halv fire** means 3:30.

If you are unsure, confirm with the twenty-four-hour time:

::: examples
- no: Mener du klokka 14.30?
- en: Do you mean 2:30 p.m.?
:::

**Accepting an unclear slot**

If two times have been discussed, **Det passer** may be too vague. Repeat the selected slot:

::: examples
- no: Fredag klokka 16.15 passer fint.
- en: Friday at 4:15 p.m. works well.
:::

**Rejecting without helping the conversation continue**

::: examples
- no: Nei, jeg kan ikke.
- en: No, I can’t.
:::

This rejects the offer, but gives the other person little guidance.

::: examples
- no: Jeg kan dessverre ikke på tirsdag. Har dere noe ledig på onsdag?
- en: Unfortunately, I can’t on Tuesday. Do you have anything available on Wednesday?
:::

This makes the next step clear.

**Confirming only one detail**

::: examples
- no: Ja, fredag stemmer.
- en: Yes, Friday is correct.
:::

The day may be right while the time or purpose is wrong. For a complete confirmation, include the full arrangement:

::: examples
- no: Ja, det stemmer: fredag 14. mars klokka 16.15 for en bremsesjekk.
- en: Yes, that’s correct: Friday, 14 March, at 4:15 p.m. for a brake check.
:::

## Integrated transfer practice {#integrated-transfer-practice role=model objectives=obj-arrange-and-confirm-an-appointment-01}

Using the request, availability, rejection, counterproposal, acceptance, and confirmation patterns practiced above, create a short appointment exchange based on a practical situation. Begin with a clear purpose, ask what is available, and respond to the first proposed slot. Make that first slot unsuitable so that you must reject it and offer another possibility. Then accept a workable slot and confirm the purpose, date, and time.

This later activity is transfer practice: it brings the whole appointment sequence back together after the controlled checkpoints rather than testing isolated phrases. Keep the exchange focused on arranging one appointment. A successful response would move logically from request to availability, rejection, counterproposal, acceptance, and final confirmation, with both speakers understanding the same booking.
