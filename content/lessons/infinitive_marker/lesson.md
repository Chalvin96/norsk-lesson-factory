## A Saturday plan {#saturday-plan role=orient objectives=obj-infinitive-marker-01}

Maja and Elias are deciding what to do on Saturday. Notice what appears before the second verb in each sentence.

::: {.reading translation="What do you feel like doing on Saturday?" dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Hva har du lyst til å gjøre på lørdag?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I want to visit the new museum." dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="elias" speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Jeg vil besøke det nye museet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That sounds good. I like seeing modern art." dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Det høres bra ut. Jeg liker å se moderne kunst.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Can you meet me in the city centre at eleven?" dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="elias" speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Kan du møte meg i sentrum klokka elleve?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I have to work in the morning, but I can come at twelve." dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jeg må jobbe om morgenen, men jeg kan komme klokka tolv.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. After the museum, I hope to eat at the new café." dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="elias" speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Fint. Etter museet håper jeg å spise på den nye kafeen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes! I always try to find time for coffee and cake." dialogue_id="saturday-plan" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Ja! Jeg prøver alltid å finne tid til kaffe og kake.
:::

Maja and Elias use several sentences containing two verbs. Sometimes the second verb has **å** before it:

::: examples
- no: **liker å se**
- en: *like seeing*
- no: **håper å spise**
- en: *hope to eat*
- no: **prøver å finne**
- en: *try to find*
:::

After a modal verb, however, there is no **å**:

::: examples
- no: **vil besøke**
- en: *want to visit*
- no: **kan møte**
- en: *can meet*
- no: **må jobbe**
- en: *have to work*
:::

This is the choice at the heart of the chapter.

## The infinitive and its marker {#infinitive-and-marker role=model objectives=obj-infinitive-marker-01}

The basic, unchanged form of a verb is called the **infinitive (infinitiv)**. Norwegian dictionary forms are often presented with **å**:

::: examples
- no: **å besøke**
- en: *to visit*
- no: **å se**
- en: *to see*
- no: **å spise**
- en: *to eat*
- no: **å finne**
- en: *to find*
- no: **å jobbe**
- en: *to work*
:::

Here, **å** is the **infinitive marker**. It corresponds roughly to English *to* in expressions such as *to visit* and *to eat*.

When an infinitive follows an ordinary verb or expression such as **liker**, **håper**, **prøver**, or **har lyst til**, use **å**:

**First part:** **Jeg liker**

**Infinitive:** **å lese.**

**Complete expression:**

::: examples
- no: **Jeg liker å lese.**
- en: *I like reading.*
:::

**First part:** **Hun håper**

**Infinitive:** **å komme.**

**Complete expression:**

::: examples
- no: **Hun håper å komme.**
- en: *She hopes to come.*
:::

**First part:** **Vi prøver**

**Infinitive:** **å forstå.**

**Complete expression:**

::: examples
- no: **Vi prøver å forstå.**
- en: *We are trying to understand.*
:::

**First part:** **De har lyst til**

**Infinitive:** **å reise.**

**Complete expression:**

::: examples
- no: **De har lyst til å reise.**
- en: *They feel like travelling.*
:::

The first verb changes when necessary, but the infinitive after **å** remains in its basic form:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg prøver å lære norsk.**
- en: *I am trying to learn Norwegian.*
- no: **Maja prøver å lære norsk.**
- en: *Maja is trying to learn Norwegian.*
:::

The form **lære** stays unchanged in both sentences.

**Practice request: notice-infinitive-marker-in-context**

**Purpose:** Help the learner notice **å** before an infinitive before having to produce it.

**Context:** Short statements about Maja and Elias preparing for Saturday, including plans to eat, travel, meet, and see something.

**Learner action:** Identify the expressions in which **å** introduces the second verb.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Recognition of **å + infinitive** after a non-modal first verb or expression.

**Successful response demonstrates:** The learner can locate the infinitive marker and connect it to the following basic verb form.

## After modal verbs: no å {#after-modal-verbs role=contrast objectives=obj-infinitive-marker-01}

A **modal verb (modalt hjelpeverb)** expresses meanings such as ability, necessity, intention, or advice. Common modal verbs include:

- **kan** — *can*
- **må** — *must / have to*
- **vil** — *will / want to*
- **skal** — *will / shall / be going to*
- **bør** — *should*

When one of these modal verbs is followed by another verb, leave out **å**.

::: examples
- no: **Jeg kan svømme.**
- en: *I can swim.*
- no: **Du må vente.**
- en: *You have to wait.*
- no: **Hun vil kjøpe kaffe.**
- en: *She wants to buy coffee.*
- no: **Vi skal besøke museet.**
- en: *We are going to visit the museum.*
- no: **Dere bør komme tidlig.**
- en: *You should come early.*
:::

The second verb is still an infinitive, but it appears without the infinitive marker.

Compare the patterns directly:

With **å**:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg liker å svømme.**
- en: *I like swimming.*
:::

After a modal verb:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg kan svømme.**
- en: *I can swim.*
:::

With **å**:

::: examples
- no: **Hun håper å komme.**
- en: *She hopes to come.*
:::

After a modal verb:

::: examples
- no: **Hun skal komme.**
- en: *She is going to come.*
:::

With **å**:

::: examples
- no: **Vi prøver å vente.**
- en: *We are trying to wait.*
:::

After a modal verb:

::: examples
- no: **Vi må vente.**
- en: *We have to wait.*
:::

With **å**:

::: examples
- no: **De ønsker å reise.**
- en: *They want to travel.*
:::

After a modal verb:

::: examples
- no: **De vil reise.**
- en: *They want to travel.*
:::

The English translation may contain *to* in both columns. Norwegian grammar makes a different choice:

::: examples
- no: **De ønsker å reise.**
- en: *They want to travel.*
- no: **De vil reise.**
- en: *They want to travel.*
:::

The meaning is similar, but **ønsker** is followed by **å reise**, while the modal verb **vil** is followed by **reise** without **å**.

::: {.rule}
Use: **ordinary verb or expression + å + infinitive** but: **modal verb + infinitive without å**.
:::

**Practice request: choose-marker-after-first-verb**

**Purpose:** Make the learner decide whether the first verb requires **å** or signals that **å** must be omitted.

**Context:** Brief everyday sentences about work, food, travel, and weekend plans.

**Learner action:** Complete each two-verb expression by adding **å** where needed and leaving it out after a modal verb.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Correct choice between **å + infinitive** and a bare infinitive after a modal verb.

**Successful response demonstrates:** The learner uses the identity of the first verb—not the English translation—to choose the Norwegian pattern.

{{exercise: choose_marker_after_first_verb}}

## One decision at a time {#one-decision-at-a-time role=model objectives=obj-infinitive-marker-01}

A useful way to build a Norwegian two-verb sentence is to look first at the verb that comes before the infinitive.

Suppose Maja wants to say that she likes working at home:

1. The first verb is **liker**.
2. **Liker** is not a modal verb.
3. Use **å** before the infinitive.

::: examples
- no: **Maja liker å jobbe hjemme.**
- en: *Maja likes working at home.*
:::

Now suppose Elias says that he must work at home:

1. The first verb is **må**.
2. **Må** is a modal verb.
3. Do not use **å**.

::: examples
- no: **Elias må jobbe hjemme.**
- en: *Elias has to work at home.*
:::

Here are further aligned contrasts:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg begynner å lage middag.**
- en: *I am beginning to make dinner.*
- no: **Jeg skal lage middag.**
- en: *I am going to make dinner.*
- no: **Hun prøver å snakke norsk.**
- en: *She is trying to speak Norwegian.*
- no: **Hun kan snakke norsk.**
- en: *She can speak Norwegian.*
- no: **Vi håper å møte Anna.**
- en: *We hope to meet Anna.*
- no: **Vi vil møte Anna.**
- en: *We want to meet Anna.*
:::

In each pair, the activity remains similar. The first verb controls whether **å** appears.

**Practice request: contrast-same-infinitive-patterns**

**Purpose:** Isolate the grammatical choice by keeping the second verb constant while changing the first verb.

**Context:** Paired statements about the same activity, such as working, speaking Norwegian, cooking, or meeting a friend.

**Learner action:** Produce or complete both versions of each pair: one with an ordinary first verb and **å**, and one with a modal verb and no **å**.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Accurate contrast between the two infinitive patterns.

**Successful response demonstrates:** The learner can change the first verb and adjust the presence of **å** while keeping the infinitive unchanged.

{{exercise: contrast_same_infinitive_patterns}}

## A common mistake {#common-mistake role=contrast objectives=obj-infinitive-marker-01}

Learners sometimes remember that the dictionary form is **å spise** and then put **å** before every infinitive.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ **Jeg kan å spise nå.**
- en: *I can eat now.*
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg kan spise nå.**
- en: *I can eat now.*
:::

The word **kan** is modal, so the next verb appears without **å**.

Another common error is removing **å** from every two-verb expression.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ **Jeg liker spise ute.**
- en: *I like eating out.*
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg liker å spise ute.**
- en: *I like eating out.*
:::

The word **liker** is not modal, so use **å**.

Do not decide only from the English word *to*. English says *I want to visit*, but Norwegian can use a modal construction:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg vil besøke museet.**
- en: *I want to visit the museum.*
:::

There is no **å** after **vil**.

**Practice request: repair-infinitive-marker-errors**

**Purpose:** Help the learner detect both possible errors: adding **å** after a modal verb and omitting it after a non-modal verb.

**Context:** Messages about Maja and Elias’s museum visit, with realistic learner errors in two-verb expressions.

**Learner action:** Find and correct the infinitive-marker error in each sentence.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Correction of misplaced or missing **å**.

**Successful response demonstrates:** The learner can explain through correction that modal verbs take a bare infinitive while other target expressions take **å + infinitive**.

{{exercise: repair_infinitive_marker_errors}}

## Back to Maja and Elias {#back-to-maja-and-elias role=recap objectives=obj-infinitive-marker-01}

At noon, Maja sends Elias a message.

::: {.reading translation="I have finished work. I can meet you in ten minutes." dialogue_id="midday-museum-message" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jeg er ferdig på jobb. Jeg kan møte deg om ti minutter.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great! I am standing outside the museum. I hope to buy tickets before it gets full." dialogue_id="midday-museum-message" speaker_id="elias" speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Flott! Jeg står utenfor museet. Jeg håper å kjøpe billetter før det blir fullt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="We should go in at once." dialogue_id="midday-museum-message" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Vi bør gå inn med en gang.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Agreed. Afterwards, I feel like eating something." dialogue_id="midday-museum-message" speaker_id="elias" speaker_name="Elias" character_id="elias"}
Enig. Etterpå har jeg lyst til å spise noe.
:::

The same contrast now helps them manage the plan:

- **kan møte** — modal verb, no **å**
- **håper å kjøpe** — non-modal verb, use **å**
- **bør gå** — modal verb, no **å**
- **har lyst til å spise** — use **å** before the infinitive

Notice that extra information does not change the pattern:

::: examples
- no: **Jeg kan møte deg om ti minutter.**
- en: *I can meet you in ten minutes.*
- no: **Jeg kan møte deg i sentrum om ti minutter.**
- en: *I can meet you in the city centre in ten minutes.*
- no: **Jeg håper å kjøpe billetter.**
- en: *I hope to buy tickets.*
- no: **Jeg håper å kjøpe billetter før det blir fullt.**
- en: *I hope to buy tickets before it gets full.*
:::

The core choice remains **kan møte** versus **håper å kjøpe**.

**Practice request: complete-contextual-two-verb-messages**

**Purpose:** Move from isolated contrasts to complete, meaningful messages.

**Context:** Maja and Elias coordinate where to meet, when to enter the museum, and what to do afterwards.

**Learner action:** Complete short messages with the supplied infinitive, deciding whether to include **å**.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Correct infinitive-marker use inside complete contextual sentences.

**Successful response demonstrates:** The learner maintains the target pattern even when time, place, and object information appears around the verbs.

{{exercise: complete_contextual_two_verb_messages}}

## Building short personal statements {#building-short-personal-statements role=model objectives=obj-infinitive-marker-01}

You can now use the pattern to describe ordinary intentions, abilities, obligations, preferences, and attempts.

::: examples
- no: **Jeg liker å lage mat.**
- en: *I like cooking.*
- no: **Jeg kan lage mat.**
- en: *I can cook.*
- no: **Jeg prøver å lese på norsk.**
- en: *I am trying to read in Norwegian.*
- no: **Jeg må lese i kveld.**
- en: *I have to read tonight.*
- no: **Jeg håper å besøke Bergen.**
- en: *I hope to visit Bergen.*
- no: **Jeg vil besøke Bergen.**
- en: *I want to visit Bergen.*
:::

Before speaking or writing, ask one question: Is the first verb a modal verb such as **kan**, **må**, **vil**, **skal**, or **bør**?

- If yes, leave out **å**.
- If no, use **å** in the target patterns from this chapter.

**Practice request: produce-bounded-everyday-sentences**

**Purpose:** Support controlled production without turning the task into an open speaking assessment.

**Context:** Familiar A1 situations such as studying tonight, visiting a place, preparing food, or meeting a friend.

**Learner action:** Write short sentences from tightly defined situation cues, using the given first verb and activity.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Production of the correct infinitive pattern in an everyday sentence.

**Successful response demonstrates:** The learner can independently place or omit **å** after seeing which first verb the situation requires.

{{exercise: produce_bounded_everyday_sentences}}

**Context:** Nora’s evening includes making dinner, calling a friend, studying Norwegian, and going to bed early.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Consistent selection of the infinitive pattern across several connected sentences in a new context.

**Successful response demonstrates:** The learner applies the rule beyond memorized examples and keeps both patterns distinct throughout a short text.

## Retrieval and review {#retrieval-and-review role=recap objectives=obj-infinitive-marker-01}

Without looking back, state the compact rule for **å** and modal verbs. Then write two short Norwegian sentences using the same infinitive: one after an expression that requires **å**, and one after a modal verb that requires no **å**. Translate both sentences into English.
