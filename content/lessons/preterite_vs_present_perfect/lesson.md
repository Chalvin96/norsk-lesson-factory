## An evening plan {#an-evening-plan role=orient objectives=obj-preterite-vs-present-perfect-01}

Leila and Jonas work in the same office. They are deciding whether to get something to eat after work.

::: {.reading translation="Have you been to Fjordhuset before?" dialogue_id="fjordhuset-evening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Har du vært på Fjordhuset før?

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I was there last Friday. First we had dinner, and then we listened to a band." dialogue_id="fjordhuset-evening" speaker_id="leila" speaker_name="Leila" voice_profile="feminine"}
Ja, jeg var der forrige fredag. Først spiste vi middag, og så hørte vi på et band.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I have never eaten there, but I have heard many positive things about the place." dialogue_id="fjordhuset-evening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}

:::

::: {.reading translation="Shall we go there now, then?" dialogue_id="fjordhuset-evening" speaker_id="leila" speaker_name="Leila" voice_profile="feminine"}
Skal vi gå dit nå, da?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Sure, but I have already eaten. I can have a coffee." dialogue_id="fjordhuset-evening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}

:::

::: {.reading translation="You ate early!" dialogue_id="fjordhuset-evening" speaker_id="leila" speaker_name="Leila" voice_profile="feminine"}
Du spiste tidlig!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. I have worked since eight o’clock today, so I had lunch before eleven." dialogue_id="fjordhuset-evening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. Jeg har jobbet siden klokka åtte i dag, så jeg tok lunsj før elleve.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I have lived in this area for three years, but I still find new places." dialogue_id="fjordhuset-evening" speaker_id="leila" speaker_name="Leila" voice_profile="feminine"}
Jeg har bodd i dette området i tre år, men jeg finner fortsatt nye steder.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I lived in Bergen for three years before I moved here. There I often went to small concerts." dialogue_id="fjordhuset-evening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg bodde i Bergen i tre år før jeg flyttet hit. Der gikk jeg ofte på små konserter.
:::

Both speakers are talking about the past, but they do not always use the same tense. Sometimes they place an event inside a completed past time: last Friday, before eleven, or before a move. At other times, they connect the past with the present: an experience at some time before now, a meal whose result matters now, or a situation that began earlier and continues.

## The central choice {#the-central-choice role=model objectives=obj-preterite-vs-present-perfect-01}

This chapter contrasts the **past tense (preteritum)** with the **present perfect (presens perfektum)**. You already know how these forms are made. The focus here is choosing between them.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg var på Fjordhuset forrige fredag.
- en: I was at Fjordhuset last Friday.
- no: Jeg har vært på Fjordhuset før.
- en: I have been to Fjordhuset before.
:::

The past tense places the visit in a completed past time: **forrige fredag**. The present perfect presents it as part of Leila’s experience before now. It does not say when the visit happened.

A useful first rule is:

- Use the past tense when the event belongs to a specific, completed past time.
- Use the present perfect when the past event is connected to now and no completed past time is being stated.

This is a meaning choice, not merely a choice between two verb forms.

**Activity request: anchor-time-connections**

**Purpose:** Notice whether each tense in the opening dialogue points to a completed past time or connects an earlier situation to the present.

**Context:** Leila and Jonas’s conversation about Fjordhuset, work, meals, and places where they have lived.

**Learner action:** Revisit each past-tense and present-perfect verb in the dialogue. For each one, identify the time expression or present consequence that motivates the tense.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Correctly distinguish completed past-time reference from a past-to-present connection. Other details in the dialogue provide context but are not scored.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner is interpreting the speakers’ viewpoint rather than selecting tense from verb form alone.

## Specific past events {#specific-past-events role=contrast objectives=obj-preterite-vs-present-perfect-01}

The past tense is the normal choice when you say when a completed event happened.

::: examples
- no: Jeg besøkte Tromsø i fjor.
- en: I visited Tromsø last year.
- no: Hun kjøpte bilen i mandags.
- en: She bought the car last Monday.
- no: Vi så filmen i går kveld.
- en: We watched the film yesterday evening.
- no: De flyttet hit i 2022.
- en: They moved here in 2022.
:::

Expressions such as **i går** (*yesterday*), **forrige uke** (*last week*), **i mandags** (*last Monday*), and **i 2022** identify completed past times.

The same principle applies when a question asks for a specific past occasion:

::: examples
- no: Når kjøpte du bilen?
- en: When did you buy the car?
- no: Hvor bodde dere i fjor?
- en: Where did you live last year?
:::

A common mistake is to combine the present perfect with an explicitly completed past time:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg har sett filmen i går.
- en: Intended meaning: I watched the film yesterday.
:::

Because **i går** is finished, Norwegian normally requires:

::: examples
- no: Jeg så filmen i går.
- en: I watched the film yesterday.
:::

The problem is not that the film has no relevance now. The problem is that the speaker has placed the event inside a completed past time.

**Activity request: completed-past-time-choice**

**Purpose:** Build reliable tense choice when a sentence contains a specific completed time.

**Context:** Short reports about travel, purchases, meetings, and evening activities.

**Learner action:** Complete controlled sentences with the correct past-tense or present-perfect form, using the supplied time context.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Select the past tense when an event is located in a completed past period.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner recognizes completed time expressions as part of the sentence’s meaning and avoids present perfect with expressions such as **i går** and **i fjor**.

{{exercise: completed_past_time_choice}}

## Experiences before now {#experiences-before-now role=contrast objectives=obj-preterite-vs-present-perfect-01}

Use the present perfect to ask or say whether something has happened at any time before now when the exact occasion is not important.

::: examples
- no: Har du vært i Tromsø?
- en: Have you been to Tromsø?
- no: Jeg har aldri reist alene.
- en: I have never travelled alone.
- no: Hun har sett den filmen tre ganger.
- en: She has seen that film three times.
- no: Vi har prøvd den nye restauranten.
- en: We have tried the new restaurant.
:::

These sentences describe experience accumulated up to the present. Words such as **før** (*before*), **aldri** (*never*), **noen gang** (*ever*), and **tre ganger** (*three times*) often appear in this use.

Compare the questions:

::: examples
- no: Har du noen gang kjørt til Bergen?
- en: Have you ever driven to Bergen?
- no: Kjørte du til Bergen i fjor sommer?
- en: Did you drive to Bergen last summer?
:::

The first asks about experience at any time before now. The second asks about one completed period.

In a fuller answer, Leila could combine both: «Ja, jeg har vært på Fjordhuset før. Jeg var der forrige fredag.»

::: examples
- no: Ja, jeg har vært på Fjordhuset før.
- en: Yes, I have been to Fjordhuset before.
- no: Jeg var der forrige fredag.
- en: I was there last Friday.
:::

The first sentence establishes the experience. The second identifies a particular visit.

**Activity request: experience-or-occasion**

**Purpose:** Separate general experience from a particular completed occasion.

**Context:** Conversations about places visited, foods tried, films seen, and activities done.

**Learner action:** Supply an appropriate tense in paired prompts where one speaker asks about experience and another asks about a specified occasion.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Choose present perfect for unspecified experience and past tense for a specified past event.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner can preserve the difference between “at some time before now” and “at that particular past time.”

{{exercise: experience_or_occasion}}

## Results that matter now {#results-that-matter-now role=contrast objectives=obj-preterite-vs-present-perfect-01}

Present perfect can also emphasize a present result. The event happened earlier, but its consequence explains the current situation.

In the dialogue, Jonas says:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har allerede spist.
- en: I have already eaten.
:::

His meal matters because Leila has suggested getting food now. He is not telling the story of when he ate; he is explaining his present state.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har mistet nøklene.
- en: I have lost the keys.
:::

This normally suggests that the keys are still missing or that the loss is a current problem.

::: examples
- no: Jeg mistet nøklene på bussen i går.
- en: I lost the keys on the bus yesterday.
:::

This places the loss in a completed past setting and reports what happened.

Another useful contrast:

::: examples
- no: Har du gjort leksene?
- en: Have you done the homework?
:::

The question asks whether the homework is complete now.

::: examples
- no: Gjorde du leksene etter skolen?
- en: Did you do the homework after school?
:::

The question asks what happened during a particular past period.

Present perfect does not automatically mean “recent.” A recent event can still take the past tense if the speaker locates it at a finished time:

::: examples
- no: Jeg sendte meldingen klokka tre.
- en: I sent the message at three o’clock.
:::

Conversely, an older event can use present perfect if it is presented as relevant experience:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har lest den boka flere ganger.
- en: I have read that book several times.
:::

**Activity request: present-result-choice**

**Purpose:** Practise identifying when an earlier action explains a current condition or need.

**Context:** Missing objects, completed tasks, meals, messages, and preparations.

**Learner action:** Complete short exchanges in which the second sentence establishes either a present result or a completed past occasion.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Select present perfect when current relevance is the intended focus.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner can choose tense from the communicative purpose, not simply from how recently the event occurred.

{{exercise: present_result_choice}}

## Finished and unfinished periods {#finished-and-unfinished-periods role=contrast objectives=obj-preterite-vs-present-perfect-01}

Some time expressions can refer to a period that is still continuing. In that case, present perfect often reports what has happened within the period up to now.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har lest to kapitler i dag.
- en: I have read two chapters today.
- no: Vi har hatt tre møter denne uka.
- en: We have had three meetings this week.
- no: Hun har ringt meg flere ganger i år.
- en: She has called me several times this year.
:::

Here, **i dag**, **denne uka**, and **i år** are viewed as unfinished periods. The speaker is taking stock of events up to the present.

Compare them with completed periods:

::: examples
- no: Jeg leste to kapitler i går.
- en: I read two chapters yesterday.
- no: Vi hadde tre møter forrige uke.
- en: We had three meetings last week.
- no: Hun ringte meg flere ganger i fjor.
- en: She called me several times last year.
:::

A period can be unfinished while one event inside it is located at a specific completed point:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har jobbet mye i dag.
- en: I have worked a lot today.
- no: Jeg snakket med sjefen klokka ni i dag.
- en: I spoke with the manager at nine today.
:::

The first sentence summarizes the day up to now. The second places one completed conversation at nine o’clock. Therefore, **i dag** does not mechanically force present perfect. The speaker’s viewpoint still matters.

**Activity request: unfinished-period-viewpoint**

**Purpose:** Interpret whether a time period is continuing and whether the sentence summarizes that period up to now.

**Context:** A worker reviewing today, this week, and this year, alongside reports about exact events within those periods.

**Learner action:** Choose and form the tense required by each time frame. Treat broad ongoing summaries separately from events located at a definite past point.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Select present perfect for events accumulated within an unfinished period.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner understands why an expression such as **i dag** can support different tenses in different contexts.

{{exercise: unfinished_period_viewpoint}}

## Duration: still true or finished? {#duration-still-true-or-finished role=contrast objectives=obj-preterite-vs-present-perfect-01}

Duration creates a particularly important contrast.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har bodd i dette området i tre år.
- en: I have lived in this area for three years.
:::

This means that Leila moved there three years ago and still lives there.

::: examples
- no: Jeg bodde i Bergen i tre år.
- en: I lived in Bergen for three years.
:::

This presents the period of living in Bergen as finished. Jonas no longer lives there.

More contrasts:

::: examples
- no: Hun har jobbet her siden 2021.
- en: She has worked here since 2021.
:::

She still works here.

::: examples
- no: Hun jobbet der fra 2021 til 2024.
- en: She worked there from 2021 to 2024.
:::

That job belongs to a finished period.

::: examples
- no: Vi har ventet i en time.
- en: We have waited for an hour.
:::

The waiting began an hour ago and is still relevant, usually because it is continuing now.

::: examples
- no: Vi ventet i en time før bussen kom.
- en: We waited for an hour before the bus came.
:::

The arrival of the bus completed the waiting period.

Time expressions with **siden** (*since*) naturally point from a starting point to now when the situation continues:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har kjent Nora siden videregående.
- en: I have known Nora since secondary school.
:::

An expression with **i** plus a duration can occur with either tense. The tense tells you whether the situation continues:

::: examples
- no: Han har studert norsk i to år.
- en: He has studied Norwegian for two years.
:::

He is still studying it.

::: examples
- no: Han studerte norsk i to år.
- en: He studied Norwegian for two years.
:::

That period is presented as finished.

**Activity request: duration-continuation**

**Purpose:** Use tense to show whether a situation continues at present or belongs to a finished period.

**Context:** Short biographical statements about residence, employment, study, friendship, and waiting.

**Learner action:** Complete each statement after reading contextual information that establishes whether the situation is still true.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Distinguish continuing duration with present perfect from completed duration with past tense.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner can use tense itself to communicate whether a state or activity continues.

{{exercise: duration_continuation}}

## Telling a sequence of events {#telling-a-sequence-of-events role=model objectives=obj-preterite-vs-present-perfect-01}

When you narrate a sequence of completed events, the past tense moves the story forward.

Her visit, told as a full sequence:

::: examples
- no: Først spiste vi middag. Så hørte vi på et band. Etterpå tok vi bussen hjem.
- en: First we had dinner. Then we listened to a band. Afterwards we took the bus home.
:::

All three actions form a completed sequence. Present perfect would not normally be used for each step in this kind of narrative.

A speaker can begin with present perfect to establish relevance and then switch to past tense for details:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har vært på Fjordhuset én gang. Jeg dro dit med kollegaene mine. Vi spiste middag, hørte på musikk og gikk hjem ved midnatt.
- en: I have been to Fjordhuset once. I went there with my colleagues. We had dinner, listened to music, and went home at midnight.
:::

The first sentence answers an experience question. The following sentences tell the story of that particular occasion.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Det har skjedd noe på kontoret.
- en: Something has happened at the office.
:::

This introduces a current situation.

::: examples
- no: Først sluttet internettet å virke. Så ringte vi IT-avdelingen. Til slutt startet vi alle maskinene på nytt.
- en: First the internet stopped working. Then we called the IT department. Finally we restarted all the computers.
:::

The details form a completed narrative sequence, so they use the past tense.

**Activity request: narrative-sequence**

**Purpose:** Maintain the past tense through a connected sequence of completed events.

**Context:** A short account of an evening out or a minor problem at work.

**Learner action:** Complete a structured narrative using supplied events and sequence markers. Keep any introductory experience or current-result statement separate from the event sequence.

**Primary scored evidence goal:** Use the past tense consistently for successive completed narrative events.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner can shift from a present-perfect introduction to past-tense narration without treating every past-related sentence alike.

## Returning to Leila and Jonas {#returning-to-leila-and-jonas role=contrast objectives=obj-preterite-vs-present-perfect-01}

Jonas’s tense choices reveal what kind of information he is giving.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har allerede spist.
- en: I have already eaten.
:::

This explains why he does not need dinner now.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har jobbet siden klokka åtte i dag.
- en: I have worked since eight o’clock today.
:::

His working period began earlier and continues up to the present context.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tok lunsj før elleve.
- en: I had lunch before eleven.
:::

This meal is placed at a completed past time.

::: examples
- no: Jeg bodde i Bergen i tre år før jeg flyttet hit.
- en: I lived in Bergen for three years before I moved here.
:::

Both living in Bergen and moving away belong to a finished past sequence.

Leila also moves naturally between the two tenses:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har bodd i dette området i tre år.
- en: I have lived in this area for three years.
:::

She still lives there.

::: examples
- no: Jeg var på Fjordhuset forrige fredag.
- en: I was at Fjordhuset last Friday.
:::

Her visit happened during a completed past period.

## A compact decision guide {#a-compact-decision-guide role=recap objectives=obj-preterite-vs-present-perfect-01}

Before choosing a tense, ask what the listener needs to understand.

Use the **past tense (preteritum)** when:

- the event occurred at a specific completed past time;
- the duration is finished;
- the sentence advances a completed narrative sequence.

Use the **present perfect (presens perfektum)** when:

- the sentence describes experience before now without saying when;
- an earlier event has a result that matters now;
- events are counted or summarized within an unfinished period;
- a state or activity began earlier and continues now.

Do not decide from “recent” versus “long ago.” Decide whether the speaker places the event in a finished past frame or connects it to the present.
