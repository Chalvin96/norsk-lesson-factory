## A busy morning at the cabin {#sec-busy-morning-cabin role=orient objectives=obj-reflexive-possessives-01}

Lina, Oskar, Jonas, Nora, and Emil are leaving a cabin after a weekend away. Everyone is packing, but several belongings are still scattered around.

::: {.reading translation="Have you seen my keys?" dialogue_id="busy-morning-cabin" speaker_id="lina" speaker_name="Lina" character_id="lina"}
Har du sett nøklene mine?
:::

::: {.reading translation="They’re next to your backpack." dialogue_id="busy-morning-cabin" speaker_id="oskar" speaker_name="Oskar"}
De ligger ved siden av sekken din.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. Jonas is also looking for his own keys." dialogue_id="busy-morning-cabin" speaker_id="lina" speaker_name="Lina" character_id="lina"}
Bra. Jonas leter også etter nøklene sine.
:::

::: {.reading translation="He’s packing his own car now. He has put his own jacket on the back seat, but his phone is still on the table." dialogue_id="busy-morning-cabin" speaker_id="oskar" speaker_name="Oskar"}
Han pakker bilen sin nå. Han har lagt jakka si i baksetet, men mobilen hans ligger fortsatt på bordet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then he has to get his own phone before he leaves." dialogue_id="busy-morning-cabin" speaker_id="lina" speaker_name="Lina" character_id="lina"}
Da må han hente mobilen sin før han drar.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Nora and Emil are tidying their own room and collecting their own cups." dialogue_id="busy-morning-cabin" speaker_id="oskar" speaker_name="Oskar"}
Ja. Nora og Emil rydder rommet sitt og samler koppene sine.
:::

The situation is simple: each person needs to find and pack the things that belong to them. Norwegian marks this relationship more clearly than English does.

Look at what belongs to Jonas:

::: examples
- no: Jonas leter etter nøklene sine.
- en: Jonas is looking for his own keys.
- no: Han pakker bilen sin.
- en: He is packing his own car.
- no: Han har lagt jakka si i baksetet.
- en: He has put his own jacket on the back seat.
:::

In each sentence, the subject and the owner are the same person. Jonas is looking for Jonas’s keys, packing Jonas’s car, and moving Jonas’s jacket. Norwegian uses a **reflexive possessive (refleksivt possessiv)** to show this: **sin, si, sitt, sine**.

## When the subject owns the noun {#sec-when-subject-owns-the-noun role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-possessives-01}

Use **sin, si, sitt, or sine** when a third-person subject owns the noun in the same clause.

::: examples
- no: Jonas vasker bilen sin.
- en: Jonas is washing his own car.
- no: Nora pakker veska si.
- en: Nora is packing her own bag.
- no: Emil selger huset sitt.
- en: Emil is selling his own house.
- no: De finner billettene sine.
- en: They find their own tickets.
:::

The subject is Jonas. The car belongs to Jonas, so Norwegian uses **sin**.

The subject is Nora. The bag belongs to Nora, so Norwegian uses **si**.

The subject is Emil. The house belongs to Emil, so Norwegian uses **sitt**.

The subject is de. The tickets belong to them, so Norwegian uses **sine**.

This pattern works with the third-person subjects **han, hun, den, det, de**, as well as with names and noun phrases.

::: examples
- no: Han tar på seg jakka si.
- en: He puts on his own jacket.
- no: Hun åpner brevet sitt.
- en: She opens her own letter.
- no: Katten spiser maten sin.
- en: The cat eats its own food.
- no: Studentene leverer oppgavene sine.
- en: The students submit their own assignments.
:::

**Compact rule**

Ask one question first:

**Does the possessed noun belong to the subject of this clause?**

- If yes, use **sin, si, sitt, or sine**.
- If no, use an ordinary third-person possessive such as **hans, hennes, or deres**.

The choice between **sin, si, sitt, and sine** comes next.

## The ending follows the possessed noun {#sec-ending-follows-possessed-noun role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-possessives-01}

The form does not tell you whether the owner is male, female, or plural. It agrees with the noun that is owned.

- masculine singular — **sin** — **bilen sin**
- feminine singular — **si** — **jakka si**
- neuter singular — **sitt** — **huset sitt**
- plural — **sine** — **nøklene sine**

Compare four things belonging to the same person:

::: examples
- no: Jonas finner sekken sin.
- en: Jonas finds his own backpack.
- no: Jonas finner boka si.
- en: Jonas finds his own book.
- no: Jonas finner passet sitt.
- en: Jonas finds his own passport.
- no: Jonas finner brillene sine.
- en: Jonas finds his own glasses.
:::

Jonas remains the owner in every sentence, but the form changes because the possessed noun changes.

- **sekk** is masculine, so use **sin**
- **bok** is feminine here, so use **si**
- **pass** is neuter, so use **sitt**
- **briller** is plural, so use **sine**

In Bokmål, many feminine nouns can also be treated as masculine. You may therefore encounter both **boka si** and **boken sin**, depending on the noun forms the speaker uses. Keep the noun form and possessive form consistent.

::: examples
- no: Hun legger boka si på bordet.
- en: She puts her own book on the table.
- no: Hun legger boken sin på bordet.
- en: She puts her own book on the table.
:::

## Sin is not simply another word for his {#sec-sin-is-not-simply-another-word-for-his role=contrast objectives=obj-reflexive-possessives-01}

English often uses **his**, **her**, or **their** without clearly showing whether the owner is the subject. Norwegian makes an important contrast.

::: examples
- no: Jonas vasker bilen sin.
- en: Jonas is washing his own car.
- no: Jonas vasker bilen hans.
- en: Jonas is washing his car—the car of another male person.
:::

In the first sentence, the car belongs to Jonas, the subject. In the second, it belongs to another man or boy already known from the context.

The same contrast appears with a female subject:

::: examples
- no: Nora pakker veska si.
- en: Nora is packing her own bag.
- no: Nora pakker veska hennes.
- en: Nora is packing her bag—the bag of another female person.
:::

And with a plural subject:

::: examples
- no: Nora og Emil rydder rommet sitt.
- en: Nora and Emil are tidying their own room.
- no: Nora og Emil rydder rommet deres.
- en: Nora and Emil are tidying their room—the room belonging to other people.
:::

The English translations can sound similar, so it helps to add **own** mentally when you see **sin, si, sitt, or sine**.

**One item, different owners**

Consider Jonas and Oskar at the cabin.

::: examples
- no: Jonas legger mobilen sin i sekken.
- en: Jonas puts his own phone in the backpack.
:::

The subject is Jonas, and the phone belongs to Jonas.

::: examples
- no: Oskar legger mobilen hans i sekken.
- en: Oskar puts his phone in the backpack.
:::

Here, **hans** can refer to Jonas. The subject is Oskar, but the phone belongs to someone else.

Now make Jonas the subject again:

::: examples
- no: Jonas tar mobilen sin ut av sekken.
- en: Jonas takes his own phone out of the backpack.
:::

The physical phone may be the same in all three sentences. The possessive changes because the clause subject changes.

## A common mistake: matching the owner {#sec-common-mistake-matching-the-owner role=contrast objectives=obj-reflexive-possessives-01}

A frequent mistake is to choose **sin** for a male owner, **si** for a female owner, and **sine** for several owners. That is not how these forms work.

Incorrect reasoning:

- Jonas is male, so use **sin**
- Nora is female, so use **si**
- Nora and Emil are plural, so use **sine**

Correct reasoning:

- First decide whether the subject owns the noun.
- Then match the possessive to the possessed noun.

Compare Jonas and Nora:

::: examples
- no: Jonas tar på seg jakka si.
- en: Jonas puts on his own jacket.
- no: Nora tar på seg jakka si.
- en: Nora puts on her own jacket.
:::

Both use **si** because **jakka** is feminine. The owner’s gender makes no difference.

Now compare one person and several people:

::: examples
- no: Nora finner passet sitt.
- en: Nora finds her own passport.
- no: Nora og Emil finner passet sitt.
- en: Nora and Emil find their own passport.
:::

Both use **sitt** because **passet** is neuter singular. A plural subject does not automatically require **sine**.

Use **sine** only when the possessed noun is plural:

::: examples
- no: Nora finner billettene sine.
- en: Nora finds her own tickets.
- no: Nora og Emil finner billettene sine.
- en: Nora and Emil find their own tickets.
:::

## Word order with the possessive {#sec-word-order-with-the-possessive role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-possessives-01}

In the examples in this chapter, the possessive follows the noun. The noun then normally has its definite form.

::: examples
- no: bilen sin
- en: his or her own car
- no: jakka si
- en: his or her own jacket
- no: huset sitt
- en: his, her, or their own house
- no: nøklene sine
- en: his, her, or their own keys
:::

Notice the pairings:

- **bil → bilen sin**
- **jakke → jakka si**
- **hus → huset sitt**
- **nøkler → nøklene sine**

Use the whole phrase as a unit when speaking and writing.

::: examples
- no: Hun låser døra si.
- en: She locks her own door.
- no: Han rydder rommet sitt.
- en: He tidies his own room.
- no: De tar med koffertene sine.
- en: They bring their own suitcases.
:::

Do not leave the noun indefinite in this pattern.

::: examples
- no: Hun finner veska si.
- en: She finds her own bag.
:::

Not: *Hun finner veske si.*

The important target remains the possessive choice, but producing the familiar definite-noun pattern helps the result sound natural.

## Returning to the cabin {#sec-returning-to-the-cabin role=model objectives=obj-reflexive-possessives-01}

Jonas comes back into the room. Lina has found his keys, and Oskar points to the phone on the table.

::: {.reading translation="Jonas, I found your keys under the sofa." dialogue_id="returning-to-cabin" speaker_id="lina" speaker_name="Lina" character_id="lina"}
Jonas, jeg fant nøklene dine under sofaen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thanks! I’m always looking for my things." dialogue_id="returning-to-cabin" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Takk! Jeg leter alltid etter tingene mine.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Your phone is also here." dialogue_id="returning-to-cabin" speaker_id="oskar" speaker_name="Oskar"}
Mobilen din ligger også her.
:::

Lina now gives Jonas the keys.

::: examples
- no: Lina gir Jonas nøklene hans.
- en: Lina gives Jonas his keys.
:::

Why **hans**? Lina is the subject, but Jonas owns the keys. The subject and owner are different.

Jonas puts them away.

::: examples
- no: Jonas legger nøklene sine i lomma.
- en: Jonas puts his own keys in his pocket.
:::

Why **sine**? Jonas is now the subject and the owner of the keys. **Nøklene** is plural.

Oskar hands him the phone.

::: examples
- no: Oskar gir ham mobilen hans.
- en: Oskar gives him his phone.
:::

Jonas checks the phone.

::: examples
- no: Jonas sjekker mobilen sin.
- en: Jonas checks his own phone.
:::

These pairs show why you must identify the subject in each clause. Ownership has not changed, but the grammatical subject has.

## Transfer to a new situation: closing the office {#sec-transfer-to-a-new-situation-closing-the-office role=recap objectives=obj-reflexive-possessives-01}

The setting now changes. Three colleagues are leaving an office at the end of the day. They need to put away their own equipment and avoid taking someone else’s belongings.

::: examples
- no: Amina slår av datamaskinen sin.
- en: Amina turns off her own computer.
- no: Petter lukker vinduet sitt.
- en: Petter closes his own window.
- no: De samler papirene sine.
- en: They collect their own papers.
:::

But Petter also notices Amina’s bag:

::: examples
- no: Petter setter veska hennes ved døra.
- en: Petter puts her bag by the door.
:::

Amina then picks it up:

::: examples
- no: Amina tar veska si.
- en: Amina takes her own bag.
:::

Again, ask the same two questions:

1. Does the noun belong to the subject?
2. If it does, which form matches the possessed noun?
