## A late arrival {#late-arrival role=orient objectives=obj-pluperfect-01}

Mari is meeting Jonas and Amina at a café after work. Her bus is delayed, and she arrives much later than planned.

::: {.reading translation="Sorry I’m so late!" dialogue_id="cafe-arrival" speaker_id="mari" speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Beklager at jeg er så sen!
:::

::: {.reading translation="It’s fine. We had already ordered when you sent the message." dialogue_id="cafe-arrival" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Det går bra. Vi hadde allerede bestilt da du sendte meldingen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And the waiter had put the food on the table before you arrived." dialogue_id="cafe-arrival" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Og servitøren hadde satt maten på bordet før du kom.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Oh no. Had you started the meal?" dialogue_id="cafe-arrival" speaker_id="mari" speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Å nei. Hadde dere begynt på middagen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, we waited for you. But Amina had eaten all the bread." dialogue_id="cafe-arrival" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nei, vi ventet på deg. Men Amina hadde spist alt brødet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I was hungry! I hadn’t eaten since breakfast." dialogue_id="cafe-arrival" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Jeg var sulten! Jeg hadde ikke spist siden frokost.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I understand. I had also forgotten my packed lunch at home." dialogue_id="cafe-arrival" speaker_id="mari" speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Jeg forstår. Jeg hadde også glemt matpakken hjemme.
:::

The conversation is about several past events. Mari sent a message. She arrived at the café. Jonas and Amina ordered. The waiter put the food on the table. Amina ate the bread.

Some of these events happened earlier than other past events. Jonas does not simply say that they ordered. He places the ordering before Mari’s message:

::: examples
- no: Vi hadde allerede bestilt da du sendte meldingen.
- en: We had already ordered when you sent the message.
:::

This is the central purpose of the **past perfect (pluskvamperfektum)**: it shows that something was already completed before another past point.

## Looking back from a past point {#looking-back-from-a-past-point role=model objectives=obj-pluperfect-01}

The past perfect consists of:

**hadde + past participle**

You already know the past participle from the present perfect. The participle does not change here; only the form of ha changes.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har spist.
- en: I have eaten.
- no: Jeg hadde spist før møtet begynte.
- en: I had eaten before the meeting began.
:::

In the first sentence, har spist connects the completed action to the present. In the second, hadde spist looks back from a point in the past: the beginning of the meeting. Eating was already complete at that point.

Common combinations include:

::: examples
- no: hadde spist
- en: had eaten
- no: hadde gått
- en: had gone
- no: hadde sett
- en: had seen
- no: hadde gjort
- en: had done
- no: hadde kjøpt
- en: had bought
- no: hadde glemt
- en: had forgotten
- no: hadde bestilt
- en: had ordered
- no: hadde kommet
- en: had arrived
:::

The compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use hadde plus a past participle when you need to mark an action as completed before another past event or past situation.
:::

**Request: Notice-the-earlier-event**

**Purpose:** Notice the meaning of the past perfect before producing it.

**Context:** Use Mari’s arrival at the café as the past reference point.

**Learner action:** Read short statements about the café scene. For each statement, identify the action expressed with hadde plus a past participle and state which later past event provides the reference point.

**Success meaning:** A successful response shows that the learner understands the two-level timeline: one action was already complete, and another past event came later. This activity scores recognition of the earlier completed action, not past-participle spelling.

{{exercise: notice_the_earlier_event}}

## What happened first? {#what-happened-first role=contrast objectives=obj-pluperfect-01}

Compare these two sentences:

::: examples
- no: Da Mari kom, bestilte vi mat.
- en: When Mari arrived, we ordered food.
:::

::: examples
- no: Da Mari kom, hadde vi bestilt mat.
- en: When Mari arrived, we had ordered food.
:::

In the first sentence, Mari arrived and then the group ordered. The simple past, or **past tense (preteritum)**, presents the ordering as part of the main sequence of events.

In the second sentence, the ordering happened before Mari arrived. The past perfect sends the listener one step further back.

A simple timeline makes the difference clear:

**Earlier:** The group ordered.

**Later past point:** Mari arrived.

::: examples
- no: Vi hadde bestilt mat før Mari kom.
- en: We had ordered food before Mari arrived.
:::

The order can also be expressed with the later event first:

::: examples
- no: Da Mari kom, hadde vi bestilt mat.
- en: When Mari arrived, we had ordered food.
:::

The position of the clauses does not decide which action happened first. The form hadde bestilt marks the earlier completed action.

Here are further contrasts:

::: examples
- no: Da filmen begynte, fant vi plassene våre.
- en: When the film began, we found our seats.
- no: Da filmen begynte, hadde vi funnet plassene våre.
- en: When the film began, we had found our seats.
:::

In the first sentence, finding the seats belongs to the sequence beginning when the film started. In the second, the seats were found before the film started.

::: examples
- no: Da læreren kom, åpnet elevene bøkene.
- en: When the teacher arrived, the pupils opened their books.
- no: Da læreren kom, hadde elevene åpnet bøkene.
- en: When the teacher arrived, the pupils had opened their books.
:::

Again, the second version places the opening of the books before the teacher’s arrival.

**Request: Contrast-the-timeline**

**Purpose:** Connect the choice between simple past and past perfect to a change in event order.

**Context:** Use brief everyday scenes involving arrivals, departures, meals, meetings, and films.

**Learner action:** Read a stated sequence of two past events, then complete or rewrite one sentence so that the earlier completed event uses hadde plus the correct past participle.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the learner can choose the past perfect because of the intended timeline. This activity scores tense choice; other vocabulary only supplies context.

{{exercise: contrast_the_timeline}}

## Building the form {#building-the-form role=model objectives=obj-pluperfect-01}

The auxiliary hadde stays the same with every subject:

::: examples
- no: Jeg hadde spist.
- en: I had eaten.
- no: Du hadde spist.
- en: You had eaten.
- no: Hun hadde spist.
- en: She had eaten.
- no: Vi hadde spist.
- en: We had eaten.
- no: De hadde spist.
- en: They had eaten.
:::

This makes the structure relatively economical. Once you have chosen the correct past participle, you do not need to change hadde for person or number.

The participle is the same one used after har:

::: examples
- no: Han har gått hjem.
- en: He has gone home.
- no: Han hadde gått hjem før jeg ringte.
- en: He had gone home before I called.
- no: De har sett filmen.
- en: They have seen the film.
- no: De hadde sett filmen før.
- en: They had seen the film before.
:::

A common mistake is to put a past-tense verb after hadde:

::: examples
- no: Han hadde gikk hjem.
- en: He had went home.
:::

This is not correct. After hadde, use the past participle:

::: examples
- no: Han hadde gått hjem.
- en: He had gone home.
:::

Similarly:

::: examples
- no: Vi hadde så filmen.
- en: We had saw the film.
:::

The correct form is:

::: examples
- no: Vi hadde sett filmen.
- en: We had seen the film.
:::

Do not create a new participle rule for the past perfect. Reuse the participles you know from the present perfect: har gått becomes hadde gått, har sett becomes hadde sett, and har gjort becomes hadde gjort.

**Request: Build-hadde-plus-participle**

**Purpose:** Make the form hadde plus a past participle automatic.

**Context:** Use short sentences about completed preparations before a past event, such as leaving home, starting work, or receiving guests.

**Learner action:** Supply the correct past participle after hadde, using familiar verbs from the present perfect.

**Success meaning:** A successful response contains hadde followed by the required participle rather than an infinitive or simple-past form. This activity scores form construction, while the event sequence remains supplied by the context.

{{exercise: build_hadde_plus_participle}}

## Making the earlier completion clear {#making-the-earlier-completion-clear role=contrast objectives=obj-pluperfect-01}

Words such as allerede and før often make the timeline especially clear.

::: examples
- no: Toget hadde allerede gått da vi kom til stasjonen.
- en: The train had already left when we arrived at the station.
- no: Nora hadde låst døren før hun gikk til bussen.
- en: Nora had locked the door before she went to the bus.
- no: Jeg hadde lest rapporten før møtet startet.
- en: I had read the report before the meeting started.
- no: De hadde ryddet kjøkkenet da gjestene kom.
- en: They had cleaned the kitchen when the guests arrived.
:::

The past point may be an event, as in da gjestene kom, or a past situation:

::: examples
- no: Erik var trøtt fordi han hadde sovet dårlig.
- en: Erik was tired because he had slept badly.
:::

Erik’s tiredness is in the past. His poor sleep happened earlier and explains that past situation.

::: examples
- no: Leiligheten var tom. De forrige eierne hadde flyttet ut.
- en: The flat was empty. The previous owners had moved out.
:::

The second sentence looks further back and explains why the flat was empty.

Sometimes the later past point is clear from the surrounding story and does not need to appear in the same sentence:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kom til kontoret klokka ni. Alle de andre hadde begynt å jobbe.
- en: I arrived at the office at nine. Everyone else had started working.
:::

The arrival at the office provides the reference point. The others started working before that arrival.

**Request: Link-completion-to-past-point**

**Purpose:** Use context, not only words such as før, to locate the earlier action.

**Context:** Present short two-sentence narratives in which the first sentence establishes a past scene and the second explains what had already happened.

**Learner action:** Complete the explanatory sentence with hadde plus an appropriate supplied verb in its past-participle form.

**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the learner can use a past reference point established across sentences. This activity scores connection between context and past-perfect meaning.

{{exercise: link_completion_to_past_point}}

## Returning to Mari, Jonas, and Amina {#returning-to-mari-jonas-and-amina role=model objectives=obj-pluperfect-01}

After dinner, Mari explains why she was late.

::: {.reading translation="I went to the wrong bus stop because I followed the old bus route to the café." dialogue_id="cafe-delay" speaker_id="mari" speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Jeg gikk til feil bussholdeplass fordi jeg fulgte den gamle bussruta til kafeen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="But hadn’t you checked the new bus route to the café?" dialogue_id="cafe-delay" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Men hadde du ikke sjekket den nye bussruta til kafeen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but I had saved the old route on my phone." dialogue_id="cafe-delay" speaker_id="mari" speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Jo, men jeg hadde lagret den gamle ruta på telefonen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="When you discovered the mistake, had the bus on the new route left?" dialogue_id="cafe-delay" speaker_id="amina" speaker_name="Amina" character_id="amina"}
Da du oppdaget feilen, hadde bussen på den nye ruta gått?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. It had just left the correct stop. That’s why I had to wait for the next one." dialogue_id="cafe-delay" speaker_id="mari" speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Ja. Den hadde akkurat kjørt fra riktig holdeplass. Derfor måtte jeg vente på den neste.
:::

The main past sequence is that Mari followed the old route to the wrong stop, discovered the mistake, and waited for another bus. The past perfect supplies earlier background:

- She had checked the new bus route to the café.
- She had saved the old route on her phone before the trip.
- The bus on the new route had left the correct stop before she could take it.

This is useful in storytelling because it separates the main sequence from earlier completed events. It lets the listener understand why a past situation developed as it did.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Mari oppdaget feilen, og bussen gikk.
- en: Mari discovered the mistake, and the bus left.
:::

This presents two events in the main sequence.

::: examples
- no: Da Mari oppdaget feilen, hadde bussen gått.
- en: When Mari discovered the mistake, the bus had left.
:::

This says the bus was already gone when she discovered the error.

**Request: Reconstruct-Maris-delay**

**Purpose:** Use the past perfect to explain causes that occurred before the main past events.

**Context:** Use Mari’s trip to the café, the old bus route saved on her phone, the wrong stop, and the bus that left the correct stop.

**Learner action:** Write a short, guided account from supplied facts. Keep the main events in the past tense and mark the supplied earlier completed events with hadde plus a past participle.

**Success meaning:** A successful response distinguishes earlier background from the later past sequence. This activity scores accurate use of hadde plus a participle for prior completion, not general storytelling style.

{{exercise: reconstruct_maris_delay}}

## A likely misconception: not every earlier event needs hadde {#likely-misconception-not-every-earlier-event-needs-hadde role=contrast objectives=obj-pluperfect-01}

The past perfect is most useful when you are looking back from an established past point and need to make the relationship clear. It does not have to replace every past-tense verb in a chronological story.

::: examples
- no: Jeg sto opp, spiste frokost og gikk til jobben.
- en: I got up, ate breakfast, and went to work.
:::

The sequence is already presented in order. Simple past is natural throughout.

Now imagine that the story begins at work:

::: examples
- no: Da jeg kom på jobben, hadde jeg allerede spist frokost.
- en: When I arrived at work, I had already eaten breakfast.
:::

The arrival is the later past point. The breakfast is earlier background, so hadde spist is useful.

Another common problem is reversing the forms. Suppose the stated order is: first the film began, and then we arrived at the cinema.

::: examples
- no: Da vi hadde kommet til kinoen, begynte filmen.
- en: When we had arrived at the cinema, the film began.
:::

This incorrectly places the past perfect on the later arrival. The film’s beginning is the earlier completed event:

::: examples
- no: Da vi kom til kinoen, hadde filmen begynt.
- en: When we arrived at the cinema, the film had begun.
:::

Ask yourself one practical question:

::: {.rule}
At that later moment in the past, what had already happened?
:::

The answer to that question is the event that normally takes hadde plus a past participle.

**Request: Repair-the-reversed-timeline**

**Purpose:** Prevent the learner from attaching the past perfect to the wrong event.

**Context:** Use pairs of past events with an explicitly stated order, including missed transport, late arrivals, and completed preparations.

**Learner action:** Correct sentences in which the past perfect marks the later event instead of the earlier completed event.

**Success meaning:** A successful correction places hadde plus the past participle on the event completed first. This activity scores timeline-to-form mapping rather than general error correction.

{{exercise: repair_the_reversed_timeline}}

## Transfer: arriving at a holiday cabin {#transfer-arriving-at-a-holiday-cabin role=recap objectives=obj-pluperfect-01}

Now move to a different situation. Henrik and Lea reached a rented cabin late on Friday evening. Their friends had arrived earlier and prepared several things before Henrik and Lea got there.

Relevant facts might include lighting the fire, buying food, making the beds, and finding the key. The later past point is Henrik and Lea’s arrival.

Useful models are:

::: examples
- no: Da Henrik og Lea kom fram, hadde vennene tent opp i peisen.
- en: When Henrik and Lea arrived, their friends had lit the fire.
- no: De hadde kjøpt mat før Henrik og Lea kom fram.
- en: They had bought food before Henrik and Lea arrived.
- no: Vennene hadde redd opp sengene før Henrik og Lea kom fram.
- en: The friends had made the beds before Henrik and Lea arrived.
- no: De hadde gjort sengene klare før gjestene kom.
- en: They had prepared the beds before the guests arrived.
:::

**Request: Cabin-arrival-transfer**

**Purpose:** Transfer the target form from the café story to a new everyday context.

**Context:** Use the arrival at the holiday cabin as the later past point and supplied preparation facts with familiar past participles as earlier events.

**Learner action:** Produce a short set of affirmative written statements showing what had been completed before Henrik and Lea arrived.

**Success meaning:** A successful response independently uses hadde plus accurate past participles to place completed preparations before the arrival. This activity scores transfer of the approved past-perfect meaning to a changed context.

{{exercise: cabin_arrival_transfer}}
