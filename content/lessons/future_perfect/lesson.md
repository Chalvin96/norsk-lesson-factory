Nobody is claiming that the introduction, budget check, or testing is complete now. Each action will be complete before a later point:

- the introduction before tomorrow’s meeting
- the budget check before the meeting begins
- the tests before lunch

This relationship between two points in time is the focus of the chapter.

## Looking back from a future point {#looking-back-from-a-future-point role=model objectives=obj-future-perfect-01}

Norwegian uses the **future perfect (futurum perfektum)** to present an action as complete before a later point. The central pattern is:

**future expression + ha + past participle**

The **past participle (perfektum partisipp)** is the same verb form used after *har* in the present perfect.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har skrevet rapporten.
- en: I have written the report.
:::

To move the point of completion into the future, replace *har* with a future expression followed by *ha*:

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal ha skrevet rapporten innen fredag.
- en: I will have written the report by Friday.
:::

The three approved future markers (the words that come before *ha*) are:

- *skal*
- *vil*
- *kommer til å*

When each marker is followed by *ha*, it forms the complete future-perfect frame:

- *skal ha*
- *vil ha*
- *kommer til å ha*

All three can show that something is complete before a later point. Their usual contexts differ slightly.

## From present completion to future completion {#from-present-to-future-completion role=model objectives=obj-future-perfect-01}

Compare these three sentences:

::: examples
- no: Eirik skriver innledningen nå.
- en: Eirik is writing the introduction now.
- no: Eirik har skrevet innledningen.
- en: Eirik has written the introduction.
- no: Eirik skal ha skrevet innledningen innen møtet.
- en: Eirik will have written the introduction by the meeting.
:::

The first sentence presents the activity as happening now. The second says that it is already complete. The third says that it is not necessarily complete now, but it will be complete before the meeting.

Another contrast:

::: examples
- no: Vi gjennomfører testene i morgen.
- en: We are carrying out the tests tomorrow.
- no: Vi har gjennomført testene.
- en: We have carried out the tests.
- no: Før lunsj kommer vi til å ha gjennomført testene.
- en: Before lunch, we will have carried out the tests.
:::

The future perfect does more than place an action in the future. It asks us to imagine a later point and look back from there at a completed action.

A compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use *skal ha*, *vil ha*, or *kommer til å ha* followed by a past participle when an action will be complete before a future reference point.
:::

That reference point is often introduced with expressions such as *innen fredag* (by Friday), *før lunsj* (before lunch), or *når møtet begynner* (when the meeting begins).

## Building the form {#building-the-form role=model objectives=obj-future-perfect-01}

The form of *ha* remains *ha* after the future expression. The main verb appears as a past participle.

- Infinitive: *skrive*; Past participle: *skrevet*; Future completion: *skal ha skrevet*
- Infinitive: *kontrollere*; Past participle: *kontrollert*; Future completion: *vil ha kontrollert*
- Infinitive: *gjennomføre*; Past participle: *gjennomført*; Future completion: *kommer til å ha gjennomført*
- Infinitive: *sende*; Past participle: *sendt*; Future completion: *skal ha sendt*
- Infinitive: *gjøre*; Past participle: *gjort*; Future completion: *vil ha gjort*
- Infinitive: *lese*; Past participle: *lest*; Future completion: *kommer til å ha lest*

Examples:

::: examples
- no: Innen klokka fire skal jeg ha sendt e-posten.
- en: By four o’clock, I will have sent the email.
- no: Om to uker vil de ha gjort mesteparten av arbeidet.
- en: In two weeks, they will have done most of the work.
- no: Før måneden er over, kommer hun til å ha lest hele boka.
- en: Before the month is over, she will have read the whole book.
:::

**A common form mistake**

Do not put a present-tense or past-tense verb after *ha*.

Incorrect: *Jeg skal ha skriver rapporten innen fredag.* / *I will have writes the report by Friday.*

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal ha skriver rapporten innen fredag.
- en: 'Incorrect: I will have writes the report by Friday.'
:::

Incorrect: *Jeg skal ha skrev rapporten innen fredag.* / *I will have wrote the report by Friday.*

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal ha skrev rapporten innen fredag.
- en: 'Incorrect: I will have wrote the report by Friday.'
:::

Use the past participle:

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal ha skrevet rapporten innen fredag.
- en: I will have written the report by Friday.
:::

Also keep *ha* in its infinitive form:

::: examples
- no: Hun vil ha kontrollert tallene før møtet.
- en: She will have checked the figures before the meeting.
:::

Not: *Hun vil har kontrollert tallene før møtet.* / *She will has checked the figures before the meeting.*

::: examples
- no: Hun vil har kontrollert tallene før møtet.
- en: 'Incorrect: She will has checked the figures before the meeting.'
:::

## Using skal ha for a plan or commitment {#using-skal-ha-plan-or-commitment role=model objectives=obj-future-perfect-01}

In Maja and Eirik’s conversation, Eirik says:

::: examples
- no: Innen møtet i morgen skal jeg ha skrevet ferdig innledningen.
- en: By tomorrow’s meeting, I will have finished writing the introduction.
:::

Here, *skal ha* presents completion as part of a plan, commitment, instruction, or schedule. Eirik is responsible for the introduction and commits himself to finishing it before the meeting.

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal ha levert søknaden innen mandag.
- en: I will have submitted the application by Monday.
- no: Vi skal ha ryddet lokalet før gjestene kommer.
- en: We will have tidied the venue before the guests arrive.
- no: Teknikeren skal ha installert programmet innen klokka to.
- en: The technician will have installed the software by two o’clock.
:::

The important meaning is not simply that the action happens later. The action reaches completion by the stated future point.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal skrive rapporten på fredag.
- en: I am going to write the report on Friday.
- no: Jeg skal ha skrevet rapporten innen fredag.
- en: I will have written the report by Friday.
:::

In the first sentence, Friday is when the writing is planned to happen. In the second, Friday is the deadline: the report will already be written by then.

## Using vil ha for a future prediction {#using-vil-ha-future-prediction role=model objectives=obj-future-perfect-01}

Return to Nora’s task:

::: examples
- no: Når møtet begynner, vil Nora ha kontrollert alle tallene.
- en: When the meeting begins, Nora will have checked all the figures.
:::

Here, the speaker predicts Nora’s completed situation at the time of the meeting. *Vil ha* often works well when the speaker adopts a neutral future viewpoint and states what will already be complete then.

::: examples
- no: Når du kommer hjem, vil jeg ha laget middag.
- en: When you come home, I will have made dinner.
- no: Ved slutten av året vil selskapet ha åpnet tre nye kontorer.
- en: By the end of the year, the company will have opened three new offices.
- no: Om noen måneder vil hun ha fullført kurset.
- en: In a few months, she will have completed the course.
- no: Når filmen begynner, vil de fleste gjestene ha funnet plassene sine.
- en: When the film begins, most of the guests will have found their seats.
:::

The future point may come first or later in the sentence:

::: examples
- no: Innen neste sommer vil de ha bygget ferdig huset.
- en: By next summer, they will have finished building the house.
- no: De vil ha bygget ferdig huset innen neste sommer.
- en: They will have finished building the house by next summer.
:::

The time expression helps make the intended future-completion meaning clear.

## Using kommer til å ha for an expected result {#using-kommer-til-aa-ha-expected-result role=model objectives=obj-future-perfect-01}

Eirik also says:

::: examples
- no: Med den farten vi holder nå, kommer vi til å ha gjennomført alle testene før lunsj.
- en: At our current rate, we will have completed all the tests before lunch.
:::

*Kommer til å ha* often presents future completion as an expected result, especially when the prediction is based on a current situation, tendency, or visible evidence.

::: examples
- no: Hvis hun fortsetter slik, kommer hun til å ha spart nok penger innen juni.
- en: If she continues like this, she will have saved enough money by June.
- no: Med dette tempoet kommer vi til å ha malt hele huset før helga.
- en: At this rate, we will have painted the entire house before the weekend.
- no: Kari og Ola kan ikke vente på oss. Toget vårt er forsinket, så de kommer til å ha reist når vi kommer fram.
- en: Kari and Ola cannot wait for us. Our train is delayed, so they will have left by the time we arrive.
- no: Etter fem dager med sol kommer snøen til å ha smeltet.
- en: After five days of sunshine, the snow will have melted.
:::

The form is longer, but its structure is regular:

**kommer til å + ha + past participle**

::: examples
- no: De kommer til å ha avsluttet arbeidet innen kvelden.
- en: They will have finished the work by the evening.
:::

A frequent mistake is to omit *å*:

::: examples
- no: De kommer til ha avsluttet arbeidet.
- en: They will have finished the work. (This is the intended meaning; English does not reproduce the omitted-*å* error.)
:::

Keep the complete future expression:

::: examples
- no: De kommer til å ha avsluttet arbeidet.
- en: They will have finished the work.
:::

## Choosing among the three expressions {#choosing-among-three-expressions role=model objectives=obj-future-perfect-01}

All three constructions share the same time relationship:

- current completion is not asserted;
- a later reference point is established;
- the action is presented as complete by that point.

The choice of future expression adds a viewpoint:

- *skal ha*: planned, required, or promised completion
- *vil ha*: prediction viewed from a later point
- *kommer til å ha*: expected result, often supported by present evidence

Compare one situation presented in three ways:

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal ha lest boka innen mandag.
- en: I will have read the book by Monday.
:::

The speaker treats reading the book as a plan or commitment.

::: examples
- no: Innen mandag vil jeg ha lest boka.
- en: By Monday, I will have read the book.
:::

The speaker predicts the completed situation on Monday.

::: examples
- no: Med den farten jeg leser nå, kommer jeg til å ha lest boka innen mandag.
- en: At the rate I am reading now, I will have read the book by Monday.
:::

The current reading pace supports the prediction.

These differences are useful, but they are not rigid labels for every possible sentence. Context matters. The central requirement remains the same: *ha* is followed by a past participle, and the completed action is viewed relative to a later point.

## Common meaning problems {#common-meaning-problems role=contrast objectives=obj-future-perfect-01}

**Describing a future activity instead of future completion**

::: examples
- no: Vi skal teste systemet før lunsj.
- en: We are going to test the system before lunch.
:::

This says when the testing will happen. It does not necessarily present the tests as complete at a later reference point.

::: examples
- no: Vi skal ha testet systemet før lunsj.
- en: We will have tested the system before lunch.
:::

This presents the testing as complete before lunch.

**Using the present perfect for a future deadline**

::: examples
- no: Vi har sendt dokumentene.
- en: We have sent the documents.
:::

The sending is complete now.

::: examples
- no: Innen klokka tre skal vi ha sendt dokumentene.
- en: By three o’clock, we will have sent the documents.
:::

The sending will be complete by a future time.

**Mentioning the future without a clear completion relationship**

A time expression does not automatically create future-perfect meaning.

::: examples
- no: Hun vil arbeide på rapporten i morgen.
- en: She will work on the report tomorrow.
:::

The sentence places the work tomorrow, but it does not say that the report will be complete.

::: examples
- no: Innen i morgen vil hun ha skrevet rapporten.
- en: By tomorrow, she will have written the report.
:::

Now the report is presented as complete by the future point.

## Transfer: preparing for a weekend trip {#transfer-preparing-for-a-weekend-trip role=recap objectives=obj-future-perfect-01}

Change the situation. A family is leaving for a cabin at ten o’clock on Saturday. Several tasks remain: packing food, charging the car, checking the weather forecast, and giving a key to a neighbor.

Use the future-completion forms to describe what will be complete before departure.

A planned responsibility can use *skal ha*:

::: examples
- no: Før vi drar, skal jeg ha pakket all maten.
- en: Before we leave, I will have packed all the food.
:::

A prediction viewed from departure time can use *vil ha*:

::: examples
- no: Når klokka er ti, vil Sara ha sjekket værmeldingen.
- en: When it is ten o’clock, Sara will have checked the weather forecast.
:::

A result supported by current evidence can use *kommer til å ha*:

::: examples
- no: Bilen lader raskt, så den kommer til å ha ladet ferdig før vi drar.
- en: The car is charging quickly, so it will have finished charging before we leave.
:::
