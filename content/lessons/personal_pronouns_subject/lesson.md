## At the community café {#at-the-community-cafe role=orient objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-subject-01}

The volunteers repeatedly use short words such as *jeg*, *du* and *de* instead of repeating names. To understand each sentence, you need to know who is speaking, who is being addressed, and who is being discussed.

## Replacing a name with a subject pronoun {#replacing-a-name-with-a-subject-pronoun role=model objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-subject-01}

A **subject pronoun (subjektspronomen)** is a short word that identifies the person or people a sentence is about. It normally comes before the verb in the simple sentences used here.

Compare the introductions:

::: examples
- no: Maja heter Maja.
- en: Maja’s name is Maja.
:::

::: examples
- no: Jeg heter Maja.
- en: My name is Maja.
:::

Maja uses *jeg* because she is speaking about herself.

::: examples
- no: Emil lager kaffe.
- en: Emil makes coffee.
:::

::: examples
- no: Han lager kaffe.
- en: He makes coffee.
:::

The second sentence uses *han* because Emil is one male person already known from the context.

Norwegian has seven subject pronouns in this lesson:

- *jeg* — I — the speaker
- *du* — you — one person being addressed
- *han* — he — one male person being discussed
- *hun* — she — one female person being discussed
- *vi* — we — the speaker and one or more other people
- *dere* — you — two or more people being addressed
- *de* — they — two or more people being discussed

The central question is not simply “How many people are there?” You must also ask what relationship those people have to the conversation: Are they speaking, being addressed, or being discussed?

**Checkpoint request: identify-pronoun-reference-in-cafe**

**Purpose:** Notice who each pronoun represents before trying to produce the forms independently.
**Context:** Use the café dialogue and its named volunteers.
**Learner action:** For selected sentences, identify the person or group represented by the highlighted subject pronoun.
**Success meaning:** A successful response shows that the learner can connect a Bokmål subject pronoun to the correct speaker, listener, or discussed person. The scored goal is reference identification, not translation.

{{exercise: identify_pronoun_reference_in_cafe}}

## The speaker: *jeg* and *vi* {#the-speaker-jeg-and-vi role=contrast objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-subject-01}

Use **jeg** when one speaker means “I.”

::: examples
- no: Jeg heter Sara.
- en: My name is Sara.
- no: Jeg lager kaffe.
- en: I make coffee.
- no: Jeg jobber her.
- en: I work here.
:::

Use **vi** when the speaker includes at least one other person.

::: examples
- no: Vi jobber på kafeen.
- en: We work at the café.
- no: Vi begynner klokka ni.
- en: We start at nine.
- no: Vi åpner klokka ti.
- en: We open at ten.
:::

The contrast depends on inclusion:

::: examples
- no: Jeg begynner klokka ni.
- en: I start at nine.
- no: Vi begynner klokka ni.
- en: We start at nine.
:::

In the first sentence, the speaker refers only to themself. In the second, the speaker includes other people.

Return to Sara’s words:

::: examples
- no: Jeg baker.
- en: I’m baking.
- no: Vi åpner klokka ti.
- en: We open at ten.
:::

Sara alone is doing the baking, so she says *jeg*. Opening the café is the group’s shared activity, so she says *vi*.

**Common mistake: using *vi* for any group**

*Vi* does not mean every group of several people. It means a group that includes the speaker.

If Sara is speaking about herself and Emil, she can say:

::: examples
- no: Vi jobber her.
- en: We work here.
:::

If Maja is speaking about Sara and Emil, but not herself, she says:

::: examples
- no: De jobber her.
- en: They work here.
:::

The number is the same in both cases. What changes is whether the speaker belongs to the group.

**Checkpoint request: choose-jeg-or-vi-for-speaker**

**Purpose:** Practise the contrast between the speaker alone and a group that includes the speaker.
**Context:** Short statements by café volunteers about individual and shared tasks.
**Learner action:** Complete each simple sentence with *jeg* or *vi*, using information about who is speaking and who performs the task.
**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates correct selection based on whether the speaker is alone or included in a group. The scored goal is the *jeg–vi* meaning contrast.

{{exercise: choose_jeg_or_vi_for_speaker}}

## The person or people addressed: *du* and *dere* {#the-person-or-people-addressed-du-and-dere role=contrast objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-subject-01}

Use **du** when speaking directly to one person.

Sara speaks to Emil:

::: examples
- no: Emil, du lager kaffe.
- en: Emil, you make coffee.
:::

Emil speaks to Sara:

::: examples
- no: Og du, Sara?
- en: And you, Sara?
:::

More examples:

::: examples
- no: Du jobber her.
- en: You work here.
- no: Du begynner klokka ni.
- en: You start at nine.
:::

Use **dere** when speaking directly to two or more people.

Sara addresses Maja and Jonas together:

::: examples
- no: Maja og Jonas, dere setter stolene her.
- en: Maja and Jonas, you put the chairs here.
:::

Other examples:

::: examples
- no: Dere jobber på kafeen.
- en: You work at the café.
- no: Dere begynner klokka ni.
- en: You start at nine.
:::

English uses *you* for both one person and several people. Bokmål makes the difference explicit:

::: examples
- no: Emil, du lager kaffe.
- en: Emil, you make coffee.
- no: Emil og Maja, dere lager kaffe.
- en: Emil and Maja, you make coffee.
:::

Compact rule:

- Addressing one person: *du*
- Addressing two or more people: *dere*

**Common mistake: choosing *du* because English says “you”**

English translation alone does not tell you which Norwegian form to use. You must inspect the situation.

A manager speaking to one volunteer uses *du*:

::: examples
- no: Du åpner døra.
- en: You open the door.
:::

A manager speaking to three volunteers uses *dere*:

::: examples
- no: Dere åpner døra.
- en: You open the door.
:::

The English sentence can remain unchanged, but the Norwegian pronoun changes with the number of listeners.

**Checkpoint request: distinguish-du-and-dere-by-addressee-count**

**Purpose:** Make the singular–plural distinction that English *you* can hide.
**Context:** Café instructions addressed either to one named volunteer or to a named group.
**Learner action:** Supply *du* or *dere* for each instruction after identifying how many people are being addressed.
**Success meaning:** A successful response shows accurate choice of singular *du* versus plural *dere*. The scored goal is addressee number.

{{exercise: distinguish_du_and_dere_by_addressee_count}}

## One person being discussed: *han* and *hun* {#one-person-being-discussed-han-and-hun role=contrast objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-subject-01}

Use **han** for one male person being discussed.

::: examples
- no: Han heter Emil.
- en: His name is Emil.
- no: Han lager kaffe.
- en: He makes coffee.
- no: Han kommer klokka ni.
- en: He is coming at nine.
:::

Use **hun** for one female person being discussed.

::: examples
- no: Hun heter Sara.
- en: Her name is Sara.
- no: Hun baker.
- en: She is baking.
- no: Hun jobber på kafeen.
- en: She works at the café.
:::

Compare direct address with discussion:

::: examples
- no: Sara, du baker.
- en: Sara, you are baking.
- no: Hun baker.
- en: She is baking.
:::

In the first sentence, Sara is the listener, so the speaker uses *du*. In the second, the speaker is talking about Sara to someone else, so the speaker uses *hun*.

The same contrast applies to Emil:

::: examples
- no: Emil, du lager kaffe.
- en: Emil, you make coffee.
- no: Han lager kaffe.
- en: He makes coffee.
:::

**Common mistake: keeping *du* when the listener changes**

Imagine Jonas says this directly to Sara:

::: examples
- no: Du begynner klokka ni.
- en: You start at nine.
:::

Later, Jonas tells Maja about Sara:

::: examples
- no: Hun begynner klokka ni.
- en: She starts at nine.
:::

Sara is the same person, but her role in the conversation changes. When she is addressed, use *du*. When she is discussed, use *hun*.

**Checkpoint request: choose-han-or-hun-for-named-person**

**Purpose:** Connect *han* and *hun* with a known person in context.
**Context:** Brief café updates about Emil, Sara, Maja, Jonas, Nora, and Ali.
**Learner action:** Replace one named individual with *han* or *hun* in a simple sentence.
**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates correct use of a third-person singular subject pronoun for the named person. The scored goal is the *han–hun* choice.

{{exercise: choose_han_or_hun_for_named_person}}

**Checkpoint request: shift-from-direct-address-to-discussion**

**Purpose:** Show that pronoun choice depends on conversational role, not only personal identity.
**Context:** One sentence is spoken directly to a volunteer; a related sentence reports the same information to someone else.
**Learner action:** Rewrite the subject pronoun when the person changes from listener to person being discussed.
**Success meaning:** A successful response shows a correct shift from *du* to *han* or *hun*. The scored goal is adapting the pronoun to changed conversational perspective.

{{exercise: shift_from_direct_address_to_discussion}}

## Several people being discussed: *de* {#several-people-being-discussed-de role=model objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-subject-01}

Use **de** for two or more people being discussed. The speaker is not included, and the people are not being addressed.

Maja asks about Sara and Emil:

::: examples
- no: Jobber de her også?
- en: Do they work here too?
:::

Sara speaks about Nora and Ali:

::: examples
- no: De kommer snart.
- en: They are coming soon.
:::

Further examples:

::: examples
- no: Nora og Ali jobber på kafeen.
- en: Nora and Ali work at the café.
- no: De jobber på kafeen.
- en: They work at the café.
- no: Sara, Emil og Jonas begynner klokka ni.
- en: Sara, Emil and Jonas start at nine.
- no: De begynner klokka ni.
- en: They start at nine.
:::

Use *de* for a mixed group as well as for a group consisting only of men or only of women. The important point is that the group contains several people being discussed.

## *Dere* or *de*? {#dere-or-de role=contrast objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-subject-01}

These forms both refer to more than one person, but they have different conversational roles.

::: examples
- no: Dere jobber her.
- en: You work here.
:::

The speaker is talking directly to the group.

::: examples
- no: De jobber her.
- en: They work here.
:::

The speaker is talking about the group to someone else.

Picture Sara standing in front of Maja and Jonas:

::: examples
- no: Dere setter stolene her.
- en: You put the chairs here.
:::

Now picture Sara speaking to Emil about Maja and Jonas:

::: examples
- no: De setter stolene her.
- en: They put the chairs here.
:::

The same people perform the same task. Only the direction of the conversation changes.

Compact rule:

- Group being addressed: *dere*
- Group being discussed: *de*

**Checkpoint request: distinguish-dere-and-de-by-conversation-role**

**Purpose:** Prevent confusion between plural “you” and “they.”
**Context:** Paired café situations involving the same named group, first as listeners and then as people discussed.
**Learner action:** Choose *dere* or *de* from the described direction of speech.
**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates whether the learner can distinguish a group being addressed from a group being discussed. The scored goal is the *dere–de* role contrast.

{{exercise: distinguish_dere_and_de_by_conversation_role}}

## A useful perspective map {#a-useful-perspective-map role=contrast objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-subject-01}

You can organize all seven pronouns by asking three questions.

**1. Is the speaker included?**

If yes, use:

- *jeg* for the speaker alone
- *vi* for the speaker with other people

::: examples
- no: Jeg åpner døra.
- en: I open the door.
- no: Vi åpner døra.
- en: We open the door.
:::

**2. Is the speaker addressing the person or group?**

If yes, use:

- *du* for one listener
- *dere* for several listeners

::: examples
- no: Du er klar.
- en: You are ready.
- no: Dere er klare.
- en: You are ready.
:::

**3. Is the speaker discussing someone else?**

If yes, use:

- *han* for one male person
- *hun* for one female person
- *de* for several people

::: examples
- no: Han er klar.
- en: He is ready.
- no: Hun er klar.
- en: She is ready.
- no: De er klare.
- en: They are ready.
:::

This perspective map is more reliable than translating one English word in isolation. First understand the situation; then select the Norwegian pronoun.

**Checkpoint request: select-subject-pronoun-from-perspective**

**Purpose:** Bring all seven forms together through one consistent decision process.
**Context:** Short descriptions identify speaker, listener, and people being discussed during the café morning.
**Learner action:** Complete each simple sentence with the subject pronoun that matches the described perspective.
**Success meaning:** A successful response shows independent selection among *jeg, du, han, hun, vi, dere,* and *de*. The scored goal is whole-set pronoun choice; vocabulary and verb forms provide context only.

{{exercise: select_subject_pronoun_from_perspective}}

## Returning to the café {#returning-to-the-cafe role=model objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-subject-01}

The chairs are now in place, and Nora and Ali arrive.

:::

::: {.reading translation="Do you work here, Maja?" dialogue_id="community-cafe-return" speaker_id="ali" speaker_name="Ali" voice_profile="masculine"}

:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I’m working here today. Sara and I are baking. We’ll soon be finished." dialogue_id="community-cafe-return" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}

Notice how Ali uses *du* while speaking directly to Maja. Maja uses *jeg* for herself, then *vi* for herself and Sara. Jonas uses *dere* because he addresses Nora and Ali together. Emil uses *de* because he comments about Nora and Ali rather than speaking directly to them.

**Purpose:** Move from recognizing pronouns to using them naturally to avoid repeated names.
**Context:** A short continuation of the café scene containing repeated references to volunteers.
**Learner action:** Rewrite selected simple sentences by replacing repeated subject names with suitable subject pronouns.
**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates accurate pronoun production while preserving who each sentence is about. The scored goal is replacement of a named subject with the correct pronoun.

## Transfer to a new situation {#transfer-to-a-new-situation role=recap objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-subject-01}

The next day, four classmates meet at the library to prepare a class display. The setting has changed, but the same perspective choices still apply.

One student introduces herself and another student. A classmate asks one person whether that person is ready. Someone gives an instruction to two classmates together. Later, one student reports what those two classmates are doing. Finally, the speaker describes a task shared with another person.

**Cumulative request: use-subject-pronouns-at-the-library**

**Purpose:** Transfer the key subject-pronoun contrasts from the café to a new everyday situation.
**Context:** Classmates organizing materials at a library table.
**Learner action:** Write short, simple Bokmål sentences for the described exchanges, choosing subject pronouns according to speaker, listener, and discussed person or group.
**Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates flexible use of the pronoun forms elicited by the described exchanges in a changed context. The scored goal is accurate subject-pronoun selection, not open-ended speaking or additional grammar.

{{exercise: use_subject_pronouns_at_the_library}}

## Short retrieval review {#short-retrieval-review role=recap objectives=obj-personal-pronouns-subject-01}

Without looking back, write the Bokmål subject pronoun for each meaning: the speaker alone, one listener, one male person discussed, one female person discussed, the speaker with others, several listeners, and several people discussed. Then write three simple sentences: one with *vi*, one with *dere*, and one with *de*. Your sentences should make clear who is included, addressed, or discussed.
