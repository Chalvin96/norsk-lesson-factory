## A conversation at the community centre {#conversation-community-centre role=orient objectives=obj-snakke-med-or-snakke-om-01}

Mina has recently started helping at a community centre. She and her colleague Jonas are preparing an evening event, but the room booking is unclear.

::: {.reading translation="Have you spoken with Ingrid?" dialogue_id="community-centre-opening" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Har du snakket med Ingrid?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I spoke with her this morning." dialogue_id="community-centre-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, jeg snakket med henne i morges.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Did you talk about the room?" dialogue_id="community-centre-opening" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Snakket dere om rommet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, we talked about the food. Ingrid is ordering pizza." dialogue_id="community-centre-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nei, vi snakket om maten. Ingrid bestiller pizza.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then we need to talk with her about the room too." dialogue_id="community-centre-opening" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Da må vi snakke med henne om rommet også.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I can call her now." dialogue_id="community-centre-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg kan ringe henne nå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. Can you also talk with her about the time?" dialogue_id="community-centre-opening" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Fint. Kan du også snakke med henne om tidspunktet?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. I’ll talk with her about both the room and the time." dialogue_id="community-centre-opening" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. Jeg snakker med henne om både rommet og tidspunktet.
:::

Mina’s questions distinguish two roles in a conversation. Ingrid is the person Jonas speaks with. The room, food, and time are the topics they speak about.

## Two useful patterns {#two-useful-patterns role=model objectives=obj-snakke-med-or-snakke-om-01}

With the verb **snakke**, use **med** for the conversation partner and **om** for the topic.

**Conversation partner: snakke med**

The pattern **snakke med noen** means to speak or talk with someone.

::: examples
- no: Mina snakker med Jonas.
- en: Mina is talking with Jonas.
- no: Har du snakket med Ingrid?
- en: Have you spoken with Ingrid?
- no: Jeg må snakke med sjefen.
- en: I need to speak with the manager.
- no: Kan jeg snakke med deg?
- en: Can I speak with you?
:::

The word after **med** answers the question: Who is taking part in the conversation with the subject?

In the opening dialogue, Jonas says:

::: examples
- no: Jeg snakket med henne i morges.
- en: I spoke with her this morning.
:::

Here, **henne** refers to Ingrid. Ingrid is Jonas’s conversation partner.

**Topic: snakke om**

The pattern **snakke om noe** means to speak or talk about something.

::: examples
- no: De snakker om maten.
- en: They are talking about the food.
- no: Vi må snakke om rommet.
- en: We need to talk about the room.
- no: Snakket dere om tidspunktet?
- en: Did you talk about the time?
- no: Jeg vil ikke snakke om jobben nå.
- en: I don’t want to talk about work now.
:::

The word after **om** answers the question: What is the conversation about?

In the dialogue, Mina asks:

::: examples
- no: Snakket dere om rommet?
- en: Did you talk about the room?
:::

The room is not a participant in the conversation. It is the topic.

## Compare the meanings {#compare-the-meanings role=contrast objectives=obj-snakke-med-or-snakke-om-01}

Changing the preposition can change the meaning sharply, even when the noun remains the same.

::: examples
- no: Jonas snakker med Ingrid.
- en: Jonas is talking with Ingrid.
- no: Jonas snakker om Ingrid.
- en: Jonas is talking about Ingrid.
:::

In the first sentence, Ingrid is part of the conversation. In the second, Ingrid is the topic. She may not be present.

The same contrast appears here:

::: examples
- no: Jeg snakker med legen.
- en: I am talking with the doctor.
- no: Jeg snakker om legen.
- en: I am talking about the doctor.
:::

And here:

::: examples
- no: Vi snakket med naboene.
- en: We spoke with the neighbours.
- no: Vi snakket om naboene.
- en: We spoke about the neighbours.
:::

A person can therefore appear after either preposition. What matters is the person’s role.

**Conversation partner**

::: examples
- no: snakke med Ingrid
- en: talk with Ingrid
:::

**Topic**

::: examples
- no: snakke om Ingrid
- en: talk about Ingrid
:::

**Conversation partner**

::: examples
- no: snakke med legen
- en: talk with the doctor
:::

**Topic**

::: examples
- no: snakke om legen
- en: talk about the doctor
:::

**Conversation partner**

::: examples
- no: snakke med naboene
- en: talk with the neighbours
:::

**Topic**

::: examples
- no: snakke om naboene
- en: talk about the neighbours
:::

Do not decide only by asking whether the following word refers to a person. Ask whether that person participates in the conversation or is discussed.

## Both roles in one sentence {#both-roles-one-sentence role=model objectives=obj-snakke-med-or-snakke-om-01}

A conversation normally has both participants and a topic. Norwegian can state both:

**snakke med noen om noe**

::: examples
- no: Jonas snakker med Ingrid om rommet.
- en: Jonas is talking with Ingrid about the room.
:::

This sentence gives three pieces of information:

- Jonas is the subject.
- Ingrid is the conversation partner introduced by **med**.
- The room is the topic introduced by **om**.

More examples:

::: examples
- no: Jeg må snakke med sjefen om arbeidstiden.
- en: I need to talk with the manager about the working hours.
- no: Sara snakker med legen om søvnproblemene.
- en: Sara is talking with the doctor about the sleep problems.
- no: Vi snakket med naboene om festen.
- en: We spoke with the neighbours about the party.
- no: Kan du snakke med barna om skolen?
- en: Can you talk with the children about school?
- no: Amir vil snakke med læreren om prøven.
- en: Amir wants to talk with the teacher about the test.
:::

The common order is:

**snakke + med + conversation partner + om + topic**

This order makes each role easy to follow.

## Useful questions {#useful-questions role=model objectives=obj-snakke-med-or-snakke-om-01}

The same distinction appears in questions.

**Asking about the conversation partner**

::: examples
- no: Hvem snakker du med?
- en: Who are you talking with?
- no: Hvem snakket Mina med?
- en: Who did Mina speak with?
- no: Hvem skal vi snakke med?
- en: Who are we going to talk with?
:::

These questions ask for a person participating in the conversation.

**Asking about the topic**

::: examples
- no: Hva snakker dere om?
- en: What are you talking about?
- no: Hva snakket de om?
- en: What did they talk about?
- no: Hva skal vi snakke om?
- en: What are we going to talk about?
:::

These questions ask for the subject of the conversation.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Hvem snakker Jonas med?
- en: Who is Jonas talking with?
- no: Hvem snakker Jonas om?
- en: Who is Jonas talking about?
:::

The first answer would identify Jonas’s conversation partner. The second would identify a person being discussed.

This contrast is useful when someone mentions a name but you do not know the person’s role.

::: examples
- no: Snakker du med Erik, eller snakker du om Erik?
- en: Are you talking with Erik, or are you talking about Erik?
:::

## Short answers and natural pronouns {#short-answers-natural-pronouns role=model objectives=obj-snakke-med-or-snakke-om-01}

After a person has already been named, Norwegian often uses an object pronoun after **med** or **om**.

::: examples
- no: Har du snakket med Ingrid?
- en: Have you spoken with Ingrid?
- no: Ja, jeg har snakket med henne.
- en: Yes, I have spoken with her.
:::

The same pronoun can follow **om**, but the meaning changes.

::: examples
- no: Snakket dere om Ingrid?
- en: Did you talk about Ingrid?
- no: Ja, vi snakket om henne.
- en: Yes, we talked about her.
:::

Other frequent combinations include:

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg snakke med deg?
- en: Can I speak with you?
- no: Vi snakket om deg.
- en: We talked about you.
- no: Læreren vil snakke med oss.
- en: The teacher wants to talk with us.
- no: Læreren vil snakke om oss.
- en: The teacher wants to talk about us.
:::

Again, the pronoun itself does not determine the role. **Med** marks the partner; **om** marks the topic.

This activity belongs here because questions test whether you can work backward from meaning. A successful response uses **hvem … med?** when seeking a partner and **hva … om?** or **hvem … om?** when seeking a topic.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-snakke-med-or-snakke-om-01}

**Using om for the person you contact**

A learner may produce:

::: examples
- no: Jeg må snakke om sjefen.
- en: I need to talk about the manager.
:::

This is correct Norwegian, but it does not mean that you need a conversation with the manager. It means that the manager is the topic.

If the manager is the person you need to contact, use:

::: examples
- no: Jeg må snakke med sjefen.
- en: I need to talk with the manager.
:::

If you need to contact the manager about a problem, include both roles:

::: examples
- no: Jeg må snakke med sjefen om problemet.
- en: I need to talk with the manager about the problem.
:::

**Using med for the topic**

This sentence treats the room as a conversation partner:

::: examples
- no: Jonas snakker med rommet.
- en: Jonas is talking with the room.
:::

For an ordinary discussion about the room, use:

::: examples
- no: Jonas snakker om rommet.
- en: Jonas is talking about the room.
:::

Compare a more realistic pair:

::: examples
- no: Jonas snakker med Ingrid.
- en: Jonas is talking with Ingrid.
- no: Jonas snakker om rommet.
- en: Jonas is talking about the room.
:::

Then combine them:

::: examples
- no: Jonas snakker med Ingrid om rommet.
- en: Jonas is talking with Ingrid about the room.
:::

**Assuming every person must follow med**

People can be topics too.

::: examples
- no: Vi snakker med den nye læreren.
- en: We are talking with the new teacher.
- no: Vi snakker om den nye læreren.
- en: We are talking about the new teacher.
:::

The first sentence places the teacher in the conversation. The second makes the teacher the subject of the discussion.

**Leaving the relationship unclear**

This sentence is complete:

::: examples
- no: Mina snakker med Jonas.
- en: Mina is talking with Jonas.
:::

But if the purpose of the conversation matters, add **om**:

::: examples
- no: Mina snakker med Jonas om arrangementet.
- en: Mina is talking with Jonas about the event.
:::

Likewise, this sentence gives a topic:

::: examples
- no: Mina snakker om arrangementet.
- en: Mina is talking about the event.
:::

If the listener needs to know the partner too, add **med**:

::: examples
- no: Mina snakker med Ingrid om arrangementet.
- en: Mina is talking with Ingrid about the event.
:::

## Back at the community centre {#back-at-community-centre role=model objectives=obj-snakke-med-or-snakke-om-01}

::: {.reading translation="Hi, Ingrid. Do you have time to talk about the event?" dialogue_id="community-centre-later" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hei, Ingrid. Har du tid til å snakke om arrangementet?

::: {.reading translation="Yes. What do you want to talk about?" dialogue_id="community-centre-later" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}

:::

::: {.reading translation="I want to talk with you about the room and the time." dialogue_id="community-centre-later" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg vil snakke med deg om rommet og tidspunktet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="The room is available from six o’clock. I spoke with the caretaker about it yesterday." dialogue_id="community-centre-later" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Rommet er ledig fra klokka seks. Jeg snakket med vaktmesteren om det i går.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Mina also wants to talk with you about the food." dialogue_id="community-centre-later" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Flott. Mina vil også snakke med deg om maten.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That works well. I can talk with her afterwards." dialogue_id="community-centre-later" speaker_id="ingrid" speaker_name="Ingrid" character_id="ingrid"}
Det passer bra. Jeg kan snakke med henne etterpå.
:::

Notice how the conversation now supplies information missing from the opening dialogue. Ingrid spoke **with the caretaker** and **about the room**. Jonas speaks **with Ingrid** and **about the room and time**. Mina plans to speak **with Ingrid** and **about the food**.

## Guided reuse in new situations {#guided-reuse-new-situations role=model objectives=obj-snakke-med-or-snakke-om-01}

The pattern is equally useful outside event planning.

**At work**

::: examples
- no: Ella snakker med kollegaen sin om møtet.
- en: Ella is talking with her colleague about the meeting.
- no: Hvem skal du snakke med om ferieplanen?
- en: Who are you going to talk with about the holiday plan?
:::

**At school**

::: examples
- no: Foreldrene snakker med læreren om barnet sitt.
- en: The parents are talking with the teacher about their child.
- no: Elevene snakker om den nye læreren.
- en: The pupils are talking about the new teacher.
:::

**At a medical appointment**

::: examples
- no: David vil snakke med legen om medisinen.
- en: David wants to talk with the doctor about the medicine.
- no: Legen snakker med David om søvnen hans.
- en: The doctor is talking with David about his sleep.
:::

**At home**

::: examples
- no: Vi må snakke med barna om skjermtid.
- en: We need to talk with the children about screen time.
- no: Barna snakker om ferien.
- en: The children are talking about the holiday.
:::

Across all these contexts, the decision remains the same:

- Use **med** for the conversation partner.
- Use **om** for the topic.
- Use both in **snakke med noen om noe** when both roles matter.

## Final practice: build complete messages {#final-practice-build-complete-messages role=recap objectives=obj-snakke-med-or-snakke-om-01}

Write short Norwegian messages for practical situations such as arranging a meeting, contacting a teacher, discussing a household problem, or asking a colleague for information. Each situation should specify either a partner, a topic, or both. Keep the response focused on choosing **med**, **om**, or the combined pattern.

This activity belongs near the end because it moves from identifying roles to expressing a complete communicative purpose. New situations test whether the pattern transfers beyond Mina and Jonas’s event. A successful response clearly shows who participates in the conversation, what the conversation concerns, or both.

## Retrieval and review {#retrieval-and-review role=recap objectives=obj-snakke-med-or-snakke-om-01}

Without looking back, write three Norwegian sentences:

1. one naming only a conversation partner with **snakke med**;
2. one naming only a topic with **snakke om**;
3. one combining both roles as **snakke med noen om noe**.

Then write one question asking who someone is speaking with and one question asking what they are speaking about. Successful review keeps partner and topic distinct even when both are people.
