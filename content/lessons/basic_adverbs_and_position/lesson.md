## Getting ready for a party {#getting-ready-for-a-party role=orient objectives=obj-basic-adverbs-and-position-01}

Anna and Erik are preparing food before some friends arrive. Nora is sleeping in the next room.

::: {.reading translation="You’re early!" dialogue_id=party-preparations speaker_id=anna speaker_name="Anna"}
Du er tidlig!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I often arrive late, but I’m not late today." dialogue_id=party-preparations speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Ja, jeg kommer ofte sent, men jeg er ikke sen i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. I’m working slowly today." dialogue_id=party-preparations speaker_id=anna speaker_name="Anna"}
Bra. Jeg arbeider langsomt i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I can help. I’ll cut the vegetables quickly." dialogue_id=party-preparations speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Jeg kan hjelpe. Jeg kutter grønnsakene raskt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thanks. Put the glasses carefully on the table." dialogue_id=party-preparations speaker_id=anna speaker_name="Anna"}
Takk. Legg glassene forsiktig på bordet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="All right. Am I speaking too loudly?" dialogue_id=party-preparations speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Greit. Snakker jeg for høyt?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, you’re speaking a little loudly. Nora is sleeping." dialogue_id=party-preparations speaker_id=anna speaker_name="Anna"}
Ja, du snakker litt høyt. Nora sover.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’ll work quietly now." dialogue_id=party-preparations speaker_id=erik speaker_name="Erik" character_id="erik"}
Jeg jobber stille nå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. You always help when we have a party." dialogue_id=party-preparations speaker_id=anna speaker_name="Anna"}
Flott. Du hjelper alltid når vi har fest.
:::

The practical problem is simple: Anna needs to say not only what people do, but also **how**, **how often**, and **when** they do it. Compare:

::: examples
- no: Erik kutter grønnsakene.
- en: Erik cuts the vegetables.
- no: Erik kutter grønnsakene raskt.
- en: Erik cuts the vegetables quickly.
:::

The second sentence gives more information about the action. The word **raskt** tells us how Erik cuts.

## What an adverb does {#what-an-adverb-does role=model objectives=obj-basic-adverbs-and-position-01}

An **adverb (adverb)** can describe an action or add information to a whole clause. An **adjective (adjektiv)** usually describes a person, thing, or other noun.

In the dialogue, these common adverbs have different jobs:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kommer ofte sent.
- en: I often arrive late.
:::

**Ofte** tells us how frequently Erik arrives late.

::: examples
- no: Jeg er ikke sen i dag.
- en: I am not late today.
:::

**Ikke** makes the statement negative.

::: examples
- no: Jeg arbeider langsomt.
- en: I work slowly.
:::

**Langsomt** tells us how Anna works.

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobber stille nå.
- en: I am working quietly now.
:::

**Stille** tells us how Erik works, while **nå** tells us when.

Useful common adverbs include:

- **ikke** — *not*
- **ofte** — *often*
- **alltid** — *always*
- **aldri** — *never*
- **nå** — *now*
- **her** — *here*
- **ute** — *outside*
- **sent** — *late*
- **fort** — *quickly*

These words already function as adverbs. You do not need to build them from adjectives.

::: examples
- no: Hun kommer aldri sent.
- en: She never arrives late.
- no: Vi spiser ofte ute.
- en: We often eat outside.
- no: Han bor her.
- en: He lives here.
- no: De arbeider ikke nå.
- en: They are not working now.
:::

**Activity request: anchor-adverb-noticing**

**Purpose:** Build recognition of common adverbs before asking the learner to produce them.

**Context:** Anna and Erik’s party preparations.

**Learner action:** Find the word in each selected dialogue sentence that expresses frequency, negation, manner, or time. Connect that word to its meaning in the scene.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner recognizes common adverbs and understands what information each one contributes. The scored evidence goal is adverb recognition, not word order production.

{{exercise: anchor_adverb_noticing}}

## Using an adjective to describe an action {#using-an-adjective-to-describe-an-action role=contrast objectives=obj-basic-adverbs-and-position-01}

Norwegian often uses a form related to an adjective to say how an action happens. This is **adverbial use of an adjective (adverbial bruk av adjektiv)**.

Compare a quality with an action:

::: examples
- no: Bilen er rask.
- en: The car is fast.
- no: Bilen kjører raskt.
- en: The car travels quickly.
:::

In the first sentence, **rask** describes the car. It is an adjective. In the second, **raskt** describes the action **kjører**. It is used adverbially.

A useful basic rule is:

::: {.rule}
To describe how an action happens, Norwegian commonly uses the neuter singular form of the adjective. This often means adding **-t**.
:::

Here are several aligned contrasts:

::: examples
- no: Erik er langsom.
- en: Erik is slow.
- no: Erik arbeider langsomt.
- en: Erik works slowly.
- no: Bilen er rask.
- en: The car is fast.
- no: Bilen kjører raskt.
- en: The car travels quickly.
- no: Musikken er høy.
- en: The music is loud.
- no: De spiller høyt.
- en: They play loudly.
- no: Stemmen er lav.
- en: The voice is low.
- no: Nora snakker lavt.
- en: Nora speaks quietly.
- no: Sangeren er god.
- en: The singer is good.
- no: Sangeren synger godt.
- en: The singer sings well.
:::

Notice the common adjective-adverb forms:

- **rask → raskt** — *fast → quickly*
- **langsom → langsomt** — *slow → slowly*
- **høy → høyt** — *loud/high → loudly*
- **lav → lavt** — *low → quietly/at low volume*
- **god → godt** — *good → well*

Some adjectives do not visibly gain **-t**. Adjectives ending in **-ig** commonly remain unchanged in this use:

::: examples
- no: Anna er forsiktig.
- en: Anna is careful.
- no: Anna setter glasset forsiktig på bordet.
- en: Anna puts the glass carefully on the table.
:::

The same happens with some other forms:

::: examples
- no: Barnet er rolig.
- en: The child is calm.
- no: Barnet sover rolig.
- en: The child sleeps calmly.
- no: Rommet er stille.
- en: The room is quiet.
- no: Erik arbeider stille.
- en: Erik works quietly.
:::

The form may look unchanged, but its job differs. In **Barnet er rolig**, the word describes the child. In **Barnet sover rolig**, it describes the sleeping.

**One form for every person**

An adverbial adjective does not change according to who performs the action:

::: examples
- no: Han løper raskt.
- en: He runs quickly.
- no: Hun løper raskt.
- en: She runs quickly.
- no: De løper raskt.
- en: They run quickly.
:::

The action is described in the same way each time.

**Activity request: quality-or-action-form**

**Purpose:** Establish the choice between an adjective describing a person or thing and an adjective used adverbially to describe an action.

**Context:** Short paired statements about people, vehicles, music, and household tasks.

**Learner action:** Complete each controlled sentence with the appropriate form of a supplied adjective. Each pair should contrast a sentence with **er** and a sentence describing an action.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner selects forms such as **rask** versus **raskt** and **god** versus **godt** according to meaning. Word position remains fixed in the sentence frames and is not scored here.

{{exercise: quality_or_action_form}}

**Activity request: unchanged-adverbial-form**

**Purpose:** Prevent the learner from assuming that every adjective used adverbially must visibly end in **-t**.

**Context:** Calm, careful, and quiet actions during the party preparations.

**Learner action:** Complete controlled sentence pairs using adjectives such as **rolig**, **forsiktig**, and **stille**, then indicate whether each word describes a person or thing or describes an action.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner recognizes adverbial use from meaning and sentence function even when the written form does not change.

{{exercise: unchanged_adverbial_form}}

## Position of frequency and negative adverbs {#position-of-frequency-and-negative-adverbs role=contrast objectives=obj-basic-adverbs-and-position-01}

In a simple neutral clause, **ikke**, **ofte**, **alltid**, and **aldri** normally come after the verb.

The basic pattern is:

::: {.rule}
subject + verb + **ikke/ofte/alltid/aldri** + remaining information
:::

::: examples
- no: Jeg kommer ofte sent.
- en: I often arrive late.
- no: Erik hjelper alltid Anna.
- en: Erik always helps Anna.
- no: Nora kommer aldri tidlig.
- en: Nora never arrives early.
- no: Vi spiller ikke musikk.
- en: We are not playing music.
:::

The same pattern works when the verb is **er**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er ikke sen.
- en: I am not late.
- no: Anna er alltid forsiktig.
- en: Anna is always careful.
:::

A common English-influenced mistake is to put the adverb before the verb:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg ofte kommer sent.
- en: Intended meaning: I often arrive late.
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kommer ofte sent.
- en: I often arrive late.
:::

Likewise:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Han ikke arbeider nå.
- en: Intended meaning: He is not working now.
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: Han arbeider ikke nå.
- en: He is not working now.
:::

Keep the compact rule in mind: in these simple clauses, put the frequency or negative adverb **after the verb**.

**Activity request: frequency-and-negation-position**

**Purpose:** Practise the neutral position of **ofte**, **alltid**, **aldri**, and **ikke**.

**Context:** Statements about Anna, Erik, and Nora’s ordinary habits and what they are or are not doing before the party.

**Learner action:** Insert one supplied adverb into each complete sentence frame without changing the other words.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner places the frequency or negative adverb after the verb. Adverb meaning is supplied by context; natural position is the primary scored evidence goal.

{{exercise: frequency_and_negation_position}}

**Activity request: simple-clause-position-repair**

**Purpose:** Correct the likely misconception that Norwegian normally places frequency and negative adverbs before the verb as English sometimes does.

**Context:** Brief messages written by a beginner helping with the party.

**Learner action:** Rewrite sentences in which **ikke**, **ofte**, **alltid**, or **aldri** has been placed before the verb.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner repairs the word order while preserving the original meaning. Only placement of the supplied adverb is scored.

{{exercise: simple_clause_position_repair}}

## Position of manner adverbs {#position-of-manner-adverbs role=model objectives=obj-basic-adverbs-and-position-01}

Adverbs that say **how** an action happens commonly come after the verb. If the verb has an object, a reliable neutral pattern is to put the manner adverb after that object.

Without an object:

::: examples
- no: Nora snakker lavt.
- en: Nora speaks quietly.
- no: Erik arbeider raskt.
- en: Erik works quickly.
- no: Barnet sover rolig.
- en: The child sleeps calmly.
:::

With an object:

::: examples
- no: Erik kutter grønnsakene raskt.
- en: Erik cuts the vegetables quickly.
- no: Anna åpner døra forsiktig.
- en: Anna opens the door carefully.
- no: Hun leser meldingen langsomt.
- en: She reads the message slowly.
:::

A place expression can follow the manner adverb:

::: examples
- no: Anna setter glassene forsiktig på bordet.
- en: Anna puts the glasses carefully on the table.
:::

A short time adverb can also occur later in the clause:

::: examples
- no: Erik arbeider stille nå.
- en: Erik is working quietly now.
- no: Vi spiser ute i dag.
- en: We are eating outside today.
:::

For this lesson, use these dependable patterns:

::: {.rule}
subject + verb + manner adverb
:::

::: {.rule}
subject + verb + object + manner adverb
:::

::: {.rule}
subject + verb + object + manner adverb + place
:::

**Combining two kinds of adverb**

A clause may contain both a frequency or negative adverb and a manner adverb. Their positions remain different:

::: examples
- no: Erik arbeider alltid raskt.
- en: Erik always works quickly.
- no: Nora snakker ikke høyt.
- en: Nora does not speak loudly.
- no: Anna setter alltid glassene forsiktig på bordet.
- en: Anna always puts the glasses carefully on the table.
:::

Here, **alltid** and **ikke** come after the verb. The manner words **raskt**, **høyt**, and **forsiktig** come later.

**Activity request: manner-adverb-position**

**Purpose:** Practise placing an adverb that describes how an action happens.

**Context:** Controlled descriptions of cooking, reading, driving, speaking, and moving objects.

**Learner action:** Add a supplied manner adverb to each sentence. In clauses with an object, place it in the neutral position after the object.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner distinguishes manner-adverb position from frequency and negation position. Adverb forms are supplied and are not scored.

{{exercise: manner_adverb_position}}

**Activity request: two-adverb-position-contrast**

**Purpose:** Make the positional contrast visible when one clause contains two adverbs.

**Context:** Actions during party preparation, including working, speaking, carrying, and setting the table.

**Learner action:** Complete sentence frames containing one supplied frequency or negative adverb and one supplied manner adverb. Place each in its natural neutral position.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner places the frequency or negative adverb after the verb and the manner adverb later in the clause. Primary scored evidence is the contrast between the two positions; adjective formation is not required.

{{exercise: two_adverb_position_contrast}}

## Do you need an adjective or an adverb? {#do-you-need-an-adjective-or-an-adverb role=contrast objectives=obj-basic-adverbs-and-position-01}

The verb **er** often links a person or thing to an adjective:

::: examples
- no: Erik er rask.
- en: Erik is fast.
- no: Anna er forsiktig.
- en: Anna is careful.
- no: Musikken er høy.
- en: The music is loud.
:::

Do not automatically add **-t** merely because the word comes after a verb. Ask what the word describes.

If it describes the subject after **er**, use the adjective:

::: examples
- no: Bilen er rask.
- en: The car is fast.
:::

If it describes an action, use the adverbial form:

::: examples
- no: Bilen kjører raskt.
- en: The car travels quickly.
:::

Compare these common errors:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Bilen kjører rask.
- en: Intended meaning: The car travels quickly.
- no: Bilen kjører raskt.
- en: The car travels quickly.
- no: ✗ Bilen er raskt.
- en: Intended meaning: The car is fast.
- no: Bilen er rask.
- en: The car is fast.
:::

The key question is not simply “Does the word follow a verb?” Instead ask:

::: {.rule}
Does it describe a person or thing, or does it describe how an action happens?
:::

**Activity request: adjective-adverb-misconception-repair**

**Purpose:** Correct confusion between a subject description after **er** and an adverb describing an action.

**Context:** Paired statements about a fast car, a careful person, loud music, and someone performing an action well.

**Learner action:** Repair only the adjective or adverb form in each sentence while keeping the rest of the sentence unchanged.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner identifies what the target word describes and chooses the corresponding form. Position is already correct and is not scored.

{{exercise: adjective_adverb_misconception_repair}}

## Transfer: helping at a school event {#transfer-helping-at-a-school-event role=recap objectives=obj-basic-adverbs-and-position-01}

Imagine a changed everyday situation. Mina and Jonas are arranging chairs and preparing drinks before a school event. The vocabulary changes, but the grammar job stays the same.

Useful model sentences are:

::: examples
- no: Mina arbeider raskt.
- en: Mina works quickly.
- no: Jonas bærer stolene forsiktig.
- en: Jonas carries the chairs carefully.
- no: De snakker ikke høyt.
- en: They do not speak loudly.
- no: Jonas kommer ofte tidlig.
- en: Jonas often arrives early.
- no: Mina er rolig, og hun arbeider rolig.
- en: Mina is calm, and she works calmly.
:::

The final pair shows why meaning matters even when the form looks identical. The first **rolig** describes Mina. The second describes how she works.

**Activity request: school-event-adverb-form-transfer**

**Purpose:** Transfer adjective-adverb formation to a new situation.

**Context:** Mina and Jonas preparing a room for a school event.

**Learner action:** Complete controlled contrasts between descriptions of the people or objects and descriptions of their actions.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner chooses adjective or adverbial form from meaning. Sentence position is fixed and unscored.

{{exercise: school_event_adverb_form_transfer}}

**Activity request: school-event-adverb-position-transfer**

**Purpose:** Transfer adverb placement beyond the original party scene.

**Context:** Simple statements about arriving, carrying chairs, making drinks, speaking, and working.

**Learner action:** Place supplied common and manner adverbs into sentence frames. All manner forms are already correct.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner naturally places **ikke**, **ofte**, **alltid**, or **aldri** after the verb and places manner adverbs after the verb or object as appropriate.

## Short retrieval review {#short-retrieval-review role=recap objectives=obj-basic-adverbs-and-position-01}

Remember three points:

1. Common adverbs such as **ikke**, **ofte**, **alltid**, and **aldri** normally follow the verb in simple neutral clauses.
2. An adjective used to describe an action often takes the neuter form, commonly with **-t**: **rask → raskt**.
3. A manner adverb usually comes after the verb, or after the object when there is one.

**Activity request: final-position-retrieval**

**Purpose:** End with brief retrieval of the main placement patterns.

**Context:** Three new controlled clauses about an ordinary morning routine.

**Learner action:** Insert supplied, correctly formed adverbs from memory: one frequency adverb, one negative adverb, and one manner adverb.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner recalls natural adverb position in simple Norwegian clauses. Adverb formation and vocabulary recognition provide context but are not separately scored.

{{exercise: final_position_retrieval}}
