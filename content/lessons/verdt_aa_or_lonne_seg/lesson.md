## A weekend decision {#a-weekend-decision role=orient objectives=obj-verdt-aa-or-lonne-seg-01}

Jonas and Selma are planning a weekend trip from Oslo to Røros. The train is comfortable but expensive, while the bus is cheaper and takes longer. Selma also wants to visit a small museum outside the town centre.

::: {.reading translation="The train tickets are quite expensive. Do you think it is worth taking the train?" dialogue_id="a-weekend-decision" speaker_id="selma" speaker_name="Selma" character_id="selma"}
Togbillettene er ganske dyre. Tror du det er verdt å ta toget?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, perhaps. The view is beautiful, and we can relax along the way." dialogue_id="a-weekend-decision" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, kanskje. Utsikten er fin, og vi kan slappe av underveis.
:::

::: {.reading translation="But it pays to book today. The prices will probably be higher tomorrow." dialogue_id="a-weekend-decision" speaker_id="selma" speaker_name="Selma" character_id="selma"}
Men det lønner seg å bestille i dag. Prisene blir sikkert høyere i morgen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Agreed. What about the museum on Sunday? It is some distance outside the town centre." dialogue_id="a-weekend-decision" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Enig. Hva med museet på søndag? Det ligger et stykke utenfor sentrum.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I have heard that it is small but very interesting. Apparently, it is worth visiting." dialogue_id="a-weekend-decision" speaker_id="selma" speaker_name="Selma" character_id="selma"}
Jeg har hørt at det er lite, men veldig interessant. Det er visst verdt å besøke.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then it may pay to buy a weekend bus pass. We are going to travel several times, after all." dialogue_id="a-weekend-decision" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Da kan det lønne seg å kjøpe et helgekort til bussen. Vi skal jo reise flere ganger.
:::

Two expressions do similar work here, but they point to different kinds of value:

- **verdt å** gives a general evaluation: an experience, action, or thing deserves the time, effort, attention, or cost.
- **lønne seg** emphasizes a practical payoff: an action produces an advantage, saves something, or gives a useful result.

Jonas and Selma think the train and museum may be good experiences. They also calculate when to book and which ticket will give them a practical advantage.

## General worth: verdt å {#general-worth-verdt-a role=model objectives=obj-verdt-aa-or-lonne-seg-01}

Use **verdt å + infinitive** when you evaluate whether doing something is worthwhile in a broad sense.

::: examples
- no: Det er verdt å ta toget.
- en: It is worth taking the train.
- no: Museet er verdt å besøke.
- en: The museum is worth visiting.
- no: Denne boka er verdt å lese.
- en: This book is worth reading.
- no: Det er verdt å vente litt.
- en: It is worth waiting a little.
:::

The value may come from enjoyment, quality, interest, importance, or a meaningful experience. It does not have to involve a measurable benefit.

Compare two common patterns:

::: examples
- no: Det er verdt å se utstillingen.
- en: It is worth seeing the exhibition.
- no: Utstillingen er verdt å se.
- en: The exhibition is worth seeing.
:::

In the first pattern, **det** introduces an evaluation of an action. In the second, the thing being evaluated comes first. Both patterns use **å** before the infinitive.

You can also make the evaluation negative:

::: examples
- no: Det er ikke verdt å krangle om det.
- en: It is not worth arguing about it.
- no: Filmen er ikke verdt å se.
- en: The film is not worth seeing.
:::

Here, the speaker judges that the action does not deserve the required time, effort, or attention.

**Checkpoint: identify broad evaluations**

Read several short statements about travel, books, films, events, and everyday decisions. Mark the statements that evaluate general quality, interest, or experience rather than a concrete advantage.

This activity belongs here because the first step is recognizing the meaning of **verdt å** before producing it. A successful response would consistently identify evaluations such as whether a museum deserves a visit or whether a film deserves the viewer’s time.

{{exercise: identify_broad_evaluations}}

## Practical payoff: lønne seg {#practical-payoff-lonne-seg role=model objectives=obj-verdt-aa-or-lonne-seg-01}

Use **lønne seg** when an action gives a practical benefit. The benefit is often financial, but it may also involve saving time, avoiding difficulty, or getting a better result.

::: examples
- no: Det lønner seg å bestille tidlig.
- en: It pays to book early.
- no: Det lønner seg å sammenligne prisene.
- en: It pays to compare the prices.
- no: Det lønner seg å ta med matpakke.
- en: It is worthwhile to bring a packed lunch.
- no: Det lønner seg å spørre før du kjøper billetten.
- en: It pays to ask before you buy the ticket.
:::

The Norwegian expression contains the reflexive pronoun **seg**. Keep the whole expression together: **lønne seg**.

A very common frame is:

::: examples
- no: Det lønner seg å + infinitive.
- en: It pays to + infinitive.
:::

You can add **kan** when the benefit depends on the situation:

::: examples
- no: Det kan lønne seg å kjøpe et helgekort.
- en: It may pay to buy a weekend pass.
- no: Det kan lønne seg å vente på et bedre tilbud.
- en: It may be worthwhile to wait for a better offer.
:::

The negative form means that an action does not provide enough practical benefit:

::: examples
- no: Det lønner seg ikke å ta taxi når bussen går hvert tiende minutt.
- en: It does not pay to take a taxi when the bus runs every ten minutes.
- no: Det lønner seg ikke å kjøpe enkeltbilletter hvis du skal reise mye.
- en: It does not pay to buy single tickets if you are going to travel a lot.
:::

**Checkpoint: find the practical advantage**

For each short situation, state what would be gained or saved by the suggested action. Use contexts such as booking early, comparing prices, taking a direct bus, or buying several items together.

This activity makes the practical meaning explicit. A successful response would identify a concrete payoff—money saved, less time used, fewer problems, or a better result—rather than merely saying that the action is pleasant or interesting.

{{exercise: find_practical_advantage}}

## The central contrast {#central-contrast role=contrast objectives=obj-verdt-aa-or-lonne-seg-01}

Both expressions can correspond to English phrases such as *worth it*, *worthwhile*, or *it pays to*. The Norwegian choice depends on the speaker’s intended reason.

**Experience versus saving money**

::: examples
- no: Det er verdt å ta toget fordi utsikten er fantastisk.
- en: It is worth taking the train because the view is fantastic.
- no: Det lønner seg å ta toget fordi barn reiser gratis i helgene.
- en: It pays to take the train because children travel free at weekends.
:::

The action is the same, but the reason changes. A beautiful view supports a broad evaluation with **verdt å**. Free travel creates a practical benefit, so **lønner seg** is more natural.

**Quality versus useful result**

::: examples
- no: Kurset er verdt å ta fordi læreren er svært dyktig.
- en: The course is worth taking because the teacher is highly skilled.
- no: Det lønner seg å ta kurset før du søker på jobben.
- en: It pays to take the course before you apply for the job.
:::

The first sentence evaluates the course’s quality. The second presents the course as a useful step toward a result.

**Interest versus efficiency**

::: examples
- no: Det er verdt å gå gjennom gamlebyen.
- en: It is worth walking through the old town.
- no: Det lønner seg å gå gjennom gamlebyen når hovedveien er stengt.
- en: It pays to walk through the old town when the main road is closed.
:::

In the first sentence, the walk is worth experiencing. In the second, the route solves a practical problem.

**Personal significance versus calculation**

::: examples
- no: Det er verdt å reise langt for å møte gamle venner.
- en: It is worth travelling a long way to meet old friends.
- no: Det lønner seg å kjøpe billettene sammen.
- en: It pays to buy the tickets together.
:::

Meeting friends has personal value. Buying tickets together offers a calculable advantage.

## When either expression is possible {#either-expression role=contrast objectives=obj-verdt-aa-or-lonne-seg-01}

Sometimes both forms are grammatically possible, but they frame the action differently.

::: examples
- no: Det er verdt å lære noen norske uttrykk før reisen.
- en: It is worth learning some Norwegian expressions before the trip.
:::

This suggests that learning the expressions is valuable or rewarding in general.

::: examples
- no: Det lønner seg å lære noen norske uttrykk før reisen.
- en: It pays to learn some Norwegian expressions before the trip.
:::

This emphasizes a practical result: communication may become easier, and the traveller may avoid misunderstandings.

Another pair:

::: examples
- no: Det er verdt å stå opp tidlig for å se soloppgangen.
- en: It is worth getting up early to see the sunrise.
:::

The experience deserves the effort.

::: examples
- no: Det lønner seg å stå opp tidlig hvis du vil unngå kø.
- en: It pays to get up early if you want to avoid the queue.
:::

Getting up early produces a concrete advantage.

The distinction is therefore not always about the action itself. It is about how the speaker presents the action.

**Checkpoint: choose according to the reason**

Complete paired sentences in which the action remains the same but the reason changes. One reason should concern quality, interest, enjoyment, or personal importance; the other should concern cost, time, convenience, or results.

This controlled contrast belongs here because it prevents learners from choosing only from the verb or topic. A successful response would use **verdt å** and **lønne seg** according to the stated reason.

## Useful sentence frames {#useful-sentence-frames role=model objectives=obj-verdt-aa-or-lonne-seg-01}

These frames cover many everyday situations.

**Evaluating an experience or thing**

::: examples
- no: Det er verdt å prøve.
- en: It is worth trying.
- no: Stedet er verdt å besøke.
- en: The place is worth visiting.
- no: Boka er ikke verdt å kjøpe.
- en: The book is not worth buying.
- no: Er det verdt å vente?
- en: Is it worth waiting?
:::

**Recommending a practical action**

::: examples
- no: Det lønner seg å planlegge på forhånd.
- en: It pays to plan in advance.
- no: Det kan lønne seg å ringe først.
- en: It may pay to call first.
- no: Det lønner seg ikke å kjøpe den dyreste modellen.
- en: It does not pay to buy the most expensive model.
- no: Lønner det seg å bestille på nettet?
- en: Does it pay to book online?
:::

Notice the question forms:

::: examples
- no: Er det verdt å ta en omvei?
- en: Is it worth taking a detour?
- no: Lønner det seg å ta en omvei?
- en: Does it pay to take a detour?
:::

The first asks whether the detour deserves the effort, perhaps because the route is beautiful. The second asks whether the detour brings a practical advantage, perhaps because it avoids traffic.

**Checkpoint: build complete frames**

Given brief prompts containing an action and a reason, write complete sentences with either **det er verdt å** or **det lønner seg å**. Include affirmative, negative, and question forms.

This activity moves from recognition to controlled production. A successful response would preserve the complete expression, place **å** before the infinitive, and choose the frame that matches the kind of value.

{{exercise: build_complete_frames}}

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-verdt-aa-or-lonne-seg-01}

**Do not omit å after verdt**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Det er verdt besøke museet.
- en: ✗ It is worth visit the museum.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Det er verdt å besøke museet.
- en: It is worth visiting the museum.
:::

The fixed frame is **verdt å + infinitive**.

**Do not omit seg from lønne seg**

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Det lønner å bestille tidlig.
- en: ✗ It pays to book early.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Det lønner seg å bestille tidlig.
- en: It pays to book early.
:::

The reflexive pronoun is part of the expression.

**Do not use lønne seg only for direct profit**

**Lønne seg** often concerns money, but its practical meaning is wider.

::: examples
- no: Det lønner seg å sjekke værmeldingen før turen.
- en: It pays to check the weather forecast before the trip.
:::

The speaker may not earn money. The payoff is better preparation and fewer problems.

::: examples
- no: Det lønner seg å skrive ned adressen.
- en: It pays to write down the address.
:::

The practical benefit is avoiding confusion later.

**Do not treat the expressions as automatic synonyms**

::: examples
- no: Konserten var fantastisk. Det var verdt å vente.
- en: The concert was fantastic. It was worth waiting.
- no: Vi fikk billetter til halv pris. Det lønte seg å vente.
- en: We got tickets at half price. It paid to wait.
:::

The concert made the wait worthwhile as an experience.

The wait produced a concrete benefit.

The surrounding information determines the intended meaning.

**Checkpoint: repair meaning and form**

Revise short sentences containing either a missing element or an expression that conflicts with the reason given. Keep the original situation but make the recommendation natural.

This activity combines form and meaning after both have been introduced. A successful response would restore **å** or **seg** where necessary and distinguish general worth from practical payoff.

{{exercise: repair_meaning_and_form}}

## Back to Jonas and Selma {#back-to-jonas-and-selma role=recap objectives=obj-verdt-aa-or-lonne-seg-01}

Later that evening, Jonas checks the transport website.

::: {.reading translation="I found a cheaper train. It leaves at six in the morning." dialogue_id="back-to-jonas-and-selma" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg fant et billigere tog. Det går klokka seks om morgenen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That early? Does it really pay off?" dialogue_id="back-to-jonas-and-selma" speaker_id="selma" speaker_name="Selma" character_id="selma"}
Så tidlig? Lønner det seg virkelig?
:::

::: {.reading translation="We save almost eight hundred kroner, and we get the whole afternoon in Røros." dialogue_id="back-to-jonas-and-selma" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Vi sparer nesten åtte hundre kroner, og vi får hele ettermiddagen i Røros.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then it probably pays to get up early." dialogue_id="back-to-jonas-and-selma" speaker_id="selma" speaker_name="Selma" character_id="selma"}
Da lønner det seg nok å stå opp tidlig.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I also found a guided tour in an old mine. It is a little expensive." dialogue_id="back-to-jonas-and-selma" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg fant også en guidet tur i en gammel gruve. Den er litt dyr.
:::

::: {.reading translation="My sister took that tour last year. She said that it was definitely worth taking." dialogue_id="back-to-jonas-and-selma" speaker_id="selma" speaker_name="Selma" character_id="selma"}
Søsteren min tok den turen i fjor. Hun sa at den absolutt var verdt å ta.
:::

::: {.reading translation="All right. We will save money on the train, so it is worth spending a little more on the mine tour." dialogue_id="back-to-jonas-and-selma" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Greit. Vi sparer penger på toget, så det er verdt å bruke litt mer på gruveturen.
:::

The dialogue now contains both sides of the distinction. Getting up early has a practical payoff: they save money and gain time. The mine tour is evaluated as a worthwhile experience despite its price.

## Guided application {#guided-application role=recap objectives=obj-verdt-aa-or-lonne-seg-01}

Continue Jonas and Selma’s planning in writing. Respond to short developments such as a discount on advance tickets, an excellent but distant restaurant, a slow route with beautiful scenery, or a travel card that covers several journeys. Use one target expression in each response and include a brief reason.

This activity returns to the chapter’s main context while introducing new decisions. A successful response would connect enjoyable or meaningful experiences with **verdt å**, and measurable advantages with **lønne seg**.

{{exercise: guided_application_decisions}}

Next, revise pairs of recommendations so that the same action receives two different interpretations. For example, present an early departure once as a way to avoid traffic and once as a chance to see the sunrise. Keep the action constant and change the target expression to match the reason.

{{exercise: revise_dual_interpretations}}

Success here would demonstrate the core outcome: choosing according to practical payoff versus broader worth, not according to the action alone.

Without looking back, write one sentence recommending an experience with **verdt å**, one sentence recommending a practical action with **lønne seg**, and one question that could take either expression depending on the reason. Add a short reason to each sentence so the intended distinction is clear.
