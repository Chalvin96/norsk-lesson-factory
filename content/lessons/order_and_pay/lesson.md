## At the café {#at-the-cafe role=model objectives=obj-order-and-pay-01}

Sara and Emil have stopped at a café after work. They need to choose from the menu, say how many items they want, check the total, and pay.

Sara and Emil complete the transaction in four clear stages. They choose their items, specify quantities and options, ask about the total and payment method, and then close the exchange politely.

## Choosing an item {#choosing-an-item role=model objectives=obj-order-and-pay-01}

Three useful ways to order are:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil gjerne ha en kaffe.
- en: I’d like a coffee.
:::

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg få en kaffe?
- en: Can I have a coffee?
:::

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar en kaffe.
- en: I’ll have a coffee.
:::

Jeg vil gjerne ha is polite and useful in almost any café or shop. Gjerne helps make the request sound friendly.

Kan jeg få literally means can I get. It is a natural way to ask for something in Norwegian.

Jeg tar is common when you are choosing from a menu or from several visible items. Here, tar means will have rather than physically take.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil ha en kaffe.
- en: I want a coffee.
:::

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil gjerne ha en kaffe.
- en: I’d like a coffee.
:::

Both are grammatical, but the second version normally sounds more courteous in a transaction.

You can use the same patterns with many items:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil gjerne ha et rundstykke med ost.
- en: I’d like a bread roll with cheese.
:::

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg få en kopp te?
- en: Can I have a cup of tea?
:::

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar en flaske vann.
- en: I’ll have a bottle of water.
:::

Practice request: making the first request

Return to Sara’s first turn. Replace her coffee and cinnamon bun with two other café items, but keep Jeg vil gjerne ha. Then make the same request again using Kan jeg få.

This activity belongs here because the first step in the transaction is making a clear choice. A successful response names the desired items and includes a complete ordering expression rather than only saying the item names.

## Choosing between options {#choosing-between-options role=contrast objectives=obj-order-and-pay-01}

The server may ask you to choose a size, type, or addition.

::: examples
- no: Vil du ha liten eller stor kaffe?
- en: Would you like a small or large coffee?
:::

::: examples
- no: Liten, takk.
- en: Small, please.
:::

::: examples
- no: Vil du ha kaffe eller te?
- en: Would you like coffee or tea?
:::

::: examples
- no: Te, takk.
- en: Tea, please.
:::

::: examples
- no: Vil du ha melk i kaffen?
- en: Would you like milk in the coffee?
:::

::: examples
- no: Ja takk.
- en: Yes, please.
:::

::: examples
- no: Nei takk.
- en: No, thank you.
:::

::: examples
- no: Med eller uten kullsyre?
- en: With or without carbonation?
:::

::: examples
- no: Uten kullsyre, takk.
- en: Still, please.
:::

A short answer is natural when the choice is already clear from the question. Adding takk keeps it polite.

You can also include the option in your original order:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil gjerne ha en stor kaffe med melk.
- en: I’d like a large coffee with milk.
:::

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg få en flaske vann uten kullsyre?
- en: Can I have a bottle of still water?
:::

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar et rundstykke med ost.
- en: I’ll have a bread roll with cheese.
:::

Notice the useful contrast:

::: examples
- no: med melk
- en: with milk
:::

::: examples
- no: uten melk
- en: without milk
:::

Practice request: responding to a choice

Imagine that Nora asks Sara to choose between a small and a large drink, and then between milk and no milk. Give Sara’s two short responses. Next, combine both choices into one complete order.

This controlled step follows the first request because real orders often require clarification. A successful response selects one option unambiguously and uses takk or a complete ordering phrase naturally.

{{exercise: responding_to_choice}}

## Saying how many {#saying-how-many role=model objectives=obj-order-and-pay-01}

Use a number before the item when you need more than one. The one-item form is singular (entall); forms used for several items are plural (flertall).

::: examples
- no: en kanelbolle
- en: one cinnamon bun
:::

::: examples
- no: to kanelboller
- en: two cinnamon buns
:::

::: examples
- no: et rundstykke
- en: one bread roll
:::

::: examples
- no: tre rundstykker
- en: three bread rolls
:::

::: examples
- no: en flaske vann
- en: one bottle of water
:::

::: examples
- no: to flasker vann
- en: two bottles of water
:::

The plural ending varies, so it is useful to learn common menu items in practical pairs: en bolle–to boller, et rundstykke–to rundstykker, en flaske–to flasker.

In a complete order:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil gjerne ha to kanelboller.
- en: I’d like two cinnamon buns.
:::

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg få tre rundstykker med ost?
- en: Can I have three bread rolls with cheese?
:::

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar en kaffe og to flasker vann.
- en: I’ll have one coffee and two bottles of water.
:::

When speaking quickly, café staff and customers may sometimes use shortened menu language. As a learner, a complete form such as to kopper kaffe or to kaffer makes the quantity clear.

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg få to kopper kaffe?
- en: Can I have two cups of coffee?
:::

::: examples
- no: Vi tar to kaffer.
- en: We’ll have two coffees.
:::

Common quantity mistake

Do not leave a singular item unchanged after a number when its normal plural form is required.

Instead of:

::: examples
- no: ✗ to kanelbolle
- en: two cinnamon bun
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: to kanelboller
- en: two cinnamon buns
:::

Practice request: changing quantities

Start with Emil’s order: «Jeg tar en kaffe og to flasker vann.» Change it to two coffees and one bottle of water. Adjust the item forms and say the complete order using «Jeg tar». You may name either item first.

This activity keeps the original café context while focusing attention on quantity. A successful response makes both the number and the noun form clear.

{{exercise: changing_quantities}}

## Checking the price {#checking-the-price role=contrast objectives=obj-order-and-pay-01}

To ask about one named item, use Hva koster …?

::: examples
- no: Hva koster kaffen?
- en: How much does the coffee cost?
:::

::: examples
- no: Hva koster et rundstykke med ost?
- en: How much does a bread roll with cheese cost?
:::

::: examples
- no: Hva koster denne?
- en: How much does this one cost?
:::

To ask about the whole order, use:

::: examples
- no: Hvor mye koster det?
- en: How much does it cost?
:::

::: examples
- no: Hvor mye koster det til sammen?
- en: How much does it cost altogether?
:::

The server often gives the total with Det blir …, meaning that comes to …

::: examples
- no: Det blir 85 kroner.
- en: That comes to 85 kroner.
:::

::: examples
- no: Det blir 124 kroner til sammen.
- en: That comes to 124 kroner altogether.
:::

Hva blir det? can also be used by a customer asking for the total.

::: examples
- no: Hva blir det til sammen?
- en: What does that come to altogether?
:::

For an A1 transaction, you do not need a long sentence. A clear price question is enough.

Practice request: item price or total

Imagine that Sara first wants to know the price of one cinnamon bun. After Emil adds his food, she asks for the total. Form both questions.

This contrast belongs here because the customer may check either one item or the complete purchase. A successful response uses the named item in the first question and det or til sammen for the whole order.

{{exercise: item_price_or_total}}

## Checking how to pay {#checking-how-to-pay role=model objectives=obj-order-and-pay-01}

The most useful payment question is:

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg betale med kort?
- en: Can I pay by card?
:::

For cash, use kontant:

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg betale kontant?
- en: Can I pay in cash?
:::

The server may ask:

::: examples
- no: Vil du betale kontant eller med kort?
- en: Would you like to pay in cash or by card?
:::

A short response is enough:

::: examples
- no: Med kort, takk.
- en: By card, please.
:::

::: examples
- no: Kontant, takk.
- en: In cash, please.
:::

If several people are together, they may ask whether they can pay together or separately:

::: examples
- no: Kan vi betale sammen?
- en: Can we pay together?
:::

::: examples
- no: Vi betaler sammen.
- en: We’re paying together.
:::

::: examples
- no: Kan vi betale hver for oss?
- en: Can we pay separately?
:::

These questions still serve the same immediate goal: completing payment for the order.

Common payment mistake

English uses by card, but Norwegian uses med kort, literally with card.

Use:

::: examples
- no: Jeg betaler med kort.
- en: I’m paying by card.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: Jeg betaler av kort.
- en: Incorrect: I’m paying of card.
:::

Also avoid copying English can I have with ha:

::: examples
- no: Kan jeg få en kaffe?
- en: Can I have a coffee?
:::

Kan jeg få is the natural request. Kan jeg ha usually asks whether you are allowed or able to keep or possess something, not whether a server can give it to you.

Practice request: confirming payment

In the original dialogue, change the situation so Nora asks how Emil wants to pay. Give Emil a short answer, then expand it into a complete sentence. After that, have him ask whether they can pay together.

This activity places payment language after the total, where it normally occurs. A successful response names the payment method with med or uses the appropriate question for the shared bill.

{{exercise: confirming_payment}}

## Closing the transaction {#closing-the-transaction role=model objectives=obj-order-and-pay-01}

A transaction needs a clear ending. When the server asks whether you want anything else, you can say:

::: examples
- no: Nei takk, det var alt.
- en: No, thank you, that was everything.
:::

::: examples
- no: Ikke noe mer, takk.
- en: Nothing else, thank you.
:::

The server may offer a receipt:

::: examples
- no: Vil du ha kvitteringen?
- en: Would you like the receipt?
:::

::: examples
- no: Ja takk.
- en: Yes, please.
:::

::: examples
- no: Nei takk.
- en: No, thank you.
:::

Then close with a brief thanks and farewell:

::: examples
- no: Takk skal du ha.
- en: Thank you very much.
:::

::: examples
- no: Takk, ha det!
- en: Thanks, bye!
:::

::: examples
- no: Ha en fin dag!
- en: Have a nice day!
:::

Vær så god often accompanies the item, card terminal, change, or receipt.

::: examples
- no: Vær så god. Her er kaffen.
- en: Here you are. Here is the coffee.
:::

::: examples
- no: Takk.
- en: Thank you.
:::

::: examples
- no: Bare hyggelig.
- en: You’re welcome.
:::

## Guided transaction: connect the stages {#guided-transaction role=recap objectives=obj-order-and-pay-01}

Before creating a whole exchange from scratch, practise the customer’s lines in a new café situation. The server’s lines give you the next turn, so you can focus on keeping the order, choice, total, payment, and closing in the right sequence.

{{exercise: guided_transaction}}

## Integrated practice: a complete transaction {#integrated-practice role=recap objectives=obj-order-and-pay-01}

Create a short café exchange between a customer and a server. Keep it tightly guided by the sequence from Sara and Emil’s visit:

- The customer orders a drink and a food item.
- The customer makes one choice about size, type, or an addition.
- The order includes a clear quantity.
- The customer asks for the total.
- The customer checks or states the payment method.
- The speakers close the transaction politely.

This final practice combines the parts in their natural order. A successful exchange lets the server understand exactly what the customer wants, how many items are needed, and how payment will be made. It also ends clearly rather than stopping after the price.

{{exercise: integrated_transaction}}
