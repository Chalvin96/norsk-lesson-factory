## At a quiz night {#at-a-quiz-night role=orient objectives=obj-ordinal-numbers-01}

Emil has arrived at a neighborhood quiz night. Mari is organizing the teams and showing everyone where to sit.

**Mari:**

::: {.reading translation="Hi, Emil! Is this the first time you have taken part in the quiz?" dialogue_id="quiz-night-opening" speaker_id="mari" speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Hei, Emil! Er dette første gang du er med på quizen?
:::

**Emil:**

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Which table should my team sit at?" dialogue_id="quiz-night-opening" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ja. Hvilket bord skal laget mitt sitte ved?
:::

**Mari:**

::: {.reading translation="You should sit at the third table, by the window." dialogue_id="quiz-night-opening" speaker_id="mari" speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Dere skal sitte ved det tredje bordet, ved vinduet.
:::

**Emil:**

::: {.reading translation="Is this the first round?" dialogue_id="quiz-night-opening" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Er dette den første runden?
:::

**Mari:**

::: {.reading translation="Yes. The second round begins at eight, and the third begins after the break." dialogue_id="quiz-night-opening" speaker_id="mari" speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Ja. Den andre runden begynner klokka åtte, og den tredje begynner etter pausen.
:::

**Emil:**

::: {.reading translation="Where is our team placed now?" dialogue_id="quiz-night-opening" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Hvor ligger laget vårt nå?
:::

**Mari:**

::: {.reading translation="You are in second place. The team at the first table is leading." dialogue_id="quiz-night-opening" speaker_id="mari" speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Dere ligger på andreplass. Laget ved det første bordet leder.
:::

**Emil:**

::: {.reading translation="Not bad! Perhaps we will be number one after the second round." dialogue_id="quiz-night-opening" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Ikke verst! Kanskje vi blir nummer én etter andre runde.
:::

Mari uses numbers to describe order and position. The quiz has a first, second, and third round. Emil’s team sits at the third table and is currently in second place. These numbers do not tell us how many rounds, tables, or teams there are. They tell us where something comes in an order.

## Showing order rather than quantity {#showing-order-rather-than-quantity role=model objectives=obj-ordinal-numbers-01}

Numbers such as one, two, and three tell us quantity or identify a number. They are called cardinal numbers (grunntall):

::: examples
- no: Vi har tre runder.
- en: We have three rounds.
- no: Laget har fire personer.
- en: The team has four people.
:::

Numbers such as first, second, and third show order, rank, or position. They are called ordinal numbers (ordenstall):

::: examples
- no: Dette er den tredje runden.
- en: This is the third round.
- no: Laget ligger på andreplass.
- en: The team is in second place.
:::

Compare the meaning:

**Quantity or number**

::: examples
- no: Vi har tre bord.
- en: We have three tables.
- no: Det er to runder.
- en: There are two rounds.
- no: Hun har nummer fire.
- en: She has number four.
:::

**Order or position**

::: examples
- no: Vi sitter ved det tredje bordet.
- en: We are sitting at the third table.
- no: Vi spiller den andre runden.
- en: We are playing the second round.
- no: Hun er den fjerde deltakeren.
- en: She is the fourth participant.
:::

The compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use a cardinal number to say how many or to give a number. Use an ordinal number to show a place in an order.
:::

**Checkpoint request: notice-order-in-the-quiz**

The learner should return to Mari and Emil’s dialogue and identify what each ordinal number places in order: a visit, a table, a round, or a team’s rank. This belongs here because the learner has met the forms in a meaningful scene but has not yet had to produce them. A successful response would demonstrate that the learner understands the order or position expressed by each form, rather than merely recognizing a number word.

{{exercise: notice-order-in-the-quiz}}

## Common ordinal numbers {#common-ordinal-numbers role=model objectives=obj-ordinal-numbers-01}

The most useful forms must be learned as complete words. Several are not formed by simply adding an ending to the cardinal number.

**Number — Cardinal number — Ordinal number**

- 1 — én / ett — første
- 2 — to — andre
- 3 — tre — tredje
- 4 — fire — fjerde
- 5 — fem — femte
- 6 — seks — sjette
- 7 — sju — sjuende
- 8 — åtte — åttende
- 9 — ni — niende
- 10 — ti — tiende
- 11 — elleve — ellevte
- 12 — tolv — tolvte

The first four forms are especially important:

::: examples
- no: første
- en: first
- no: andre
- en: second
- no: tredje
- en: third
- no: fjerde
- en: fourth
:::

Notice that *andre* means *second* when it shows order:

::: examples
- no: Den andre bussen går til sentrum.
- en: The second bus goes to the city center.
:::

Some later ordinal numbers are easier to connect with their cardinal forms:

::: examples
- no: fem – femte
- en: five – fifth
- no: ni – niende
- en: nine – ninth
- no: tolv – tolvte
- en: twelve – twelfth
:::

However, the endings vary, and some spelling changes occur. At this level, it is safer to learn each common ordinal as a word rather than trying to create every form from one universal ending.

**Checkpoint request: retrieve-common-ordinal-forms**

The learner should produce the ordinal forms corresponding to familiar cardinal numbers from one to twelve. The activity should begin with the high-frequency irregular forms and then move to later forms such as *femte*, *åttende*, and *tolvte*. Its primary scored goal is accurate retrieval of common ordinal words. A successful response would demonstrate that the learner can recall the forms without treating them as ordinary cardinal numbers.

{{exercise: retrieve-common-ordinal-forms}}

## Ordinal numbers before nouns {#ordinal-numbers-before-nouns role=model objectives=obj-ordinal-numbers-01}

An ordinal number often comes before the thing being placed in order:

::: examples
- no: første runde
- en: first round
- no: andre etasje
- en: second floor
- no: tredje bord
- en: third table
- no: fjerde dag
- en: fourth day
:::

The ordinal form does not change for gender:

::: examples
- no: den første dagen
- en: the first day
- no: det første møtet
- en: the first meeting
- no: den første uka
- en: the first week
:::

The word remains *første*. In the same way, *andre*, *tredje*, and the other ordinal forms remain unchanged.

When a specific item in a sequence is meant, Norwegian commonly uses *den* or *det* before the ordinal and a definite noun:

::: examples
- no: den andre runden
- en: the second round
- no: det tredje spørsmålet
- en: the third question
- no: den fjerde deltakeren
- en: the fourth participant
:::

You will also meet shorter expressions without *den* or *det*, especially in fixed labels and after prepositions:

::: examples
- no: i første etasje
- en: on the first floor
- no: på tredje rad
- en: in the third row
- no: etter andre runde
- en: after the second round
:::

For this lesson, focus on choosing and using the correct ordinal word. The surrounding phrase shows what is being ordered.

## A likely mistake {#a-likely-mistake role=contrast objectives=obj-ordinal-numbers-01}

Do not use a cardinal number simply because English contains the same basic number idea.

Incorrect for an ordered round:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Vi spiller to runde.
- en: We play two round.
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Vi spiller den andre runden.
- en: We are playing the second round.
:::

The cardinal number *to* means that there are two of something:

::: examples
- no: Vi spiller to runder.
- en: We are playing two rounds.
:::

The ordinal *andre* selects one round by its position:

::: examples
- no: Vi spiller den andre runden.
- en: We are playing the second round.
:::

**Checkpoint request: choose-quantity-or-order**

The learner should complete short everyday contexts by choosing between a familiar cardinal number and its corresponding ordinal number. Contexts should include quantities, numbered objects, rounds, floors, rows, and places in a sequence. The primary scored goal is the meaning choice between quantity and order. Exact phrase structure may provide context but should not become a separate scored grammar goal. A successful response would demonstrate that the learner uses an ordinal only when position or sequence is intended.

{{exercise: choose-quantity-or-order}}

## Position and rank {#position-and-rank role=model objectives=obj-ordinal-numbers-01}

Ordinal numbers are useful when people or teams are ranked.

::: examples
- no: Nora kom på førsteplass.
- en: Nora came in first place.
- no: Emil og laget hans ligger på andreplass.
- en: Emil and his team are in second place.
- no: Vi kom på tredjeplass i konkurransen.
- en: We came in third place in the competition.
:::

The forms are often joined to *plass* in these common expressions:

::: examples
- no: førsteplass
- en: first place
- no: andreplass
- en: second place
- no: tredjeplass
- en: third place
:::

You can also describe a person directly:

::: examples
- no: Nora er den første deltakeren.
- en: Nora is the first participant.
- no: Ali er den andre i køen.
- en: Ali is the second person in the queue.
- no: Jeg er den tredje.
- en: I am the third one.
:::

When the noun is clear from the situation, Norwegian can leave it out. In *Jeg er den tredje*, the context tells us whether the speaker is the third participant, customer, student, or person in line.

Compare ordinal rank with an identifying number:

::: examples
- no: Hun har nummer tre.
- en: She has number three.
- no: Hun er den tredje deltakeren.
- en: She is the third participant.
:::

In the first sentence, *tre* is the number on her card or shirt. In the second, *tredje* gives her position in an ordered group.

**Checkpoint request: express-rank-and-position**

The learner should describe the positions of people or teams in a simple race, queue, or competition. Each prompt should provide a clear visible order so the learner can form expressions with *første*, *andre*, *tredje*, and other common ordinals. The primary scored goal is using ordinal numbers to express rank or position. A successful response would demonstrate that the learner can move from recognizing an order to stating it naturally in Norwegian.

{{exercise: express-rank-and-position}}

## Finding places in an ordered setting {#finding-places-in-an-ordered-setting role=model objectives=obj-ordinal-numbers-01}

Ordinal numbers also help people locate rooms, seats, stops, and objects.

::: examples
- no: Kontoret ligger i andre etasje.
- en: The office is on the second floor.
- no: Vi sitter på tredje rad.
- en: We are sitting in the third row.
- no: Hun går av på det femte stoppet.
- en: She gets off at the fifth stop.
- no: Nøkkelen ligger i den fjerde skuffen.
- en: The key is in the fourth drawer.
- no: Ta den andre døra til venstre.
- en: Take the second door on the left.
:::

Here, the learner does not need to count the total number of floors, rows, stops, drawers, or doors. The ordinal identifies one item by its place in a sequence.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Hotellet har fem etasjer.
- en: The hotel has five floors.
- no: Rommet ligger i femte etasje.
- en: The room is on the fifth floor.
:::

The first sentence gives a quantity. The second locates the room.

**Checkpoint request: locate-by-ordered-position**

The learner should follow or give short location instructions in a hotel, building, bus route, or seating area. The activity should require ordinal forms to identify a floor, row, stop, door, or similar ordered location. Its primary scored goal is using an ordinal to locate something by position. A successful response would demonstrate that the learner can apply the target forms beyond competitions and rankings.

{{exercise: locate-by-ordered-position}}

## Returning to the quiz {#returning-to-the-quiz role=model objectives=obj-ordinal-numbers-01}

The quiz has now finished.

**Emil:**

::: {.reading translation="How did it go in the third round?" dialogue_id="quiz-night-return" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Hvordan gikk det i den tredje runden?
:::

**Mari:**

::: {.reading translation="You answered the first question and the fourth question correctly." dialogue_id="quiz-night-return" speaker_id="mari" speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Dere svarte riktig på det første spørsmålet og det fjerde spørsmålet.
:::

**Emil:**

::: {.reading translation="And what place did we get?" dialogue_id="quiz-night-return" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Og hvilken plass fikk vi?
:::

**Mari:**

::: {.reading translation="You came in first place! The team at the first table came in second place." dialogue_id="quiz-night-return" speaker_id="mari" speaker_name="Mari" character_id="mari"}
Dere kom på førsteplass! Laget ved det første bordet kom på andreplass.
:::

**Emil:**

::: {.reading translation="Great! Then we will come back next week for the second time." dialogue_id="quiz-night-return" speaker_id="emil" speaker_name="Emil" character_id="emil"}
Flott! Da kommer vi tilbake neste uke for andre gang.
:::

The same forms now do several related jobs. *Den tredje runden* identifies a stage in the quiz. *Det første spørsmålet* and *det fjerde spørsmålet* identify questions by order. *Førsteplass* and *andreplass* express rank. *Andre gang* shows that a future visit will be the second occurrence.

**Checkpoint request: complete-the-quiz-result**

The learner should reconstruct a short account of the quiz result from supplied facts about rounds, questions, tables, and final positions. The learner should use common ordinal numbers in complete Norwegian phrases. The primary scored goal is selecting the ordinal form that matches each stated position. A successful response would demonstrate controlled use of the target across several contexts while remaining within one familiar situation.

{{exercise: complete-the-quiz-result}}

## Transfer to a new situation {#transfer-to-a-new-situation role=recap objectives=obj-ordinal-numbers-01}

Imagine that Sara has started a new job in a large building. Her office is on the fourth floor. She attends her first meeting in the morning, visits the second office along the corridor, and takes the third bus home. The next day, she arrives second in a queue at the café.

**Cumulative request: describe-saras-ordered-day**

The learner should describe Sara’s day in short Norwegian sentences, using ordinal numbers for the floor, meeting, office, bus, and queue position. This activity belongs at the end because it transfers the same meaning choice from the quiz to a new everyday setting. Its primary scored goal is independent use of common ordinal numbers to show order and position. A successful response would demonstrate that the learner can recognize where order matters and supply the appropriate ordinal form without copying the anchor dialogue.

{{exercise: describe-saras-ordered-day}}

## Quick retrieval and review {#quick-retrieval-and-review role=recap objectives=obj-ordinal-numbers-01}

**Review request: recall-and-use-three-ordinals**

Without looking back, the learner should recall the Norwegian words for *first*, *second*, and *third*, then use each one in a new sentence showing order, rank, or position. Success means retrieving the three core forms accurately and using each as an ordinal rather than as a quantity.
