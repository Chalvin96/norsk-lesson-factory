## A dinner that did not go as planned {#dinner-that-did-not-go-as-planned role=orient objectives=obj-conditional-perfect-01}

Mina has invited several friends to dinner. Her brother, Jonas, promised to help, but delays and misunderstandings have left them with too little food and no dessert.

::: {.reading translation="You were supposed to come at five." dialogue_id="dinner" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Du skulle ha kommet klokka fem.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I know. I should have sent you a message." dialogue_id="dinner" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg vet det. Jeg burde ha sendt deg en melding.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then I could have asked Nora for help." dialogue_id="dinner" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Da kunne jeg ha bedt Nora om hjelp.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I would have come earlier, but the train was cancelled." dialogue_id="dinner" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg ville ha kommet tidligere, men toget ble innstilt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And you were supposed to buy bread." dialogue_id="dinner" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Og du skulle ha kjøpt brød.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. I could have bought it at the station, but I didn’t think of it." dialogue_id="dinner" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. Jeg kunne ha kjøpt det på stasjonen, men jeg tenkte ikke på det.
:::

::: {.reading translation="We should have planned this better." dialogue_id="dinner" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Vi burde ha planlagt dette bedre.
:::

::: {.reading translation="If I had known that the train was cancelled, I would have taken the bus." dialogue_id="dinner" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hvis jeg hadde visst at toget var innstilt, ville jeg ha tatt bussen.
:::

The dinner has not started yet, but Mina and Jonas are looking backward. In this failed-plan context, they present actions as expected, advisable, possible but unrealized, or dependent on a different past situation. The dialogue establishes which actions did not occur or were not completed as intended.

The recurring pattern is:

**skulle, ville, burde, or kunne + ha + past participle**

A past participle (perfektum partisipp) is the verb form already used after *har* in the present perfect. Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jonas har kjøpt brød.
- en: Jonas has bought bread.
- no: Jonas skulle ha kjøpt brød.
- en: Jonas was supposed to buy bread.
:::

In the second sentence, *skulle* is the finite verb and is followed by the infinitive *ha*, while *kjøpt* remains the past participle.

## Looking back from the present {#looking-back-from-the-present role=model objectives=obj-conditional-perfect-01}

These forms concern past actions or outcomes. This lesson combines each modal with failed-plan context to practice a particular past perspective. The context establishes nonfulfillment or nonoccurrence; the modal marks how the speaker presents the past.

- **skulle ha + past participle:** an earlier plan, arrangement, duty, or expectation that the context establishes was not fulfilled
- **ville ha + past participle:** an unreal past outcome or intended response under different circumstances
- **burde ha + past participle:** retrospective advice, criticism, or regret about a contextually established omission or inadequate action
- **kunne ha + past participle:** a past possibility or opportunity that the context establishes was not realized

The forms overlap in some situations, but these target readings do not present the failed past from the same perspective.

**Checkpoint request: notice-modal-perspective**

**Purpose:** Help the learner connect each modal form with its target past perspective in a failed-plan context before producing it.

**Context:** Short statements about Mina and Jonas preparing dinner establish what did not occur or was not completed as intended.

**Learner action:** Identify whether each statement presents an unfulfilled expectation, an unreal outcome, retrospective advice, or an unrealized possibility.

**Success meaning:** Using the statement context, the learner distinguishes the target readings associated with *skulle*, *ville*, *burde*, and *kunne*. Recognition of perspective is scored; identifying participle forms is supporting context only.

{{exercise: notice_modal_perspective}}

## Unfulfilled plans and expectations: skulle ha {#unfulfilled-plans-and-expectations role=model objectives=obj-conditional-perfect-01}

In the failed-plan reading taught here, use **skulle ha + past participle** to present something as planned, arranged, promised, or expected. Surrounding context establishes that it did not happen as intended.

::: examples
- no: Jonas skulle ha kommet klokka fem.
- en: Jonas was supposed to come at five.
- no: Han skulle ha kjøpt brød.
- en: He was supposed to buy bread.
- no: Nora skulle ha laget dessert.
- en: Nora was supposed to make dessert.
:::

These sentences look back at an earlier arrangement. In context, Jonas did not arrive at five, he did not buy bread, and Nora did not make dessert.

*Skulle ha* can also express an intention that was not carried out:

::: examples
- no: Jeg skulle ha ringt deg, men mobilen var tom for strøm.
- en: I was going to call you, but my phone was out of power.
- no: Vi skulle ha bestilt bord, men vi glemte det.
- en: We were going to reserve a table, but we forgot.
:::

The compact rule for this lesson is:

::: {.rule}
Use skulle ha to present the earlier plan or expectation; use the failed-plan context to establish that it was not fulfilled.
:::

## Skulle ha and burde ha are not identical {#skulle-ha-and-burde-ha-are-not-identical role=contrast objectives=obj-conditional-perfect-01}

English *should have* can sometimes tempt learners to use *skulle ha* everywhere. Norwegian usually separates an earlier arrangement from a later judgment.

::: examples
- no: Jeg skulle ha møtt henne klokka seks.
- en: I was supposed to meet her at six.
:::

This reports the arrangement.

::: examples
- no: Jeg burde ha møtt henne tidligere.
- en: I should have met her earlier.
:::

This evaluates the past and says that meeting her earlier would have been better.

In Mina’s complaint, the arrangement is central:

::: examples
- no: Du skulle ha kommet klokka fem.
- en: You were supposed to come at five.
:::

If she instead says:

::: examples
- no: Du burde ha kommet tidligere.
- en: You should have come earlier.
:::

she is giving retrospective criticism or advice. There may have been no fixed arrangement for five o’clock.

**Practice request: complete-unfulfilled-arrangements**

**Purpose:** Establish the structure and meaning of *skulle ha + past participle*.

**Context:** Plans for Mina’s dinner that were not carried out.

**Learner action:** Complete short statements about promised or arranged actions using the given verb in the past participle.

**Success meaning:** The learner forms *skulle ha + past participle* and uses it for an unfulfilled past arrangement. Distinctions involving the other modal verbs are not scored here.

{{exercise: complete_unfulfilled_arrangements}}

**Practice request: contrast-arrangement-and-retrospective-advice**

**Purpose:** Prevent confusion between *skulle ha* and *burde ha*.

**Context:** People explain both what had been arranged and what would have been wiser.

**Learner action:** Choose the modal perspective that matches either an earlier arrangement or a later judgment, then produce the full phrase with *ha* and the past participle.

**Success meaning:** The learner distinguishes expectation from retrospective advice. Past-participle formation supports the task but is not its primary scored goal.

{{exercise: contrast_arrangement_and_retrospective_advice}}

## Unreal past outcomes: ville ha {#unreal-past-outcomes-ville-ha role=model objectives=obj-conditional-perfect-01}

Use **ville ha + past participle** for an outcome that would have happened under different circumstances.

Jonas says:

::: examples
- no: Hvis jeg hadde visst at toget var innstilt, ville jeg ha tatt bussen.
- en: If I had known that the train was cancelled, I would have taken the bus.
:::

He did not know, so he did not take the bus. *Ville ha tatt* describes the unreal result.

Other examples:

::: examples
- no: Uten forsinkelsen ville han ha kommet i tide.
- en: Without the delay, he would have arrived on time.
- no: Med mer mat ville gjestene ha blitt lenger.
- en: With more food, the guests would have stayed longer.
- no: Jeg ville ha hjulpet deg hvis jeg hadde vært hjemme.
- en: I would have helped you if I had been at home.
:::

The condition does not always need to be stated. Shared context can make it clear:

::: examples
- no: Jeg ville ha sagt ja, men jeg fikk aldri spørsmålet.
- en: I would have said yes, but I was never asked.
- no: Det ville ha vært hyggelig.
- en: That would have been nice.
:::

The compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Use ville ha when the important fact is an unreal past result: what would have happened in different circumstances.
:::

## Do not replace ville with skulle automatically {#do-not-replace-ville-with-skulle-automatically role=contrast objectives=obj-conditional-perfect-01}

Because both *ville* and *skulle* can correspond to English *would* in some contexts, learners may treat them as interchangeable. In this lesson’s past-looking pattern, keep the central contrast clear:

::: examples
- no: Jonas skulle ha tatt bussen klokka fire.
- en: Jonas was supposed to take the bus at four.
:::

There was a plan or expectation.

::: examples
- no: Jonas ville ha tatt bussen hvis han hadde visst om problemet.
- en: Jonas would have taken the bus if he had known about the problem.
:::

Taking the bus was an unreal result dependent on different knowledge.

**Practice request: build-unreal-past-outcomes**

**Purpose:** Develop accurate use of *ville ha* for consequences that did not occur.

**Context:** Travel disruptions and changed plans before the dinner.

**Learner action:** Complete past-result statements from supplied circumstances, using *ville ha + past participle*.

**Success meaning:** The learner expresses an unreal past outcome with *ville ha*. Forming or analyzing the condition is not separately scored.

{{exercise: build_unreal_past_outcomes}}

**Practice request: contrast-expected-and-conditional-actions**

**Purpose:** Reinforce the difference between an expected action and an unreal result.

**Context:** Jonas’s original travel plan is compared with what he would have done if he had known about the cancelled train.

**Learner action:** Select and produce either *skulle ha* or *ville ha* according to the intended meaning.

**Success meaning:** The learner chooses *skulle ha* for an earlier expectation and *ville ha* for an unreal outcome.

{{exercise: contrast_expected_and_conditional_actions}}

## Regret, advice, and criticism: burde ha {#regret-advice-and-criticism-burde-ha role=model objectives=obj-conditional-perfect-01}

Use **burde ha + past participle** to say that a past action would have been better, wiser, safer, or more appropriate. It often communicates regret or criticism.

::: examples
- no: Jeg burde ha sendt deg en melding.
- en: I should have sent you a message.
- no: Vi burde ha planlagt dette bedre.
- en: We should have planned this better.
- no: Du burde ha sjekket rutetidene.
- en: You should have checked the timetable.
- no: De burde ha bestilt mer mat.
- en: They should have ordered more food.
:::

In this lesson’s scenarios, context establishes that the action did not happen or did not happen adequately. *Burde ha* supplies the speaker’s retrospective evaluation.

When the subject is *jeg* or *vi*, the sentence often sounds self-critical:

::: examples
- no: Jeg burde ha lyttet til deg.
- en: I should have listened to you.
- no: Vi burde ha reist tidligere.
- en: We should have left earlier.
:::

With *du* or another person, it may sound like criticism:

::: examples
- no: Du burde ha sagt fra.
- en: You should have let us know.
:::

Context and tone determine how strong that criticism feels.

The compact rule for this lesson is:

::: {.rule}
Use burde ha for retrospective judgment that a different or better past action was preferable; context establishes what actually happened.
:::

## Negating burde ha {#negating-burde-ha role=contrast objectives=obj-conditional-perfect-01}

In main clauses, place *ikke* after the modal verb:

::: examples
- no: Jeg burde ikke ha ventet så lenge.
- en: I should not have waited so long.
- no: Vi burde ikke ha invitert så mange.
- en: We should not have invited so many people.
:::

The regret now concerns an action that did happen but should not have happened.

Compare these readings in contexts that establish what happened:

::: examples
- no: Jeg burde ha ringt.
- en: I should have called.
:::

The context establishes that the desired action did not happen.

::: examples
- no: Jeg burde ikke ha ringt så sent.
- en: I should not have called so late.
:::

The context establishes that the call happened, and the speaker now judges it negatively.

**Practice request: express-past-regret**

**Purpose:** Move from recognizing retrospective judgment to producing it.

**Context:** Mina and Jonas review preparation mistakes after the guests arrive.

**Learner action:** Reformulate factual descriptions of mistakes as regrets or retrospective advice with *burde ha* or *burde ikke ha*.

**Success meaning:** The learner uses affirmative or negative *burde ha* to evaluate a completed or omitted past action.

{{exercise: express_past_regret}}

**Practice request: separate-duty-from-judgment**

**Purpose:** Clarify the difference between a missed obligation and later criticism.

**Context:** A host had assigned tasks before a gathering, then comments on what went wrong afterward.

**Learner action:** Express assigned tasks with *skulle ha* and later judgments with *burde ha*.

**Success meaning:** The learner chooses between prior expectation and retrospective evaluation. Whether the criticism sounds strong or mild is not scored.

{{exercise: separate_duty_from_judgment}}

## Missed possibilities: kunne ha {#missed-possibilities-kunne-ha role=model objectives=obj-conditional-perfect-01}

In the missed-possibility reading taught here, use **kunne ha + past participle** to present an action or outcome as possible in the past. Surrounding context establishes that it did not become reality.

::: examples
- no: Jeg kunne ha bedt Nora om hjelp.
- en: I could have asked Nora for help.
- no: Jonas kunne ha kjøpt brød på stasjonen.
- en: Jonas could have bought bread at the station.
- no: De kunne ha bestilt pizza.
- en: They could have ordered pizza.
- no: Vi kunne ha spist ute.
- en: We could have eaten out.
:::

These sentences do not necessarily say that the action was required or advisable. They identify an available possibility, while the dinner context establishes that it was not used.

Compare the perspectives:

::: examples
- no: Jonas skulle ha kjøpt brød.
- en: Jonas was supposed to buy bread.
:::

Buying bread was expected.

::: examples
- no: Jonas burde ha kjøpt brød.
- en: Jonas should have bought bread.
:::

The speaker now thinks buying bread would have been the better choice.

::: examples
- no: Jonas kunne ha kjøpt brød.
- en: Jonas could have bought bread.
:::

Buying bread was possible; the dinner context establishes that he did not do it.

The compact rule for this lesson is:

::: {.rule}
Use kunne ha to present a past possibility or opportunity; use context to establish that it was not realized.
:::

Sometimes *kunne ha* refers to a possible outcome rather than a deliberate choice:

::: examples
- no: Det kunne ha gått galt.
- en: It could have gone wrong.
- no: Toget kunne ha kommet enda senere.
- en: The train could have arrived even later.
:::

In the readings practiced here, context establishes that the outcome did not occur, while *kunne ha* presents it as possible.

**Practice request: identify-missed-possibilities**

**Purpose:** Isolate the meaning of *kunne ha* from obligation and regret.

**Context:** Alternative ways Mina and Jonas might have solved practical problems.

**Learner action:** Express available but unused options with *kunne ha + past participle*.

**Success meaning:** The learner presents an action as possible rather than expected or necessarily advisable.

{{exercise: identify_missed_possibilities}}

**Practice request: contrast-possible-and-advisable-actions**

**Purpose:** Distinguish a neutral possibility from a judgment about the best action.

**Context:** Several responses were available after the train cancellation, but some were wiser than others.

**Learner action:** Use *kunne ha* to state what was possible and *burde ha* to state what would have been better.

**Success meaning:** The learner distinguishes missed possibility from retrospective advice.

{{exercise: contrast_possible_and_advisable_actions}}

## Keeping the structure together {#keeping-structure-together role=contrast objectives=obj-conditional-perfect-01}

All four patterns use the same order:

**modal verb + ha + past participle**

::: examples
- no: skulle ha kommet
- en: was supposed to have come / was supposed to come
- no: ville ha kommet
- en: would have come
- no: burde ha kommet
- en: should have come
- no: kunne ha kommet
- en: could have come
:::

The modal verb carries tense and perspective. Do not put *å* before *ha*, and do not change *ha* to *har* or *hadde*.

Incorrect pattern: *skulle å ha kommet*

Correct pattern:

::: examples
- no: skulle ha kommet
- en: was supposed to have come
:::

Incorrect pattern: *ville hadde kommet*

Correct pattern:

::: examples
- no: ville ha kommet
- en: would have come
:::

Use the past participle, not the infinitive or simple past:

::: examples
- no: Hun kunne ha skrevet til oss.
- en: She could have written to us.
:::

Here *skrevet* is the past participle of *skrive*. Forms such as *skrive* and *skrev* cannot fill the same position in this pattern.

In main clauses with negation, *ikke* normally follows the modal verb:

::: examples
- no: Han skulle ikke ha ventet.
- en: He was not supposed to wait.
- no: Hun ville ikke ha kommet alene.
- en: She would not have come alone.
- no: Vi burde ikke ha brukt så mye penger.
- en: We should not have spent so much money.
- no: De kunne ikke ha visst det.
- en: They could not have known that.
:::

**Practice request: assemble-modal-perfect-forms**

**Purpose:** Secure word order and verb form after the meaning contrasts have been introduced.

**Context:** Brief comments about the dinner, journey, and preparations.

**Learner action:** Produce complete main clauses containing a supplied modal meaning, *ha*, and the correct past participle, including several negative statements.

**Success meaning:** In main clauses, the learner maintains the order modal + optional *ikke* + *ha* + past participle. Modal meaning is supplied and is not the main scored dimension.

{{exercise: assemble_modal_perfect_forms}}

## Returning to Mina and Jonas {#returning-to-mina-and-jonas role=recap objectives=obj-conditional-perfect-01}

The doorbell rings. Nora arrives and sees that Mina and Jonas are still preparing the food.

::: {.reading translation="You were supposed to be finished by now." dialogue_id="dinner" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Dere skulle ha vært ferdige nå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, and we should have started earlier." dialogue_id="dinner" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Ja, og vi burde ha begynt tidligere.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I could have made a salad, but we had almost no vegetables." dialogue_id="dinner" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg kunne ha laget en salat, men vi hadde nesten ingen grønnsaker.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I would have brought something if I had known." dialogue_id="dinner" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Jeg ville ha tatt med noe hvis jeg hadde visst.
:::

::: {.reading translation="You could have saved the whole dinner." dialogue_id="dinner" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Du kunne ha reddet hele middagen.
:::

Each speaker looks at the same unsuccessful preparation from a different angle:

- *skulle ha vært* refers to the expected state.
- *burde ha begynt* evaluates the earlier decision.
- *kunne ha laget* presents an unrealized option.
- *ville ha tatt med* describes an unreal outcome under different circumstances.
- *kunne ha reddet* presents a possible result that did not occur.

**Practice request: interpret-dinner-postmortem**

**Purpose:** Check integrated understanding of all four modal perspectives in one coherent scene.

**Context:** Nora, Mina, and Jonas discuss why dinner is late.

**Learner action:** Explain what each modal phrase contributes: expectation, unreal outcome, retrospective judgment, or missed possibility.

**Success meaning:** The learner interprets the modal perspective from context. Producing new sentences is not scored in this checkpoint.

{{exercise: interpret_dinner_postmortem}}
