## A weekend trip {#weekend-trip role=orient objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

Maja and Jonas are planning a short trip to Lillehammer. Some parts of the weekend are already arranged, while others depend on the weather.

::: {.reading translation="Are you doing anything this weekend?" dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Skal du gjøre noe i helga?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I’m going to visit my sister in Lillehammer. Do you want to come along?" dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja, jeg skal besøke søsteren min i Lillehammer. Vil du bli med?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’d love to. When does the train leave?" dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Gjerne. Når går toget?
:::

::: {.reading translation="It leaves Oslo at 8:12 on Saturday. We’ll meet at the station at quarter to eight." dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Det går fra Oslo klokka 08.12 på lørdag. Vi møtes på stasjonen kvart på åtte.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s early! But I’ll buy the ticket this evening." dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Det er tidlig! Men jeg skal kjøpe billetten i kveld.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. I think the train will be quite full." dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Bra. Jeg tror toget vil være ganske fullt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And look at the weather forecast. It’s going to rain all day Saturday." dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Og se på værmeldingen. Det kommer til å regne hele lørdagen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then I’ll bring a rain jacket. On Sunday it may be better." dialogue_id="weekend-trip" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Da tar jeg med regnjakke. På søndag blir det kanskje bedre.
:::

The dialogue refers to the future in four different ways:

- **skal** presents a plan, intention, or commitment.
- **vil** can present a prediction, but it can also express desire or willingness.
- **kommer til å** presents an expected future result, often supported by present signs or circumstances.
- **present tense (presens)** can describe a scheduled or firmly arranged future event when the future context is clear.

Norwegian therefore does not need one separate future tense for every future meaning. The form depends on how the speaker views the event.

## First checkpoint: notice the speaker’s perspective {#first-checkpoint-speaker-perspective role=contrast objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

**Request: future-anchor-noticing**

**Purpose:** Notice that all four forms can refer to future time but do not present events in the same way.

**Context:** Use Maja and Jonas’s weekend plans.

**Learner action:** Identify each future reference in the dialogue and connect it to its role: personal commitment, timetable or arrangement, prediction, or expected result based on available information.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner recognizes that choosing a future expression depends on meaning, not merely on whether an event happens later.

## Plans and commitments with skal {#plans-and-commitments-with-skal role=model objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

Use **skal + infinitive (infinitiv)** when the subject has a plan, intention, agreement, or commitment.

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal besøke søsteren min på lørdag.
- en: I’m going to visit my sister on Saturday.
- no: Vi skal bo hos henne.
- en: We’re going to stay with her.
- no: Maja skal kjøpe billetten i kveld.
- en: Maja is going to buy the ticket this evening.
- no: Jeg skal ringe deg etter jobb.
- en: I’ll call you after work.
:::

These sentences do more than locate an action in the future. They present it as decided or intended.

The infinitive follows **skal** without **å**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal kjøpe billetten.
- en: I’m going to buy the ticket.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal å kjøpe billetten.
- en: 'Incorrect: I’m going to buy the ticket.'
:::

The negative form places **ikke** after **skal**:

::: examples
- no: Vi skal ikke reise på fredag.
- en: We aren’t going to travel on Friday.
:::

In questions, **skal** comes before the subject:

::: examples
- no: Skal du jobbe i morgen?
- en: Are you going to work tomorrow?
- no: Når skal dere reise?
- en: When are you going to travel?
:::

## Compact rule {#compact-rule-skal role=model objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

::: {.rule}
Use **skal + infinitive** when you want to present a future action as someone’s plan, intention, or commitment.
:::

## Likely misconception {#likely-misconception-skal role=contrast objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

English **will** does not always correspond to Norwegian **vil**. For a decided first-person plan, Norwegian commonly uses **skal**.

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal hente deg klokka seks.
- en: I’ll pick you up at six.
- no: Jeg vil hente deg klokka seks.
- en: I want to pick you up at six.
:::

The second sentence can express desire or willingness rather than a neutral future plan.

**Request: skal-plan-formation**

**Purpose:** Establish the correct form of planned future statements before contrasting meanings.

**Context:** Maja and Jonas divide practical tasks before their trip, such as buying tickets, packing food, and contacting Jonas’s sister.

**Learner action:** Complete controlled statements and questions with the correct form of **skal** and the supplied verb in the infinitive. Include one negative statement.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner forms **skal + infinitive** without adding **å** and places **skal** correctly in statements and questions.

{{exercise: skal_plan_formation}}

## Scheduled and arranged events in the present tense {#scheduled-arranged-events-present role=model objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

Norwegian often uses the present tense for future events when a time expression or shared context makes the future meaning clear.

::: examples
- no: Toget går klokka 08.12 på lørdag.
- en: The train leaves at 8:12 on Saturday.
- no: Vi møtes på stasjonen kvart på åtte.
- en: We’ll meet at the station at quarter to eight.
- no: Kurset begynner neste uke.
- en: The course begins next week.
- no: Jeg reiser til Bergen i morgen.
- en: I’m travelling to Bergen tomorrow.
:::

This use is especially common for timetables, appointments, and settled arrangements. Words such as **i morgen**, **på lørdag**, **neste uke**, and a future clock time prevent confusion with the present moment.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Vi skal møtes på stasjonen.
- en: We’re going to meet at the station.
- no: Vi møtes på stasjonen klokka 07.45.
- en: We’ll meet at the station at 7:45.
:::

The first sentence highlights the plan. The second treats the meeting as an arranged event.

The difference is often one of perspective rather than objective fact. The same event can be described differently:

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal reise til Trondheim på mandag.
- en: I’m going to travel to Trondheim on Monday.
- no: Jeg reiser til Trondheim på mandag.
- en: I’m travelling to Trondheim on Monday.
:::

The sentence with **skal** emphasizes intention. The present-tense sentence presents the journey as settled.

## Common mistake: present tense without future context {#common-mistake-present-context role=contrast objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

A present-tense form does not automatically mean future time.

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobber.
- en: I’m working.
:::

Without a future expression or a clear shared arrangement, this normally refers to the present.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg jobber i morgen.
- en: I’m working tomorrow.
:::

Here, **i morgen** supplies the future context.

**Request: plan-or-arrangement-choice**

**Purpose:** Contrast a personal commitment with a scheduled or settled future event.

**Context:** A trip itinerary contains train times and meeting times, while short messages describe what each traveller has promised to do.

**Learner action:** Choose between **skal + infinitive** and present tense, then form the complete sentence. Timetable facts should be distinguished from personal commitments.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner selects the form according to the intended perspective rather than using **skal** for every future event.

{{exercise: plan_or_arrangement_choice}}

## Predictions with vil {#predictions-with-vil role=model objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

Use **vil + infinitive** for a prediction or assessment about the future, especially when it reflects an opinion, calculation, or general expectation.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror toget vil være fullt.
- en: I think the train will be full.
- no: Prisene vil stige neste år.
- en: Prices will rise next year.
- no: Det vil ta omtrent tre timer.
- en: It will take about three hours.
- no: Turen vil bli lettere med godt utstyr.
- en: The trip will become easier with good equipment.
:::

As with **skal**, the verb after **vil** is an infinitive without **å**.

::: examples
- no: Det vil regne senere.
- en: It will rain later.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: Det vil å regne senere.
- en: 'Incorrect: It will rain later.'
:::

The negative form places **ikke** after **vil**:

::: examples
- no: Det vil ikke ta lang tid.
- en: It won’t take long.
:::

A phrase such as **jeg tror** often makes the predictive meaning clear:

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror været vil bli bedre på søndag.
- en: I think the weather will improve on Sunday.
:::

## The two meanings of vil {#two-meanings-of-vil role=contrast objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

With a person as subject, **vil** can mean **want to** or **be willing to**, not simply future **will**.

::: examples
- no: Jeg vil reise til Lillehammer.
- en: I want to travel to Lillehammer.
- no: Jeg vil hjelpe deg.
- en: I want to help you. / I’m willing to help you.
:::

For a decided plan, use **skal**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal reise til Lillehammer på lørdag.
- en: I’m going to travel to Lillehammer on Saturday.
:::

For a prediction, make the predictive context clear:

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror Maja vil like Lillehammer.
- en: I think Maja will like Lillehammer.
:::

The point is not that **vil** can never refer to a person’s future action. The important issue is possible ambiguity. If you mean a plan, **skal** is usually clearer.

**Request: vil-prediction-interpretation**

**Purpose:** Separate predictive **vil** from **vil** meaning desire or willingness.

**Context:** Short statements concern the journey, weather, costs, and the travellers’ personal wishes.

**Learner action:** Determine the intended meaning of **vil** from context and reformulate prompts that are meant to express a decided plan with **skal**.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner does not mechanically translate English **will** as **vil** and can preserve the intended future meaning.

{{exercise: vil_prediction_interpretation}}

## Expected results with kommer til å {#expected-results-kommer-til-a role=model objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

Use **kommer til å + infinitive** for an expected future event or result. It is especially natural when present information, visible signs, or known circumstances point toward that result.

::: examples
- no: Det kommer til å regne hele lørdagen.
- en: It’s going to rain all day Saturday.
- no: Toget kommer til å bli fullt.
- en: The train is going to be full.
- no: Vi kommer til å trenge regnjakker.
- en: We’re going to need rain jackets.
- no: Hun kommer til å bli glad når vi kommer.
- en: She’s going to be happy when we arrive.
:::

The complete pattern is:

**subject + kommer til å + infinitive**

In this construction, **kommer** carries the present-tense ending. The main verb remains in the infinitive.

::: examples
- no: Det kommer til å snø.
- en: It’s going to snow.
- no: De kommer til å vente lenge.
- en: They’re going to wait a long time.
:::

The negative form is:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer ikke til å snø.
- en: It isn’t going to snow.
:::

Do not omit **til å**:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer til å regne.
- en: It’s going to rain.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: Det kommer regne.
- en: 'Incorrect: It’s going to rain.'
:::

## Vil or kommer til å? {#vil-or-kommer-til-a role=contrast objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

Both forms can make predictions. A useful A2-level distinction is:

- **vil** often presents a prediction as an opinion, assessment, or neutral forecast.
- **kommer til å** often presents an expected result, particularly when circumstances or signs point toward it.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror toget vil være fullt.
- en: I think the train will be full.
:::

The speaker offers an assessment.

::: examples
- no: Se på køen! Toget kommer til å bli fullt.
- en: Look at the queue! The train is going to be full.
:::

The visible queue supports the prediction.

Another contrast:

::: examples
- no: Ekspertene tror prisene vil stige.
- en: The experts think prices will rise.
- no: Med disse nye kostnadene kommer prisene til å stige.
- en: With these new costs, prices are going to rise.
:::

This is a useful tendency, not an absolute boundary. In everyday Norwegian, both expressions can sometimes fit. The speaker chooses how to frame the prediction.

**Request: prediction-basis-choice**

**Purpose:** Choose between two common predictive expressions by attending to the basis of the prediction.

**Context:** Some statements report opinions or forecasts; others respond to visible signs such as dark clouds, a long queue, or an almost empty fuel tank.

**Learner action:** Complete each controlled prediction with **vil + infinitive** or **kommer til å + infinitive** and retain the supplied contextual clue.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner connects **vil** with an assessment and **kommer til å** with an expected result supported by circumstances. Only prediction basis is scored; word order and vocabulary serve as context.

{{exercise: prediction_basis_choice}}

## Four perspectives on future time {#four-perspectives-future-time role=contrast objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

The same general situation can support all four expressions.

**A personal commitment**

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal kjøpe togbilletten i kveld.
- en: I’m going to buy the train ticket this evening.
:::

**A settled arrangement**

::: examples
- no: Vi møtes på Oslo S klokka 07.45.
- en: We’ll meet at Oslo Central Station at 7:45.
:::

**An assessment**

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror reisen vil ta omtrent to timer.
- en: I think the journey will take about two hours.
:::

**An expected result based on signs**

::: examples
- no: Det kommer til å bli en våt helg.
- en: It’s going to be a wet weekend.
:::

A compact decision guide is:

- Choose **skal** for a plan, intention, agreement, or commitment.
- Choose present tense for a timetable or settled arrangement with clear future context.
- Choose **vil** for a prediction framed as an assessment or expectation.
- Choose **kommer til å** for an expected result, especially when current evidence or circumstances point toward it.

## Common form errors {#common-form-errors role=contrast objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

**Adding å after skal or vil**

::: examples
- no: Vi skal ta toget.
- en: We’re going to take the train.
- no: Reisen vil ta to timer.
- en: The journey will take two hours.
:::

Use no **å** after **skal** or **vil**.

**Removing å from kommer til å**

::: examples
- no: Det kommer til å regne.
- en: It’s going to rain.
:::

Keep the whole expression **kommer til å**.

**Using vil for every translation of English will**

::: examples
- no: Jeg skal sende deg en melding i kveld.
- en: I’ll send you a message this evening.
:::

This is a commitment, so **skal** is clearer than **vil**.

**Using present tense without a future marker**

::: examples
- no: Bussen går.
- en: The bus is leaving. / The bus goes.
- no: Bussen går klokka ni i morgen.
- en: The bus leaves at nine tomorrow.
:::

The second sentence has an unmistakable future setting.

**Request: future-form-repair**

**Purpose:** Consolidate the structure of all four future expressions by correcting frequent form errors.

**Context:** Maja has written hurried messages about tickets, departure times, weather, and packing.

**Learner action:** Rewrite each message by removing an unnecessary **å**, restoring **til å**, selecting the correct verb form, or adding the supplied future-time expression where needed.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner accurately forms each target construction. This activity scores grammatical formation, not the finer meaning contrast between two otherwise possible predictions.

## Back to Maja and Jonas {#back-to-maja-and-jonas role=model objectives=obj-future-expressions-01}

Søsteren min skal møte oss på stasjonen i Lillehammer.

::: {.reading translation="Great. The train arrives at 10:26, right?" dialogue_id="friday-evening-update" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Fint. Toget kommer fram klokka 10.26, ikke sant?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. I think she’ll wait by the main entrance." dialogue_id="friday-evening-update" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. Jeg tror hun vil vente ved hovedinngangen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. I’ll send her a message when we leave Oslo." dialogue_id="friday-evening-update" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Bra. Jeg skal sende henne en melding når vi drar fra Oslo.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Bring warm clothes too. It’s going to be cold in the evening." dialogue_id="friday-evening-update" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ta med varme klær også. Det kommer til å bli kaldt om kvelden.
:::

The four choices still serve different purposes:

- Jonas’s sister has a plan: **skal møte**.
- The arrival belongs to the timetable: **kommer fram**.
- Jonas gives his assessment: **vil vente**.
- The expected cold is presented as a likely result: **kommer til å bli**.

**Learner action:** Form one appropriate future expression in each message from a supplied verb and a clear contextual cue.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner chooses and forms **skal**, **vil**, **kommer til å**, and present tense according to communicative meaning within connected discourse.
