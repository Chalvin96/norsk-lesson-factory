## A new place to meet {#a-new-place-to-meet role=orient objectives=obj-s-verbs-01}

Amir has recently moved to Bergen for work. His colleague Nora asks how he is settling in and suggests meeting after work.

::: {.reading translation="How do you like living in Bergen?" dialogue_id="new-place-to-meet" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Hvordan trives du i Bergen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’m quite happy here, but I don’t know many people here yet." dialogue_id="new-place-to-meet" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Jeg trives ganske godt, men jeg kjenner ikke så mange her ennå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="What do you think of the city?" dialogue_id="new-place-to-meet" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Hva synes du om byen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I think it is nice, especially when it isn’t raining. Is there a pleasant café nearby?" dialogue_id="new-place-to-meet" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Jeg synes den er fin, særlig når det ikke regner. Finnes det en hyggelig kafé i nærheten?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, there is a small café by the library. We often meet there after work." dialogue_id="new-place-to-meet" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, det finnes en liten kafé ved biblioteket. Vi møtes ofte der etter jobb.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That sounds good. Shall we meet there tomorrow?" dialogue_id="new-place-to-meet" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Det høres bra ut. Skal vi møtes der i morgen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Gladly. See you at five!" dialogue_id="new-place-to-meet" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Gjerne. Vi ses klokka fem!
:::

The conversation contains five verbs ending in **-s**: **synes, trives, finnes, møtes** and **ses**. You may already know that Norwegian can use an **s-form** to express the passive:

::: examples
- no: Kafeen stenges klokka seks.
- en: The café is closed at six.
:::

Here, **stenges** describes something being done to the café. However, not every verb ending in **-s** is passive. In the dialogue, the **s** belongs to the established meaning of each verb. These are **lexical s-verbs (s-verb)**. Learn the complete form together with its meaning.

## First checkpoint: notice the difference {#first-checkpoint-notice-difference role=contrast objectives=obj-s-verbs-01}

**Request: identify-lexical-s-verbs-in-context**

**Purpose:** Distinguish the five target s-verbs from familiar passive s-forms before trying to produce them.

**Context:** Use short descriptions related to Amir’s move, his workplace and the café.

**Learner action:** Identify which s-ending verbs express one of the target meanings—having an opinion, feeling comfortable, existing, meeting one another or seeing one another—and which describe an action being done to something.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner recognizes lexical meaning rather than treating every final **-s** as a passive marker.

{{exercise: identify_lexical_s_verbs_in_context}}

## Synes: expressing an opinion {#synes-expressing-an-opinion role=model objectives=obj-s-verbs-01}

**Synes** is used when you give or ask for a personal opinion or impression. It often corresponds to **think**, **find** or **feel** in English.

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes Bergen er en vakker by.
- en: I think Bergen is a beautiful city.
- no: Nora synes kafeen er hyggelig.
- en: Nora thinks the café is pleasant.
- no: Hva synes du om den nye jobben?
- en: What do you think of the new job?
- no: Jeg synes den er interessant.
- en: I think it is interesting.
:::

A frequent pattern is:

**synes + a statement**

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes kaffen er god.
- en: I think the coffee is good.
:::

Another frequent pattern is:

**synes om + a person or thing**

::: examples
- no: Hva synes du om kaffen?
- en: What do you think of the coffee?
- no: Jeg synes godt om den nye sjefen.
- en: I have a good opinion of the new manager.
:::

In everyday speech, **Hva synes du om …?** is a useful way to invite an opinion.

The final **-s** in **synes** does not mean that someone’s opinion is being acted upon. It is part of the verb. Do not remove it and use **syne** for this meaning.

**Common mistake**

**Incorrect:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg syner byen er fin.
- en: 'Incorrect: I think the city is nice.'
:::

**Correct:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg synes byen er fin.
- en: I think the city is nice.
:::

The error comes from assuming that the **-s** is optional. In this meaning, the verb is **synes**.

**Request: choose-synes-for-personal-opinion**

**Purpose:** Connect **synes** with personal evaluation rather than merely noticing its spelling.

**Context:** Amir comments on his new city, apartment, workplace and local café.

**Learner action:** Complete short statements and questions with the appropriate form of **synes**, using either a following statement or the pattern **synes om**.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner selects **synes** when the intended meaning is a personal opinion.

{{exercise: choose_synes_for_personal_opinion}}

## Trives: feeling comfortable in a situation {#trives-feeling-comfortable-in-a-situation role=model objectives=obj-s-verbs-01}

**Trives** describes feeling comfortable, happy or at ease in a place, group, job or situation. English translations vary because English has no single everyday verb with exactly the same range.

::: examples
- no: Amir trives i Bergen.
- en: Amir is happy living in Bergen.
- no: Trives du på jobben?
- en: Are you happy at work?
- no: Barna trives på den nye skolen.
- en: The children are settling in well at the new school.
- no: Jeg trives godt sammen med kollegene mine.
- en: I feel comfortable with my colleagues.
:::

Notice the common combinations:

::: examples
- no: trives i byen
- en: be happy in the city
- no: trives på jobben
- en: be happy at work
- no: trives med kollegene
- en: get along well and feel comfortable with the colleagues
- no: trives sammen
- en: enjoy being together
:::

**Trives** describes someone’s experience of a situation. It is not a passive meaning. In **Amir trives på jobben**, nobody is doing an action to Amir.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Amir trives på jobben.
- en: Amir is happy at work.
- no: Amir ansettes av et norsk firma.
- en: Amir is employed by a Norwegian company.
:::

The first sentence contains lexical **trives**. The second contains passive **ansettes**: the company performs the action of employing Amir.

**Common mistake**

Learners sometimes translate English **like** too directly:

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker i Bergen.
- en: 'Incorrect: I am happy living in Bergen.'
:::

That does not express living happily in Bergen. Use:

::: examples
- no: Jeg trives i Bergen.
- en: I’m happy living in Bergen.
:::

You can use **liker** with a noun:

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker Bergen.
- en: I like Bergen.
:::

The distinction is useful:

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker byen, men jeg trives ikke på jobben.
- en: I like the city, but I’m not happy at work.
:::

This contrast helps clarify **trives**, but the target remains recognizing and using the complete s-verb.

**Request: use-trives-for-comfort-in-a-situation**

**Purpose:** Establish the specific meaning of **trives** through places and social settings.

**Context:** People describe how they feel in a new home, school, workplace or group.

**Learner action:** Form short statements or questions with **trives** and a suitable context expression such as **i**, **på**, **med** or **sammen**.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner uses **trives** to describe comfort and well-being in a situation, retaining the final **-s**.

{{exercise: use_trives_for_comfort_in_a_situation}}

## Finnes: saying that something exists or is available {#finnes-saying-that-something-exists-or-is-available role=model objectives=obj-s-verbs-01}

**Finnes** means **exist**, **be found** or, very often, **there is/there are**.

::: examples
- no: Det finnes en kafé ved biblioteket.
- en: There is a café by the library.
- no: Finnes det et apotek i nærheten?
- en: Is there a pharmacy nearby?
- no: Det finnes mange turstier rundt Bergen.
- en: There are many hiking trails around Bergen.
- no: Det finnes ingen enkel løsning.
- en: There is no simple solution.
:::

A common structure is:

**Det finnes + noun**

::: examples
- no: Det finnes et ledig bord.
- en: There is an available table.
:::

In questions, the order changes:

**Finnes det + noun?**

::: examples
- no: Finnes det en buss som går dit?
- en: Is there a bus that goes there?
:::

Although **finnes** is historically related to **finne**, do not treat the two as interchangeable.

::: examples
- no: Jeg finner kafeen.
- en: I find the café.
- no: Det finnes en kafé her.
- en: There is a café here.
:::

In the first sentence, a person finds something. In the second, the café exists or is available in that location.

**Common mistake**

**Incorrect for existence:**

::: examples
- no: Det finner en kafé ved biblioteket.
- en: 'Not: There is a café by the library. (for the existence meaning)'
:::

**Correct:**

::: examples
- no: Det finnes en kafé ved biblioteket.
- en: There is a café by the library.
:::

The compact rule is:

- Use **finne** when someone finds something.
- Use **finnes** when something exists or is available.

**Request: distinguish-finnes-from-finne**

**Purpose:** Prevent confusion between existence and the action of finding.

**Context:** Amir searches for useful places and services in his new neighbourhood.

**Learner action:** Supply either a sentence with **finnes** to state or ask about availability, or a sentence with **finne** when a person locates something.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner chooses **finnes** specifically for existence and keeps its lexical **-s**.

{{exercise: distinguish_finnes_from_finne}}

## Møtes: meeting one another {#motes-meeting-one-another role=model objectives=obj-s-verbs-01}

**Møtes** commonly means **meet one another**. The participants are both involved.

::: examples
- no: Amir og Nora møtes på kafeen.
- en: Amir and Nora meet at the café.
- no: Vi møtes etter jobb.
- en: We meet after work.
- no: Skal vi møtes utenfor biblioteket?
- en: Shall we meet outside the library?
- no: De møtes hver fredag.
- en: They meet every Friday.
:::

Compare **møte** and **møtes**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg møter Nora på kafeen.
- en: I meet Nora at the café.
- no: Nora møter meg på kafeen.
- en: Nora meets me at the café.
- no: Vi møtes på kafeen.
- en: We meet each other at the café.
:::

With **møte**, the subject meets a person expressed as the object. With **møtes**, the subject normally includes the people who meet one another.

This mutual meaning is sometimes called **reciprocal**: two or more participants do the same thing in relation to each other. You do not need to classify every sentence with this term. The practical question is simpler: does the subject include all the people who are meeting?

**Common mistake**

**Incorrect:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg møtes Nora etter jobb.
- en: 'Incorrect: I’m meeting Nora after work.'
:::

**Correct alternatives:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg møter Nora etter jobb.
- en: I’m meeting Nora after work.
- no: Nora og jeg møtes etter jobb.
- en: Nora and I are meeting after work.
- no: Vi møtes etter jobb.
- en: We are meeting after work.
:::

Do not combine **møtes** with a direct object naming the other participant. Either use **møte + person**, or include both participants in the subject and use **møtes**.

**Request: build-mutual-meetings-with-motes**

**Purpose:** Practise the participant structure required by **møtes**.

**Context:** Colleagues and friends arrange meetings at different times and places.

**Learner action:** Recast descriptions with separate participants as sentences in which a plural subject uses **møtes**. Include an appropriate meeting place or time where relevant.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner forms mutual meeting statements with all participants represented in the subject and no direct object after **møtes**.

{{exercise: build_mutual_meetings_with_motes}}

## Ses: seeing one another {#ses-seeing-one-another role=model objectives=obj-s-verbs-01}

**Ses** means **see one another**. It is especially common when people talk about meeting again.

::: examples
- no: Vi ses ofte på jobb.
- en: We often see each other at work.
- no: De ses nesten hver dag.
- en: They see each other almost every day.
- no: Når ses vi igjen?
- en: When will we see each other again?
- no: Vi ses i morgen!
- en: See you tomorrow!
:::

The last example is extremely common as a farewell. Grammatically, **vi ses** describes seeing one another, but in context it functions like English **see you**.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Jeg ser Nora på kafeen.
- en: I see Nora at the café.
- no: Nora ser meg på kafeen.
- en: Nora sees me at the café.
- no: Vi ses på kafeen.
- en: We see each other at the café.
:::

As with **møtes**, all participants normally belong in the subject of **ses**.

**Incorrect:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg ses Nora i morgen.
- en: 'Incorrect: I’ll see Nora tomorrow.'
:::

**Correct alternatives:**

::: examples
- no: Jeg ser Nora i morgen.
- en: I’ll see Nora tomorrow.
- no: Nora og jeg ses i morgen.
- en: Nora and I will see each other tomorrow.
- no: Vi ses i morgen.
- en: See you tomorrow.
:::

## Møtes and ses together {#motes-and-ses-together role=contrast objectives=obj-s-verbs-01}

**Møtes** focuses on coming together for a meeting or encounter. **Ses** focuses on seeing one another and is particularly natural when arranging future contact or saying goodbye.

::: examples
- no: Vi møtes på stasjonen klokka åtte.
- en: We’ll meet at the station at eight.
- no: Flott. Vi ses der!
- en: Great. See you there!
- no: Gruppen møtes hver torsdag.
- en: The group meets every Thursday.
- no: Nå jobber vi i samme bygning, så vi ses ofte.
- en: Now we work in the same building, so we see each other often.
:::

There is overlap in ordinary conversation. The goal is not to force a sharp distinction in every situation. Instead, learn their central uses:

- **møtes**: arrange or describe a mutual meeting
- **ses**: see one another; commonly used in farewells and references to future contact

Both are complete s-verbs in these meanings. Their **-s** is not simply an instruction to translate them as English passives.

**Request: select-motes-or-ses-by-intended-meaning**

**Purpose:** Develop control of the central difference between arranging a meeting and referring to seeing one another.

**Context:** Amir and his colleagues make plans, describe regular contact and end short conversations.

**Learner action:** Use **møtes** when the focus is a meeting and **ses** when the focus is seeing one another or saying goodbye.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner selects between the two lexical s-verbs according to communicative meaning.

{{exercise: select_motes_or_ses_by_intended_meaning}}

## One spelling, different grammatical jobs {#one-spelling-different-grammatical-jobs role=contrast objectives=obj-s-verbs-01}

Now compare lexical s-verbs with passive s-forms.

::: examples
- no: Det finnes en kafé her.
- en: There is a café here.
- no: Kafeen stenges klokka seks.
- en: The café is closed at six.
- no: Vi møtes utenfor kafeen.
- en: We meet outside the café.
- no: Bordene vaskes hver kveld.
- en: The tables are washed every evening.
:::

In **finnes** and **møtes**, the full s-verbs carry their established meanings. In **stenges** and **vaskes**, something is being done to the subject.

A useful meaning test is:

1. Does the verb mean having an opinion, feeling comfortable, existing, meeting one another or seeing one another?
2. If so, it is likely one of the lexical s-verbs in this lesson.
3. Does the sentence instead describe an action being done to the subject?
4. If so, the form may be passive.

This is a meaning distinction, not merely a spelling distinction.

**Request: contrast-lexical-s-meaning-with-passive-meaning**

**Purpose:** Consolidate recognition of the lexical **-s** against the learner’s prior knowledge of passive s-forms.

**Context:** A short description covers the café, the people who visit it and routine actions performed there.

**Learner action:** Decide whether each s-ending verb carries one of the target lexical meanings or describes an action being done to the subject. Briefly state the meaning that supports the decision.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner interprets the whole sentence and does not classify the verb from its final letter alone.

{{exercise: contrast_lexical_s_meaning_with_passive_meaning}}

## Back at the café {#back-at-the-cafe role=model objectives=obj-s-verbs-01}

The next day, Amir arrives at the café before Nora. Nora sends him a message.

::: {.reading translation="Sorry, the bus is delayed. Is there an available table?" dialogue_id="back-at-the-cafe" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Beklager, bussen er forsinket. Finnes det et ledig bord?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I’ve found a table by the window. I think it is pleasant here." dialogue_id="back-at-the-cafe" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, jeg har funnet et bord ved vinduet. Jeg synes det er hyggelig her.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great! The others are coming too. We’ll meet there in ten minutes." dialogue_id="back-at-the-cafe" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Så bra! De andre kommer også. Vi møtes der om ti minutter.
:::

When Nora arrives, she asks about Amir’s first weeks in the city.

::: {.reading translation="Are you still happy in the new apartment?" dialogue_id="back-at-the-cafe" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Trives du fortsatt i den nye leiligheten?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, and the neighbours are pleasant. We often see each other in the stairwell." dialogue_id="back-at-the-cafe" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, og naboene er hyggelige. Vi ses ofte i oppgangen.
:::

The same five verbs now serve a connected purpose:

- Nora asks whether a table **exists or is available**: **finnes**
- Amir gives an **opinion**: **synes**
- The group will **meet one another**: **møtes**
- Nora asks whether Amir **feels comfortable** in his home: **trives**
- Amir and his neighbours **see one another** regularly: **ses**

None of these sentences has a passive meaning.

## Transfer to a new situation {#transfer-to-a-new-situation role=recap objectives=obj-s-verbs-01}

Maja has started an evening course. She is writing to her friend Jonas about it. The school, classmates and plans are new, but the same s-verbs remain useful.

::: examples
- no: Jeg trives godt på kurset.
- en: I’m happy on the course.
- no: Jeg synes læreren forklarer tydelig.
- en: I think the teacher explains clearly.
- no: Det finnes et stille leserom i andre etasje.
- en: There is a quiet reading room on the second floor.
- no: Klassen møtes der før timen.
- en: The class meets there before the lesson.
- no: Jonas og jeg går på forskjellige kurs, men vi ses i pausen.
- en: Jonas and I attend different courses, but we see each other during the break.
:::

**Request: transfer-s-verbs-to-an-evening-course**

**Purpose:** Use the target forms outside the original Bergen café situation.

**Context:** Maja describes her evening course, its facilities, her opinion, her comfort in the group and contact between classmates.

**Learner action:** Produce a short connected message containing the five approved s-verbs. Each sentence should express its central meaning naturally; any meeting or seeing sentence should include all mutual participants in the subject.

**Success demonstrates:** The learner transfers the complete lexical s-verbs to a new everyday context without interpreting them as passive forms.

{{exercise: transfer_s_verbs_to_an_evening_course}}
