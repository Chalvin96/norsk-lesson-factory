## Weather small talk {#weather-small-talk role=orient objectives=obj-make-weather-small-talk-01}

Weather gives strangers, neighbours, and colleagues an easy reason to begin a short conversation. You do not need an unusual observation or a detailed forecast. A simple comment can open the exchange. The other person can acknowledge it, add a contrast, and ask one small follow-up question. After that, either person can close politely or keep talking.

## At the bus stop {#at-the-bus-stop role=model objectives=obj-make-weather-small-talk-01}

Nora and Amir live in the same neighbourhood, but they have only spoken once or twice. They meet while waiting for a bus on a cold spring morning.

::: {.reading translation="It is cold today." dialogue_id="at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det er kaldt i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, it really is. But the sun is shining." dialogue_id="at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, virkelig. Men sola skinner.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Fortunately! Do you think it will get warmer later?" dialogue_id="at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Heldigvis! Tror du det blir varmere senere?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I hope so. It was much warmer yesterday." dialogue_id="at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Jeg håper det. Det var mye varmere i går.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, the weather changes quickly now. Is it going to rain this afternoon?" dialogue_id="at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, været skifter fort nå. Skal det regne i ettermiddag?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I don’t think so. But I have an umbrella with me, just in case." dialogue_id="at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Jeg tror ikke det. Men jeg har med paraply, for sikkerhets skyld.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That was sensible. Here comes the bus. Have a nice day!" dialogue_id="at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det var lurt. Der kommer bussen. Ha en fin dag!
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thanks, same to you!" dialogue_id="at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Takk, i like måte!
:::

This conversation is short, but it has a clear shape. Nora opens with an observation. Amir acknowledges it and adds a contrast. Nora asks a follow-up question. They continue for a few turns and then close when the bus arrives.

You can use the same shape in many everyday situations:

**Open → acknowledge or contrast → ask one follow-up → close or continue**

You do not need to use every possible expression. One natural sentence at each stage is enough.

## Opening with a shared observation {#opening-shared-observation role=model objectives=obj-make-weather-small-talk-01}

A weather opening usually comments on something both people can see or feel. Norwegian commonly uses det er for weather descriptions.

::: examples
- no: Det er fint vær i dag.
- en: The weather is nice today.
- no: Det er varmt ute.
- en: It is warm outside.
- no: Det er kaldt i morges.
- en: It is cold this morning.
- no: Det er grått i dag.
- en: It is grey today.
:::

You can also open by saying what the weather is doing.

::: examples
- no: Det regner mye i dag.
- en: It is raining a lot today.
- no: Det snør igjen.
- en: It is snowing again.
- no: Det blåser ganske mye.
- en: It is quite windy.
- no: Sola skinner endelig.
- en: The sun is finally shining.
:::

Words such as i dag, i morges, and endelig make the opening feel connected to the immediate situation.

::: examples
- no: Det er kjølig i dag.
- en: It is chilly today.
- no: Det er kjølig.
- en: It is chilly.
:::

Both sentences are correct, but i dag makes it clearer that you are commenting on today’s shared experience.

A short opening can also show a reaction.

::: examples
- no: Så fint vær!
- en: What lovely weather!
- no: Så kaldt det er!
- en: It is so cold!
:::

These are more expressive than a neutral statement. At this stage, however, a simple det er sentence is usually the safest and most useful opening.

## First checkpoint: make the opening {#first-checkpoint-make-opening role=model objectives=obj-make-weather-small-talk-01}

Imagine that you and Nora are standing outside the same café. It is raining heavily, and both of you are waiting for the door to open. Make one short weather observation to begin a conversation.

This activity belongs here because the first step is noticing a shared condition and turning it into a natural opening. Use only the immediate café context. A successful response would make the weather clear and sound like something one person could comfortably say to another nearby person.

## Acknowledging the observation {#acknowledging-the-observation role=model objectives=obj-make-weather-small-talk-01}

The simplest response shows that you have heard the opening and share the reaction.

::: examples
- no: Ja, virkelig.
- en: Yes, really.
- no: Ja, det er kaldt.
- en: Yes, it is cold.
- no: Ja, det er det.
- en: Yes, it is.
- no: Ja, dessverre.
- en: Yes, unfortunately.
- no: Heldigvis!
- en: Fortunately!
:::

Ja, virkelig works with many kinds of weather comments, so it is useful when you want a natural response without repeating the whole sentence.

::: {.reading translation="It is very windy today." dialogue_id="acknowledge-windy" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det blåser mye i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, really." dialogue_id="acknowledge-windy" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, virkelig.
:::

Ja, det er det refers back to a description with det er.

::: {.reading translation="It is warm in here." dialogue_id="acknowledge-description" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det er varmt her inne.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, it is." dialogue_id="acknowledge-description" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, det er det.
:::

Notice the complete form:

::: examples
- no: Ja, det er det.
- en: Yes, it is.
:::

Do not stop at Ja, det er when you mean Yes, it is. Ja, det er can sound unfinished unless another word follows.

::: examples
- no: Ja, det er varmt.
- en: Yes, it is warm.
- no: Ja, det er det.
- en: Yes, it is.
:::

Both are complete. The first repeats varmt; the second replaces the description with det.

Dessverre and heldigvis add a small personal reaction.

::: {.reading translation="It is raining again." dialogue_id="weather-reaction" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det regner igjen.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, unfortunately." dialogue_id="weather-reaction" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, dessverre.
:::

::: {.reading translation="It is sunny today." dialogue_id="weather-reaction" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det er sol i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, fortunately!" dialogue_id="weather-reaction" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, heldigvis!
:::

## Adding a contrast {#adding-a-contrast role=contrast objectives=obj-make-weather-small-talk-01}

You do not always have to agree completely. A weather exchange often develops through ja, men: yes, but.

::: examples
- no: Ja, men det er ganske varmt.
- en: Yes, but it is quite warm.
- no: Ja, men sola skinner.
- en: Yes, but the sun is shining.
- no: Det er kaldt, men det blåser ikke.
- en: It is cold, but it is not windy.
- no: Det regner, men det er ikke kaldt.
- en: It is raining, but it is not cold.
:::

A contrast should connect directly to the first observation.

::: {.reading translation="The weather is nice today." dialogue_id="adding-contrast" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det er fint vær i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, but it is a little windy." dialogue_id="adding-contrast" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, men det blåser litt.
:::

Amir does not reject Nora’s comment. He acknowledges the pleasant weather and adds another detail. This keeps the tone friendly.

Compare the effect:

::: examples
- no: Nei, det er dårlig vær.
- en: No, the weather is bad.
- no: Ja, men det blåser litt.
- en: Yes, but it is a little windy.
:::

The first response directly contradicts the speaker. It may be appropriate if you strongly disagree, but the second is usually smoother for light small talk.

## Second checkpoint: acknowledge, then add one detail {#second-checkpoint-acknowledge-add-detail role=contrast objectives=obj-make-weather-small-talk-01}

Return to the rainy café. Nora says that it is raining a lot. Respond with a brief acknowledgement and add one contrasting or supporting weather detail from the same scene.

This activity follows the opening because small talk needs a response, not another unrelated statement. A successful response would show that you understood Nora and could connect a second observation with her first one.

## Asking one follow-up question {#asking-one-follow-up-question role=model objectives=obj-make-weather-small-talk-01}

A follow-up question shows interest and gives the other person an easy way to continue. Keep it short and connected to the weather already mentioned.

You can ask what the other person expects.

::: examples
- no: Tror du det blir varmere senere?
- en: Do you think it will get warmer later?
- no: Tror du det slutter å regne snart?
- en: Do you think it will stop raining soon?
- no: Skal det regne i ettermiddag?
- en: Is it going to rain this afternoon?
- no: Blir det sol i morgen?
- en: Will it be sunny tomorrow?
:::

You can ask about the other person’s reaction or preference.

::: examples
- no: Liker du sånt vær?
- en: Do you like this kind of weather?
- no: Synes du det er for kaldt?
- en: Do you think it is too cold?
:::

You can also ask a practical question that fits the immediate situation.

::: examples
- no: Har du paraply?
- en: Do you have an umbrella?
- no: Har du varme klær med deg?
- en: Do you have warm clothes with you?
:::

A good follow-up grows naturally from the opening.

::: {.reading translation="It is raining a lot today." dialogue_id="follow-up-practical" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Det regner mye i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, unfortunately. Do you have an umbrella?" dialogue_id="follow-up-practical" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, dessverre. Har du paraply?
:::

The practical question works because both people are outside in the rain.

::: {.reading translation="It is warm today." dialogue_id="follow-up-expectation" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det er varmt i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, really. Do you think it will be just as warm tomorrow?" dialogue_id="follow-up-expectation" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, virkelig. Tror du det blir like varmt i morgen?
:::

The second sentence continues the same topic without requiring specialist knowledge about the forecast.

Avoid making the follow-up too broad. Hvordan er været? means What is the weather like?, but it sounds unnecessary when both speakers are standing outside and can already see the weather. A more specific question is usually more natural.

::: examples
- no: Tror du det begynner å regne?
- en: Do you think it will start raining?
:::

This asks about a possible change and gives the other person something clear to answer.

## Keeping the answer light {#keeping-the-answer-light role=model objectives=obj-make-weather-small-talk-01}

Weather small talk does not require certainty. You can answer with a brief opinion or hope.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror det.
- en: I think so.
- no: Jeg tror ikke det.
- en: I don’t think so.
- no: Jeg håper det.
- en: I hope so.
- no: Jeg håper ikke det.
- en: I hope not.
- no: Kanskje.
- en: Maybe.
- no: Det vet jeg ikke.
- en: I don’t know.
:::

These short answers can be followed by one weather detail.

::: examples
- no: Jeg tror det. Det blir nok varmere senere.
- en: I think so. It will probably get warmer later.
- no: Jeg tror ikke det. Himmelen er ganske klar.
- en: I don’t think so. The sky is quite clear.
- no: Kanskje. Været skifter fort.
- en: Maybe. The weather changes quickly.
:::

You are not trying to provide an official forecast. You are simply keeping the exchange moving.

## Third checkpoint: build the middle of the exchange {#third-checkpoint-build-middle role=model objectives=obj-make-weather-small-talk-01}

Imagine that Amir comments on the strong wind while you are waiting for a ferry. Acknowledge his observation, add a short contrast, and ask one relevant follow-up question.

This activity combines the middle stages because real small talk often moves from reaction to question in a single turn. The ferry setting gives you a shared outdoor context. A successful response would remain focused on the weather, connect its sentences naturally, and make it easy for Amir to answer.

## Closing or continuing {#closing-or-continuing role=contrast objectives=obj-make-weather-small-talk-01}

A weather exchange can end after only two or three turns. A short closing is not rude when the situation changes, someone needs to leave, or there is nothing more to add.

::: examples
- no: Vi får håpe det blir bedre.
- en: We’ll have to hope it gets better.
- no: Vi får håpe det holder seg tørt.
- en: We’ll have to hope it stays dry.
- no: Godt å komme inn i varmen.
- en: It is good to get inside where it is warm.
- no: Ha en fin dag!
- en: Have a nice day!
- no: Ha en fin tur!
- en: Have a nice trip!
- no: Takk, i like måte!
- en: Thanks, same to you!
:::

The closing can refer back to the weather before moving to a polite farewell.

::: {.reading translation="Do you think it will stop raining soon?" dialogue_id="weather-closing" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Tror du det slutter å regne snart?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I hope so. We’ll have to hope it gets better. Have a nice day!" dialogue_id="weather-closing" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Jeg håper det. Vi får håpe det blir bedre. Ha en fin dag!
:::

If both people want to continue, add another connected question instead of a farewell.

::: {.reading translation="The weather is nice today." dialogue_id="weather-continuing" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det er fint vær i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, finally! Are you going to be outside this afternoon?" dialogue_id="weather-continuing" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, endelig! Skal du være ute i ettermiddag?
:::

The question still grows from the weather observation. It allows the conversation to continue without abruptly changing subject.

## Common problems {#common-problems role=contrast objectives=obj-make-weather-small-talk-01}

**Making the opening too formal**

Været er fint i dag is grammatically correct, but Det er fint vær i dag often sounds more natural as a casual opening.

::: examples
- no: Været er fint i dag.
- en: The weather is nice today.
- no: Det er fint vær i dag.
- en: The weather is nice today.
:::

The first describes the weather directly. The second is a common conversational way to comment on the day.

**Repeating without responding**

If one person says it is cold, simply making another separate weather statement can feel disconnected.

::: {.reading translation="It is cold today." dialogue_id="repeating-without-responding" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det er kaldt i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="It is raining." dialogue_id="repeating-without-responding" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Det regner.
:::

Both statements may be true, but Amir has not clearly acknowledged Nora. A connector makes the relationship clearer.

::: {.reading translation="Yes, and it is raining too." dialogue_id="repeating-with-connector" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, og det regner også.
:::

**Asking an unrelated question too quickly**

A weather opening creates a small shared topic. Stay with it for at least one response.

::: {.reading translation="It is very windy today." dialogue_id="unrelated-question" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det blåser mye i dag.
:::

A question about rain, clothing, or tomorrow’s weather would continue naturally. An abrupt question about an unrelated personal matter may make the opening feel unfinished.

**Giving too much information**

Small talk works well with short turns. A detailed explanation of several days of forecasts can place too much pressure on a new conversation partner. At A1, aim for an acknowledgement, one detail, and one question.

::: examples
- no: Ja, virkelig. Men det er ikke kaldt. Tror du det begynner å regne?
- en: Yes, really. But it is not cold. Do you think it will start raining?
:::

This is enough to show attention and invite a reply.

## Back at the bus stop {#back-at-the-bus-stop role=model objectives=obj-make-weather-small-talk-01}

Later that week, Nora and Amir meet again. This time the weather has changed.

::: {.reading translation="Hi! It is much warmer today." dialogue_id="back-at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Hei! Det er mye varmere i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, fortunately. But it is still a little windy." dialogue_id="back-at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, heldigvis. Men det blåser fortsatt litt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="It does. Do you think it will rain later?" dialogue_id="back-at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Det gjør det. Tror du det blir regn senere?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Maybe. I have an umbrella with me today." dialogue_id="back-at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Kanskje. Jeg har med paraply i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That was sensible. We’ll have to hope it stays dry." dialogue_id="back-at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Det var lurt. Vi får håpe det holder seg tørt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. Here comes the bus again!" dialogue_id="back-at-the-bus-stop" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja. Der kommer bussen igjen!
:::

(Det gjør det means It does and echoes an action verb such as blåser; Ja, det er det means Yes, it is and echoes a description with det er.)

This second exchange reuses the same pattern in a new weather situation. Amir opens. Nora acknowledges and contrasts. Amir asks a follow-up. Nora answers briefly, and Amir moves toward a natural close.
