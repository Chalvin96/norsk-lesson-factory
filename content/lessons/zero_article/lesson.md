## At the language café {#at-the-language-cafe role=orient objectives=obj-zero-article-01}

Amir has recently moved to Trondheim. His neighbor, Nora, has invited him to help at a language café on Saturday.

::: {.reading translation="Hi, Amir! Can you help at the language café on Saturday?" dialogue_id="language_cafe" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Hei, Amir! Kan du hjelpe på språkkafeen på lørdag?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, gladly. I work as a teacher, so I like talking with people." dialogue_id="language_cafe" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Ja, gjerne. Jeg jobber som lærer, så jeg liker å snakke med folk.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great! Which languages do you speak?" dialogue_id="language_cafe" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Flott! Hvilke språk snakker du?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I speak Arabic, English, and a little Norwegian." dialogue_id="language_cafe" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Jeg snakker arabisk, engelsk og litt norsk.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Perfect. We also play football in the park afterwards." dialogue_id="language_cafe" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Perfekt. Vi spiller også fotball i parken etterpå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That suits me well. I play football, but I don’t ski." dialogue_id="language_cafe" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Det passer bra. Jeg spiller fotball, men jeg går ikke på ski.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That doesn’t matter. At the café we have coffee, tea, bread, and fruit." dialogue_id="language_cafe" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Det gjør ingenting. På kafeen har vi kaffe, te, brød og frukt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Should I bring cups?" dialogue_id="language_cafe" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Skal jeg ta med kopper?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, please. We need cups and plates. How are you getting there?" dialogue_id="language_cafe" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, takk. Vi trenger kopper og tallerkener. Hvordan kommer du dit?
:::

::: {.reading translation="I’m coming by bus." dialogue_id="language_cafe" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Jeg kommer med buss.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. I’m coming by bicycle if it doesn’t rain." dialogue_id="language_cafe" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Bra. Jeg kommer på sykkel hvis det ikke regner.
:::

Amir and Nora are discussing work, languages, sports, food, transport, and things needed for an event. Norwegian often uses nouns in these situations without en, ei, or et. This absence is called the **zero article**: the noun has no article before it.

Notice the highlighted pattern in these phrases:

::: examples
- no: jobber som lærer
- en: works as a teacher
- no: snakker norsk
- en: speaks Norwegian
- no: spiller fotball
- en: plays football
- no: har kaffe
- en: has coffee
- no: med buss
- en: by bus
- no: trenger kopper
- en: needs cups
:::

English sometimes uses an article where Norwegian normally does not, especially with occupations: I am **a** teacher, but Jeg er lærer.

## Roles and occupations {#roles-and-occupations role=model objectives=obj-zero-article-01}

When you say what role or occupation someone has, Norwegian commonly uses no article after være or after expressions such as jobbe som.

::: examples
- no: Amir er lærer.
- en: Amir is a teacher.
- no: Nora er student.
- en: Nora is a student.
- no: Faren min er lege.
- en: My father is a doctor.
- no: Hun jobber som kokk.
- en: She works as a cook.
- no: Jeg vil bli journalist.
- en: I want to become a journalist.
:::

The noun identifies the person’s role. It does not introduce one separate, countable person or thing.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Sara er lege.
- en: Sara is a doctor.
- no: Sara snakker med en lege.
- en: Sara is talking to a doctor.
:::

In the first sentence, lege names Sara’s occupation. In the second, en lege is another person whom Sara is talking to.

A compact rule:

::: {.rule}
**Use no article when a common role or occupation tells what someone is, becomes, or works as.**
:::

A common mistake is to transfer the English article directly:

::: examples
- no: Jeg er lærer.
- en: I am a teacher.
:::

Not normally: Jeg er en lærer.

An article can appear when the noun phrase contains more description:

::: examples
- no: Hun er en dyktig lærer.
- en: She is a skilled teacher.
:::

For this lesson, concentrate on the basic role pattern: Hun er lærer.

**Practice request: identify-role-zero-article**

**Purpose:** Notice when a noun names someone’s role rather than another person or object.
**Context:** Short descriptions of people at a community event, including occupations and conversations with other workers.
**Learner action:** Identify the sentences in which the role noun should stand without an article.
**Success demonstrates:** Recognition of the zero-article role pattern after expressions such as er, blir, and jobber som.

{{exercise: identify_role_zero_article}}

**Practice request: produce-common-role**

**Purpose:** Retrieve the role pattern instead of translating the English article.
**Context:** Brief profiles of people and their occupations.
**Learner action:** Complete each Norwegian profile with the given occupation, using the appropriate article pattern.
**Success demonstrates:** Production of common role nouns without en, ei, or et.

{{exercise: produce_common_role}}

## Languages {#languages role=model objectives=obj-zero-article-01}

Names of languages normally have no article when you say which language someone speaks, understands, reads, writes, or learns.

::: examples
- no: Jeg snakker norsk.
- en: I speak Norwegian.
- no: Amir forstår engelsk.
- en: Amir understands English.
- no: Nora lærer spansk.
- en: Nora is learning Spanish.
- no: Vi leser norsk på skolen.
- en: We read Norwegian at school.
- no: Skriver du arabisk?
- en: Do you write Arabic?
:::

A learner can also say Hun øver på norsken sin (She is practising her Norwegian), but there the noun has the definite suffix -en, so this is not a zero-article example. The central pattern is simpler:

::: examples
- no: Hun lærer norsk.
- en: She is learning Norwegian.
:::

Do not add et before a language name:

::: examples
- no: De snakker tysk.
- en: They speak German.
:::

Not: De snakker et tysk.

**Practice request: complete-language-activity**

**Purpose:** Connect zero-article language names with familiar communication verbs.
**Context:** Visitors at the language café describe what they speak, understand, read, and learn.
**Learner action:** Complete each statement with a supplied language name in natural Norwegian.
**Success demonstrates:** Correct omission of articles before language names.

{{exercise: complete_language_activity}}

**Practice request: contrast-role-and-language**

**Purpose:** Keep two zero-article uses distinct while retrieving both accurately.
**Context:** Short introductions such as a person’s occupation followed by languages they use.
**Learner action:** Build two-sentence introductions from profile information.
**Success demonstrates:** Zero article with a common role and with language names. Scoring should focus on the language noun; the role pattern provides context and review.

{{exercise: contrast_role_and_language}}

## Sports and activities {#sports-and-activities role=model objectives=obj-zero-article-01}

Norwegian normally uses no article after spille when naming a sport or game.

::: examples
- no: Vi spiller fotball.
- en: We play football.
- no: Barna spiller håndball.
- en: The children play handball.
- no: Spiller du tennis?
- en: Do you play tennis?
- no: Han spiller sjakk med naboen.
- en: He plays chess with the neighbor.
:::

The taught expressions gå på ski and trene karate also use the sport name without an article.

::: examples
- no: Nora går på ski om vinteren.
- en: Nora skis in winter.
- no: Amir trener karate.
- en: Amir practises karate.
:::

Compare the activity with a physical object:

::: examples
- no: Barna spiller fotball.
- en: The children play football.
- no: Amir kjøper en fotball.
- en: Amir buys a football.
:::

In the first sentence, fotball names the sport. In the second, en fotball is one ball.

The useful meaning question is: **Am I naming the sport, or one physical object?**

**Practice request: distinguish-sport-from-object**

**Purpose:** Prevent automatic insertion of an article before a sport name.
**Context:** People prepare for activities in a park and also collect equipment.
**Learner action:** Complete sentences so that each noun either names an activity or one piece of equipment, according to the context.
**Success demonstrates:** Zero article when the noun names a sport. References to physical objects serve only as contrast.

{{exercise: distinguish_sport_from_object}}

**Practice request: describe-sport-habits**

**Purpose:** Move from recognition to short personal-style statements without requiring open speaking assessment.
**Context:** Written profiles describing what different people do after work or school.
**Learner action:** Write brief Norwegian statements from supplied profile facts about sports and games.
**Success demonstrates:** Accurate zero-article sport names in complete sentences.

{{exercise: describe_sport_habits}}

## Mass nouns: food, drink, and other substances {#mass-nouns-food-drink-and-other-substances role=model objectives=obj-zero-article-01}

A **mass noun** names something treated as a substance or material rather than as separate countable units. Common examples include water, coffee, bread, rice, and cheese. Norwegian normally uses no article when the noun refers either to a substance generally or to an unspecified quantity of that substance rather than one countable serving or unit.

::: examples
- no: Vi har kaffe og te.
- en: We have coffee and tea.
- no: Jeg drikker vann.
- en: I drink water.
- no: Hun kjøper ost.
- en: She buys cheese.
- no: De spiser brød til frokost.
- en: They eat bread for breakfast.
- no: Amir lager ris til middagen.
- en: Amir is making rice for dinner.
:::

Compare an unspecified amount of a substance with one specified unit:

::: examples
- no: Nora drikker kaffe.
- en: Nora drinks coffee.
- no: Nora bestiller en kaffe.
- en: Nora orders a coffee.
:::

In everyday café language, en kaffe means one serving or cup of coffee. Kaffe without an article can mean coffee generally or an unspecified amount of coffee as a drink or substance.

Compare general and specific reference:

::: examples
- no: Jeg liker kaffe.
- en: I like coffee.
- no: Kaffen er varm.
- en: The coffee is hot.
:::

The first sentence refers to coffee generally. Earlier examples such as **Vi har kaffe og te** refer to unspecified quantities in a situation. The second sentence refers to particular coffee already known in the situation. This lesson teaches zero article for both generic substance reference and unspecified non-count quantities.

A compact rule:

::: {.rule}
**Use no article when food, drink, or material is presented as an unspecified substance rather than one countable item.**
:::

**Practice request: recognize-mass-noun-meaning**

**Purpose:** Link article choice to meaning, not merely to memorized vocabulary.
**Context:** Food and drink at the language café, with generic preferences, unspecified quantities of supplies, and particular servings.
**Learner action:** Decide where a mass noun refers to a substance generally or to an unspecified non-count quantity and should appear without an article.
**Success demonstrates:** Recognition of the zero article for generic substance reference and unspecified non-count quantities.

{{exercise: recognize_mass_noun_meaning}}

**Practice request: complete-cafe-supplies**

**Purpose:** Produce several mass nouns naturally in one coherent context.
**Context:** Nora checks what food and drink the café already has and what it still needs.
**Learner action:** Complete inventory statements using supplied mass nouns.
**Success demonstrates:** Omission of articles before unspecified food and drink substances.

{{exercise: complete_cafe_supplies}}

## Taught transport expressions {#taught-transport-expressions role=model objectives=obj-zero-article-01}

Several common transport expressions use med plus a transport noun without an article. This lesson teaches med buss, med tog, med bil, and med fly. Bicycle travel normally uses på sykkel.

::: examples
- no: Jeg kommer med buss.
- en: I’m coming by bus.
- no: Vi reiser med tog.
- en: We travel by train.
- no: Hun drar til jobb med bil.
- en: She goes to work by car.
- no: De kommer med fly.
- en: They are coming by plane.
- no: Nora kommer på sykkel.
- en: Nora is coming by bicycle.
:::

In the taught expressions with med, the noun names the mode of transport. It does not point to one particular bus, train, car, or plane. På sykkel is a separate fixed expression for bicycle travel.

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Amir kommer med buss.
- en: Amir is coming by bus.
- no: Amir sitter på bussen.
- en: Amir is sitting on the bus.
:::

Med buss answers the question of how he travels. På bussen says where he is and refers to a particular bus.

Another contrast:

::: examples
- no: Vi reiser med tog.
- en: We travel by train.
- no: Toget går klokka åtte.
- en: The train leaves at eight.
:::

Do not copy the English structure word for word. Use med in the taught expressions med buss, med tog, med bil, and med fly, but use på in på sykkel.

**Practice request: select-transport-mode-form**

**Purpose:** Select among the taught zero-article transport expressions.
**Context:** Travel plans to the café using **med buss**, **med tog**, **med bil**, **med fly**, and **på sykkel**. The particular-vehicle examples above remain an unscored recognition contrast.
**Learner action:** Complete each sentence with the supplied taught transport expression required by the travel plan.
**Success demonstrates:** Correct use of **med buss**, **med tog**, **med bil**, **med fly**, and **på sykkel**.

{{exercise: select_transport_mode_form}}

**Practice request: write-journey-plans**

**Purpose:** Retrieve the taught transport expressions across several everyday destinations.
**Context:** People explain how they will travel to work, school, the station, or a social event, using med buss, med tog, med bil, med fly, or på sykkel.
**Learner action:** Write complete Norwegian journey statements from supplied person, destination, and transport information, choosing among the taught expressions.
**Success demonstrates:** Productive use of med buss, med tog, med bil, med fly, or på sykkel as appropriate.

{{exercise: write_journey_plans}}

## Indefinite plural nouns {#indefinite-plural-nouns role=model objectives=obj-zero-article-01}

You already know that a singular countable noun can use en, ei, or et:

::: examples
- no: Jeg kjøper en bok.
- en: I am buying a book.
:::

When the noun is plural and means some unspecified people or things, Norwegian uses no article.

::: examples
- no: Jeg kjøper bøker.
- en: I am buying books.
- no: Vi trenger kopper.
- en: We need cups.
- no: Nora tar med tallerkener.
- en: Nora is bringing plates.
- no: Det kommer studenter på lørdag.
- en: Students are coming on Saturday.
- no: Amir kjøper epler.
- en: Amir buys apples.
:::

English may use some, but Norwegian often leaves the noun phrase without an equivalent word.

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger stoler.
- en: We need some chairs.
- no: Hun har venner i Bergen.
- en: She has friends in Bergen.
:::

The noun must still have the correct plural form. Zero article does not mean that the noun stays singular.

::: examples
- no: Vi trenger kopper.
- en: We need cups.
:::

Not: Vi trenger kopp when the meaning is more than one cup.

Compare the basic number contrast:

::: examples
- no: Jeg tar med en kopp.
- en: I am bringing a cup.
- no: Jeg tar med kopper.
- en: I am bringing cups.
:::

A compact rule:

::: {.rule}
**Use no article before an indefinite plural noun when you mean unspecified people or things.**
:::

**Practice request: change-singular-to-indefinite-plural**

**Purpose:** Coordinate plural formation with removal of the singular article.
**Context:** A preparation list changes because the language café expects more visitors.
**Learner action:** Rewrite singular statements so that they refer to several unspecified items.
**Success demonstrates:** Correct plural noun forms with no article.

{{exercise: change_singular_to_indefinite_plural}}

**Practice request: supply-indefinite-plural-items**

**Purpose:** Produce zero-article plurals from meaning rather than through mechanical deletion alone.
**Context:** Volunteers report which objects, foods, and participants are needed for an event.
**Learner action:** Complete full sentences using supplied plural meanings.
**Success demonstrates:** Natural indefinite plural noun phrases without en, ei, et, or an unnecessary equivalent of some.

{{exercise: supply_indefinite_plural_items}}

## One meaning, several zero-article patterns {#one-meaning-several-zero-article-patterns role=contrast objectives=obj-zero-article-01}

The same surface pattern—no article—serves several related purposes. In each case, the noun is not being introduced as one unspecified countable item.

**Meaning:** basic role after er

::: examples
- no: Nora er student.
- en: Nora is a student.
:::

**Meaning:** language with a taught communication verb

::: examples
- no: Hun lærer norsk.
- en: She is learning Norwegian.
:::

**Meaning:** sport in a taught activity expression

::: examples
- no: Hun spiller håndball.
- en: She plays handball.
:::

**Meaning:** mass noun

::: examples
- no: Hun drikker te.
- en: She drinks tea.
:::

**Meaning:** taught transport expression

::: examples
- no: Hun kommer med buss.
- en: She comes by bus.
:::

**Meaning:** indefinite plural

::: examples
- no: Hun kjøper kopper.
- en: She buys cups.
:::

Do not use one mechanical test for every noun. Ask what the noun is doing in the sentence:

- Does it name a person’s basic role after er or blir, or after jobber som?
- Is it a language name used with one of the taught communication verbs, such as snakker, forstår, leser, skriver, or lærer?
- Does it name a sport or game in one of the taught activity expressions, such as spiller fotball, spiller sjakk, går på ski, or trener karate?
- Is it an unspecified substance?
- Is it one of the taught transport expressions, such as med buss or på sykkel?
- Is it an indefinite plural?

If the noun matches one of these taught constructions and meanings, the approved pattern in this lesson uses no article.

**Practice request: classify-zero-article-context**

**Purpose:** Consolidate the six meanings before freer sentence production.
**Context:** Short statements about volunteers, their skills, travel, activities, refreshments, and supplies.
**Learner action:** Identify why each zero-article noun is natural in its sentence.
**Success demonstrates:** Accurate recognition of the intended zero-article context. This activity scores context recognition, not noun-form production.

{{exercise: classify_zero_article_context}}

**Practice request: repair-unnecessary-articles**

**Purpose:** Address English-influenced errors across the whole lesson.
**Context:** Amir has drafted a message about the event and has inserted articles before several roles, languages, sports, mass nouns, nouns in the taught transport expressions, and plurals.
**Learner action:** Rewrite each affected sentence in natural Bokmål, removing only articles that are unnecessary in the target patterns.
**Success demonstrates:** Accurate correction of unnecessary articles without deleting articles from ordinary singular countable nouns.

{{exercise: repair_unnecessary_articles}}

## Back at the café {#back-at-the-cafe role=contrast objectives=obj-zero-article-01}

On Saturday morning, Amir sends Nora a message.

::: {.reading translation="Hi! I’m coming by bus and will be there at ten. I’m bringing cups and apples." dialogue_id="back_at_cafe" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Hei! Jeg kommer med buss og er der klokka ti. Jeg har med kopper og epler.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Great. Fatima is a cook, and she is bringing bread and cheese." dialogue_id="back_at_cafe" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Supert. Fatima er kokk, og hun tar med brød og ost.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Does she speak Norwegian?" dialogue_id="back_at_cafe" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Snakker hun norsk?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, she speaks Norwegian and French. Her brother is coming too. He is a student." dialogue_id="back_at_cafe" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, hun snakker norsk og fransk. Broren hennes kommer også. Han er student.
:::

::: {.reading translation="What are Fatima and her brother going to do afterwards?" dialogue_id="back_at_cafe" speaker_id="amir" speaker_name="Amir" character_id="amir"}
Hva skal Fatima og broren hennes gjøre etterpå?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Fatima is going to play football, but her brother is going to play chess." dialogue_id="back_at_cafe" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Fatima skal spille fotball, men broren hennes skal spille sjakk.
:::

The scene now contains all six patterns:

- Fatima er kokk. — common role
- Hun snakker norsk og fransk. — languages
- Fatima spiller fotball. — sport
- Hun tar med brød og ost. — mass nouns
- Jeg kommer med buss. — taught transport expression
- Jeg har med kopper og epler. — indefinite plurals

## Cumulative transfer: planning a study evening {#cumulative-transfer-planning-a-study-evening role=recap objectives=obj-zero-article-01}

The situation now changes. A group of students is planning an evening at the library. They need to introduce the participants, mention the languages they use, plan a break activity, organize food and drink, explain how they will travel, and list the materials they need.

**Practice request: build-study-evening-message**

**Purpose:** Transfer all six taught zero-article patterns to a new everyday purpose.

**Learner action:** Write connected Norwegian sentences from supplied facts, using common roles, languages, sports or games, mass nouns, the taught transport expressions, and indefinite plurals.
**Success demonstrates:** Integrated use of no article across the approved patterns. Scoring should focus on article omission and the supplied transport expression; other familiar grammar provides sentence context.

{{exercise: build_study_evening_message}}

**Practice request: contrast-one-item-and-general-category**

**Purpose:** Confirm that zero article depends on meaning rather than on the noun alone.

**Learner action:** Complete paired statements so that each noun phrase matches the intended singular-item, general, or plural meaning.
**Success demonstrates:** Correct zero-article use in the approved contexts while retaining an indefinite article for one countable item.

{{exercise: contrast_one_item_and_general_category}}

## Short retrieval review {#short-retrieval-review role=recap objectives=obj-zero-article-01}

Without looking back, write one Norwegian sentence for each of these purposes: identify an occupation, name a language someone speaks, name a sport someone plays, mention an unspecified food or drink, say that someone travels med buss, med tog, med bil, med fly, or på sykkel, and mention several unspecified objects.

Then reread your six sentences. Check that target nouns have no article and that indefinite plural nouns use correct plural forms.
