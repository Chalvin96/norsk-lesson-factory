## A new key at the office {#a-new-key-at-the-office role=orient objectives=obj-ha-or-fa-for-possession-and-acquisition-01}

Nora has recently started a new job. On Monday morning, she meets her colleague Henrik near the entrance.

::: {.reading translation="Do you have a key to the main entrance?" dialogue_id="office-key-opening" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Har du nøkkel til hoveddøra?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I have a key. Didn’t you get one on Friday?" dialogue_id="office-key-opening" speaker_id="henrik" speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Ja, jeg har nøkkel. Fikk du ikke en på fredag?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No. I got an access card, but I didn’t get a key." dialogue_id="office-key-opening" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Nei. Jeg fikk adgangskort, men jeg fikk ikke nøkkel.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then you need to talk to Fatima. She has the keys for the new employees." dialogue_id="office-key-opening" speaker_id="henrik" speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Da må du snakke med Fatima. Hun har nøklene til de nye ansatte.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Will I get the key straight away?" dialogue_id="office-key-opening" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Får jeg nøkkelen med én gang?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Probably. When you get it, you can keep it on the same key ring as your house key." dialogue_id="office-key-opening" speaker_id="henrik" speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Sannsynligvis. Når du får den, kan du ha den på samme nøkkelknippe som husnøkkelen.
:::

A few minutes later, Nora finds Fatima.

::: {.reading translation="Hi, Fatima. I have an access card, but I don’t have a key to the main entrance." dialogue_id="office-key-handoff" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Hei, Fatima. Jeg har adgangskort, men jeg har ikke nøkkel til hoveddøra.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s right. You’re getting the key now." dialogue_id="office-key-handoff" speaker_id="fatima" speaker_name="Fatima" voice_profile="feminine"}
Det stemmer. Du får nøkkelen nå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Thanks! Now I have both a card and a key." dialogue_id="office-key-handoff" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Takk! Nå har jeg både kort og nøkkel.
:::

The key changes hands only once, but Nora talks about it in two different ways. Before Fatima gives it to her, Nora does not **have** it. At the moment it comes into her possession, she **gets** it. Afterwards, she **has** it.

That contrast is the focus of this chapter:

- **ha** describes possession: someone has or possesses something.
- **få** describes receiving or acquiring something: something comes into someone’s possession.

## Possession with ha {#possession-with-ha role=model objectives=obj-ha-or-fa-for-possession-and-acquisition-01}

Use **ha** when the important point is that a person possesses something or has it available.

The present-tense form is **har**.

::: examples
- no: Henrik har nøkkel.
- en: Henrik has a key.
- no: Nora har adgangskort.
- en: Nora has an access card.
- no: Fatima har nøklene.
- en: Fatima has the keys.
:::

These sentences describe a current situation. They do not tell us when or how Henrik, Nora, or Fatima obtained the objects.

The negative form works in the same way:

::: examples
- no: Nora har ikke nøkkel.
- en: Nora does not have a key.
- no: Vi har ikke kaffe.
- en: We do not have any coffee.
- no: Amir har ikke bil.
- en: Amir does not have a car.
:::

Questions about possession also use **har**:

::: examples
- no: Har du penn?
- en: Do you have a pen?
- no: Har dere billetter?
- en: Do you have tickets?
- no: Har hun en kopi av kontrakten?
- en: Does she have a copy of the contract?
:::

In each case, the speaker wants to know what is already in someone’s possession or available to them.

## Receiving or acquiring with få {#receiving-or-acquiring-with-fa role=model objectives=obj-ha-or-fa-for-possession-and-acquisition-01}

Use **få** when the important point is that someone receives or acquires something. The object was not in that person’s possession before, but it comes to them.

The present-tense form is **får**.

::: examples
- no: Nora får en nøkkel.
- en: Nora gets a key.
- no: Vi får nye datamaskiner på kontoret.
- en: We are getting new computers at the office.
- no: Barna får is etter middagen.
- en: The children are getting ice cream after dinner.
:::

You will also see **fikk** in the dialogue and examples when receipt happened earlier. Recognize it as a past form of **få**; you will not need to choose between **får** and **fikk** in the activities.

Questions with **få** ask about receiving or acquiring:

::: examples
- no: Får jeg en nøkkel i dag?
- en: Will I get a key today?
- no: Får de nye ansatte adgangskort?
- en: Do the new employees get access cards?
- no: Fikk du pakken i går?
- en: Did you get the package yesterday?
:::

Compare this with a question using **ha**:

::: examples
- no: Har du pakken?
- en: Do you have the package?
- no: Fikk du pakken?
- en: Did you get the package?
:::

The first question asks about the present possession of the package. The second asks whether the package reached the person.

## The same object, two viewpoints {#same-object-two-viewpoints role=contrast objectives=obj-ha-or-fa-for-possession-and-acquisition-01}

The easiest way to understand the contrast is to follow one object across time.

**Before receiving it**

::: examples
- no: Nora har ikke nøkkel.
- en: Nora does not have a key.
:::

**At the receiving event**

::: examples
- no: Nora får en nøkkel av Fatima.
- en: Nora gets a key from Fatima.
:::

**After receiving it**

::: examples
- no: Nå har Nora en nøkkel.
- en: Now Nora has a key.
:::

The middle sentence marks the acquisition. The first and third describe possession at different times.

Here is the same pattern with a package:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har ikke pakken ennå.
- en: I do not have the package yet.
- no: Jeg får pakken i ettermiddag.
- en: I am getting the package this afternoon.
- no: Nå har jeg pakken.
- en: Now I have the package.
:::

And with concert tickets:

::: examples
- no: Sondre har ikke billetter.
- en: Sondre does not have tickets.
- no: Han får to billetter av en venn.
- en: He gets two tickets from a friend.
- no: Etterpå har han to billetter.
- en: Afterwards, he has two tickets.
:::

Norwegian therefore distinguishes the **state of possession** from the **event of receiving or acquiring**. Ask yourself one practical question:

**Is the sentence about what someone possesses, or about something coming to them?**

If it is about possession, choose **ha**. If it is about receiving or acquiring, choose **få**.

## Aligned contrasts {#aligned-contrasts role=contrast objectives=obj-ha-or-fa-for-possession-and-acquisition-01}

Study these pairs. Each pair refers to the same kind of object, but the viewpoint changes.

**Money**

::: examples
- no: Lea har fem hundre kroner.
- en: Lea has five hundred kroner.
- no: Lea får fem hundre kroner for arbeidet.
- en: Lea gets five hundred kroner for the work.
:::

The first sentence describes the money Lea possesses. The second describes money coming to her as payment.

**A room**

::: examples
- no: Vi har et rom på hotellet.
- en: We have a room at the hotel.
- no: Vi får et rom med utsikt.
- en: We are getting a room with a view.
:::

The first sentence describes the reservation or room available to us. The second focuses on which room is assigned to us.

**A document**

::: examples
- no: Daniel har rapporten.
- en: Daniel has the report.
- no: Daniel får rapporten etter møtet.
- en: Daniel will get the report after the meeting.
:::

One sentence tells us where the report is now. The other tells us when it will come to Daniel.

**A gift**

::: examples
- no: Mina har en ny klokke.
- en: Mina has a new watch.
- no: Mina fikk en ny klokke til bursdagen.
- en: Mina got a new watch for her birthday.
:::

The first sentence describes current possession. The second presents the watch as something she received.

**A place to sit**

::: examples
- no: Jeg har en plass ved vinduet.
- en: I have a seat by the window.
- no: Jeg fikk en plass ved vinduet.
- en: I got a seat by the window.
:::

Both can be true. **Har** describes the seat that is mine or available to me now. **Fikk** tells us that the seat was assigned or given to me.

## Checkpoint: switch viewpoint {#checkpoint-switch-viewpoint role=contrast objectives=obj-ha-or-fa-for-possession-and-acquisition-01}

The learner must be able to view the same situation first as a change and then as a resulting state. A successful response would demonstrate a consistent shift from acquisition to possession without changing the basic facts.

## Sources and times often support få {#sources-and-times-often-support-fa role=model objectives=obj-ha-or-fa-for-possession-and-acquisition-01}

A sentence with **få** often includes information about where something comes from or when it arrives.

::: examples
- no: Jeg fikk boka av en venn.
- en: I got the book from a friend.
- no: Nora får nøkkelen av Fatima.
- en: Nora gets the key from Fatima.
- no: Vi får svar fra banken i morgen.
- en: We will get an answer from the bank tomorrow.
- no: De fikk pakken på mandag.
- en: They got the package on Monday.
:::

Expressions such as **av en venn**, **fra banken**, **i morgen**, and **på mandag** fit naturally with an event of receiving.

However, these expressions are clues, not mechanical rules. The meaning still decides the verb.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har boka fra kurset.
- en: I have the book from the course.
:::

This sentence describes the book currently in my possession. **Fra kurset** identifies it; it does not necessarily describe the moment I received it.

Likewise, a sentence with **ha** can contain a time expression if it describes possession during that time.

::: examples
- no: Jeg har bilen hele uka.
- en: I have the car all week.
:::

The speaker possesses or has use of the car throughout the week. Nothing in the sentence focuses on acquiring it.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-ha-or-fa-for-possession-and-acquisition-01}

**Using ha for the moment of receiving**

A learner may produce:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har en gave fra kollegaene mine i går.
- en: I have a gift from my colleagues yesterday.
:::

Suppose the intended situation is that the colleagues handed the gift to the speaker yesterday. That explicit context establishes a receiving event; **i går** only locates it in time and does not by itself determine the choice between possession and acquisition. In this example, the receiving event is expressed with **fikk**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg fikk en gave fra kollegaene mine i går.
- en: I got a gift from my colleagues yesterday.
:::

If the speaker wants to describe present possession, **har** is appropriate, but the sentence needs a present-state meaning:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har en gave fra kollegaene mine på kontoret.
- en: I have a gift from my colleagues at the office.
:::

**Using få for something already possessed**

A learner may say:

::: examples
- no: Jeg får nøkkelen i lomma.
- en: I get the key in my pocket.
:::

If the intended meaning is that the key is currently in the speaker’s possession and located in the pocket, use **har**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg har nøkkelen i lomma.
- en: I have the key in my pocket.
:::

For the possession-versus-receiving contrast taught here, use **får** when the intended meaning is that the speaker receives the key. Other constructions with **få** may express other meanings, so **Jeg får nøkkelen i lomma** is not inherently ungrammatical outside the intended static-possession reading:

::: examples
- no: Jeg får nøkkelen av resepsjonisten.
- en: I get the key from the receptionist.
:::

**Treating får as a general replacement for har**

English **get** has many uses, but in this lesson **få** is tied to receiving or acquiring something. Do not choose **får** merely because an English translation could contain **get**. Look at the Norwegian situation.

::: examples
- no: Har du billett?
- en: Do you have a ticket?
:::

This asks whether the ticket is already in the person’s possession.

::: examples
- no: Får du billett?
- en: Are you getting a ticket?
:::

This asks whether a ticket will come to the person.

**Forgetting the result after receiving**

Once a person receives an object, the next description normally changes to **ha** if the focus is possession.

::: examples
- no: I går fikk jeg et nytt adgangskort. Nå har jeg det i lommeboka.
- en: Yesterday I got a new access card. Now I have it in my wallet.
:::

Repeating **få** would continue to present new receiving events. It would not simply describe the card remaining in the wallet.

This practice brings together the two viewpoints in a fresh setting while keeping the same communicative problem as Nora’s office key. A successful response would demonstrate that the learner can follow one object through changing circumstances and select the verb according to possession or acquisition at each moment.

## Back at the office {#back-at-the-office role=recap objectives=obj-ha-or-fa-for-possession-and-acquisition-01}

At the end of the day, Henrik sees Nora near the entrance again.

::: {.reading translation="Did you get the key?" dialogue_id="office-key-closing" speaker_id="henrik" speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Fikk du nøkkelen?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes, I got it from Fatima. Now I have it on my key ring." dialogue_id="office-key-closing" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Ja, jeg fikk den av Fatima. Nå har jeg den på nøkkelknippet mitt.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Good. Do you have everything you need now?" dialogue_id="office-key-closing" speaker_id="henrik" speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Bra. Har du alt du trenger nå?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Almost. I’m getting a computer tomorrow." dialogue_id="office-key-closing" speaker_id="nora" speaker_name="Nora" character_id="nora"}
Nesten. Jeg får en datamaskin i morgen.
:::

Nora’s answer moves naturally between the two verbs. **Fikk** reports the completed transfer of the key. **Har** describes her present possession. **Får** presents the computer as something that will come to her tomorrow.

A successful review will keep the object and situation consistent while changing the verb to match possession or acquisition.
