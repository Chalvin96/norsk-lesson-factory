## A message before the journey {#a-message-before-the-journey role=orient}

Mina is meeting her colleague Jonas at Oslo Central Station. They are travelling to Lillehammer for a work seminar, but the train information has changed.

::: {.reading translation="Do you know which platform the train leaves from?" dialogue_id="station" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Vet du hvilket spor toget går fra?
:::

::: {.reading translation="No, but I can check where we need to go." dialogue_id="station" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nei, men jeg kan sjekke hvor vi skal gå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Can you also find out whether the train is delayed?" dialogue_id="station" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Kan du også finne ut om toget er forsinket?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Yes. It doesn’t say here why the departure has been changed." dialogue_id="station" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Ja. Det står ikke her hvorfor avgangen er endret.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I wonder whether we have time to buy coffee." dialogue_id="station" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Jeg lurer på om vi har tid til å kjøpe kaffe.
:::

::: {.reading translation="First we need to find out where platform 12 is." dialogue_id="station" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Først må vi finne ut hvor spor 12 er.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Wait, now I see it. I know where we need to go!" dialogue_id="station" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Vent, nå ser jeg det. Jeg vet hvor vi skal!
:::

Mina and Jonas need information, but most of their sentences are not direct questions. Mina does not simply ask:

::: examples
- no: Hvilket spor går toget fra?
- en: Which platform does the train leave from?
:::

Instead, she places that question inside a larger sentence:

::: examples
- no: Vet du hvilket spor toget går fra?
- en: Do you know which platform the train leaves from?
:::

This kind of question inside another sentence is an **embedded question (indirekte spørresetning)**. Embedded questions use Norwegian subordinate-clause word order.

## Direct and embedded questions {#direct-and-embedded-questions role=model objectives=obj-embedded-questions-01}

A direct question stands on its own and asks someone for an answer.

::: examples
- no: Hvor er spor 12?
- en: Where is platform 12?
- no: Når kommer toget?
- en: When does the train arrive?
- no: Hvorfor er avgangen endret?
- en: Why has the departure been changed?
:::

An embedded question forms part of a larger sentence. It can follow expressions such as:

::: examples
- no: Vet du …
- en: Do you know …
- no: Kan du sjekke …
- en: Can you check …
- no: Jeg lurer på …
- en: I wonder …
- no: Vi må finne ut …
- en: We need to find out …
- no: Hun forklarte …
- en: She explained …
:::

Compare the direct and embedded versions:

**Direct question**

::: examples
- no: Hvor er spor 12?
- en: Where is platform 12?
:::

**Embedded question**

::: examples
- no: Vet du hvor spor 12 er?
- en: Do you know where platform 12 is?
:::

**Direct question**

::: examples
- no: Når kommer toget?
- en: When does the train arrive?
:::

**Embedded question**

::: examples
- no: Kan du sjekke når toget kommer?
- en: Can you check when the train arrives?
:::

**Direct question**

::: examples
- no: Hvorfor er avgangen endret?
- en: Why has the departure been changed?
:::

**Embedded question**

::: examples
- no: Jeg lurer på hvorfor avgangen er endret.
- en: I wonder why the departure has been changed.
:::

Notice the change in word order. In a direct question, the finite verb often comes before the subject:

::: examples
- no: Hvor er spor 12?
- en: Where is platform 12?
:::

In the embedded question, the subject comes before the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: Vet du hvor spor 12 er?
- en: Do you know where platform 12 is?
:::

For the taught cases where the question phrase is not the subject, the compact rule is:

::: {.rule}
Question word + subject + finite verb
:::

::: examples
- no: hvor + spor 12 + er
- en: where + platform 12 + is
- no: når + toget + kommer
- en: when + the train + arrives
- no: hvorfor + avgangen + er
- en: why + the departure + is
:::

When the question phrase is itself the subject, use this branch:

::: {.rule}
Subject question phrase + som + finite verb
:::

::: examples
- no: hvilket tog + som + stopper
- en: which train + that + stops
:::

The larger sentence may itself be a question:

::: examples
- no: Vet du hvor spor 12 er?
- en: Do you know where platform 12 is?
:::

However, the question inside it still has subordinate-clause word order. The opening words Vet du make the whole sentence a question; they do not turn hvor spor 12 er into a direct question.

**Request name: notice-embedded-question-order**

The learner should compare direct questions about Mina and Jonas’s journey with corresponding sentences beginning with expressions such as Vet du, Kan du sjekke, and Jeg lurer på. The learner identifies which version uses correct subject–verb order inside the embedded question.

This belongs here because the first essential step is noticing that direct-question inversion disappears inside the larger sentence when the question phrase is not the subject. The travel context keeps attention on meaningful information: platform, departure time, and delay. A successful response demonstrates recognition of question word + subject + finite verb in these non-subject cases.

## Question words stay in place {#question-words-stay-in-place role=model objectives=obj-embedded-questions-01}

When a direct question begins with a question word, the embedded question normally keeps that same question word.

::: examples
- no: hvor
- en: where
- no: når
- en: when
- no: hvorfor
- en: why
- no: hvordan
- en: how
- no: hvem
- en: who
- no: hva
- en: what
- no: hvilket spor
- en: which platform
:::

When the question phrase is not the subject, the important change is the word order after the question word. When it is the subject, the separate som pattern applies.

**Where**

::: examples
- no: Hvor bor Jonas?
- en: Where does Jonas live?
- no: Mina vet hvor Jonas bor.
- en: Mina knows where Jonas lives.
:::

**When**

::: examples
- no: Når begynner seminaret?
- en: When does the seminar begin?
- no: De må sjekke når seminaret begynner.
- en: They need to check when the seminar begins.
:::

**Why**

::: examples
- no: Hvorfor er toget forsinket?
- en: Why is the train delayed?
- no: Ingen forklarer hvorfor toget er forsinket.
- en: No one explains why the train is delayed.
:::

**How**

::: examples
- no: Hvordan kommer de seg til hotellet?
- en: How do they get to the hotel?
- no: Jonas undersøker hvordan de kommer seg til hotellet.
- en: Jonas investigates how they can get to the hotel.
:::

**Which**

::: examples
- no: Hvilket tog stopper på Hamar?
- en: Which train stops at Hamar?
- no: Kan du se hvilket tog som stopper på Hamar?
- en: Can you see which train stops at Hamar?
:::

The final pair contains som because hvilket tog is itself the subject of the embedded question. No direct-question inversion is removed in this pair; the embedded form uses subject question phrase + som + finite verb. At this stage, the learner only needs to use natural complete forms, not classify different kinds of subjects.

## A common mistake: keeping direct-question inversion {#common-mistake-direct-question-inversion role=contrast objectives=obj-embedded-questions-01}

A frequent mistake is to put the verb before the subject inside the embedded question.

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Vet du hvor er spor 12?
- en: 'Incorrect: Do you know where is platform 12?'
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Vet du hvor spor 12 er?
- en: Do you know where platform 12 is?
:::

Incorrect:

::: examples
- no: Kan du sjekke når kommer toget?
- en: 'Incorrect: Can you check when arrives the train?'
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Kan du sjekke når toget kommer?
- en: Can you check when the train arrives?
:::

The incorrect versions copy the order of the direct questions Hvor er spor 12? and Når kommer toget? Once those questions are placed after Vet du or Kan du sjekke, the subject must come before the finite verb.

English sometimes helps because standard English makes a similar contrast:

- Direct English: *Where is the station?*
- Embedded English: *Do you know where the station is?*

Still, Norwegian word order must be learned from the Norwegian sentence itself. The reliable check is to locate the embedded question word and then look for the subject before the finite verb.

**Request name: change-direct-to-embedded-question**

The learner should turn direct questions from the station scene into embedded questions after a supplied opening such as Mina vil vite, Jonas sjekker, or Vet du. The information requested remains the same, but the learner must change the word order. Using the subject-question pattern taught above, the learner should also convert one supplied subject question, such as Hvilket tog stopper på Hamar?, into an embedded question with som.

This activity belongs after the direct–embedded contrast because it requires active control rather than recognition. A successful response demonstrates that the learner can preserve the question word and meaning while moving the subject before the finite verb in non-subject cases, and can correctly produce subject question phrase + som + finite verb in the supplied subject-question case.

{{exercise: change_direct_to_embedded_question}}

## Questions answered with yes or no: use om {#questions-answered-with-yes-or-no-use-om role=model objectives=obj-embedded-questions-01}

Some direct questions do not begin with a question word. They can normally be answered with yes or no.

::: examples
- no: Er toget forsinket?
- en: Is the train delayed?
- no: Har de nok tid?
- en: Do they have enough time?
- no: Stopper toget på Hamar?
- en: Does the train stop at Hamar?
:::

To embed this kind of question, use **om**, meaning whether or if.

::: examples
- no: Kan du sjekke om toget er forsinket?
- en: Can you check whether the train is delayed?
- no: Mina lurer på om de har nok tid.
- en: Mina wonders whether they have enough time.
- no: Vet du om toget stopper på Hamar?
- en: Do you know whether the train stops at Hamar?
:::

Again, the embedded question has subject before finite verb:

::: examples
- no: om + toget + er
- en: whether + the train + is
- no: om + de + har
- en: whether + they + have
- no: om + toget + stopper
- en: whether + the train + stops
:::

Compare the two types:

**Information sought: A specific place**

**Direct question**

::: examples
- no: Hvor er hotellet?
- en: Where is the hotel?
:::

**Embedded question**

::: examples
- no: Vet du hvor hotellet er?
- en: Do you know where the hotel is?
:::

**Information sought: Whether something is true**

**Direct question**

::: examples
- no: Er hotellet langt unna?
- en: Is the hotel far away?
:::

**Embedded question**

::: examples
- no: Vet du om hotellet er langt unna?
- en: Do you know whether the hotel is far away?
:::

**Information sought: A specific time**

**Direct question**

::: examples
- no: Når begynner møtet?
- en: When does the meeting begin?
:::

**Embedded question**

::: examples
- no: Jeg lurer på når møtet begynner.
- en: I wonder when the meeting begins.
:::

**Information sought: Whether something will happen**

**Direct question**

::: examples
- no: Begynner møtet snart?
- en: Does the meeting begin soon?
:::

**Embedded question**

::: examples
- no: Jeg lurer på om møtet begynner snart.
- en: I wonder whether the meeting begins soon.
:::

Use a question word when the missing information is where, when, why, how, who, what, or which. Use om when the underlying question asks whether something is true.

**Request name: select-question-word-or-om**

The learner should complete short embedded questions about the journey by deciding whether the missing link is a question word already implied by the context or om for a yes-or-no question. The scored goal is choosing the correct way to introduce the embedded question; word order appears in the sentences as supporting context.

This checkpoint belongs here because learners must first distinguish the kind of information being requested before they can build the clause. A successful response demonstrates understanding that om introduces an embedded yes-or-no question, while specific missing information requires the relevant question word.

## Negation inside an embedded question {#negation-inside-an-embedded-question role=contrast objectives=obj-embedded-questions-01}

Norwegian subordinate-clause word order also affects words such as ikke. In a main clause, ikke usually follows the finite verb.

::: examples
- no: Toget stopper ikke på Hamar.
- en: The train does not stop at Hamar.
:::

Inside an embedded question, ikke comes before the finite verb.

::: examples
- no: Vet du hvorfor toget ikke stopper på Hamar?
- en: Do you know why the train does not stop at Hamar?
:::

Compare:

::: examples
- no: Hvorfor stopper ikke toget på Hamar?
- en: Why doesn’t the train stop at Hamar?
- no: Vet du hvorfor toget ikke stopper på Hamar?
- en: Do you know why the train doesn’t stop at Hamar?
:::

In the direct question Hvorfor stopper ikke toget på Hamar?, the sequence is:

::: {.rule}
question word + finite verb + ikke + subject
:::

::: examples
- no: hvorfor + stopper + ikke + toget
- en: why + stops + not + the train
:::

The embedded question has:

::: {.rule}
question word + subject + ikke + finite verb
:::

::: examples
- no: hvorfor + toget + ikke + stopper
- en: why + the train + not + stops
:::

The same pattern appears after om:

::: examples
- no: Kommer ikke Mina på seminaret?
- en: Isn’t Mina coming to the seminar?
- no: Vet du om Mina ikke kommer på seminaret?
- en: Do you know whether Mina isn’t coming to the seminar?
:::

In natural conversation, the exact meaning of a negative question depends on context. The grammatical point here is narrower: when ikke belongs inside an embedded question, it comes before the finite verb.

More examples:

::: examples
- no: Jeg forstår ikke hvorfor bussen ikke kommer.
- en: I don’t understand why the bus isn’t coming.
- no: Kan du sjekke om hotellet ikke serverer frokost på søndager?
- en: Can you check whether the hotel doesn’t serve breakfast on Sundays?
- no: Hun spurte hvorfor døren ikke åpnet seg.
- en: She asked why the door didn’t open.
:::

Do not confuse an ikke in the larger sentence with an ikke inside the embedded question:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vet ikke hvor hotellet er.
- en: I don’t know where the hotel is.
:::

Here, ikke belongs to Jeg vet ikke. The embedded question itself is hvor hotellet er.

::: examples
- no: Jeg vet hvor hotellet ikke ligger.
- en: I know where the hotel is not located.
:::

Here, ikke belongs inside hvor hotellet ikke ligger. This sentence has a different meaning.

**Request name: place-negation-in-embedded-question**

The learner should reformulate negative direct questions as embedded questions in short travel-related situations. The learner must place ikke before the finite verb inside the embedded part.

This activity belongs after basic subject–verb order is secure because negation adds one more element to the same subordinate-clause pattern. A successful response demonstrates the sequence question word or om + subject + ikke + finite verb. The activity scores placement of negation; choosing between question words and om is contextual support rather than the primary scored goal.

{{exercise: place_negation_in_embedded_question}}

## Statements that contain questions {#statements-that-contain-questions role=model objectives=obj-embedded-questions-01}

An embedded question does not have to appear after a direct request. It can be part of a statement.

::: examples
- no: Jeg vet hvor hotellet er.
- en: I know where the hotel is.
- no: Vi må finne ut når resepsjonen stenger.
- en: We need to find out when reception closes.
- no: Jonas husker ikke hvilket rom møtet er i.
- en: Jonas does not remember which room the meeting is in.
- no: Mina er usikker på om de har bestilt lunsj.
- en: Mina is unsure whether they have ordered lunch.
- no: Ingen forstår hvorfor programmet er endret.
- en: No one understands why the schedule has been changed.
:::

The whole sentence is a statement, so it normally ends with a full stop. Its embedded part still represents information that could be requested with a direct question.

Direct question:

::: examples
- no: Hvor er hotellet?
- en: Where is the hotel?
:::

Statement containing an embedded question:

::: examples
- no: Jeg vet ikke hvor hotellet er.
- en: I don’t know where the hotel is.
:::

Direct question:

::: examples
- no: Har de bestilt lunsj?
- en: Have they ordered lunch?
:::

Statement containing an embedded question:

::: examples
- no: Vi må finne ut om de har bestilt lunsj.
- en: We need to find out whether they have ordered lunch.
:::

The punctuation belongs to the whole sentence. Compare:

::: examples
- no: Vet du hvor hotellet er?
- en: Do you know where the hotel is?
- no: Jeg vet hvor hotellet er.
- en: I know where the hotel is.
:::

Both sentences contain hvor hotellet er. Only the first sentence is itself a direct question.

**Request name: complete-embedded-information-statement**

The learner should complete short statements reporting what Mina and Jonas know, do not know, remember, or need to find out. Each prompt supplies an everyday information gap from the trip, and the learner produces a complete embedded question with subordinate-clause word order.

This belongs here because it prevents the learner from associating embedded questions only with sentences beginning Vet du. A successful response demonstrates use of the same embedded structure inside statements as well as requests.

## Returning to Mina and Jonas {#returning-to-mina-and-jonas role=contrast objectives=obj-embedded-questions-01}

Mina and Jonas reach the correct platform. A little later, Jonas prepares a message for the seminar organizer.

::: {.reading translation="Now we know which platform the train leaves from, but do you know when we arrive?" dialogue_id="journey-update" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Nå vet vi hvor toget går fra, men vet du når vi kommer fram?
:::

::: {.reading translation="The app says that we arrive at ten past eleven. I wonder whether the organizer knows that the train is delayed." dialogue_id="journey-update" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Appen sier at vi kommer fram klokka ti over elleve. Jeg lurer på om arrangøren vet at toget er forsinket.
:::

::: {.reading translation="I can send a message and ask where we should meet the others." dialogue_id="journey-update" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Jeg kan sende en melding og spørre hvor vi skal møte de andre.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Also ask whether we have to go straight to the hotel." dialogue_id="journey-update" speaker_id="mina" speaker_name="Mina" character_id="mina"}
Spør også om vi må gå rett til hotellet.
:::

A moment later, the organizer replies.

::: {.reading translation="She is replying now. She explains where we should meet the others and clarifies whether we have to go straight to the hotel. She also explains where the bus stops, but she doesn’t say why it doesn’t go all the way to the hotel." dialogue_id="journey-update" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Hun svarer nå. Hun forklarer hvor vi skal møte de andre og avklarer om vi må gå rett til hotellet. Hun forklarer også hvor bussen stopper, men hun sier ikke hvorfor den ikke går helt til hotellet.
:::

The situation has moved forward, but the same structure continues to be useful. Mina and Jonas use embedded questions to organize information rather than asking a series of isolated direct questions.

Notice the target forms:

::: examples
- no: når vi kommer fram
- en: when we arrive
- no: om arrangøren vet
- en: whether the organizer knows
- no: hvor vi skal møte de andre
- en: where we should meet the others
- no: om vi må gå rett til hotellet
- en: whether we have to go straight to the hotel
:::

These two forms first express the requested information and then reappear in Jonas’s report of the organizer’s answer.

::: examples
- no: hvor bussen stopper
- en: where the bus stops
- no: hvorfor den ikke går helt til hotellet
- en: why it does not go all the way to the hotel
:::

In each case, the subject precedes the finite verb. When ikke occurs inside the final embedded question, it precedes the finite verb går.

## A practical editing check {#a-practical-editing-check role=contrast objectives=obj-embedded-questions-01}

When writing an embedded question, use this short procedure:

1. Identify the underlying direct question.
2. Decide whether it begins with a question word or is a yes-or-no question.
3. Keep the question word, or add om for a yes-or-no question.
4. If the question phrase is not the subject, put the subject before the finite verb. If the question phrase is the subject, use subject question phrase + som + finite verb.
5. If ikke belongs inside the embedded question, put it before the finite verb.
6. Give the whole sentence punctuation that matches the larger sentence.

For example, begin with:

::: examples
- no: Hvorfor går ikke bussen til hotellet?
- en: Why doesn’t the bus go to the hotel?
:::

Place it after Hun forklarer:

::: examples
- no: Hun forklarer hvorfor bussen ikke går til hotellet.
- en: She explains why the bus does not go to the hotel.
:::

The embedded sequence is:

::: examples
- no: hvorfor + bussen + ikke + går
- en: why + the bus + not + goes
:::

For a yes-or-no question:

::: examples
- no: Går bussen til hotellet?
- en: Does the bus go to the hotel?
:::

Place it after Vet du:

::: examples
- no: Vet du om bussen går til hotellet?
- en: Do you know whether the bus goes to the hotel?
:::

The embedded sequence is:

::: examples
- no: om + bussen + går
- en: whether + the bus + goes
:::

This checkpoint belongs after all parts of the pattern have been introduced. It asks the learner to diagnose and correct a likely real-world error rather than build every sentence from scratch. A successful response demonstrates reliable correction to question word or om + subject + optional ikke + finite verb.

## Transfer to a new situation {#transfer-to-a-new-situation role=recap}

The next day, Mina is preparing to leave the hotel. She needs to ask reception about breakfast, checkout, and transport to the station. The setting has changed, but the information problem is similar.

Possible direct questions include:

::: examples
- no: Når slutter frokosten?
- en: When does breakfast end?
- no: Kan jeg la kofferten stå her?
- en: Can I leave my suitcase here?
- no: Hvor stopper flybussen?
- en: Where does the airport bus stop?
- no: Hvorfor virker ikke nøkkelkortet?
- en: Why doesn’t the key card work?
:::

Mina can make these questions part of longer, more connected sentences:

::: examples
- no: Kan du fortelle meg når frokosten slutter?
- en: Can you tell me when breakfast ends?
- no: Jeg lurer på om jeg kan la kofferten stå her.
- en: I wonder whether I can leave my suitcase here.
- no: Vet du hvor flybussen stopper?
- en: Do you know where the airport bus stops?
- no: Kan du sjekke hvorfor nøkkelkortet ikke virker?
- en: Can you check why the key card doesn’t work?
:::

The grammatical decision remains unchanged. A question word is retained when Mina seeks specific information. Om introduces an embedded yes-or-no question. The subject comes before the finite verb, and an internal ikke comes before that verb.

**Request name: use-embedded-questions-at-hotel-reception**

The learner should turn a short set of Mina’s direct information needs into natural sentences addressed to hotel reception. Some underlying questions seek specific information and therefore retain a question word; others are yes-or-no questions and require om. At least one prompt should contain negation inside the embedded question.

This transfer activity belongs at the end because it combines the lesson’s decisions in a new everyday setting without changing the learner outcome. A successful response demonstrates independent use of embedded questions with Norwegian subordinate-clause word order. Selection of question word or om, subject–verb order, and placement of internal ikke should each be visible, while the primary scored evidence goal is production of the complete embedded structure.
