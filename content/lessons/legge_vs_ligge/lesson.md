## Where are the cabin keys? {#cabin-keys role=orient objectives=obj-legge-vs-ligge-01}

Maja and Henrik are preparing a cabin for friends who will arrive later that evening. They are carrying in food, making the beds, and trying to remember where everything belongs.

:::

:::

::: {.reading translation="There are already some towels lying there." dialogue_id="cabin-keys" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}

:::

This small search depends on one important contrast. In the patterns taught here, Maja and Henrik use **legge** when someone puts or places a suitable object somewhere. They use **ligge** to describe the location of objects and places that naturally occur with **ligge**.

## An action and its result {#action-and-result role=contrast objectives=obj-legge-vs-ligge-01}

Compare these two sentences:

::: examples
- no: Henrik legger nøkkelen i skålen.
- en: Henrik puts the key in the bowl.
- no: Nøkkelen ligger i skålen.
- en: The key is in the bowl.
:::

In the first sentence, Henrik performs an action. He moves the key to a place. In the second sentence, nobody is moving anything. The sentence tells us the key’s location.

For the patterns and referents taught here, a useful question is therefore:

- Is someone **putting the object somewhere**? Use **legge**.
- Are you **describing that object’s location with the corresponding location pattern**? Use **ligge**.

The usual present forms are **legger** and **ligger**:

::: examples
- no: Jeg legger boka på bordet.
- en: I put the book on the table.
- no: Boka ligger på bordet.
- en: The book is on the table.
:::

Notice how the sentence pattern changes. With **legge**, the person doing the action normally comes first, followed by the object being moved:

::: examples
- no: Sara legger mobilen i veska.
- en: Sara puts the phone in her bag.
:::

With **ligge**, the object itself is normally the subject:

::: examples
- no: Mobilen ligger i veska.
- en: The phone is in the bag.
:::

English often uses *put* for the action and simply *be* for the resulting location. Norwegian frequently uses **ligge** in location statements where a natural English translation contains *is* or *are*. However, not every English *is* or *are* location statement uses **ligge** in Norwegian. You do not always need to translate **ligge** as *lie*.

::: examples
- no: Apoteket ligger ved siden av banken.
- en: The pharmacy is next to the bank.
- no: Dokumentene ligger på skrivebordet.
- en: The documents are on the desk.
- no: Hytta ligger nær sjøen.
- en: The cabin is near the sea.
:::

All three supplied examples use **ligge** to describe location. Nothing is being placed or moved in them.

## Following the object through a situation {#following-the-object role=model objectives=obj-legge-vs-ligge-01}

The two verbs often appear together when we describe an action and then its result:

::: examples
- no: Jeg legger kartet på bordet. Nå ligger det ved siden av nøkkelen.
- en: I put the map on the table. Now it is next to the key.
- no: Maja legger maten i kjøleskapet. Etterpå ligger osten og grønnsakene på øverste hylle.
- en: Maja puts the food in the refrigerator. Afterwards, the cheese and vegetables are on the top shelf.
- no: Vi legger teppene på sofaen. Der ligger de klare til gjestene kommer.
- en: We put the blankets on the sofa. There they are, ready for the guests to arrive.
:::

For the supplied, **ligge**-compatible referents, this action-result sequence is a practical way to remember the contrast:

**legge → placing the referent somewhere**

**ligge → its resulting or existing location**

In these taught patterns, a **ligge**-compatible object of **legge** can become the subject of **ligge**:

::: examples
- no: Henrik legger nøkkelen i skålen.
- en: Henrik puts the key in the bowl.
- no: Nøkkelen ligger i skålen.
- en: The key is in the bowl.
- no: Maja legger håndklærne i skapet.
- en: Maja puts the towels in the cupboard.
- no: Håndklærne ligger i skapet.
- en: The towels are in the cupboard.
:::

The location phrase may remain almost identical. What changes is the perspective: first the action, then the resulting location.

## Commands and questions {#commands-and-questions role=contrast objectives=obj-legge-vs-ligge-01}

In the placement patterns taught here, use **legge** when asking someone to put the supplied object somewhere. The command form is **legg**:

::: examples
- no: Legg nøkkelen i skålen.
- en: Put the key in the bowl.
- no: Legg posten på kjøkkenbordet.
- en: Put the mail on the kitchen table.
- no: Ikke legg de våte klærne på senga.
- en: Don’t put the wet clothes on the bed.
:::

Questions about the same taught placement action also use **legge**:

::: examples
- no: Hvor skal jeg legge håndklærne?
- en: Where should I put the towels?
- no: Kan du legge brødet i skapet?
- en: Can you put the bread in the cupboard?
- no: Hvor pleier dere å legge duken?
- en: Where do you usually put the tablecloth?
:::

For the **ligge**-compatible referents used here, questions about an existing location use **ligge**:

::: examples
- no: Hvor ligger nøkkelen?
- en: Where is the key?
- no: Ligger kartet i skuffen?
- en: Is the map in the drawer?
- no: Hvorfor ligger jakka på gulvet?
- en: Why is the jacket on the floor?
:::

Compare the two kinds of question directly:

::: examples
- no: Hvor skal jeg legge kartet?
- en: Where should I put the map?
- no: Hvor ligger kartet?
- en: Where is the map?
:::

The first speaker has the map and needs to choose a destination. The second speaker is trying to find it.

## Present, past, and completed actions {#time-frames role=contrast objectives=obj-legge-vs-ligge-01}

The same meaning distinction continues in other time frames. In the past tense (**preteritum**), **legge** becomes **la**, while **ligge** becomes **lå**.

::: examples
- no: Jeg la nøkkelen på bordet.
- en: I put the key on the table.
- no: Nøkkelen lå på bordet.
- en: The key was on the table.
:::

The vowel mark matters in writing: **la** and **lå** are different forms with different meanings.

Compare another pair:

::: examples
- no: Maja la håndklærne på benken.
- en: Maja put the towels on the counter.
- no: Håndklærne lå på benken hele ettermiddagen.
- en: The towels were on the counter all afternoon.
:::

For a completed action connected to the present, the forms are **har lagt** and **har ligget**:

::: examples
- no: Henrik har lagt nøkkelen i jakkelomma.
- en: Henrik has put the key in his jacket pocket.
- no: Nøkkelen har ligget i jakkelomma hele dagen.
- en: The key has been in his jacket pocket all day.
:::

For these taught forms and **ligge**-compatible referents, ask whether the sentence focuses on putting something somewhere or on its location.

::: examples
- no: Hvem har lagt sekken her?
- en: Who has put the backpack here?
- no: Hvor lenge har sekken ligget her?
- en: How long has the backpack been here?
:::

The first question asks about the person responsible for an action. The second asks about the duration of a location.

## Using ligge for an action {#mistake-ligge-for-action role=contrast objectives=obj-legge-vs-ligge-01}

A learner may produce:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg ligger boka på bordet.
- en: ✗ I put the book on the table.
:::

This is not the intended form because the speaker is doing something to **boka**. The correct sentence is:

::: examples
- no: Jeg legger boka på bordet.
- en: I put the book on the table.
:::

In this taught placement pattern, use **legge** when the sentence includes both a person performing the action and an object being placed.

## Using legge for a location {#mistake-legge-for-location role=contrast objectives=obj-legge-vs-ligge-01}

Another common error is:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Boka legger på bordet.
- en: ✗ The book is on the table.
:::

The book is not performing an action, and the sentence only describes its location. Use **ligge**:

::: examples
- no: Boka ligger på bordet.
- en: The book is on the table.
:::

## Choosing from the English translation alone {#english-translation role=contrast objectives=obj-legge-vs-ligge-01}

English *is* does not automatically point to one specific Norwegian verb. Look at the meaning of the whole situation:

::: examples
- no: Hotellet ligger i sentrum.
- en: The hotel is in the city centre.
:::

In this example, **hotellet** is a referent naturally described with **ligger**.

Likewise, English *lay* can be confusing because it may refer to different time frames and meanings. Build the Norwegian sentence from the action-location contrast instead:

::: examples
- no: Hun la brevet på pulten.
- en: She put the letter on the desk.
- no: Brevet lå på pulten.
- en: The letter was on the desk.
:::

## Forgetting the object after legge {#missing-object role=contrast objectives=obj-legge-vs-ligge-01}

Because **legge** means putting something somewhere, the listener usually needs to know what is being put down.

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jeg legger på kjøkkenbordet.
- en: ✗ I put on the kitchen table.
:::

Without context, this feels incomplete. Name the object or use a pronoun:

::: examples
- no: Jeg legger pakken på kjøkkenbordet.
- en: I put the package on the kitchen table.
- no: Jeg legger den på kjøkkenbordet.
- en: I put it on the kitchen table.
:::

By contrast, **ligge** does not take an object being moved:

::: examples
- no: Pakken ligger på kjøkkenbordet.
- en: The package is on the kitchen table.
:::

## At work {#at-work role=model objectives=obj-legge-vs-ligge-01}

The distinction is not limited to household objects. It is useful at work, while travelling, and when talking about places.

::: examples
- no: Kan du legge kontrakten på skrivebordet mitt?
- en: Can you put the contract on my desk?
- no: Kontrakten ligger allerede der.
- en: The contract is already there.
- no: Jeg la notatene i den blå mappa.
- en: I put the notes in the blue folder.
- no: Notatene lå i feil mappe i går.
- en: The notes were in the wrong folder yesterday.
:::

## While travelling {#while-travelling role=model objectives=obj-legge-vs-ligge-01}

::: examples
- no: Hvor skal vi legge bagasjen?
- en: Where should we put the luggage?
- no: Bagasjen ligger på hylla over setene.
- en: The luggage is on the shelf above the seats.
- no: Jeg har lagt passet i sidelomma på sekken.
- en: I have put the passport in the side pocket of the backpack.
- no: Passet har ligget der siden vi dro hjemmefra.
- en: The passport has been there since we left home.
:::

## Describing places {#describing-places role=model objectives=obj-legge-vs-ligge-01}

::: examples
- no: Museet ligger bak rådhuset.
- en: The museum is behind the town hall.
- no: Leiligheten ligger i tredje etasje.
- en: The apartment is on the third floor.
:::

In these supplied place descriptions, **museet** and **leiligheten** are referents naturally described with **ligge**. No placement action is expressed.
