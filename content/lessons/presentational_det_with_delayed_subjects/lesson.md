## At the community centre {#at-the-community-centre role=orient}

Maja is opening a community centre for the morning. Jonas, the manager, is upstairs preparing a meeting room.

::: {.reading translation="Hi, Jonas. There’s a man standing by reception. Is he waiting for you?" dialogue_id="community-centre-morning" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Hei, Jonas. Det står en mann ved resepsjonen. Venter han på deg?
:::

::: {.reading translation="Maybe. An electrician is coming today." dialogue_id="community-centre-morning" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Kanskje. Det kommer en elektriker i dag.
:::

::: {.reading translation="There’s a package lying on the counter too." dialogue_id="community-centre-morning" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Det ligger en pakke på disken også.
:::

::: {.reading translation="It’s for the kitchen." dialogue_id="community-centre-morning" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Den er til kjøkkenet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="And there’s a woman sitting in the meeting room." dialogue_id="community-centre-morning" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Og det sitter en dame i møterommet.
:::

::: {.reading translation="That’s probably the new teacher. I’m coming downstairs." dialogue_id="community-centre-morning" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}
Det er sikkert den nye læreren. Jeg kommer ned.
:::

Maja is not telling Jonas what familiar people and objects are doing. She is bringing several new participants into the conversation: first **en mann**, then **en elektriker**, **en pakke**, and **en dame**.

Notice what comes before each new participant:

::: examples
- no: Det står en mann ved resepsjonen.
- en: There’s a man standing by reception.
- no: Det kommer en elektriker i dag.
- en: An electrician is coming today.
- no: Det ligger en pakke på disken.
- en: There’s a package lying on the counter.
- no: Det sitter en dame i møterommet.
- en: There’s a woman sitting in the meeting room.
:::

This is a presentational sentence (**presenteringssetning**). It lets you present someone or something that is new to the conversation.

## The meaning choice {#the-meaning-choice role=model objectives=obj-presentational-det-with-delayed-subjects-01}

Use this pattern when you want the listener to notice a new person or thing: **det + verb + new indefinite subject**.

::: examples
- no: Det står en mann ved resepsjonen.
- en: There’s a man standing by reception.
:::

Here, **det** fills the usual subject position at the beginning of the main clause. The person being introduced, **en mann**, comes after the verb.

The new subject is indefinite: **en mann**, not **mannen**; **ei pakke**, not **pakka**; **et bud**, not **budet**. The speaker is introducing one member of a group rather than referring to an already identified individual.

English often translates presentational **det** with “there,” not “it”:

::: examples
- no: Det er en kafé i første etasje.
- en: There is a café on the first floor.
- no: Det står en sykkel foran døra.
- en: There is a bicycle standing in front of the door.
- no: Det ligger et brev på bordet.
- en: There is a letter lying on the table.
- no: Det sitter en katt i vinduet.
- en: There is a cat sitting in the window.
- no: Det kommer en buss snart.
- en: A bus is coming soon.
:::

The Norwegian verb can give a more exact picture than English “there is.” **Står** presents something upright or standing, **ligger** presents something lying somewhere, and **sitter** presents a person or animal sitting. **Kommer** presents someone or something arriving.

::: {.rule}
When a person or thing is new to the conversation, use **det**, then the verb, then the new indefinite subject: **Det + verb + en/ei/et + noun**
:::

## New subject or familiar subject? {#new-subject-or-familiar-subject role=contrast objectives=obj-presentational-det-with-delayed-subjects-01}

Compare these two situations.

**Someone new appears**

Maja looks toward the entrance and sees someone Jonas does not know about.

::: examples
- no: Det står en elektriker ved døra.
- en: There’s an electrician standing by the door.
:::

The electrician is new to the conversation, so Maja presents him after the verb.

**They are already discussing the electrician**

Jonas has asked where the expected electrician is.

::: examples
- no: Elektrikeren står ved døra.
- en: The electrician is standing by the door.
:::

Now **elektrikeren** is already identified. It can be the ordinary subject before the verb.

The contrast is not simply between correct and incorrect grammar. Both sentences are possible, but they organize the message differently. With a new participant:

::: examples
- no: Det ligger en pakke på disken.
- en: There’s a package on the counter.
- no: Det sitter en kunde i gangen.
- en: There’s a customer sitting in the corridor.
- no: Det kommer et bud snart.
- en: A courier is coming soon.
:::

With a familiar participant:

::: examples
- no: Pakka ligger på disken.
- en: The package is on the counter.
- no: Kunden sitter i gangen.
- en: The customer is sitting in the corridor.
- no: Budet kommer snart.
- en: The courier is coming soon.
:::

In the new-participant sentences, the speaker introduces **en pakke**, **en kunde**, and **et bud**. In the familiar-participant sentences, both speakers can already identify the package, customer, or courier.

**One person across two sentences**

A presentational sentence often begins a short chain:

::: examples
- no: Det står en mann ved resepsjonen. Han har en stor koffert.
- en: There’s a man standing by reception. He has a large suitcase.
:::

The first sentence introduces **en mann**. After that, **han** can refer back to him.

Likewise:

::: examples
- no: Det ligger et brev på bordet. Brevet er til Jonas.
- en: There’s a letter on the table. The letter is for Jonas.
:::

The first mention is indefinite, **et brev**. Once the letter has been introduced, the second sentence can use the definite form **brevet**.

The target choice in this chapter is the first step: using **det + verb + an indefinite subject** to introduce the participant.

## Building the sentence {#building-the-sentence role=model objectives=obj-presentational-det-with-delayed-subjects-01}

The word order is stable in a simple main clause:

1. **Det**
2. the verb
3. the new indefinite subject
4. other information, such as place or time

::: examples
- no: Det står en bil utenfor.
- en: There’s a car standing outside.
- no: Det sitter en student på kontoret.
- en: There’s a student sitting in the office.
- no: Det ligger ei jakke på stolen.
- en: There’s a jacket lying on the chair.
- no: Det kommer et tog om ti minutter.
- en: A train is coming in ten minutes.
- no: Det er en ledig plass her.
- en: There is a free seat here.
:::

The noun phrase may contain extra information, but it remains indefinite:

::: examples
- no: Det står en høy mann ved vinduet.
- en: There’s a tall man standing by the window.
- no: Det ligger en blå mappe på disken.
- en: There’s a blue folder lying on the counter.
- no: Det kommer et nytt bud i ettermiddag.
- en: A new courier is coming this afternoon.
:::

The whole phrase—**en høy mann**, **en blå mappe**, **et nytt bud**—comes after the verb.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes role=contrast objectives=obj-presentational-det-with-delayed-subjects-01}

**Leaving out det**

Incorrect for an ordinary statement:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Er en pakke på disken.
- en: Is a package on the counter?
:::

Correct:

::: examples
- no: Det er en pakke på disken.
- en: There is a package on the counter.
:::

Norwegian needs **det** in the subject position. Without it, the opening **er** looks like the beginning of a question.

**Putting the new subject before the verb**

::: examples
- no: En mann står ved resepsjonen.
- en: A man is standing by reception.
:::

This sentence is grammatical, but it does not give the same neutral presentation. It makes **en mann** the starting point of the message.

If your communicative purpose is “Look—there is someone new here,” use:

::: examples
- no: Det står en mann ved resepsjonen.
- en: There’s a man standing by reception.
:::

**Using a definite noun for a new participant**

For the intended meaning, avoid:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Det står mannen ved resepsjonen.
- en: There is the man standing by reception.
:::

If Jonas does not yet know about the person, say:

::: examples
- no: Det står en mann ved resepsjonen.
- en: There’s a man standing by reception.
:::

If both speakers already know which man they mean, use an ordinary subject:

::: examples
- no: Mannen står ved resepsjonen.
- en: The man is standing by reception.
:::

**Translating det as “it”**

In this pattern, **det** usually does not point to a particular object.

::: examples
- no: Det sitter en hund utenfor.
- en: There’s a dog sitting outside.
:::

This does not mean “It is sitting a dog outside.” Translate the whole presentational pattern according to its function.

## Back at reception {#back-at-reception role=orient}

Jonas comes downstairs. More people and things are arriving.

Det står en varebil utenfor nå.
:::

::: {.reading translation="Then the electrician is here." dialogue_id="back-at-reception" speaker_id="jonas" speaker_name="Jonas" character_id="jonas"}

:::

::: {.reading translation="There’s a courier coming up the stairs too." dialogue_id="back-at-reception" speaker_id="maja" speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}

Maja again uses presentational sentences for first mentions: **en varebil**, **et bud**, and **en ny pakke**. Once Jonas knows which electrician and courier she means, the conversation can use **elektrikeren** and **budet**.

**Exercise: completing-reception-updates**

- **Context:** Continue Maja’s reception report using controlled visual or written cues about additional new arrivals.
- **Learner action:** Complete each update with a full presentational sentence. Select a suitable supplied verb and place the new indefinite participant after it.
- **Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates sustained use of the target construction across several connected first mentions.

## Transfer: moving into a new flat {#transfer-moving-into-a-new-flat role=recap objectives=obj-presentational-det-with-delayed-subjects-01}

Now change the setting. Emil has just received the keys to a flat. His friend Nora enters first and reports what she discovers: an old table in the kitchen, a lamp in the bedroom, a cat on the balcony, and a neighbour approaching the door.

This situation has the same communicative need as Maja’s report. Nora sees people and things that Emil has not yet noticed, so she presents them as new information.

Possible models from similar situations are:

::: examples
- no: Det står et bord på kjøkkenet.
- en: There’s a table standing in the kitchen.
- no: Det ligger en nøkkel på gulvet.
- en: There’s a key lying on the floor.
- no: Det sitter en person på balkongen.
- en: There’s a person sitting on the balcony.
- no: Det kommer en nabo bort til døra.
- en: A neighbour is coming over to the door.
:::

**Exercise: transferring-to-a-new-flat**

- **Purpose:** Transfer the grammar from reception to a changed everyday situation.
- **Context:** Nora is reporting discoveries in and around Emil’s new flat.
- **Learner action:** Produce a short sequence of controlled written reports from supplied scene cues. Introduce each previously unmentioned person or thing with **det + verb + an indefinite subject**.
- **Success meaning:** A successful response demonstrates that the learner can choose and produce the presentational construction outside the original dialogue while preserving its new-participant function.

{{exercise: transfer_to_new_flat}}
