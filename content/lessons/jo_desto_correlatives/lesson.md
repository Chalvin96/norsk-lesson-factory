## A kitchen decision {#a-kitchen-decision role=orient objectives=obj-jo-desto-correlatives-01}

Maja and Henrik are planning a kitchen renovation. They have received several offers, but they still have not chosen a supplier.

::: {.reading translation='The longer we wait, the later the kitchen will be finished.' dialogue_id='kitchen_decision' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jo lenger vi venter, desto senere blir kjøkkenet ferdig.
:::

::: {.reading translation='That is true. But the faster we decide, the greater the risk is that we will make the wrong choice.' dialogue_id='kitchen_decision' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Det er sant. Men jo fortere vi bestemmer oss, jo større er risikoen for at vi velger feil.
:::

::: {.reading translation='The more offers we compare, the easier it will be to choose.' dialogue_id='kitchen_decision' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Jo flere tilbud vi sammenligner, desto lettere blir det å velge.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Maybe. The more I look at the prices, the less certain I become.' dialogue_id='kitchen_decision' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Kanskje. Jo mer jeg ser på prisene, desto mindre sikker blir jeg.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Then we’ll compare the three best ones tonight. The earlier we send the order, the better.' dialogue_id='kitchen_decision' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Da sammenligner vi de tre beste i kveld. Jo tidligere vi sender bestillingen, jo bedre.
:::

Maja and Henrik are not merely listing separate facts. Each statement links two changing things. Waiting longer leads to later completion. Comparing more offers makes choosing easier. Looking at more prices makes Henrik less certain.

Norwegian commonly expresses this relationship with the paired patterns **jo ... desto** and **jo ... jo**.

**Request: Notice-the-linked-changes**

**Purpose:** Establish the meaning of the construction before focusing on its form.
**Context:** Maja and Henrik’s kitchen renovation.
**Learner action:** For each proportional statement in the dialogue, identify the two things that change and state whether the second increases or decreases as the first changes.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner understands that the construction presents two changes as connected, rather than as unrelated descriptions.

{{exercise: notice_the_linked_changes}}

## One change follows another {#one-change-follows-another role=model objectives=obj-jo-desto-correlatives-01}

The Norwegian construction corresponds closely to English **the more ..., the more ...**, **the less ..., the better ...**, and similar expressions.

Its two main forms are:

::: examples
- no: Jo mer vi planlegger, desto mindre tid kaster vi bort.
- en: The more we plan, the less time we waste.
- no: Jo mer vi planlegger, jo mindre tid kaster vi bort.
- en: The more we plan, the less time we waste.
:::

Both patterns express the same basic proportional relationship. **Jo ... desto** is a clear and common pairing, while **jo ... jo** is also natural and frequent. The second **jo** does not introduce a new meaning; it fills the same role as **desto** in this construction.

The words following **jo**, **desto**, and the second **jo** normally contain a comparative form (**komparativ**):

- mer — more
- mindre — less
- flere — more, with countable nouns
- færre — fewer
- bedre — better
- verre — worse
- tidligere — earlier
- senere — later
- større — bigger, greater
- lettere — easier

A compact model is:

**Jo + comparative change 1, desto/jo + comparative change 2.**

::: examples
- no: Jo mer du øver, desto tryggere blir du.
- en: The more you practise, the more confident you become.
- no: Jo mindre vi kjøper, jo mer sparer vi.
- en: The less we buy, the more we save.
:::

The two changes may move in the same direction:

::: examples
- no: Jo lenger hun arbeider med teksten, desto bedre blir den.
- en: The longer she works on the text, the better it becomes.
:::

They may also move in opposite directions:

::: examples
- no: Jo høyere renta blir, desto færre boliger har vi råd til.
- en: The higher the interest rate becomes, the fewer homes we can afford.
:::

The construction does not have to describe an exact mathematical rule. It presents a general relationship: as one thing changes, another tends to change too.

::: examples
- no: Jo bedre jeg kjenner byen, desto lettere finner jeg fram.
- en: The better I know the city, the more easily I find my way around.
:::

This does not claim that every extra day in the city produces a measurable improvement. It expresses a recognizable connection between familiarity and ease.

**Request: Match-the-direction-of-change**

**Purpose:** Practise choosing comparative meanings that create a logical proportional relationship.
**Context:** Everyday situations involving study, money, travel, and work.
**Learner action:** Complete paired statements so that the second comparative expresses the stated result of the first change. Some relationships should move in the same direction and others in opposite directions.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner can express the intended relationship between two changing scales.

{{exercise: match_the_direction_of_change}}

## Building the first part {#building-the-first-part role=model objectives=obj-jo-desto-correlatives-01}

The part beginning with the first **jo** establishes the condition or scale that changes.

::: examples
- no: Jo mer du leser ...
- en: The more you read ...
- no: Jo tidligere vi drar ...
- en: The earlier we leave ...
- no: Jo færre ting hun tar med ...
- en: The fewer things she brings ...
:::

Because this part has subordinate-clause word order, the subject normally comes before the finite verb:

::: examples
- no: Jo mer du leser ...
- en: The more you read ...
- no: Jo tidligere vi drar ...
- en: The earlier we leave ...
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jo mer leser du ...
- en: ✗ The more read you ...
:::

The comparative expression can be one word or a longer phrase:

::: examples
- no: Jo dyrere maten blir ...
- en: The more expensive the food becomes ...
- no: Jo flere detaljer vi legger til ...
- en: The more details we add ...
- no: Jo mindre tid jeg har om morgenen ...
- en: The less time I have in the morning ...
:::

Notice that **jo** is followed by the comparative information, not necessarily by the subject.

::: examples
- no: Jo større leilighet de kjøper ...
- en: The larger the apartment they buy ...
:::

Here, **større leilighet** is the comparative expression. The subject **de** follows it.

## Completing the relationship {#completing-the-relationship role=model objectives=obj-jo-desto-correlatives-01}

The second part begins with **desto** or another **jo** and states the corresponding change.

::: examples
- no: Jo mer du leser, desto større blir ordforrådet ditt.
- en: The more you read, the larger your vocabulary becomes.
- no: Jo tidligere vi drar, jo mindre trafikk møter vi.
- en: The earlier we leave, the less traffic we encounter.
:::

When the second part begins with a comparative expression, Norwegian uses main-clause verb-second order. The finite verb comes before the subject:

::: examples
- no: desto større blir ordforrådet ditt
- en: the larger your vocabulary becomes
- no: jo mindre trafikk møter vi
- en: the less traffic we encounter
:::

Compare the two parts:

::: examples
- no: Jo mer du leser, desto lettere blir teksten.
- en: The more you read, the easier the text becomes.
:::

- First part: **du leser** — subject before verb.
- Second part: **blir teksten** — verb before subject.

This contrast is easy to miss because English does not show the same change.

A common mistake is to keep subject–verb order in the second part:

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jo mer du leser, desto lettere teksten blir.
- en: ✗ The more you read, the easier the text becomes.
:::

Use:

::: examples
- no: Jo mer du leser, desto lettere blir teksten.
- en: The more you read, the easier the text becomes.
:::

The same order appears with **jo ... jo**:

::: examples
- no: Jo lenger vi venter, jo dyrere blir reisen.
- en: The longer we wait, the more expensive the trip becomes.
- no: Jo mer hun forklarer, jo mindre forstår jeg.
- en: The more she explains, the less I understand.
:::

**Request: Complete-the-proportional-frame**

**Purpose:** Build accurate full sentences from a clearly stated relationship.
**Context:** Short practical situations such as booking travel, preparing for an exam, and organizing a meeting.
**Learner action:** Complete each sentence with a first **jo** part and a second **desto** part, using the supplied comparative ideas while preserving correct clause order.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner can form a complete **jo ... desto** construction with an appropriate comparative in both parts.

{{exercise: complete_the_proportional_frame}}

**Request: Repair-the-clause-order**

**Purpose:** Prevent transfer of first-part word order into the second part.
**Context:** Proportional statements containing plausible word-order mistakes.
**Learner action:** Rewrite each statement so that the first part retains subordinate-clause order and the second part has verb-second order.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner controls the contrasting word order within the paired construction. Comparative choice is provided as context and is not the scored dimension.

{{exercise: repair_the_clause_order}}

## Choosing jo ... desto or jo ... jo {#choosing-jo-desto-or-jo-jo role=contrast objectives=obj-jo-desto-correlatives-01}

In most ordinary contexts, either pairing can express the intended relationship.

::: examples
- no: Jo nærmere vi kommer sentrum, desto dyrere blir hotellene.
- en: The closer we get to the city centre, the more expensive the hotels become.
- no: Jo nærmere vi kommer sentrum, jo dyrere blir hotellene.
- en: The closer we get to the city centre, the more expensive the hotels become.
:::

The first **jo** remains fixed. The second marker may be **desto** or **jo**:

- **jo ... desto**
- **jo ... jo**

Do not combine them in the same second part:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jo nærmere vi kommer sentrum, desto jo dyrere blir hotellene.
- en: ✗ The closer we get to the city centre, the more expensive the hotels become.
:::

Very short versions are also common when the missing meaning is obvious:

::: examples
- no: Jo før, jo bedre.
- en: The sooner, the better.
- no: Jo mer, desto bedre.
- en: The more, the better.
:::

These compact expressions follow the same pattern, even though the verbs and subjects are understood from context.

**Request: Preserve-meaning-with-the-alternative-pairing**

**Purpose:** Make both approved forms available without suggesting a difference in basic proportional meaning.
**Context:** Complete proportional statements drawn from messages, conversations, and practical advice.
**Learner action:** Rewrite **jo ... desto** statements as **jo ... jo**, and **jo ... jo** statements as **jo ... desto**, while keeping the relationship and word order unchanged.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner can use either pairing to express the same proportional change.

{{exercise: preserve_meaning_with_the_alternative_pairing}}

## Making the relationship explicit {#making-the-relationship-explicit role=contrast objectives=obj-jo-desto-correlatives-01}

Compare a simple sequence of facts with a proportional statement:

::: examples
- no: Vi venter lenger. Reisen blir dyrere.
- en: We wait longer. The trip becomes more expensive.
:::

::: examples
- no: Jo lenger vi venter, desto dyrere blir reisen.
- en: The longer we wait, the more expensive the trip becomes.
:::

The first version gives two facts. The second explicitly presents the higher price as connected with the longer wait.

Similarly:

::: examples
- no: Jeg bruker mindre tid på telefonen. Jeg får gjort mer.
- en: I spend less time on my phone. I get more done.
:::

::: examples
- no: Jo mindre tid jeg bruker på telefonen, jo mer får jeg gjort.
- en: The less time I spend on my phone, the more I get done.
:::

When building such a sentence, ask two questions:

1. What is the first changing scale?
2. What happens to the second scale as the first changes?

Then place each comparative expression immediately after its marker.

::: examples
- no: Jo flere spørsmål studentene stiller, desto tydeligere blir forklaringen.
- en: The more questions the students ask, the clearer the explanation becomes.
:::

First scale: number of questions.
Second scale: clarity of the explanation.

::: examples
- no: Jo mindre bagasje vi tar med, jo enklere blir reisen.
- en: The less luggage we bring, the easier the journey becomes.
:::

First scale: amount of luggage.
Second scale: ease of travel.

Do not replace the comparative with an ordinary positive form:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jo mye du øver, desto god blir du.
- en: ✗ The much you practise, the good you become.
:::

Use comparative forms:

::: examples
- no: Jo mer du øver, desto bedre blir du.
- en: The more you practise, the better you become.
:::

An **enn** phrase is not normally needed. The sentence compares changing degrees, not one person or object directly with another.

::: examples
- no: Jo raskere hun går, desto varmere blir hun.
- en: The faster she walks, the warmer she becomes.
:::

Not:

::: examples
- no: ✗ Jo raskere enn hun går, desto varmere blir hun.
- en: ✗ The faster than she walks, the warmer she becomes.
:::

**Request: Combine-two-observations**

**Purpose:** Move from separate facts to an explicit proportional relationship.
**Context:** Pairs of observations about habits, prices, schedules, and learning.
**Learner action:** Combine each pair into one natural sentence using either **jo ... desto** or **jo ... jo**.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner can identify two changing scales and encode their connection in the target construction.

{{exercise: combine_two_observations}}

## Back to Maja and Henrik {#back-to-maja-and-henrik role=model objectives=obj-jo-desto-correlatives-01}

That evening, Maja and Henrik compare their three preferred offers.

::: {.reading translation='The more carefully I read the offers, the clearer the differences become.' dialogue_id='kitchen_evening' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Jo nøyere jeg leser tilbudene, desto tydeligere blir forskjellene.
:::

::: {.reading translation='And the more additional costs we find, the less attractive the cheapest offer becomes.' dialogue_id='kitchen_evening' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Og jo flere ekstra kostnader vi finner, jo mindre attraktivt blir det billigste tilbudet.
:::

::: {.reading translation='The fewer changes we request, the sooner the company can finish.' dialogue_id='kitchen_evening' speaker_id='henrik' speaker_name="Henrik" character_id="henrik"}
Jo færre endringer vi ber om, desto raskere kan firmaet bli ferdig.
:::

::: {.reading translation='Then we’ll choose the simplest arrangement. The simpler the plan is, the easier it will be to stay within budget.' dialogue_id='kitchen_evening' speaker_id='maja' speaker_name="Maja" character_id="maja"}
Da velger vi det enkleste opplegget. Jo enklere planen er, jo lettere blir det å holde budsjettet.
:::

Their decision now depends on several proportional relationships:

- More careful reading produces clearer differences.
- More extra costs make the cheapest offer less attractive.
- Fewer requested changes allow earlier completion.
- A simpler plan makes the budget easier to maintain.

The grammar lets them compress each relationship into one connected statement.

**Request: Advise-Maja-and-Henrik**

**Purpose:** Use the construction for a practical conclusion within the anchor situation.
**Context:** New facts about the renovation schedule, costs, and number of requested changes.
**Learner action:** Produce short written recommendations by linking each given change to its likely result with **jo ... desto** or **jo ... jo**.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner can use the target form to express a logical proportional relationship in context, rather than merely copying a model sentence.

{{exercise: advise_maja_and_henrik}}

## Cumulative transfer: planning a journey {#cumulative-transfer-planning-a-journey role=recap objectives=obj-jo-desto-correlatives-01}

Imagine that Nora and Elias are arranging a weekend train journey. Ticket prices are rising, early trains are less crowded, and carrying more luggage makes changing trains harder.

**Request: Build-journey-planning-statements**

**Purpose:** Transfer the target construction to a new everyday situation.
**Context:** Nora and Elias must decide when to book, which departure to take, and how much luggage to bring.
**Learner action:** Turn the supplied journey facts into complete proportional statements. Use both **jo ... desto** and **jo ... jo** across the activity, including at least one relationship in which one scale increases while the other decreases.
**Success demonstrates:** The learner can independently select comparative forms, connect two changing scales, and maintain accurate word order in both variants.
